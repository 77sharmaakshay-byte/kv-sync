Hon’ble Ravindra Maithani, J.(Oral)
Instant appeal is preferred against the judgment 
and order dated 04.09.2021/ 06.09.2021, passed in Special 
Sessions Trial No.114 of 2017, by the court of 
FTC/Additional Sessions Judge/Special Judge, POCSO, 
Rudrapur, District Udham Singh Nagar. By the impugned 
judgment and order, the appellant has been convicted 
under Sections 363, 366, 376 (2) IPC and Section 6 of the 
Protection of Children From Sexual Offences Act, 2012 (for 
short, “the Act”) and sentenced under Sections 363, 366, 
376 (2) IPC. In this appeal, the appellant has filed a bail 
application.
2. Heard learned counsel for the parties and 
perused the record.2
3. According to the prosecution case, the appellant 
on 26.12.2016, at about 4:00-5:00 PM took away the victim 
girl, who was 17 years of age then on his motorcycle. The 
mother of the victim tried to stop her, she raised alarm, but 
she was not successful in her attempt. Subsequently, the 
victim was recovered. Her statement was recorded. She 
revealed that the appellant raped her in the Bank where he 
was working and under the threat of making the intimate 
photos viral raped her on multiple times. This is what the 
victim stated in the court. There have been other witnesses 
also, who supported the prosecution case. The court below 
based on the school record and other material recorded the 
finding that the date of birth of the victim is 17.09.1999, 
which means that she was 17 years of age on the date of 
occurrence. It was also established that the appellant was 
married having two children when the judgment was 
recorded. Although, today a statement is given that now the 
appellant has three children including a four month’s baby 
girl.
4. Learned counsel for the appellant would submit 
that the appellant was on bail during trial. He has three 
children. One of them is four months old, who is to be 
vaccinated. The father of the appellant is 70 years old. He is 3
suffering from spondylitis. In her statement, in para 41, the 
victim has admitted that during investigation or at the stage 
of recording statement under Section 164 of the Code of 
Criminal Procedure, 1973 (for short, “the Code”). she did 
not reveal the specific date when the incident took place. 
Therefore, it is argued that it is a case fit for bail.
5. On the other hand, learned State counsel would 
submit that the victim was minor. She was just 17 years of 
age when she was taken away from the custody of her 
father. The accused was 28 years old at the time of incident 
He was married having three children. The victim and the 
witnesses, her mother, father and brother have supported 
the prosecution case. Hence, it is not a fit case for bail.
6. The grounds for bail which are available to an
accused during trial are quite distinct and slightly different 
than the grounds that may be available to a convict in 
appeal. The presumption of innocence which an accused 
carries with him during trial is not available to him at the 
stage of appeal because today he is not an accused but a 
convict.
7. The victim in her statement given in the court 
has supported the prosecution case and has narrated as to 4
how the offence has been committed and she was taken 
away by the appellant. Her mother and father have also 
supported the prosecution case. The date of birth of the 
victim is conclusively recorded by the court as 17.09.1999. 
It means she was 17 years of age on the date of incident. 
The appellant was 33 years of age when he was examined 
under Section 313 of the Code on 18.10.2019, it means he 
was 30 years of age on the date of incident.
8. Having considered the gravity of offence, this 
Court is of the view that it is not a case fit for bail.
Accordingly, the bail application deserves to be rejected.
9. The bail application is rejected.
10. List this matter for final hearing.
