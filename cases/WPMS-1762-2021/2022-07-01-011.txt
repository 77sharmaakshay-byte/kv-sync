Mr. Bilal Ahmed, Advocate, for the applicant 
in Intervention Application No. 6 of 2022. 
The petitioner was a fair price shop licence 
holder, for running the fair price shop, which was
situated at Village Maharajpur Kalan, Block 
Laksar, District Haridwar, and on the basis of 
certain complaints and irregularities, which were 
pointed out against the malfunctioning of the fair 
price shop, conducted by the petitioner, the 
respondent Nos. 2 and 3, had proceeded to cancel 
the fair price shop licence of the petitioner, against 
which, an Appeal was preferred before the 
Commissioner the appellate authority, and the 
Appeal too was dismissed by respondent No. 2, by 
an order dated 5th February, 2021, which is now a 
subject matter of consideration in the present Writ 
Petition. 
In fact, the challenge, which has been given, 
by the petitioner to the impugned order, is qua the 
competent authority, i.e. respondent Nos. 1 and 3,
who have passed the impugned orders of 
cancellation of the fair price shop licence of the 
petitioner. 
During the pendency of the Writ Petition, the 
complainant, who claims himself to be the resident 
of the village and being aggrieved against the act of 
malfunction of the petitioner’s fair price shop, has 
filed an Intervention Application, contending 
thereof that since he was the complainant against 
the act of petitioner, he would be the necessary 
party to be impleaded in the Writ Petition for an effective adjudication of the Writ Petition. 
This Court is of the view, that the inter se 
relationship of fair price shop licence dealer is 
governed as per the terms and conditions of the 
licence issued by the competent authority, i.e. 
District Supply Officer, or any other State Agency 
as authorised as per the terms of the government 
order, which itself contemplates the procedure for 
suspension of licence and its cancellation, as well as 
the appellate provision. 
So far as the complainant is concerned, the 
complainant cannot be said to be aggrieved party at 
all against the order of cancelling the fair price shop 
licence of the petitioner, and its subsequent 
affirmation by the Appellate Authority. 
The complainant, for the purposes of 
deciding the present Writ Petition, against the two 
impugned orders, is not a necessary party at all,
merely for the reason being that the action taken 
against the petitioner was as a consequence of the 
enquiry conducted by the respondents/ State 
Agency, against the petitioner, which is now a
subject matter of judicial scrutiny and to decide the 
action taken by respondents against the petitioner, 
the complainant is not a necessary party, because it 
is not a case, where the Writ Petition cannot be 
decided in the absence of the complainant to 
intervene in the Writ Petition.
Hence the Intervention Application No. 6 of 
2022, filed by Yashveer Singh, claiming himself to 
be the complainant and resident of the village 
would not be a necessary party, qua the action taken 
against the petitioner of cancelling his fair price 
shop licence. 
Thus, the Intervention Application No. 6 of 
2022, is misconceived and the same is rejected. 
The Writ Petition, which already stands 
admitted, is directed to be listed in the 1st week of 
September, 2022, under the head of hearing.
