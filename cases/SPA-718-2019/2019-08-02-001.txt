The applicant herein was appointed as an Instructor by order 
dated 27th July, 2011 through a regular process of selection. The 
respondent-writ petitioner, whose services were regularized from 
20.06.2016, was, however, extended the benefit of seniority from 
his initial appointment as Prashikshan Mitra on 15.03.2002. 
2. The 2011 Regularisation Rules provided for services to be 
reckoned only from the date of regularisation and, despite its 
validity being questioned in the writ petition, the learned Single 
Judge, without even examining its constitutional validity, granted 
the respondent-writ petitioner relief contrary to the 2011 
Regularisation Rules. The consequence, of the order under appeal,
is that the applicant would now be junior to the respondent-writ 
petitioner. 
3. Since the learned Single Judge had relied on the order passed 
in Writ Petition (S/S) No. 2684 of 2015 dated 05.07.2018, against 
which Special Appeal No. 940 of 2018 is still pending on the file of 
this Court, we see no reason to deny the applicant leave to appeal. 
The leave to appeal is granted. The Application seeking leave to 
appeal is allowed. 2
CLMA No. 9780 of 2019
4. The limitation for preferring an appeal against the order
passed by a learned Single Judge would only apply to a party to the 
writ proceedings before the learned Single Judge. The period of 
limitation for a person, who though not a party to the writ petition 
but is aggrieved by the order of the learned Single Judge, must be 
computed from the date of his knowledge of the order under appeal. 
It would be wholly unnecessary, therefore, to file an application for 
condonation of delay, if the appeal is filed within time from the date 
of knowledge of the order. The Application for condonation of 
delay is, accordingly, closed. 
5. Admit.
