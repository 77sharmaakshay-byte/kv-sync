Upon hearing the learned Counsel, the Court made the following Order: 
1. By the instant writ petition, the petitioner is praying 
for the following reliefs:-
(i) A writ order or direction in the nature of certiorari 
quashing the impugned order dated 05.10.2023 
(Annexure-10) passed by the DM Haridwar.
(ii) A writ order or direction in the nature of 
mandamus directing the District Magistrate Haridwar to 
follow the demarcation done earlier on 14.09.2020
(Annexure-8) of the land of BHEL admeasuring 13.4 
hectare in Khasra No. 451.
2. Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior Advocate submits 
that there is no relevancy of the order dated 17.01.2019, 
passed by the Assistant Collector-1st and the order dated 
22.06.2010 passed by Chief Revenue Officer in Revision 
No. 3/2008-09.
3. Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior Advocate assisted by 
Mr. B.S. Adhikari, learned counsel for the petitioner 
submits that the land falling in Khasra No. 451 
measuring 21.07 hectare was acquired for BHEL in the 
year 1962 out of which 8.3 hectare land was released in 
the favour of the Irrigation Department, and, as such, 2
remaining 13.4 hectare was still with the possession of 
the BHEL.
4. In this writ petition, intervention application is 
moved by those who are displaced persons, as their land 
were acquired for construction of Tehri Dam Project. It is 
contended by the learned Senior Advocate Mr. Rajendra 
Dobhal, who is representing the interveners that under 
Rehabilitation Policy of the Government applicants are 
legally entitled for plot of 200 meters for residential 
purpose and half acre of the land is for the agricultural 
purposes. It is submitted by the learned senior advocate 
for the intervener that the applicants were allotted plot of 
200 meters for construction of their houses and half acre 
of land for agricultural purposes by the Director 
Rehabilitation by order dated 29.06.2010, but, despite 
the aforesaid orders of allotment and possession over the 
plots, which were allotted to the interveners were not 
given by the Director Rehabilitation. Consequently, writ 
petitions were preferred before this Court bearing WPMS 
No. 2110 of 2010, WPMS No. 1870 of 2010, WPMS No. 
2273 of 2010 and WPMS No. 305 of 2011. All these writ 
petitions were clubbed together and decided by this 
Court by judgment and order dated 27.04.2012, which 
the interveners has enclosed with the intervention 
application. All these writ petitions were allowed and 
mandamus were issued to handover the possession of 
the agriculture plots, as detailed in the allotment order 
dated 29.06.2010, within a period of eight weeks from 
the date of production of a certified copy of the order 
before the Director Rehabilitation, Tehri Project, New 
Tehri. It is submitted that despite the order of this Court, 
possession were not given. Thereafter, a contempt 
petition being CLCON No. 10 of 2013 was preferred 3
before this Court wherein Mr. Ranjeet Kumar Sinha, Addl. 
Secretary Department of Home and Mr. Y.K. Pant, 
Director Rehabilitation, New Tehri were present in 
person. The operative para of the order dated 
13.07.2015, passed in the said contempt application is 
being extracted hereunder:-
“Mr. Subhash Upadhyay, Chief Standing Counsel for 
the State of Uttarakhand and both the officers present in 
court submitted that actual possession of the allotted plots 
could not be handed over to the contempt applicants 
because of pendency of civil litigation between BHEL and 
THDC. They further contended that if civil case is decided in 
favour of the THDC, possession of the allotted plots to the 
contempt applicants shall be delivered immediately after 
pronouncement of judgment. They further contended that in 
the event, case is decided against the THDC i.e. in favour of 
the BHEL appropriate alternative developed plots of the 
same dimensions in the same locality shall be allotted to the 
contempt applicants within fifteen days after pronouncement 
of judgment by the trial court and actual possession shall be 
handed over to the contempt applicants forthwith.”
5. It is contended that in compliance of the 
undertaking given in the contempt proceedings, now, the
allotment has been made by the District Magistrate by 
order dated 05.10.2023, which has been assailed in the 
present writ petition.
6. As it appears from the order passed in the contempt 
petition, a statement was given that actual possession of 
the allotted plots could not be handed over due to the 
pendency of the civil litigation between BHEL and THDC. 
It was also contended that if civil case is decided in 
favour of the THDC, possession of the allotted plots to
the contempt applicants shall be delivered immediately 4
after pronouncement of judgment. It is also stated that if 
case is decided against the THDC, i.e., in favour of the 
BHEL appropriate alternative developed plots of the same 
dimension in the same locality shall be allotted to the 
contempt applicants within fifteen days.
7. Mr. Rajendra Dobhal, learned Senior Advocate for 
the intervenors submits that civil litigation which was 
pending between THDC and BHEL in O.S No. 196 of 
2010, preferred by the BHEL for seeking permanent 
injunction against THDC was dismissed on 21.11.2019 
and while deciding the said suit, issue no. 2 was framed, 
which reads as under:-

9. Against the judgment dated 21.11.2019, passed in 
O.S. No. 21 of 2010, a Civil Appeal No. 114 of 2019 was 5
preferred by the BHEL before the 5th Addl. District Judge, 
Haridwar and the said appeal was dismissed by judgment 
and order dated 05.12.2022. 
10. Mr. Rajendra Dobhal, learned Senior Advocate for 
the intervenors submits that the order whereby the 
appeal preferred by the BHEL was dismissed is not 
enclosed in the writ petition.
11. In para 10 of the writ petition there is a reference of 
the judgment rendered by the Civil Court in O.S. No. 19 
of 2010 and further statement has been given that 
against the judgment of the Civil Court as well as of the 
Appellate Court, BHEL is preparing to file an appeal.
12. It is submitted by Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior 
Advocate assisted by Mr. B.S. Adhikari, learned counsel
for the petitioner that after filing of the writ petition a 
second appeal, i.e. SA No. 181 of 2023, has been 
preferred by the BHEL which is a time barred appeal and 
is still in the Registry of this Court.
13. As it appears from the order passed by the Civil 
Judge (Jr. Div.) dated 21.11.2019 wherein issue no. 2 
was dealt with that it was the case of the THDC that they 
were in possession of Khasra No. 451 measuring 8.3 
hectare. The order impugned which was passed by the 
District Magistrate dated 05.10.2023, reveals that the 
allotment were done in respect of Khasra No. 451 part of 
which, as per the judgment rendered by the Civil Court,
is in possession of the THDC.
14. Mr. Rajendra Dobhal, learned Senior Advocate for 
the intervenors vehemently disputing that the order
dated 17.01.2019, passed by the Assistant Collector-1st6
Class and the order dated 22.06.2010 passed by Chief 
Revenue Officer in Revision No. 3/2008- has no 
relevance, as contended by the learned counsel for the 
petitioner, and submits that these orders are also 
relevant because this is in respect of the same land 
which was meant for the allottees, was the subject 
matter of the said revenue suit, which was also decided 
against the BHEL against which revision was also filed 
and the revision was also dismissed and thereafter writ 
petition was also preferred but the writ petition was also 
dismissed on the basis that proceedings are summary in 
nature and writ petition is not maintainable but that 
orders have not been brought on record.
15. All these aspects are required to be considered after 
inviting counter affidavit.
16. Respondent(s) may file counter affidavit within four 
weeks. Two weeks’ thereafter is granted to the learned 
counsel for the petitioner to file rejoinder affidavit. 
17. The learned counsel for the petitioner may file 
objection to the intervention application within a period 
of four weeks.
18. List in the week commencing 26.02.2024.
19. The interim relief application will be heard on that 
day.
