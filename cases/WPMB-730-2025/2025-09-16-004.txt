ORDER:(per Mr. G. Narendar, C.J.)
The instant writ petition was preferred by the 
petitioner on 23.08.2025 to seek the following reliefs:-
“(i) Issue orders, directions, writ in the nature of certiorari 
quashing the order dated 21.08.2025 (Annexure No.2) whereby the 
petitioner has been held as ineligible for consideration of Technical 
Bids and order dated 22/ 23.08.2025 (Annexure No.3), issued by 
respondent nos.1, 2, 3 whereby the Financial Bids have been 
opened and respondent no.5 has been declared as Lowest One (L￾1).
(ii) Issue orders, directions, writ in the nature of mandamus 
directing respondent no.1, 2, 3 to undertake the Technical and 
Financial Bid opening exercise de-novo after considering the 
Technical and Financial Bid of the petitioner.”
2. We have heard the learned counsel for the 
petitioner, learned Chief Standing Counsel for the State, 
and learned Senior Counsel appearing for respondent 
no.5.

5. A bare reading of Annexure-9 would disclose that 
the same has been acknowledged by the Executive 
Engineer, Public Works Department, P.D., Pithoragarh 
2on 04.08.2025, and it also details that the message is 
acknowledged at 13 hours 26 minutes and 21 seconds. 
Despite the same, the Tender Scrutiny Committee has 
given a vague reasoning that the Bank Guarantee does 
not comply with Condition 18.2.
6. It is vociferously contended by the learned Chief 
Standing Counsel and the learned Senior Counsel that 
the SWIFT is a global standard for secure international 
bank messaging, while SFMS is developed by the Banks 
for domestic financial messaging and as the message 
has not been sent by the Bank on the SFMS platform, it 
is a violation of the conditions.
7. In short, accepted global standards are of no 
consequences is the argument. Whether such argument 
can be appreciated or not, is something which requires 
hearing.
8. In that view of the matter and also in view of the 
second reason assigned by the Committee for rejecting 
the bid which, prima facie, appears to be contrary to 
the Registration Act and the Stamp Act, and also in 
view of the undisputed fact that the petitioner is the 
3lowest bidder (L1), we deem it necessary to stay all 
further proceedings.
9. At this stage, learned Senior Counsel would place 
reliance on Paragraph No.27 of the ruling of the High 
Court of Delhi, in the case of “VRC Construction 
(India) Private Limited vs. National Highway 
Authority of India, reported in 2024 SCC OnLine 
Del 8447.” Paragraph No.27 reads as under:-
“27. From the record, this Court finds that the respondent/ 
NHAI had revised the bid security on 20th February, 2024 and 
extended the time for bid security submission till 27th February, 
2024. Though the petitioner appears to have taken immediate steps 
to furnish the ABG of Rs. 4 lakhs on 22nd February, 2024 itself, yet 
due to some error and inadvertence, violated the procedure while 
undertaking the SFMS mode to upload the ABG on the portal. 
Though the mistake appears to be inadvertent, yet the respondent/ 
NHAL cannot be faulted with for having annulled the entire Tender 
on the basis that the action of acceptance of ABG subsequent to the 
date of closure of the bid may be misused as a precedent by 
unscrupulous bides. Weighing the conflicting interests, this Court is 
of the opinion that the action of the respondent/ NHAI inviting the 
petitioner as well as other bidders for a fresh bid has allayed the 
apprehension of misuse so far as the respondent/ NHAL is 
concerned and simultaneously, provided a level playing field to the 
petitioner by giving it another chance to submit its bid afresh.”
10. The facts narrated above would clearly 
demonstrate that this is not a case of omission to 
present the BG and the possession of the Bank 
Guarantee with the Authorities is also not disputed, but 
that it is not properly communicated to the Authorities. 
It is also not the case that the communication by the 
Bank has been rejected by the tender accepting 
authority. It is mere perception that it is not in 
4accordance with Condition 18.2.
11. As recorded above, in other words, the 
respondents would want this Court to accept that 
adopting international standards is not an acceptable 
standard.
