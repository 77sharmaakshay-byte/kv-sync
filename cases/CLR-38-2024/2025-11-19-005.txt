1. On 29.10.2025, record of Civil Revision No. 38 of 
2024 was summoned and the matter was heard at 
length and record of O.S. No. 341 of 1989, O.S. No. 211 
of 1990 and O.S. No. 123 of 2012 were summoned.
2. Mr. Siddhartha Singh, learned counsel for the 
respondent placed before this Court the certified copies
of certain documents of O.S. NO. 211 of 1990, the copy 
of which, in advance, was supplied to Mr. Arvind 
Vashistha, learned Senior Advocate as well as to Mr. 
Aditya Pratap Singh, learned counsel, who appears for 
the respondent in Civil Revision No. 38 of 2024. The first 
document is an application under Order 22 Rule 4 read 
with Section 9 and 151 of CPC moved by the plaintiffs of 2
Suit on 16.01.2006, giving reference of three persons 
showing them to be legal heirs of Late Brijmohan Lal 
Gupta and Ramal Rani in which notices were issued to 
the so called legal heir of Late Brijmohan Lal Gupta and 
thereafter an affidavit was filed by one of the so called 
legal heir, namely, Anil Kumar by giving pedigree of 
family of “Late Rehtulal” who have two sons 
Basheshwardayal and Kriparam; Basheshwardayal have 
two sons, namely, Brijmohal Lal Gupta and Seva Ram 
and daughter Smt. Sushila Devi and Suhila Devi was 
having one daughter Rajkumari Ramalrani Gupta;
Kriparam was having two sons, namely, Ramratan and 
Shyamlal; Ramratan have three sons, namely, Aadesh 
Kumar, Anil Kumar and Pradeep Kumar. The affidavit 
was supported with two Wills executed by Rajkumari 
Ramalrani Gupta dated 11.10.1997 and other will of 
Late Brijmohan Lal Gupta dated 11.10.1997.
On the application of plaintiff under Order 22 Rule 
4 of CPC, the defendant Abdul Gaffur filed an application 
by raising certain objections that date of death of 
deceased is not mentioned and so called legal heirs have 
no locus standi to become party in the proceeding and 
they are not the legal successor or heir of Late 
Brijmohan Lal Gupta, and, in such a situation, notices 
have to be issued to the Administration General of 
Uttarakhand. The application moved by the plaintiff and 
the affidavit of Anil Kumar as well as application of the 
defendant were dealt with by the Trial Court on 
20.11.2007 by allowing the application, and to implead 
the so called legal heirs as defendant in place of 
Brijmohan Lal Gupta directions were issued to make 
necessary compliance. Due to non-compliance, 3
ultimately O.S. No. 211 of 1990 was dismissed for want 
of prosecution and the same was not revived. 
3. By showing certified copies of certain documents of
O.S. No. 211 of 1990, Mr. Siddhartha Singh submits 
that Aadesh Kumar, Anil Kumar and Pradeep Kumar, 
being legal heirs of Brijmohan Lal Gupta became 
absolute owner of the property in question. He further 
submits that arguments, as advanced by Mr. Vashistha 
that respondent/plaintiff is neither the owner nor the 
landlord of the property in question is misconceived. Mr. 
Siddhartha Singh further argued that after O.S. No. 211 
of 1990 which was dismissed for want of prosecution, 
since, legal heirs of Brijmohan Lal Gupta were not 
substituted, another suit being O.S. No. 123 of 2012 
was filed seeking declaration over the property in 
question owned by Late Brijmohan Lal Gupta by 
impleading legal heirs of Brijmohan Lal Gupta as 
defendants. He also pointed out that the legal heirs of 
Late Brijmohan Lal Gupta, namely, Aadesh Kumar, Anil 
Kumar and Pradeep Kumar filed one eviction suit against 
Abdul Gaffur and Mohd. Afzal who earlier claimed 
adverse possession over the property in question, 
however, the proceedings of said suit was closed in 
terms of the compromise entered between the legal 
heirs of Abdul Gaffur and legal heirs of Late Brijmohan 
Lal Gupta. Simultaneously, O.S. No. 123 of 2012 was 
also decreed in terms of the compromise entered on 
21.12.2012.
4. Mr. Siddhartha Singh concluded his arguments by 
submitting that by way of compromise decree dated 4
21.12.2012, the respondent/plaintiff become owner and 
landlord of the property in question.
5. On the other side, Mr. Vashistha, learned Senior 
Advocate for the revisionist in Civil Revision No. 112 of 
2025 seriously opposed the submission as advanced by 
Mr. Siddhartha Singh by submitting that the scope of 
application moved Order 22 Rule 4 read with Section 9 
and 151 of CPC is only for limited purposes to represent 
the deceased in that particular suit, and, therefore, 
automatically being legal heir they cannot become 
absolute owner of the property. He submits that the 
scope of Order 22 Rule 4 and 5 CPC is only for limited 
purposes and he also pointed out that not only this, in 
fact, it was the case of Abdul Gaffur in O.S. No. 211 of 
1990 which is evident from the application moved by 
Abdul Gaffur on 25.01.2007, a certified copy of which 
has been placed before this Court by Mr. Siddhartha 
Singh. He submits that merely that Aadesh Kumar, Anil 
Kumar and Pradeep Kumar were substituted being legal 
heirs in place of Late Brijmohan Lal Gupta does not 
mean that they become absolute owner.
6. On the other side, Mr. Aditya Pratap Singh, who is 
appearing for respondent in CLR No. 38 of 2024 submits 
that while dismissing the suit of respondent/plaintiff
there is no decision of the court on merit to show that 
the person with whom compromise was settled by the 
predecessor were the actual legal heirs of Late 
Brijmohan Lal Gupta. This aspect has been dealt with by 
the Trial Court in para 41 onwards to para 43 which are 
being reproduced herein as under:-5
“41. One of the reason being, the plaintiff having failed to 
established that the alleged Aadesh Kumar, with who 
compromise deed was settled in respect of suit property, 
was a legal heir of Late Shree Brijmohan Lal Gupta. It is 
pertinent to note that the alleged compromise decree is 
in respect of a property over which adverse possession 
was claimed. The plaintiff has claimed this Aadesh Kumar 
to be the nephew of Late Shree Brijmohan Lal Gupta.
42. By necessary implication this would mean that Late 
Shree Brijmohan Lal Gupta and Rajkumari RamalRani 
died issueless. There is no averment, no proof that they 
demises issueless. It is not proved in what capacity was 
the alleged AadeshKumar was accepted to be a Legal heir 
of Legal Representative of Late Shree Brijmohan Lal 
Gupta. There is no will or any other testamentary 
document, or deed of conveyance etc, on record which 
was likely to show him in this picture.
43. There is no decision of a Court on merit to show 
that the person with whom a compromise was settled by 
the predecessor of the plaintiff, were the actual legal 
heirs of Shree Brijmohan Lal Gupta. For that matter, any 
Tom Dick and Harry could claim himself to be legal heir or 
representative of Late Shree Brijmohan Lal Gupta and 
enter into a compromise deed in respect of the property 
of latter for easy bucks. Thus, there is no way that the 
plaintiff’s predecessor can be said to perfected his alleged 
claim over the property by entering into a compromise 
deed with someone who is not even proved to be legal 
heir of Late Shree Brijmohan Lal Gupta.”
