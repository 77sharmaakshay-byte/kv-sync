SA No. 188 of 2019 
Hon’ble Sharad Kumar Sharma, J.
Mr. Ramji Srivastava, Advocate for the 
appellant. 
Under the certain settled pre-conditions
inter se between the parties to the Second 
Appeal for executing the sale deed in relation 
to the property, which constituted to be the 
property of the sale deed, which was 
conveyed on 11.11.1994 in favour of the 
defendant/appellant, herein, admittedly, the 
sale deed was executed and it was with the 
rider that the tenement, which happens to be 
altogether a different part of the property, 
which was occupied by the 
defendant/appellant, herein, as a tenant, that 
he would vacate the same and hand over the 
vacant possession and all the installations
existing on the super structure of the tenement 
would constitute as to be the part of the sale 
consideration for the sale deed dated 
11.11.1994, same was agreed not be removed 
by the parties to the appeal. It was agreed by 
the defendant/appellant, that he would not be 
removing the super structure existing on it. 
This fact pertaining to the super 
structure constituting the sale consideration is 
a fact which was narrated in the sale deed 
dated 11.11.1994, but, however, on account of 
one of the conditions that the 
defendant/appellant would be vacating the 
premises only after the initiation of the 
proceedings under Section 21 (1) (a) of the 
Act and ultimately, the plaintiff/respondent, 
instituted the proceedings under Section 21 
(1) (a), wherein a reference has also been 
made to the sale deed itself and also with 
regard to the conveyance of the property 
measuring 211 square feet in favour of the 
defendant/appellant. Subsequently, the registration of the 
present suit for cancellation of the sale deed 
dated 11.11.1994, allegedly based upon an 
agreement for sale dated 9th November, 1994 
was absolutely preposterous, for the reason 
being that ultimately the unregistered 
agreement dated 9th November, 1994, would 
have no relevance once the registered sale
deed has been executed by the plaintiff/
respondent in favour of the present 
defendant/appellant. 
Hence, the very foundation of the 
institution of the suit, based upon the 
unregistered agreement for sale dated 9th
November, 1994, would have lost its 
significance once a registered deed of 
conveyance has been executed. 
In that view of the matter, the 
subsequent institution of the suit for 
cancellation of the sale deed would be 
untenable, which has been ultimately decreed 
by the learned Courts below by the impugned 
judgment under challenge in the present 
Second Appeal. 
In that view of the matter, the Second 
Appeal deserves to be admitted and it is 
henceforth admitted on the substantial 
question of law to the effect that :-
1. What would be the effect of the so 
called agreement for sale dated 9th
November, 1994, which was 
unregistered and particularly 
thereafter when a registered sale 
deed has been executed on 
11.11.1994? Whether it would 
merge, if at all that agreement 
existed ?
2. What would be the effect of the 
condition of sale deed to be 
constituting as a sale consideration 
by way of an undertaking of non removal of the super structure 
existing on the tenement occupied 
by the defendant/appellant, which 
was agreed to be vacated ?
3. Whether the impugned judgments 
passed by the Courts below suffer 
from perverse finding of fact 
applying wrong and incorrect 
principles of Evidence Act by 
relying on registered agreement 
dated 09.11.1994. 
4. Whether both the courts below are 
justified in ignoring the settled 
principle of law that the sale deed 
cannot be rendered null and void on 
the ground of non payment of sale 
consideration.
Issue notice to the respondents. 
Summon the lower Courts record.
List thereafter. 
In the meantime, till the next date of 
listing, the plaintiff/respondents are restrained 
from dealing with the property, which was 
subject matter of deed of conveyance dated 
11.11.1994 in any manner whatsoever.
