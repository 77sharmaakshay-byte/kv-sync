COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
WPSS No. 208 of 2021
With connected petitions
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J. 
1. Mr. Parikshit Saini, learned counsel for the 
petitioners.
2. Mr. Tarun Mohan, learned Brief Holder for the 
State.
3. On 05.01.2026 this court take serious note of the 
fact that the impugned order has been passed by the 
Chief Education Officer, Haridwar, in flagrant violation 
of orders passed on 27.05.2020 and 29.05.2020 passed in 
different petitions, whereby, the recovery was stayed.
4. This court also further take note of one of the PIL 
bearing WPPIL No. 42 of 2020, which was disposed of 
with a direction to the respondents to decide the 
representation of the PIL petitioner.
5. This court also further take note that while 
deciding the representation of PIL petitioner the Chief 
Education Officer while passing the order impugned 
dated 15.12.2020 totally overlooked the interim order 
passed on 27.05.2020 and 29.05.2020 though the 
reference of the order has been given in the order 
impugned.
6. By the order impugned the Chief Education 
Officer passed an order for recovery in six equal 
instalments by observing that such recovery shall be 
subject to the final outcome of the pending petitions in 
which the interim order with regard to recovery of stay 
was passed on 27.05.2020 and 29.05.2020, which are 
clubbed with this petition.
7. In paragraph 19 of this petition a specific 
averment has been made that the action of Chief 
Education Officer, Haridwar while passing the 
impugned order is contemptuous act since the same 
has been passed in flagrant violation of the interim 
orders passed by this court and infact by the order 
impugned the order of recovery was revived.
8. The effect of subsequent order passed by the 
Chief Education Officer dated 15.12.2020 was also stayed on 27.01.2021 and the counter was called but 
uptill date no counter affidavit has been filed.
9. While taking note of this act of the Chief 
Education Officer that the impugned order has been 
passed in flagrant violation of interim orders the State 
counsel was directed to communicate the then Chief 
Education Officer to be remain present in court to 
explain why in flagrant violation of interim orders the 
impugned order has been passed.
10. In compliance to the order the then Chief 
Education Officer Mr. Anand Bhardwaj present in 
court on 06.01.2026 and make an attempt to justify his 
action by saying that there was a clerical mistake while 
passing the order impugned and furthermore no 
recovery has been made pursuant thereto.
11. This court further take serious note on the 
misleading statement of Mr. Anand Bhardwaj the then 
Chief Education Officer by observing that the 
justification and clarification are misleading one since 
no clerical error apparent on face of the order 
impugned and furthermore the recovery was stayed by 
the High Court.
12. Accordingly, this court was of the view that since 
the order impugned has been passed in flagrant 
violation of the impugned orders and furthermore the 
misleading statement has been given by Mr. Anand 
Bhardwaj the then Chief Education Officer, who was 
asked to file the response.
13. No response has been filed by Mr. Anand 
Bhardwaj and now this court has no option except to 
frame the charge against the then Chief Education 
Officer Mr. Anand Bhardwaj.
i. Why Mr. Anand Bhardwaj the then Chief 
Education Officer be not punished under 
the provisions of Contempt of Courts Act, 
1971 for his act while passing the order 
impugned in flagrant violation of the 
interim orders passed on 27.05.2020 and 
29.05.2020 in WPSS No. 507 of 2020 and 
WPSS No. 509 of 2020 and connected 
petitions?
ii. Why Mr. Anand Bhardwaj the then Chief 
Education Officer be not punished for 
giving misleading statement that there is a clerical mistake in the order impugned?
14. Learned counsel Mrs. Neeti Rana today put 
appearance for Mr. Anand Bhardwaj the then Chief 
Education Officer and as such Mr. Anand Bhardwaj is 
directed to be impleaded as one of the respondent.
15. Learned counsel for the petitioner make 
necessary amendment by impleading Mr. Anand 
Bhardwaj as one of the respondent.
16. Let Mr. Anand Bhardwaj the then Chief 
Education Officer file his response to the charges as 
framed above.
17. Mr. Parikshit Saini, learned counsel for the 
petitioner, apprise to this court that due to the 
pendency of all these petitions the respondents have 
withhold the consequential benefits of each of the 
petitioner.
18. In such view of the matter, the respondents are 
directed not to withhold any consequential benefits, 
which are admissible, merely on the ground that these 
petitions are pending.
19. Put up this matter on 18.02.2026 by showing the 
name of Mrs. Neeti Rana as counsel for Mr. Anand 
Bhardwaj in the cause list.
 
 (Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
