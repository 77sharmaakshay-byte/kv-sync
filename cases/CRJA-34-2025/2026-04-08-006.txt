3. Present criminal appeal has been filed by 
the appellant against the judgment and order dated 
17.05.2025 passed by learned Additional District 
& Sessions Judge/Special Judge (N.D.P.S. Act) 
Vikas Nagar, District Dehradun in Special 
Sessions Trial No.142 of 2021 (Old No.160 of 
2017) under Section 8/20 of N.D.P.S. Act, P.S. 
Kalsi, District Dehradun. By the said judgment 
the appellant has been sentenced to undergo 10 
years rigorous imprisonment alongwith fine of 
Rs.1,00,000/-and in default stipulation one year’s 
additional simple imprisonment was imposed 
upon him. 
4. The case of the prosecution is that on 
23.10.2016 at around 11:15 a.m., police team led 
by S.O. Dilwar Singh Negi alongwith police team 
was conducting the routine checking near Koti 
Road after having received the information that a 
person coming from Tuni was carrying a 
contraband named charas. The applicant was 
apprehended and after inspection by police team 
was found to be carrying 1.500 gram of 
contraband named charas.
5. The main ground, so advanced by learned 
counsel for the applicant firstly is that he has been 
falsely implicated in the present matter; secondly 
it is stated that no inventory of the said seized contraband was ever made. It is also stated that 
the directives and mandatory provisions of the 
NDPS Act have not been followed.
6. There is an anomaly in the entry of the 
General Diary wherein the FIR number was 
written before the actual filing of the FIR. It is 
also stated that in the recovery memo which was 
prepared on the spot, the FIR number has been 
mentioned that raised serious doubt about the 
prosecution story. It is further stated that 
considering the steps taken by the police team 
regarding search and seizure, it appears that the 
arrest was made at the police station and not on 
the spot and finally it is stated that the mandatory 
compliance of Section 41, 42, 43, 50, 52, 52A, 55 
and 56 of the NDPS Act have not been made and 
it is evident that the contraband, so alleged to have 
been recovered from his possession, is planted
one. 
7. Learned State counsel objects to the present 
bail application and refutes all the statement, so 
advanced by learned counsel for the applicant and 
submits that the case is well founded and there are 
no procedural error whatsoever and the court 
below after much scrutiny and detailed
appreciation of evidence had rightly convicted the 
accused person and he does not have any ground 
to be released on bail at this juncture. 
8. The contraband named, charas weighing
1.500 gram comes above the commercial quantity 
and the same has been verified as charas. As per 
the FSL report it is stated that the anomalies 
which the learned counsel for the applicant is 
trying to point out regarding the number of FIR 
being mentioned prior to lodging of the same in 
the General Diary and that there being no 
inventory report as such is not proved. The 
evidence from record has been placed before this 
Court that the inventory report has been made and 
all the proper procedure has been followed. 9. After considering the rival submission of 
the parties and on perusal of the records, at this 
juncture while considering the bail application, I 
do not find any anomalies regarding any 
procedural formalities that ought to have been 
considered while search and seizure in the present 
matter. As per record, the inventory report has 
been well established and there are no follies on 
part of the inventory team, search team and the 
arresting team thus it cannot be said that there has 
been procedural lapse in the present matter. The 
contraband so recovered from the 
appellant/convict come within the purview of 
commercial quantity and has been proved as 
charas as per the FSL report.
10. This Court does not find any sufficient 
ground for granting bail. The bail application 
stands rejected. 
11. List this criminal appeal on 15.06.2026.
