Hon’ble Ravindra Maithani, J. (Oral)
The appellant has been convicted and sentenced 
under sections 364, 302 IPC and under section 4/25 of the 
Arms Act, 1959 in Session Trial No.119 of 2011, State Vs. 
Sonu, by the court of 1st Additional Session Judge, Udham 
Singh Nagar. He seeks bail.
2. Heard learned counsel for the parties and 
perused the record.2
3. According to the prosecution case that on 
30.11.2010, the appellant with an intention to kill Ajay 
Chauhan, took him along with him in a sugarcane field, at 
Mohalla Rampura, Rudrapur, and attacked him with a
sword. As soon as the deceased was hit PW No.6 Shri Dhara 
Singh and PW No.8 Ramesh Kohli, who were at the nearby 
place rushed to the place of incident. The deceased, who 
was in an injured condition, then revealed that the 
appellant had attacked him with a sword. The deceased 
revealed the telephone number of his mother to PW No.6
Dhara Singh. PW No.6 Dhara Singh, telephonically 
contacted PW No.1 Smt. Munni Devi, the mother of the 
deceased and informed her as to what had happened to her 
son deceased Ajay Chauhan. PW No.1, Smt. Munni Devi, 
communicated it to her husband i.e. PW No.2 Shri Shankar 
Singh, about the incident, and an FIR was lodged. 
Subsequently the deceased succumbed to the injuries.
4. Learned counsel appearing for the appellant 
would submit that there is no independent witness; FIR is 
delayed; there is no independent witness of the recovery of 
the sword. Based on the statement of the PW No.1 Smt. 
Munni Devi, conviction has been recorded. The statements 
of PW No.6 Shri Dhara Singh and PW No.8 Shri Ramesh 
Kohli, are the hearsay evidence. 3
5. On the other hand, the State Counsel would 
submit that soon after the incident PW No.6 Shri Dhara 
Singh and PW No.8 Ramesh Kohli, had reached at the place 
of incident and then the deceased revealed as to how he was 
attacked by the appellant.
6. It is argued that the appellant had a motive 
because the appellant was under an impression that it is 
the deceased who got him lodged in jail. It is argued that 
PW No.6 Shri Dhara Singh, telephonically informed the PW 
No.1, Smt. Munni Devi, about the incident, and in turn PW 
No.2, Shri Shankar Singh, was communicated. 
7. Learned State Counsel would submit that the 
recovery of the weapon of the offence has also been made at 
the instance of the appellant.
8. It is the stage of bail in appeal. The benefit of 
presumption of innocence is not available to the appellant, 
who is a convict now.
9. The facts, which are considered, at this stage, 
also includes the nature of offence, the evidence and other 
surrounding circumstances; the evidence of PW No.6 Dhara 
Singh and PW No.8 Ramesh Kohli, cannot be said to be 
hearsay evidence. Both of them have stated that hearing 
loud voice they rushed to the place of incident and found 4
the deceased in an injured condition. According to them, at
that moment, the injured revealed to them that he was 
attacked by a sword by the appellant. The statement of 
these witnesses, is relevant and admissible under section 6 
of the Indian Evidence Act. It is not a hearsay. It is an 
exception to hearsay evidence as it is part of res gestae. PW 
No.1 Smt. Munni Devi and PW No.2 Shankar Singh, have
stated about the telephone calls, which they received from 
PW No.6 Shri Dhara Singh. Not only this, the Doctor who 
immediately after the incident examined the injured has 
revealed that the deceased was conscious, when he was 
examined.
10. Having considered the entirety of the facts and 
circumstances, this Court does not see any reasons to grant 
bail to the appellant. Accordingly, the bail applications 
deserve to be rejected. 
11. The bail applications are rejected.
