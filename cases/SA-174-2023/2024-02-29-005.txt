COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
29.02.2024 SA No.174 of 2023
Hon’ble Alok Kumar Verma, J.
An Original Suit (Original Suit 
No.559 of 2009), filed by the 
respondent-plaintiff, was dismissed. A 
Civil Appeal (Civil Appeal No.167 of 
2016) was filed by the respondent￾plaintiff. The said Civil Appeal has been 
allowed vide judgment dated 
29.09.2023, passed by learned VIth
Additional District Judge, Dehradun. 
2. Heard Mr. Sudhir Kumar with Mr. 
Lokendra Dobhal, learned counsel for 
appellant and Mr. Siddhartha Singh, 
learned counsel for respondent. 
3. Mr. Sudhir Kumar, Advocate, 
contended that as per the respondent￾plaintiff, one Dr. Beni Prasad executed a 
will in favour of his wife on 25.08.1952 
in Calcutta (now Kolkata). The said will 
was not probated. Smt. Ram Kaur, the 
wife of Dr. Beni Prasad, executed a will 
dated 13.11.1975. The said will dated 
25.08.1952, executed at Calcutta, was 
required to be probated in terms of 
Section 57 read with Section 213 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925. Therefore, 
Smt. Ram Kaur had no right to execute 
the will in respect of her husband’s entire 
property. The said Original Suit was filed 
on the basis of the said two wills. 
4. The present Second Appeal is 
admitted on the following substantial 
questions of law:-
(i) Whether the learned first
Appellate Court has 
committed a mistake of law 
in not considering that the 
Will in question having been 
executed at Calcutta (now 
Kolkata) was covered under 
sub-section (1) of Section 
57 of the Indian Succession 
Act and the bar of clause (i) 
of sub-section (2) of Section 
213 of the Indian 
Succession Act applied 
which bars the recognition 
of any Will in respect of 
which probate has not been 
obtained? 
(ii) Whether the learned first 
Appellate Court has 
committed an error of law in 
awarding damages @ 
Rs.1,000/= per day without 
any evidence in this regard 
and whether the damages 
awarded are highly 
excessive and exhorbitant?
