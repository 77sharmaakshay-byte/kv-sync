2. Sri B.D. Pande, Sri Bharat Tiwari and Sri Pulak Agarwal, learned 
counsel for the respondent. 
3. This Court in the present Appeal, filed under Section 37 of the 
Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 against the order of the 
Commercial Court dated 15.05.2025 rejecting the objection of the 
appellant filed under Section 34 of the said Act, has stayed the award 
subject to the appellant depositing 50 per cent of the amount, awarded by 
the Arbitrator, before this Court within four weeks. Further direction was 
that the amount shall be deposited in a nationalised bank in favour of this 
Court and the copies of the FDRs shall be handed-over to the Registrar 
(Judicial). 
4. In compliance of the said order, the appellant has deposited 50 
percent of the amount awarded in shape of FDRs and the FDR receipts 
have been handed-over to the Registry. 
5. The present application (IA No. 2 of 2026) has been filed praying 
for release of the said amount in favour of the respondent, in whose favour 
the award has been passed. 
6. It is submitted that the award is in shape of a money decree and as 
per Section 36 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, even 
executing Court cannot decline to execute the award unless the conditions 
stipulated under Order 41 Rule 5 of CPC are complied with. 
7. Learned counsel for the respondent states that he is ready to furnish security for the amount in question. 
8. The Arbitrator has allowed various money claims of the 
respondent and, as noted above, the objections filed by the appellant under 
Section 34 of the Act have already been rejected. On account of the 
interim order passed by this Court, the execution proceedings have got 
stalled. 
9. In such a situation, in our considered opinion, the respondent 
should not be left deprived of the benefit of the award, which stands 
upheld by the Court below.
10. Accordingly, the Application (IA No. 2 of 2026) is allowed. 
11. The Registry is directed to encash the FDRs and release the 
amount in favour of the respondent subject to the respondent furnishing 
adequate security for the said amount before the Commercial Court, 
Dehradun, within two weeks from today. A copy of the security, as would 
be furnished in compliance of the instant order, shall be submitted before 
the Registrar (Judicial) and, thereafter only, the Registrar (Judicial) would 
release the amount in favour of the respondent.
