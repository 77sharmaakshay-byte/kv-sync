The order of the learned Single Judge puts the respondent-writ 
petitioner above the applicant in the seniority list of instructors, 
though the applicant was appointed through a regular process of 
selection in the year 2010 whereas the respondent-writ petitioner 
was appointed only in the year 2014. 
2. Sri Kishore Kumar, learned counsel holding brief of Sri M.C. 
Pant, learned counsel for the respondent-writ petitioner, would 
contend that the respondent-writ petitioner was regularized in the 
year 2014, and was not appointed afresh. Even if that be so, the 
question which would necessitate examination is whether the 
services rendered by him prior to his regularisation can be reckoned 
for the purpose of computing seniority, more so without hearing 
those who were likely to be affected thereby. 
3. We see no reason, therefore, not to grant leave to appeal to the 
applicant. The application seeking leave to appeal is allowed. 
CLMA No. 9780 of 2019
4. The limitation for preferring an appeal against the order 
passed by a learned Single Judge would only apply to a party to the 
writ proceedings before the learned Single Judge. The period of 2
limitation for a person, who though not a party to the writ petition 
but is aggrieved by the order of the learned Single Judge, must be 
computed from the date of his knowledge of the order under appeal. 
It would be wholly unnecessary, therefore, to file an application for 
condonation of delay, if the appeal is filed within time from the date 
of knowledge of the order. The Application for condonation of 
delay is, accordingly, closed. 
5. Since a similar Special Appeal (Special Appeal No. 940 of 
2018), against the very same order passed by the learned Single 
Judge in Writ Petition (S/S) No. 2684 of 2015, is pending on the file 
of this Court, this Special Appeal is also admitted, and tagged along 
with Special Appeal No. 940 of 2018.
