C528 No. 1254 of 2026
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J. 
1. Mr. Dushyant Mainali, learned counsel for the 
applicant.
2. Mr. J.S. Virk, learned Additional Advocate 
General with Mr. Himanshu Sain, learned A.G.A. for 
the State.
3. In this petition the applicant ‘Dhananjay Giri’ 
challenging the remand order dated 22.05.2026 and 
29.05.2026 passed by the Additional Chief Judicial 
Magistrate, arising out of Case Crime No. 94 of 2025. 
By order of remand dated 22.05.2026 the applicant was 
taken on remand for the offences punishable under 
sections 111(4), 111(2)(B) of BNS, 2023.
In reference to this order of remand dated 
22.05.2026 it is argued by the learned counsel for the 
applicant the remand for section 111(4) and section 
111(2)(b) of BNS, 2023 is bad in law since in the order of 
remand there is no any reference of any such incident 
alleged to be committed after promulgation of BNS, 
2023, which is one of the ingredient for constituting an 
offence, which is termed as “continuing unlawful 
activity” as defined under explanation (iii) of section 
111 of BNS, 2023. In reference to this he placed reliance 
to a judgment of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of 
State of Gujarat vs. Sandip Omprakash Gupta 
2024(14) SCC 401, wherein, the earlier judgment of 
Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of State of 
Maharashtra vs. Shivaji 2015 (14) SCC 272 was 
discussed in para-30 onwards and then held that it is
only if an organized crime is committed by the accused 
after promulgation of the MCOCA, then he may on the 
basis of the previous charge-sheet the cognizance can 
be taken for the offence under section 3 of the MCOCA.
By referring this judgment, it is argued that for 
constituting the offence punishable under section 
111(4) it is essential to the prosecution to take a remand 
of accused if any offence is committed after 
promulgation of BNS, 2023, which came into force w.e.f. 01.07.2024. At page 46 he has given the chart of 
the criminal antecedent of the present applicant and all 
those cases are prior to the promulgation of BNS, 2023. 
The criminal antecedent may be relevant for taking the 
applicant on remand under sections 111(4) and 111(2) 
(b) of BNS, 2023, if any offence is committed by the 
applicant after promulgation of BNS, 2023 i.e. after 
01.07.2024.
4. In addition to this, he submits that for 
constituting the offence punishable under section 
111(4) the second requirement is that there should be 
an “organized crime syndicate”, which also is defined 
under section 111(1)(i) of BNS, 2023, which defines 
“organized crime syndicate”, means, a group of two or 
more persons who acting either singly or jointly as a 
syndicate or gang, indulged in any continuing 
unlawful activity. He submits that this aspect is also 
not been reflected from the order of remand since there 
is no details of those persons, who are associated in
group with the present applicant for constituting an 
“organized crime syndicate” to continue unlawful 
activity.
5. Mr. Virk, learned Additional Advocate General, 
requests that the matter be posted on 09.06.2026 as 
fresh in order to enable him to get the instructions what 
was the material before the prosecution while taking 
the remand of the present applicant.
6. Put up this matter on 09.06.2026 as fresh along 
with BA1 No. 990 of 2026.
