COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
WPSS No. 2181 of 2024
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. S.K. Shandilya and Mr. Sachin 
Kumar Sharma, learned counsel for the petitioners, 
Mr. R.C. Joshi, learned Brief Holder for the State, 
Mr. Rajesh Sharma, learned Standing Counsel for 
Union of India, Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior 
Counsel, assisted by Mr. Kanti Ram Sharma, 
learned counsel for respondent nos. 5 and 7, Mr. 
Shashank Upadhayay, learned counsel for 
respondent no. 6.
2. All these petitioners are serving as 
Teachers in the Institution, which are affiliated with 
the CBSE and controlled by the BHEL. To run all 
these institutions, there is a board known as BHEL 
Educational Management Board (for short “EMB) 
(respondent no. 5 herein). The petitioners are 
praying for the benefit of 7th pay commission on 
the ground that earlier the recommendation of 5th
pay commission was given to the petitioners and 
thereafter the benefit of 6th pay commission was 
also given to the EMB staff including the teaching 
staff, which is evident from the letter issued by the 
BHEL Educational Management Board dated 
07.01.2009, which is enclosed as Annexure-6.
3. On perusal of Annexure-6, it reveals 
that this letter pertains to revision of pay-scales of EMB staff based on 6th pay commission. The
relevant extract of this letter is extracted 
hereinunder:-
“Sub: Revision of pay scales of EMB staff based on 
6th Pay Commission
BHEL Educational Management Board is following 
KVS pattern with the approval of BHEL Corporate 
Management vide letter No. AA:EPM:ADM dated 
22.10.1988. Kendriya Vidhyalaya Sangathan, New 
Delhi have passed orders for revision of pay scales 
in respect of their teaching and non-teaching 
staff, vide their letter No. 11081-4/2008-KVS-HQ
(Admn.-I) dated 05.11.2008 with enclosures. 
Extracts of rules/orders are given below:
…………………………………………………………………………”
4. Learned counsel for the petitioners 
submits that since the recommendation of the 5th
pay commission as well as 6th pay commission has 
been given to all these petitioners including the 
other staff of EMB, however, by the order 
impugned, their claim for the benefit on the basis 
of the 7th pay commission has been rejected on the 
ground that the BHEL is not in a position to bear 
the additional expenditure on account of 
implementation of 7th pay commission of EMB 
employees and due to the financial difficulties, 
BHEL has been constrained to reduce allowance of 
all its executives and supervisors by almost 50% in 
compliance of Government Directives.
5. The question, which is required to be 
determined, is that whether the benefit pursuant 
to the 7th Pay Commission can be denied to the petitioners merely on the ground that the BHEL is 
facing the financial difficulties.
6. Learned counsel for the petitioner 
further submits that BHEL has full control over the 
EMB Institution and in reference to this, “Article of 
Association and Memorandum of Association” has 
been enclosed as Annexure-4. Definition clause 
contains the definition of ‘Board’, ‘Management 
Committee’, ‘Member’ and ‘BHEL Management’, 
which are being reproduced as hereunder:-
“3. Definition: Unless there is anything repugnant 
with reference to context-
(i) ‘Board’ means “BHEL Educational Management 
Board, Ranipur, Haridwar.
(ii) ‘Management Committee’ means and 
includes Chairman, Co-Chairman, Secretary and 
Joint Secretary of the BHEL Educational 
Management Board.
(iii) ‘Member’ means and includes any member of 
the BHEL Educational Management Board or 
Management Committee.
(iv) ‘BHEL Management’ means Head of BHEL 
Educational Management Board or Management 
Committee.”
7. Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior Counsel, 
who appears for respondent nos. 5 and 7 submits 
that BHEL is not managing these institutions, in 
fact, the BHEL Educational Management Board is 
controlling over these institutions and time to time, 
they are providing financial assistance to them.
8. This argument has been denied by 
learned counsel for the petitioner. Learned counsel 
for the petitioner further submits that the financial burden always lies upon the BHEL, which is evident 
from the fact that the benefit pursuant to the 5th
pay commission and 6th pay commission has been 
given by the BHEL.
9. Issue notice to respondent no. 4. Steps 
to be taken within three days by dasti as well as 
electronic mode.
10. Since, Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior 
Counsel accepts notice on behalf of the respondent 
nos. 5 and 7 and the respondent no. 5, according to 
the argument of Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned Senior 
Counsel, is a BHEL Educational Management Board, 
which is the controlling body over these
institutions, therefore, Mr. V.K. Kohli, learned 
Senior Counsel cannot advance his argument on 
behalf of the BHEL, to whom he is not appearing.
11. List this matter on 11.12.2024.
