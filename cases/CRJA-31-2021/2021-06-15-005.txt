Ms. Neelima Mishra Joshi, the learned counsel for the appellant. 
Mr. J.S. Virk, the learned Deputy Advocate General for the State of 
Uttarakhand. 
ORDER : (per Hon’ble The Chief Justice Sri Raghvendra Singh Chauhan)
 This Bail Application (IA No. 1 of 2021) has been 
filed on behalf of the appellant, Pramod Singh, seeking 
regular bail. Ms. Neelima Mishra Joshi, the learned counsel 
for the appellant, has raised the following contentions in 
order to seek the benefit of bail for the appellant :- 
(i) The prosecution alleges that the appellant 
has killed his mother. However, there is no motive for 
the appellant to kill his mother. 
(ii) Even Nirmala Devi (P.W. 10), the wife of the 
appellant, admits in her testimony that the appellant 
was of unsound mind. Therefore, the benefit of 2 
insanity should have been extended by the learned 
Trial Court. 
(iii) The appellant has been incarcerated in jail 
since 27.09.2009. Thus, he is languishing in jail for a 
long time. 
(iv) The present appeal is a Jail Appeal filed after 
almost ten years. Therefore, the benefit of bail should 
be granted to the appellant. 
2. On the other hand, Mr. J.S. Virk, the learned 
Deputy Advocate General for the State of Uttarakhand, has 
raised the following counter-contentions :- 
(i) That the present case is based on direct 
evidence, wherein Hayat Singh (P.W. 1), the father of 
the accused, and Nirmala Devi (P.W. 10), the wife of 
the accused, are eye-witnesses. Their presence at the 
scene of the crime cannot be doubted, as both these 
witnesses are injured witnesses. Both the witnesses 
have elaborately described the assault caused by the 
accused upon his mother with the use of a Danda. 3 
(ii) In a case of direct evidence, motive loses all 
its relevance. 
(iii) Even Niramala Devi (P.W. 10) states in her 
testimony that her husband was able to understand 
the consequence of his action. Hence, the plea of 
insanity cannot be accepted. 
3. Therefore, the learned Deputy Advocate General 
has vehemently opposed the grant of bail to the appellant. 
4. Without expressing any opinion on the merits and 
demerits of the case, this Court is not inclined to grant the 
benefit of bail to the appellant. The Bail Application (IA No. 
1 of 2021) is, hereby, dismissed. 
5. Admit. 
6. Call for the records. 
7. List this case as soon as the records are received.
