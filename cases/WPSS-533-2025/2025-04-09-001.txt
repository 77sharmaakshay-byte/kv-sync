COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
WPSS No. 533 of 2025
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Bhagwat Mehra, learned counsel for the 
petitioner.
2. Mr. Rajeev Singh Bisht, learned Additional C.S.C. 
for the State.
3. In one of the writ petitions i.e. WPSS No. 53 of 
2025 this court already passed an order on 13.02.2025 
that the respondents are restrained from transferring 
any incumbent on the basis of classification of sugam 
and durgam. This order was passed keeping in mind 
that the classification of sugam and durgam is appears 
to be a very vague classification, since no method of 
classification has been prescribed.
4. This State was carved out on 09.12.2000 and 
earlier it was the part of erstwhile State of U.P. and the 
concept of formation of State of Uttarakhand was that it 
was surrounded by most of the hilly areas, therefore, 
after formation of the State the concept of such 
classification of sugam and durgam is nothing but a 
self imagination though under any law there is no any 
such classification. A particular place, which was a 
durgam earlier place may now be a sugam place, 
therefore, such classification should not be the basis of 
the transfer.
5. It is settled principle of law that the transfer is an 
exigency of service, however, there should be some 
guidelines on this, but the criteria should not be the 
basis upon such classification i.e. sugam and dugam.
6. On perusal of the Act, it reveals that on such 
classification the marks are being given to the 
candidates and on the basis of the marks the transfer 
are being made. In order to assist this court, the 
Secretary Education joined the proceeding through 
V.C. and he also apprise to this court that in 
implementation of the Transfer Act particularly on the 
basis of the classification of sugam and durgam, the department itself is facing so many problems in 
computation of the marks of the candidates and 
manpower are being engaged for computation of 
marks. He also apprise to this court that the issue has 
also been pointed out to the Secretary Karmik earlier.
7. In view of this, since the transfer is an exigency of 
service, therefore, the concerned employee should 
serve in the place where he has been transferred but 
there are certain situations in which the transfer is 
required like if the spouse are in government service 
then the efforts should be made to post both of them in 
one place so that they may do their job sincerely and 
also to lookafter their family.
8. Another ground of transfer may the personal 
hardship of the concerned incumbent and if he is 
suffering from serious disease then that aspect can be 
looked into for transfer and other is long tenure in a 
particular place and such a person can be replaced by 
another incumbent, but the actual concept of transfer of 
an incumbent is nothing but in a public interest. No 
incumbent or employee can claim for transfer in a 
particular place, since the transfer is nothing but the 
exigency of service.
9. To examine this issue what this court observed 
that some attempts should be made to examine this 
aspect and is of the view why the present session be 
not declared as a zero session for the purposes of 
transfer.
10. Since, this court goes to the root of the matter for 
which the Secretary was asked to join the proceeding 
through V.C., let this matter be clubbed together with 
WPSS No. 53 of 2025 by keeping the same with the 
consent of the parties as part heard.
11. Put up this matter on 24.04.2025.
12. In the meantime, the Secretary to the department 
Mr. Ravinath Raman is directed to consult with the 
Chief Secretary and to apprise this court what is the 
way out now, since the transfer session has yet not 
been commenced.
13. In the meantime, the adjustment of the petitioner 
shall be subject to the decision of the writ petition.
 
 (Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
