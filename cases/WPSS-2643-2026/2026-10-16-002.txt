2. The petitioners challenge the final select list issued by the 
Commission for the post of Additional Private Secretary (APS) 
dated 14.09.2026. The core issue/grievance is that the respondent￾Commission in mid-of-selection altered the statutory evaluation 
criteria under Rule 15(3) by rendering the first test a mere 
“qualifying exam” instead of aggregating marks in both the tests 
while preparing final select list, consequently, High-scoring 
candidates in the first test have been excluded from the final select 
list.

4. In support of his arguments, Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned 
Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners pointed out that in the 
previous selection, which was conducted in the year 2017, the final 
select list was prepared by adding the marks of both the tests 
which is reflected from Anneuxre 13. In addition to this, he also 
submits that the change of criteria in mid of the selection by the 
Commission by issuing subsequent advertisement dated 
26.10.2024 is also contrary to Rule 15(3) of the “Uttarakhand 
Secretariat Additional Private Secretary Service Rules, 2011”.
For ready reference Rule 15(3) is being reproduced hereinunder:-
“(3) After the result of the written examination have been 
received and tabulated, the Commission shall, having regard to 
the need for securing due representation of the candidates 
belonging to the Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other 
categories under rule 6, prepare a list of candidates, in order of 
merit as disclosed by the marks obtained by each candidate in 
the written examination and recommend such number of 
candidates as they consider fit for appointment. If two or more 
candidates obtain equal marks in total the candidates who 
obtain higher marks in the written test shall be placed higher in 
the list. If two or more candidates obtain equal marks in the 
written examination also, the candidate senior in the age shall 
be placed higher in the selection list. The Commission shall 
forward the list to the appointing authority.”
By referring the said provision, Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, submits 
that as per the aforesaid Rule the merit list has to be prepared on 
the basis of the marks obtained by each of candidates in the 
2written examination, therefore, while preparing the merit list, 
marks obtained in the test of both the stages has to be computed. 
5. Learned Senior Counsel in furtherance of his argument also 
submits that it is settled principle of law that recruitment process 
is commenced from the date of issuance of advertisement in view 
of the law laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of 
A.A. Calton vs. The Director of Education 1983 SCC (3) 280 
which still holds a good law, therefore, once the recruitment is 
commenced the Commission has no power to change the criteria 
of selection. Apart from this, Mr. Bahuguna further placed reliance 
to another decision of the Apex Court in the case of Tej Prakash 
Pathak and others vs. Rajesthan High Court and others (2025) 2 
SCC 1, which reaffirms that the “rules of the game” cannot be 
changed after the selection process has commenced. Para 42 is 
relevant, which read as under:-
“42. We, therefore, answer the reference in the following terms:
(1) Recruitment process commences from the issuance of 
the advertisement calling for applications and ends with filling 
up of vacancies;
(2) Eligibility criteria for being placed in the Select List, 
notified at the commencement of the recruitment process, 
cannot be changed midway through the recruitment process 
unless the extant Rules so permit, or the advertisement, which is 
not contrary to the extant Rules, so permit. Even if such change 
is permissible under the extant Rules or the advertisement, the 
change would have to meet the requirement of Article 14 of the 
Constitution and satisfy the test of non-arbitrariness;
(3) The decision in K. Manjusree (supra) lays down good 
law and is not in conflict with the decision in Subash Chander 
Marwaha (supra). Subash Chander Marwaha (supra) deals with 
the right to be appointed from the Select List whereas K. 
Manjusree (supra) deals with the right to be placed in the Select 
List. The two cases therefore deal with altogether different 
issues;
(4) Recruiting bodies, subject to the extant Rules, may 
devise appropriate procedure for bringing the recruitment 
process to its logical end provided the procedure so adopted is 
transparent, non-discriminatory/ non-arbitrary and has a 
rational nexus to the object sought to be achieved.
(5) Extant Rules having statutory force are binding on the 
recruiting body both in terms of procedure and eligibility. 
3However, where the Rules are non-existent, or silent, 
administrative instructions may fill in the gaps;
(6) Placement in the select list gives no indefeasible right 
to appointment. The State or its instrumentality for bona fide 
reasons may choose not to fill up the vacancies. However, if 
vacancies exist, the State or its instrumentality cannot arbitrarily 
deny appointment to a person within the zone of consideration 
in the select list.”
6. On the other side, Mr. A.S. Rawat, learned Senior Counsel 
who appears for the Commission submits that there is no 
deviation from the criteria of preparation of select list as the test of 
first stage is only qualifying in nature.
7. On such submission Mr. C.D. Bahuguna submits that the 
submissions of Mr. A.S. Rawat, is not correct since there is clear 
cut deviation of criteria which itself is reflected from “Note￾Clause” of both the advertisements. 
8. Mr. Anil Kumar Dabral, learned Addl. C.S.C. adopt the 
argument of Mr. A.S. Rawat by reiterating the same submissions
that the first test is only for qualifying purposes and also apprise 
that the select list as forward by the Commission has not been 
accepted so far though three candidates have been given 
appointment in the Public Service Commission. In addition to this 
Mr. Pankaj Miglani, who appears for the Commission also placed 
before this Court the decision of the Coordinate Bench in WPSS
No.1585 of 2026 and submits that the identical issue was raised in 
this petition and the Coordinate Bench dismissed the same, which 
was also followed by another Coordinate Bench in WPSS No.1178 
of 2026.
In response to Mr. Pankaj Miglani, Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, 
learned Senior counsel for the petitioners submits that the petition, 
which was dismissed by the Coordinate Bench was infact pre 
mature since by that time the result of the second test was not 
declared, which itself is reflected from para 10 of the order of the 
4Coordinate Bench dated 17.06.2026 passed in WPSS No.1585 of 
2026, which reads as under:-
“10. He further contends that petitioner appeared in the second 
leg of the examination and the result of the said examination is 
yet to be declared. He also contends that writ petition is filed 
challenging the said notification dated 26.10.2024 is hit by the 
latches on the one hand and on the other hand, petitioner 
participated pursuant to the change made by the respondent￾Commission and he was successful in the qualifying 
examination. In this view of the matter, the writ petition cannot 
be sustained. “ 
9. In support of such submissions Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, furhter 
placed reliance to a decision of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of 
Salam Samarjeet Singh vs. High Court of Manipur at Imphal 
(2024) 14 SCC 179 by placing reliance to para 35, which reads as 
under:-
“35. Before we conclude, we may also advert to the contention 
that after participating in the recruitment process, the 
unsuccessful candidates cannot turn around and challenge the 
recruitment process. We are of the view that it is equally well 
settled that the principle of estoppel cannot override the law12. 
Such legal principle was reiterated by the Supreme Court in 
Meeta Sahai v Union of India13 wherein it was observed as under: 
(Meeta Sahai case13 , SCC p. 26, para 17)
"17. However, we must differentiate from this principle 
insofar as the candidate by agreeing to participate in the 
selection process only accepts the prescribed procedure 
and not the illegality in it. In a situation Where a 
candidate alleges misconstruction of statutory rules and 
discriminating consequences arising therefrom, the same 
cannot be condoned merely because a candidate has 
partaken in it. The constitutional scheme is sacrosanct 
and its violation in any manner is impermissible. In fact, 
a candidate may not have locus to assail the incurable 
illegality or derogation of the provisions of the 
Constitution, unless he/she participates in the selection 
process."
10. All these aspects requires deep scrutiny whether the change 
of criteria for preparation of merit list is permissible in the mid of 
selection.
511. Since the issue, as raised, appears to be legal in nature, let 
the matter be posted on 04.11.2026. On that day, the counsel for 
the respondent may address on the issue. 
12. In the meantime, the respondents are restrain from 
implementing the impugned final select list.
