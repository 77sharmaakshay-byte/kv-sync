1. On the previous date, Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned counsel 
for the petitioners argued at length in order to give response to 
the arguments, as advanced by learned Attorney General of India 
on 11.12.2024. By referring Clause 9.3 of 2020 Guidelines issued 
by Ministry of Tribal Affair, he submits that only the State/EMRS 
society is empowered to ensure recruitment of Teaching and 
Non-Teaching staff for the schools, based on the norms, 
guidelines and the recruitment rules prescribed by the Ministry 
from time to time. On that date i.e. 05.05.2025, Mr. C.D. 
Bahuguna, learned Sr. Advocate pointed out that MoU entered 
on 22.08.2023 in between NESTS and EMRS Society is contrary to 
the 2020 Guidelines, which does not empower the NESTS to go 
with recruitment process. Furthermore, the Guidelines does not 
empower the NESTS to enter with the MoU with EMRS Society. 
He also submits that NESTS entered into MoU with ulterior 
purposes and motive. He has further placed reliance on the D.O. 
letter dated 12.02.2020 whereby all the Principal Secretaries of 2
Tribal Welfare / Development Department of each of the State 
directed to carry out the recruitment for filling up various 
vacancies. 
2. Today, Mr. R. Ventakaramani, learned Attorney General of 
India for NESTS, argued at length, in order to give response to 
the arguments, as advanced by Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned 
counsel for the petitioner. He argued that Constitution provided 
for a comprehensive framework for the protection and 
advancement of the Tribal people. Many provisions were 
specifically provided for safeguarding interest of tribal people
and with an objective of providing more focused approach on the 
integrated socio-economic development of the Scheduled Tribes, 
the most underprivileged of the Indian society, in a coordinated 
and planned manner, Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA) was set 
up in the year 1999.
3. Since its inception, endeavor of Ministry of Tribal Affairs 
were to take various initiatives for overall upliftment of Tribals 
across the country. Amongst these objectives, providing quality 
education to ST students have been among the main focus of the 
Ministry as educational development is a stepping-stone to 
economic and social development and is also the most effective 
instrument for overall empowerment. 
4. He further submits that for establishing quality residential 
schools for the promotion of education and also to ensure all 
round development of tribal students in all areas, the Eklavya 
Model Residential Schools (EMRS), Eklavya Model Day Boarding 
Schools (EMDBS) and Centre of Excellence for Sports were 
established and these schools are aimed to provide quality upper 
primary, secondary and senior secondary level education to tribal 
students in tribal dominated areas, along with extra-curricular 3
activities, to enable them to access the best opportunities in 
education and to bring them at par with the general population. 
5. He also brings to the notice of this Court the revised 
Guidelines for setting up Eklavya Model Residential Schools 
(EMRS) issued by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, Government of 
India, in June 2010 and Clause 4 of this Guidelines pertains to 
Management and running of EMRS and Clause 7 (e) provides for 
review and monitoring. It provides that the Government of India 
is free to make any modification in the aforesaid conditions in 
consultation with selected State Government / UTs whenever 
deemed necessary. Clause 7 of the said revised Guidelines is 
extracted hereinbelow:
 “Clause 7. Review and Monitoring:
a. The progress of implementation of the scheme will be 
reviewed by the Union Ministry of Tribal Affairs through 
periodic reports from the State Government/implementing 
agencies. 
b. The Ministry of Tribal Affairs will conduct review 
meetings during which States/UTs would be required to 
make presentations on the progress of their EMRSs. 
c. The guidelines for the use of the grants under Article 275 
(1) provide for an amount up to 2% of the total allocation 
to be used for administration of the programme. This would 
be applicable in case of EMRS. 
d. A Centralized mechanism for the online monitoring of the 
EMRSs would be developed. Meanwhile States/UTs may 
strength their own systems/methods. 4
e. The Government of India is free to make any modification 
in the aforesaid conditions in consultation with selected 
State Governments/UTs whenever deemed necessary.”
6. He further submits that in fact, 2010 Guidelines is past 
history and however, new Guidelines were framed by the 
Ministry on 30.11.2020 and Guidelines are applicable to all the 
schools running under EMRS Society. Clause 4 of the said 
Guidelines pertains to Management of Schools. Clause 4.1 
pertains to National Education Society for Tribal Students 
(NESTS). Clause 4.2 provides for State / UT EMRS Societies. By 
referring Clause (e) of Clause 4.2, learned Attorney General of 
India submits that Society like EMRS act as delegated authority 
by Ministry of Tribal Affairs / NESTS and not act as an 
independent authority. By referring Clause 9 of the 2020 
Guidelines, learned Attorney General of India argued that NESTS 
is empowered to review the existing MoU and to enter into fresh 
MoU and if need be, it could entrust the recruitment of teachers 
for State to an independent agency. He has particularly placed 
reliance on sub – Clause (i), (j), (k), (l), (m) and (n) of Clause 9.1 of 
the 2020 Guidelines. He has further placed reliance on Clause 9.3 
of the 2020 Guidelines which pertains to the Roles and 
Responsibilities of the State / UT EMRS Society, which provides 
that State/UT EMRS Societies will be responsible for the 
management of Schools sanctioned/established in the particular 
State/UT in accordance to the norms and guidelines laid down 
by the NESTS. He further submits that sub Clause (e) of Clause 
9.3 provides that EMRS Society shall ensure recruitment of 
Teaching & Non-Teaching Staff for the schools based on the 
norms, guidelines and Recruitment Rules prescribed by the 
Ministry from time to time in a time bound manner.5
7. He further submits that NESTS is an autonomous society 
under the control of Ministry of Tribal Affairs and is empowered 
to do all the acts and things necessary for tribal education and 
NESTS will be guided by a Steering Committee headed by the
Hon’ble Minister for Tribal Affairs and as per 2020 Guidelines, 
the EMRS Society is under legal obligation to maintain, control 
and manage the Schools, as per the guidelines and norms 
formulated by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, Government of India 
and undertake other responsibilities as delegated by the Ministry 
of Tribal Affairs / NESTS. He further argued that EMRS is 
delegated authority of the Ministry of Tribal Affairs and NESTS 
and since 2020 Guidelines gives comprehensive responsibility to 
the NESTS to achieve the objective for upliftment of tribal 
students and provide a detailed standards and norms for 
recruitment of Teaching and Non Teaching Staff and continuation 
of existing staff and is empowered to review the existing MoUs or 
to enter into a fresh MoU, therefore, by virtue of power conferred 
by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs under 2020 Guidelines, MoU was 
entered in between NESTS and EMRS on 22.08.2023. 
8. Apart from this, he submits that in order to maintain the 
uniformity in the standard education to tribal students, Ministry 
created NESTS and EMRS in each of the State. He further submits 
that NESTS act as a Nodal Agency of Ministry of Tribal Affairs in 
order to achieve the object under 2020 Guidelines. 
9. List on 02.06.2025 at 04.00 pm along with other connected 
petitions for further arguments.
