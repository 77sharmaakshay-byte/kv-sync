COMMON ORDER:(per Hon’ble Justice Sri Rakesh Thapliyal)
 In all these writ petitions, a common issue 
has been raised whether respondent no.1- National 
Education Society for Tribal Students (for short 
“NESTS”) is authorized and competent to initiate the 
selection process to fill up the posts of Post Graduate 
Teachers (PGT) and Trained Graduate Teachers (TGT). 
2. Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned Senior Counsel, 
first of all, refer the guidelines issued by the Ministry of 
Tribal Affairs, Government of India in June, 2010, and 
particularly, he is referring Clauses 4(h) and 4(i), which 
are being reproduced here-in-below:- 
“4. Management and Running of EMRS 
(h) The tasks of school admission, appointment of 
teachers, appointment of staff, personnel matters and 
day-to-day running of the schools would be handled 
entirely by the society chosen by the State 
Government/ UT Administration and in the manner 
deemed most suitable. 
(i) The State Government/ UT Administration shall 3 
ensure and maintain the highest quality in the selection 
of teachers and staff for academic and extra-curricular 
excellence.” 
3. By referring Clauses 4(h) and 4(i) of the 
aforesaid guidelines, Mr. Bahuguna submits that the 
task of school admissions, including appointment of 
teachers, appointment of staff, would be handled 
entirely by the society chosen by the State 
Government/ UT Administration. 
4. The next document, which Mr. Bahuguna is 
referring, is the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU), 
dated 26th August, 2020, which was entered into 
between NESTS and EMRS Society of the Uttarakhand 
State, which is the society established by the State. 
Clause 4 of the MoU pertains to role and responsibilities 
of the second party, i.e. EMRS Society. The second 
party in this MoU is the State Society (EMRS Society). 
Mr. Bahuguna placed reliance to Clause 4.4 and 4.5 
which are being reproduced here-in-below:- 
“4.4 Shall ensure recruitment of Teaching & Non￾Teaching Staff for the schools based on the norms and 
guidelines prescribed by Ministry of Tribal Affairs, 
Government of India from time to time in a time bound 
manner. 
4.5 Shall ensure adherence to the reservation 
policy as applicable to the sub-groups within ST 
communities, if any, in the respective State/ UT during 
the recruitment and admission process in the schools in 
consonance with the extant guidelines of the Ministry of 4 
Tribal Affairs, Government of India.” 
5. Thereafter another guidelines were issued on 
13th November, 2020, which is annexed as Annexure-17 
to the writ petition. Clause 9 of the said guidelines 
relates to running of schools. Clause 9.3(e) and (f) of 
these guidelines are relevant, and are being extracted 
here-in-below:- 
“9.3 Roles and Responsibilities of the State/ UT EMRS 
Society 
“e) Shall ensure recruitment of Teaching & Non￾Teaching Staff for the schools based on the norms and 
guidelines prescribed by Ministry of Tribal Affairs, 
Government of India from time to time in a time bound 
manner. 
f) Shall ensure adherence to the reservation policy 
as applicable to the sub-groups within ST communities, 
if any, in the respective State/ UT during the 
recruitment and admission process in the schools in 
consonance with the extant guidelines of the Ministry.” 
6. Mr. Bahuguna, by referring these Guidelines 
issued in June, 2010 and the Guidelines dated 
13.11.2020, submits that even as per these Guidelines, 
only the Society established by the State is competent 
for initiating and concluding the selection process. 
7. Apart from this, learned Senior Counsel also 
placing reliance on the letter dated 12.02.2020, issued 
by the Joint Secretary, Ministry of Tribal Affairs, 5 
Government of India, which is addressed to the 
Principal Secretaries, Tribal Welfare Development/ 
Department of the concerned State, which contains the 
reference of statutory rules for recruitment of Teaching 
and Non-Teaching positions, which is also being 
extracted here-in-below:- 
“Dear Sir/ Madam, 
This has reference to the letter no. F. 
No.18015/1/2017- Grants dated 06.12.2019 wherein 
the comments from the States were requested on draft 
recruitment rules for Teaching and Non-Teaching Staff 
under EMRS. The last date of receiving the comments 
was 19th December 2019. 
2. After considering the comments received from 
various State Governments, the recruitment rules for 
Teaching and Non-Teaching Staff under EMRS have 
been finalized and attached at Annex-I. You are 
requested to adopt these Recruitment rules for Teaching 
and Non-teaching positions while recruitment of Staff 
under Eklavya Model Residential Schools. It is clarified 
that the State Government have to carry out the 
recruitment process for filling up various 
vacancies across the EMRSs of their States by 
following these RRs only. 
You are requested to take necessary action 
accordingly.” 
8. Mr. Bahuguna, by referring the aforesaid 
letter dated 12.02.2020, submits that even as per this 
letter, which given the reference of recruitment rules 
clearly stipulates that the Societies established by the 
State Government have to carry out the recruitment 
process for filling up various vacancies across the 6 
EMRSs of their respective States. 
9. Mr. Bahuguna submits that in view of the 
guidelines of 2010 and 2020 as well as from the 
contents of the recruitment rules, it is very clear that 
only the State Society is competent for initiating the 
selection process, and not the NESTS. 
10. Apart from this, Mr. Bahuguna submits that 
all the petitioners of this bunch of writ petitions were 
appointed, though on contractual basis, but by way of 
rigid and regular selection process by the State Society. 
In support of this argument, he has drawn the attention 
of this Court to the advertisement as well as the 
Minutes of the Selection Committee, which is appended 
as Annexure-5. Writ Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 
and 1946 of 2024 have been preferred by those who 
have been selected pursuant to the selection process 
initiated by the NESTS.

12. These Minutes also reveal that the 
advertisements, which were issued by the State 
Society, were widely circulated and published in three 
reputed & well circulated daily newspapers on 
01.06.2017. 
13. Apart from this, Mr. Bahuguna also placed on 
record letter dated 30th September, 2022, issued by the 
Joint Commissioner, NESTS, which is an Autonomous 
Organization under the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, 
Government of India (annexed as Annexure-20 to the 
writ petition), which was addressed to all the Nodal 
Officers, States/ UTs EMRS Societies. 
14. On perusal of this letter, it reveals that the 
posts of teachers, which were already filled up either by 
regular staff or on contractual basis, shall be 
considered as filled. 9 
15. Mr. Bahuguna, by referring this letter, 
submits that the petitioners of this bunch of writ 
petitions, except petitioners in Writ Petition (S/S) 
Nos.1945 of 2024 and 1946 of 2024, were appointed, 
though on contractual basis after following the regular 
selection process, cannot be filled up by regular 
selection process, since as per this letter, the posts of 
teachers filled either by regular staff or by contractual 
teachers, shall be deemed to be considered as filled. 
The letter dated 30th September, 2022 is being

19. Mr. Bahuguna submits that since NESTS is 
not empowered to initiate the selection process, and 
this power is only vest with the State Society, 
therefore, the entire selection process initiated 
pursuant to the advertisement issued by the NESTS, is 
vitiated. 
20. Apart from this, he also submits that earlier 
in some writ petitions, interim order(s) was passed, 
against which, NESTS preferred bunch of special 
appeals before the Division Bench of this Court, leading 
one is Special Appeal No.331 of 2023, and the same 
were disposed of on 26.09.2023. In this judgment, 
there is a reference of the stand taken by the 
appellants (NESTS) which is reflected from Paragraph 
No.4 of the judgment, which is reproduced here-in￾below:- 
“4. The appellants filed their counter-affidavits, 
along with an application to seek vacation of the stay 
granted by this Court against the recruitment process. 
The stand taken by the appellants in their counter￾affidavit, inter alia, is that the recruitment process in 12 
question, is not being made in respect of posts held by 
the respondents- writ petitioners. In Paragraph No.14 of 
the counter-affidavit, the stand taken by the appellants is 
as follows:- 
“14. That the contents of para 25 of the 
writ petition to make the matter abundantly 
clear it is submitted that presently NESTS would 
not post any teacher against the posts occupied 
by these 28 contractual teachers in Uttarakhand. 
As of now, NESTS has not published the posts 
held by contractual teachers as they are 
engaged by state. The contract of teaches is 
with state society. NESTS has nothing to do with

our recruitment is being conducted in such a 
way as not to disturb the current contractual 
teachers”
21. In view of the statement, as given by the 
NESTS n the aforesaid Special Appeal, the respondents 
proceed with the recruitment process. 
22. Mr. Arvind Vashisth, learned Senior Counsel 
appearing for NESTS submits that NESTS has invoked 
the power for initiating the selection process in view of 
Clause 9(k) of the guidelines issued on 13.11.2020 as 
well as in view of Clause 3.12 of MoU dated 
26.08.2020. Clause 9(k) of the Guidelines dated 
13.11.2020 and Clause 3.12 of MoU dated 26.08.2020 
are being extracted here-in-below:- 
“9(k) If need be, could entrust the recruitment of 
teachers for States to an independent agency duly 
ensuring reservation quota prescribed therein. 
3.12 If need be, could entrust the recruitment of 
teachers for States to an independent agency duly 
ensuring reservation quota prescribed therein.” 
23. On perusal of these two clauses, which are 13 
identical, it reveals that this clause only given powers ‘if 
need be’, the recruitment process could be entrusted to 
an independent agency. 
24. Mr. Vashisth submits that after invoking the 
aforesaid clauses, the task of recruitment process were 
taken over by the NESTS, however, the task for 
conducting the examination was given to the Central 
Board of Secondary Education (CBSE). 
25. Since Mr. Vashisth submits that since NESTS 
has invoked the power as per Clause 3.12 of the MoU, 
and Clause 9(k) of the guidelines issued on 
13.11.2020, therefore, there is no illegality in the said 
selection process. 
26. On perusal of Clause 3.12 of the MoU, prima 
facie, it appears that even if the NESTS has invoked the 
power by invoking these clauses, no interference are 
called for by the NESTS in respect of any recruitment 
process provided that the same could be entrusted to 
an independent agency. An independent agency means 
no interference either by the NESTS, or by the State 
agency. Since as argued by Mr. Vashisth that the task 
of written examination was given to the CBSE, which 
itself reveals that there is directed interference by the 
NEST in recruitment process which itself is against the 14 
mandate of Clause 9(k) of the Guidelines dated 
13.11.2020 and Clause 3.12 of MoU dated 26.08.2020. 
27. When a particular question was put to Mr. 
Vashisth that what are those material on the basis of 
which, the NESTS has invoked the powers as provided 
in Clause 3.12 of MoU and clause 9(k) of the guidelines 
dated 13.11.2020, he seeks two weeks’ time to place 
before this Court all those material. 
28. Mr. Rajendra Dobhal, learned Senior Counsel 
appearing on behalf of the selected candidates, 
selected through the recruitment process initiated by 
the NESTS, which are subject matter of Writ Petition 
(S/S) No.1945 of 2024 and Writ Petition (S/S) No.1946 
of 2024, has argued that the petitioners of this bunch 
of writ petition, to whom Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned 
Senior Counsel is appearing, are the contractual 
employees, and they have no right to continue and 
they have to be replaced by regular incumbents. 
29. Apart from this, Mr. Dobhal submits that 
NESTS is competent to initiate the selection process by 
invoking power as conferred by Clause 3.12 of the MoU 
dated 26.08.2020, and Clause 9(k) of the guidelines 
issued on 13.11.2020. He further submits that since 
the petitioners in Writ Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 15 
and 1946 of 2024 are regularly selected candidates, 
therefore, they may be permitted to join the posts. 
30. No doubt, the petitioners in Writ Petition 
(S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 and 1946 of 2024 are regularly 
selected candidates through a recruitment process 
initiated by the NESTS, but the question, which still 
remains to be decided whether the NESTS is empower 
to initiate the selection process, which is, admittedly, 
the subject matter of other writ petitions, which are 
listed along with Writ Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 
and 1946 of 2024. If, at this stage, petitioners of Writ 
Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 and 1946 of 2024 are 
permitted to join, then the question, which has been 
raised in the connected writ petitions, will render 
infructuous. Therefore, joining of the petitioners in Writ 
Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 and 1946 of 2024, on 
their respective posts, pursuant to interim order passed 
by this Court on 233/24.10.2024 in connected writ 
petitions, will be subject to the final outcome of other 
connected writ petitions. 
31. Since Writ Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 
and 1946 of 2024 are connected with other writ 
petitions raising the question whether NESTS is 
empowered to initiate the selection process, therefore, 16 
all these writ petitions will be decided together. The 
respondents in Writ Petition (S/S) Nos.1945 of 2024 
and 1946 of 2024 are also directed to file their counter￾affidavits within the aforesaid period, so that the 
matter be decided finally on the next date. 
32. List on 02.12.2024. 
33. Interim order(s) passed by this Court on 
23/24.10.2024 in connected writ petitions shall 
continue. In addition to this, the respondents are 
further restrained from permitting any further selected 
candidate to join the post pursuant to the selection 
process initiated on the basis of the advertisement 
issued by the NESTS. It is also made clear that those 
regularly selected candidates, who are permitted to join 
pursuant to the interim order dated 23/24.10.2024, 
passed by this Court, be given joining to other 
institutions which fall within the territory of the State of 
Uttarakhand, without disturbing the petitioners who are 
working on contractual basis.
