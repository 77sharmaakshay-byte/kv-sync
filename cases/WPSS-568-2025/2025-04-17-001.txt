1. By the instant writ petition, petitioner, who was inducted in 
service as an Assistant Teacher in Primary Institution in 2009, is 
aggrieved by the office memo. dated 05.11.2024 issued by District 
Education Officer, Elementary Education, Chamoli whereby 
petitioner has been dismissed from service. Before dismissing the 
petitioner from service, petitioner was placed under suspension 
on 11.09.2024 on the charge that at the time of induction in service 
he submitted the mark-sheet of intermediate and on comparing
the same with the gazette notification, there is difference in the 
marks.
2. The main contention of the petitioner is that after placing 
petitioner under suspension, no charge-sheet was ever been given 
to the petitioner and not only this, even no Inquiry officer was 
appointed and only by way of a show cause notice, petitioner has 
been dismissed from the service by the order impugned. It is 
further contended that not only this, even while issuing the show 
cause notice not a single material was supplied to the petitioner 
like copy of the gazette notification as well as other material on 2
the basis of which petitioner was placed under suspension. It is 
further submitted that since no material was supplied to the 
petitioner while asking explanation pursuant to the show cause 
notice there was no occasion for him to submit his response. 
3. Learned counsel for the petitioner further submits that not 
only this, entire action has been taken against the petitioner 
pursuant to the order passed by the Division Bench of this Court 
in Public Interest Litigation. In PIL, in which only general 
direction were issued to verify the credentials of the teachers 
appointed in different institutions but in that PIL there was no 
reference of any particular teacher, therefore, learned counsel for 
the petitioner submits that even if the action is taken by the 
respondents pursuant to the order passed by the Division Bench 
in the PIL, then respondents were under the legal obligation to 
deal with case of each individual separately and independently 
but herein in this case, case of the petitioner was not dealt with 
by the respondents independently and in a very cursory manner 
without issuing charge sheet and without holding full-fledged
disciplinary proceedings petitioner is dismissed from service. 
4. Apart from this, learned counsel submits that after placing 
the petitioner under suspension by order dated 11.09.2024, he has 
not given any subsistence allowance, which itself vitiates entire 
disciplinary proceeding.
5. Learned counsel for the petitioner in reference to this has 
placed reliance upon certain provisions of the Uttarakhand 
Government Servant (Discipline and Appeal) Rules, 2003, 
(hereinafter referred to as Rules, 2003). First of all, she has given 
reference of Rule 4 (9) which reads as under:3
 “4 (9) A Government Servant placed under suspension or 
deemed to have been placed under suspension under this 
rule shall be entitled to subsistence allowance in accordance 
with the provisions of Fundamental Rule-53 of the 
Financial Hand Book, Volume-II, Part II to IV.”
6. By giving reference of Rule 4 (9) of the aforesaid Rules, 
2003, learned counsel for the petitioner submits that after 
suspension, petitioner was entitled to get subsistence allowance 
in accordance with the Fundamental Rule 53 of the Financial 
Hand Book Vol. II part II to IV but herein this case no subsistence 
allowance was paid. 
7. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that non 
payment of subsistence allowance vitiates the entire disciplinary 
proceedings, therefore, she submits that on this account alone 
impugned order cannot sustain and as such, liable to the 
quashed. Learned counsel for the petitioner further submits that 
dismissal of the petitioner from service is a major penalty. To 
examine this, Rule 3 of the Uttarakhand Government Servant 
(Discipline and Appeal) Rules, 2003 is being reproduced herein
as under:
“3 Penalties - The following penalties may, for good 
and sufficient reason and as hereinafter provided, be 
imposed upon the Government Servants :--
 x x x x 
Major Penalties-- (i) Withholding of increments with 
cumulative effect; (ii) Reduction to a lower post or 
grade or time scale or to a lower stage in a time scale; 
(iii) Removal from the Service which does not 
disqualify from future employment; (iv) Dismissal 4
from the Service, which disqualifies from future 
employment.” 
8. Admittedly, as per rule 3 (b), dismissal from service is also 
a major penalty and Rule 7 pertains to procedure for imposing 
major penalty. Rule 7 of the said Rules, 2003 is also being 
extracted herein below:

9. On perusal of Rule 7, it reveals that in case, delinquent 
employee denied the charge, in such an eventuality, disciplinary 
inquiry has to be initiated for which, it is mandatory to issue 
charge-sheet. Admittedly, in the present case, no charge-sheet has 
been supplied to the petitioner.
10. In pre lunch session, this Court directed the District 
Education officer to join the proceedings through V.C. and in 
compliance thereof, Mr. Dharam Singh Rawat, District Education 
Officer joined the proceedings. He fairly submits that no such 
charge sheet was ever been served upon the petitioner, as per 
Rule 7 of the Uttarakhand Government Servant (Discipline and 
Appeal) Rules, 2003. 
11. Surprisingly, in this petition, petitioner has been placed 
under suspension, but neither any charge sheet has been issued 
nor any inquiry officer was appointed, therefore, it reveals that in 
fact, no disciplinary proceedings whatsoever has ever been 
initiated against the petitioner and if no such disciplinary has 
been initiated against the petitioner, then how the impugned 11
order can be passed dismissing the petitioner from service 
therefore, entire action appears to be in complete flagrant 
violation of the Rules, 2003. 
12. Further, it reveals that in the impugned order only this 
much reference has been given that notice was given to the 
petitioner on 10.10.2024 but no response was filed by the 
petitioner and thereafter, by subsequent order dated 29.10.2024 it 
was communicated, since no response has been filed by the 
petitioner therefore, it was presumed that petitioner procured 
appointment on the basis of forged mark-sheet. 
13. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that on plain 
reading of the contents of the impugned order it appears that 
merely on the basis presumption drawn by the District Education 
Officer, petitioner is dismissed from service. It is argued that 
impugned order is per se illegal, since it is based upon only on
presumption that too without issuing any charge sheet. 
14. Apart from this, learned counsel for the petitioner submit 
that while giving the show cause notice to the petitioner, no 
material was supplied to the petitioner and in absence of any 
such material, petitioner was not in a position to give reply. 
15. I found force in the submission of the learned counsel for 
the petitioner that if the notice has been issued asking the 
explanation from the delinquent employee, then it has to be 
supported with some material, admittedly, in the present case, 
not a single material was supplied to the petitioner, therefore, the 
question, arises if no material was supplied to the petitioner in 
support of the notice, then how response can be filed by the 
petitioner in absence of any material. Most material evidence 12
against the petitioner is gazette notification but even copy of the 
gazette notification was not supplied to the petitioner. 
16. It is really surprising that the mark-sheet, which was 
furnished by the petitioner at the time of his induction in service 
was presumed to be forged one by comparing it with the gazette 
notification which was not supplied the petitioner, as such the 
procedure as adopted by the respondent is completely faulty. It 
has been informed that an FIR was also lodged against the 
petitioner with regard to the allegation that he procured 
appointment on the basis of forged marksheet and pursuant to 
that FIR, petitioner was arrested though enlarged on bail later on.
17. Learned counsel for the petitioner submits that without 
holding a full-fledged inquiry, petitioner has been dismissed 
from service by the order impugned. It has been informed by the 
District Education Officer that only after dismissal of petitioner 
from service, the FIR was lodged against the petitioner. 
18. If it is so then it reveals that criminal prosecution was 
initiated by lodging the FIR only after passing of order of 
dismissal of the petitioner from service. Apparently, by the order 
it reveals that before passing the order of dismissal, petitioner 
was placed under suspension in contemplation of disciplinary 
proceeding, therefore, in such an eventuality, as per 2003 Rules, 
respondents should issue charge-sheet followed by appointment 
of an Inquiry officer then give opportunity to the petitioner to 
adduce his evidence. Since dismissal from service is major 
penalty, therefore, the procedure, as prescribed under Rule 7 of 
the 2003 Rules has to be followed. 
19. On the other side, learned Standing Counsel for the State 
argued that merely based on the direction issued by the Division 13
Bench of this Court in PILs entire action has been taken and 
petitioner was removed from his service. He further argued that 
in view of Rule 7 (17) of the Rules, 2003, there is no need to issue 
charge-sheet as well as appointment of an inquiry officer. Since 
the disciplinary authority was satisfied that there is no need to 
hold an inquiry and therefore neither any charge sheet was 
issued nor any inquiry officer was appointed and based on show 
cause notice impugned order was passed.
If it so then, let the respondent may produce the order of 
the Disciplinary Authority recording in writing by giving reasons 
that it is not reasonably practicable to hold inquiry.
20. Though this argument, as advanced by learned Standing 
Counsel is not convincing, since admittedly, petitioner was 
placed under suspension, meaning thereby disciplinary 
proceedings were initiated and if petitioner was placed under 
suspension, then next course of action was to issue charge sheet 
followed by appointment of inquiry officer, which is completely 
missing in the present case. Admittedly, impugned order has 
been passed in a cursory and casual manner by completely over 
looking the statutory provisions of the Rules, 2023. Prima facie, 
this Court is of the view that impugned order is wholly 
unjustified.
