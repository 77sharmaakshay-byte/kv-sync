20.05.2024 IA/1/2023 (Bail Application)
IA/2/2023 (Delay Condonation Appl. in Objection)
In 
CRLA No.456 of 2023
Hon’ble Vivek Bharti Sharma, J.
Present Mr. Harshpal Sekhon, learned 
counsel for the applicant/appellant.
2. Mr. G.S. Sandhu, learned Additional 
Advocate General along with Mrs. Mamta Joshi, 
learned Brief Holder for the State.
3. Objection is taken on record. IA No.2 of 
2023 stands disposed of.
4. Present criminal appeal is preferred by the 
appellant/convict against the judgment/order 
dated 06.07.2023 passed by learned 3rd
Additional District & Sessions Judge, Rudrapur, 
District Udham Singh Nagar in Sessions Trial 
No.200 of 2018, “State of Uttarakhand Vs. 
Farjand & Others” whereby the appellant/convict 
has been convicted under Sections 452 and 307 
of the IPC and has been sentenced to undergo 
three years rigorous imprisonment along with 
fine of ₹10,000/- and seven years rigorous 
imprisonment along with fine of ₹30,000/-
respectively.
5. Heard on the Bail Application (IA/1/2023).
6. Learned counsel for the appellant/convict 
would submit that there is no conclusive 
evidence against the appellant/convict; that, it 
has come in the statement of Sub Inspector/PW6 
the Investigating Officer that the victim PW2 
Shakeela in her testimony did not tell what was 
the weapon used that has caused the injury to 
her; that, the PW7/Medical Officer had also 
stated in his evidence that the injury found on 
the head of the victim PW2 Shakeela could not 
have been inflicted by the axe, that, victim PW2 
Shakeela stated in her cross-examination that it 
was Asgar (real brother of the present 
appellant/convict), instead, who inflicted injury on her by stick; that, the appellant/convict has
been falsely implicated in the present crime; 
that, the final disposal of the appeal may take 
considerable time, therefore, the 
appellant/convict is entitled to bail.
7. Learned Additional Advocate General 
vehemently opposed the bail application on the 
ground that the case against the 
appellant/convict has been proved beyond 
reasonable doubt. 
State counsel would further submit that 
PW2 Shakeela has unequivocally stated in her 
testimony that she has seen axe in the hands of 
the present appellant/convict, meaning thereby,
that PW2 Shakeela was not in any doubt at all; 
that, if the Investigating Officer stated in his 
testimony that victim did not tell him that what 
weapon was used and he did not know how did
he write axe as a weapon in the statement of the 
victim then it is a negligence and incompetence 
on behalf of the Investigating Officer or may be 
for some reason to help the appellant/convict 
during investigation, therefore, the benefit of the 
same cannot go to the appellant/convict.
He would further submit that 
PW7/Medical Officer has unequivocally stated 
in paragraph no.6 of his testimony that the 
injuries found on the head of PW2 Shakeela
were dangerous in nature which could have 
caused death.
8. On the query of the Court, counsel for the 
appellant/convict fairly admitted that after the 
incident the victim had undergone surgery 
during the treatment.
9. In view of the above, without expressing 
any opinion about the merits of the appeal, the 
appellant/ convict does not deserve bail at this 
stage.
10 The bail application is dismissed
accordingly.
11. List this case on 21.10.2024.
(Vivek Bharti Sharma, J.)
