The Court made the following: 
ORDER: (per Hon’ble The Chief Justice Sri Raghvendra Singh Chauhan)
 
 The petitioners have filed applications, 
namely, IA No. 263 of 2021 and IA No. 264 of 2021, for 
extension of the stay order dated 15.06.2016, granted by a 
learned Single Judge, in C-482 Petition No. 222 of 2016 and 
C-482 Petition No. 223 of 2016. 
2. By order dated 15.06.2016, the learned Single 
Judge had stayed the proceedings of Criminal Case No. 85 of 
2016, pending in the Court of the Chief Judicial Magistrate, 
Nainital. 
 
3. The learned counsel for the petitioner submits that 
by order dated 24.04.2015, a learned Single Judge directed 
that an FIR be lodged against Ms. Khushboo Chaurasia, Lalit 
Mohan Mishra, Harimohan Gulati, Tej Prakash Gulati, 
Chandra Mohan Gulati and Jitendra Mohan Gulati. 
Consequently, not only FIRs have been registered, but 
charge-sheets have also been filed against Ms. Khushboo 
Chaurasia, Lalit Mohan Mishra, Rajesh Agarwal, Dinesh 
Kumar and Deep Chandra Bhatt. Therefore, aggrieved by 
the filing of the charge-sheet, the petitioners have 
challenged the same before this Court in C-482 Petition No. 3 
222 of 2016, C-482 Petition No. 223 of 2016, C-482 Petition 
No. 249 of 2016, C-482 Petition No. 269 of 2016 and C-482 
Petition No. 270 of 2016. 
4. By order dated 15.06.2016, learned Single Judge 
has stayed the further proceedings before the Trial Court. 
The learned counsel further submits that in Special Appeal 
No. 169 of 2015, two fundamental issues have been raised 
while challenging the order dated 20.04.2015: whether the 
learned Single Judge was legally justified in directing that an 
FIR should be lodged against Ms. Khushboo Chaurasia, Lalit 
Mohan Mishra, Harimohan Gulati, Tej Prakash Gulati, 
Chandra Mohan Gulati and Jitendra Mohan Gulati, or not? 
Or, secondly, in accordance with the requirement of Section 
340 Cr.P.C., the learned Single Judge should have directed 
that a complaint be filed? Since the decision on the said 
issues would automatically affect the validity of the FIR so 
lodged, and the charge-sheet so filed by the police, it will be 
in the interest of justice to continue the stay granted by this 
Court by its order dated 15.06.2016. After all, no fruitful 
purpose would be served in commencing the trial only to 
meet with the possibility that this Court may hold that an 
FIR could not be lodged, in fact, a complaint should have 
been lodged. Therefore, the commencement of the trial 4 
would not only be a futile exercise, but would also be a 
waste of the valuable time of the learned trial court. 
 
5. On the other hand, Mr. J.S. Virk, the learned 
Deputy Advocate General, submits that the C-482 petition 
would have to be argued and decided on its own merit. 
Therefore, there is no reason to continue the stay order 
dated 15.06.2016. 
6. Heard the learned counsel for the parties. 
7. Undoubtedly, in the Special Appeal No. 169 of 
2015, the learned counsel for the appellant, Mr. Arvind 
Vashistha, the learned Senior Counsel, has raised the issues, 
whether an FIR could have been directed to be lodged or 
not? Or, whether the learned Single Judge should have 
directed that a complaint be filed before the concerned 
Magistrate? Obviously, the lodging of an FIR, and filing of a 
complaint are on two different footing; they have two 
different consequences in criminal law. Since the FIR has 
been registered, and a charge-sheet has been filed only on 
the basis of the directions issued by the learned Single 
Judge, the finding given by this Court on the aforementioned 
issue would certainly affect the legality and validity of the 
FIR, and of the charge-sheet. Till these issues are decided 5 
by this Court in the abovementioned Special Appeal No. 169 
of 2015, it would be in the interest of justice to continue the 
stay dated 15.06.2016. In case, the trial court was directed 
to commence the trial, it will lead to a futile exercise. It 
may also needlessly waste the valuable time of the learned 
Trial Court. 
8. For the reasons stated above, the stay dated 
15.06.2016 is hereby confirmed. 
9. Interim order Extension Applications (IA No. 263 
of 2021 and IA No. 264 of 2021) both stand disposed of.
