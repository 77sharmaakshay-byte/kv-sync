COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
WPMS No. 81 of 2026
Hon'ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Navnish Negi, learned counsel 
for the petitioner.
2. Mr. G.C. Kandpal, learned Dy. 
Advocate General for the State.
3. This Court has observed in so 
many cases that despite availability of 
substantive and sanctioned vacancies, 
the respondent-State are not 
proceeding to fill up the post by way of 
regular recruitment process.
4. The question is that if the 
vacancies are sanctioned and available
why the Authorities are not proceeding 
with the recruitment process.
5. The learned counsel for the 
petitioner apprise to this Court that 
against sanctioned substantive post
regular attempts are being made by the 
State authorities to fill up the vacancies 
either through outsource, through 
contractor or by stop-gap arrangement 
which is wholly unwarranted and such 
practice is apparently exploitative, 
arbitrary, unreasonable, irrational, 
illogical and also violative of Article 14, 
16 and 21 of Constitution of India and 
contrary to the mandate of Part IV of 
Constituting of India.
6. In such view of the matter this 
Court is enlarging the scope of the 
petition keeping in view the fact that 
large number of young generations who are eligible and qualified are in queue 
waiting for regular appointment. The 
vacancies are there and the 
respondent/authorities are not 
proceeding with the regular recruitment 
process which appears to be great 
inaction on the part of the State 
Officials . 
7. Keeping in view the seriousness of 
the issue that despite availability of the 
large number of substantive and 
sanctioned vacancies in each and every 
department, no regular selection 
process are being made and instead of 
proceeding with the regular selection 
process, sanction posts are being filled 
up through outsource, daily wages, 
casual adhoc and by passage of time 
they become over age which is 
certainly an alarming situation.
8. Let the Chief Secretary of the 
State after collecting all data of 
sanctioned vacancies from the 
Secretary of each department file an 
affidavit and to explain why despite 
availability of substantive, regular and 
sanctioned vacancies the regular 
recruitment process are not being 
commenced and why such vacancies 
are being filled up by outsource and 
daily wager or adhoc. Apart from this, 
the State may also clarify why Class IV
posts has been declared as dying 
cadre, since, it has been apprised to 
the Court that Class IV post has been 
declared as dying cadre by adopting 
the policy of State of U.P. though 
subsequently policy was held to be 
ultravires by the High Court of 
Allahabad in the case of Revendra Singh and others vs. State of U.P. and 
another, 2013 SCC online All. 14059 by 
holding that the same is ultravires to 
Article 14, 16 and 19 of Constitution of 
India. 
9. Put up this matter on 16.02.2026
as part heard.
(Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
