 SA No.78 of 2023 
Hon’ble Vivek Bharti Sharma, J.
 Mr. T.A. Khan, Senior Advocate with Mr. 
Mohd. Saffy, Advocate for the appellant. 
2. Mr. Narendra Bali, Advocate for respondent 
no.2. 
3. Learned Senior Counsel for the appellant would 
submit that a suit for permanent injunction was filed 
by the plaintiff/appellant before the trial court with 
the averments that original plaintiff Smt. Kunti Devi 
(since deceased) was the owner and in possession of 
the suit property by way of the Will executed by her 
husband; that, respondent no.1/defendant no.1 was 
proceeded ex parte in the trial court on 15.11.2016; 
that, respondent no.2/defendant no.2 filed his written 
statement disputing the Will execution by his father 
in favour of the plaintiff with her lifetime and after 
her life to the son of the plaintiff other than the 
defendants, however, in the written statement he 
submitted that he is in possession of two rooms on 
the ground floor without specifying the numbers of 
the property. 
4. Per contra, learned counsel for respondent no.2 
would submit that in the trial court the original Will 
was not produced and it was not proved as per the 
mandate of law; that, in the first appellate court an 
application under Order 41 Rule 27 CPC was moved 
by the appellant/plaintiff to place the Will in 
question, however, the same was not proved by the 
oral evidence of the plaintiff and attesting witnesses 
hence the trial court was right in dismissing the suit 
and so was the first appellate court by disallowing the first appeal. 
5. To this, learned Senior Counsel appearing for 
the appellant would submit that, if for the sake of 
arguments it is assumed that the Will is not proved 
even then it is admitted fact that the plaintiff is the 
wife of the father of defendant nos.1 and 2 and she 
was, as per the written statement of the respondent 
no.2/defendant no.2, in possession of the suit 
property except the two rooms which the respondent 
no.2 says he is in possession. He would submit that in 
view of the above facts the learned Trial Court erred 
in not decreeing the suit for the relief of permanent 
injunction thereby restraining the respondent no.2 
from interfering in the possession of the suit property 
except the two rooms, which the respondent 
no.2/defendant no.2 claims to be in his possession. 
6. In view of the above, appeal is admitted on the 
following substantial question of law:- 
“Whether the relief of permanent injunction 
could not have been granted to the appellant/ 
plaintiff in respect of the rest of the suit property 
when it is admitted fact in the light of the 
averments made by defendant no.2 in his written 
statement that he is in possession of only two 
rooms of the suit property.”
