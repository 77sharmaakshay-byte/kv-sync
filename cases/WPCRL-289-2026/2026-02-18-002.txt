COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
WPCRL No. 289 of 2026
Sanjay Chaudhary and others ……….Petitioners
Vs.
State of Uttarakhand and others ……respondents 
Hon'ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Lalit Sharma, learned counsel 
for the petitioners.
2. Mr. Tumul Nainwal, learned A.G.A.
for the State.
3. Mr. Neeraj Garg, learned counsel 
for the caveator.
4. The petitioners are challenging the 
First Information Report dated 
30.01.2026 bearing FIR No.0019 of 
2026 at Police Station Rajpur District 
Dehradun, wherein they have been 
implicated for the offences punishable 
under Sections 318(4), 336(3), 338, 
340(2) and 61(2) of BNS, 2023.
5. It is argued by learned counsel for 
the petitioners that they are the bonafide 
purchasers and the sale deed is as per 
law and even otherwise the allegations
as alleged in the impugned FIR prima 
facie appears to be a civil in nature. 
Learned counsel for the petitioner also 
submits that if the complainant is 
aggrieved, he has a civil remedy to 
challenge the sale deed since the sale 
deed is registered with the Sub￾Registrar. He also submits that the law is 
well settled that if the document is registered one, it has to be presumed to 
be genuine one, however, in this 
particular case instead of availing civil 
remedy the complainant has lodged the 
FIR.
6. On the other hand, Mr. Neeraj 
Garg, learned counsel for the 
complainant argued that the sale deed in 
question is a void sale deed since as per 
the circle rate the cost of the land is 
Rs. 32,14,25,000/- but the consideration 
which is shown in the sale deed is on a 
very lower side i.e. Rs. 3,75,00,000/-. 
Apart from this, he has pointed out that 
in the sale deed no possession was given 
and since it is a void sale deed therefore, 
there is no need to challenge the same. 
Apart from this, he submits that the 
predecessor’s names were recorded from 
the revenue record in the revenue record 
were struck of and the order, whereby 
the petitioner’s name were recorded in 
the revenue record. 
7. To examine whether the sale deed 
is void or not, Mr. Shrikant Yadav, Sub 
Registrar, Dehradun, IV is present in 
Court and after having gone through
with the sale deed, he submits that so 
far as the stamp duty is concerned, the 
stamp duty is paid as per law, after 
taking into consideration the value of 
land as per circle rate.
8. Mr. Tumul Nainwal learned A.G.A. 
submits that the investigation is still 
going on and prima facie the offences 
are made out. 
9. Let the respondents may file 
counter affidavit positively within two
weeks. One week’s thereafter is granted 
to the petitioner to file rejoinder 
affidavit.10. After hearing the arguments of the 
learned counsel for the parties and 
taking into consideration that there is no 
dispute that the sale deed in question is 
a registered sale deed and further taking 
into consideration that the petitioner 
herein in fact appears to be bonafide
purchaser and the person who has sold 
out the land pursuant to sale deed is also 
an accused and is not before this Court, 
this Court is of the view that fro an 
interim measure let the investigation 
may go on, however, only till 16.03.2026 
no coercive action shall be taken against 
the petitioners, they cooperate with the 
investigation. On the next date the State 
shall apprise the status of the 
investigation.
11. It is further made clear that since 
the mandate of the investigation is that 
the investigation has to be carried out 
“as a whole” and should not confined to 
persons against whom the FIR has been 
lodged rather it eludes the complainant 
also and as soon as the concerned IO 
should also investigate all other aspects 
including the conduct of the informant
also.
12. List this matter on 16.03.2026 
immediately after fresh.
(Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
