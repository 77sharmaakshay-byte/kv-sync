Hon’ble Siddhartha Sah, J.
Mr. Piyush Garg, learned counsel for the 
appellants. 
2. Mr. Siddhartha Singh, learned counsel for 
respondent no.1.
3. The instant First Appeal under Section 96 of 
the Code of Civil Procedure has been preferred on 
behalf of the appellant (third party) against the 
judgment and decree dated 23.08.2019 passed by 
the learned Civil Judge (S.D.), Vikasnagar, District 
Dehradun in O.S. No. 134 of 2018, ‘Sri Ashish 
Mohan Vs. Sri Mahesh Kumar Prajapati’, whereby 
the suit filed by the plaintiff seeking specific 
performance of an alleged agreement to sell has 
been decreed ex parte.
4. There is a delay of 1157 days in filing the 
present appeal. Since there is a delay in preferring 
the First Appeal, the same is accompanied by a 
delay condonation application being IA No. 1 of 
2025.
5. The reasons assigned for the delay are that the
prescribed limitation period for filing the present 
appeal is 90 days from the date of the impugned 
order. The appellants are bona fide purchasers of the 
property in question by virtue of a registered sale 
deed dated 07.08.2019, executed by late Respondent 
No. 2, who was the recorded owner in the revenue 
records. The appellants were unaware of any 
agreement to sell or the pendency of the suit filed by 
Respondent No. 1 against Respondent No. 2. They 
purchased the property in good faith and without 
notice of the prior alleged agreement or pending litigation. The delay in filing the present appeal 
occurred primarily because the appellants initially 
relied upon the assurances of the LRs of Respondent 
No. 2, who had represented that they were diligently 
pursuing the matter and would protect the interests 
of the appellants. It was only in November 2024, 
upon seeking independent legal advice and 
verifying the case status, that the appellants became 
aware of the lack of diligence and possible collusion 
on the part of the LRs of Respondent No. 2 with 
Respondent No. 1. Furthermore, the appellants 
faced considerable difficulty in obtaining certified 
copies of the judgment, decree, and plaint, as the 
records had been summoned before the Hon’ble 
High Court in connection with another matter, i.e., 
A.O. No. 418 of 2024. Despite repeated efforts and 
filing of applications, the necessary documents 
could be obtained only on 02.01.2025.
6. It is further contented that thus, the delay is 
neither intentional nor attributable to negligence, but 
has occurred solely due to the circumstances stated 
hereinabove. The impugned judgment and decree 
directly affect the rights of the appellants, and if the 
delay is not condoned, the appellants shall suffer 
irreparable loss and injury. The appellants have 
acted bona fide and have shown sufficient cause for 
condonation of delay in filing the present third-party 
appeal.
7. The aforesaid contentions were opposed by 
the learned counsel for respondent no. 1 by filing 
objections, inter alia, contending that no plausible or 
satisfactory explanation has been furnished by the 
appellant, who was admittedly not a party to the 
original suit. After an inordinate delay of more than 
2116 days, no third party can be permitted to 
challenge the judgment merely on the basis of some 
agreement allegedly executed between such 
defendant and the appellants. A third party can, at best, step into the shoes of its predecessor-in-interest 
and cannot claim any rights superior to or 
independent of those of its vendor. The appellants 
were negligent in pursuing their alleged rights and, 
therefore, cannot be allowed to derive any benefit 
from their own inaction. No proper, plausible, or 
satisfactory cause has been shown by the appellants, 
who are complete strangers to the original 
proceedings. No sufficient cause, much less any 
legally sustainable ground, has been disclosed in the 
delay condonation application or in the affidavit 
filed in support thereof. 
8. In view of the facts and circumstances of the 
case, and considering that the present appeal is a 
third-party appeal preferred along with an 
application seeking leave to appeal, and further that 
the impugned judgment is likely to entail civil 
consequences upon the appellant and that a lis ought 
to be decided on merits rather than being thrown out 
on technicalities and accordingly, purely in the 
interest of justice, the delay in filing the appeal is 
hereby condoned, subject to payment of costs of Rs. 
5,000/-, which shall be deposited by the appellant in 
the High Court Bar Advocate Association Welfare 
Fund within a period of two weeks. Delay 
condonation application (IA No. 1 of 2025) 
accordingly stands allowed.
