Hon'ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Arvind Vashisht, learned Sr. Advocate 
assisted by Mr. Ayush Kaushik and Ms. Devanshi 
Joshi, learned counsel for the applicant. 
2. Mr. Zoheb Hussain, learned Special Counsel, 
(through V.C.), Mr. Lalit Sharma, learned Deputy 
Solicitor General for Union of India, Ms. Monika 
Pant and Mr. Atul Bahuguna, learned Standing 
Counsels for the Central Government / 
respondents. 
3. Mr. Arvind Vashisht, learned Sr. Advocate 
submits that the counsel appearing for the 
applicant in the trial court has placed a 
compilation of judgments including judgment of 
Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Manubhai 
Ratilal Patel through Ushaben Vs. State of 
Gujarat and others (2013) 1 SCC 314 but this 
judgment is nowhere dealt with by trial court 
while rejecting the release application which itself 
is reflected from the order dated 12.06.2026 and the 
judgment as relied upon from the other side in the 
case of Suresh Kumar Bhikamchand Jain Vs. 
State of Maharastra (2013) 3 SCC 77 is only dealt 
with by the trial court. 
4. By referring the judgment of Manubhai 
Ratilal Patel (supra), learned Sr. Advocate for the 
applicant has placed reliance to paragraph 24, 
which reads as under: “24. The act of directing remand of an accused is 
fundamentally a judicial function. The Magistrate 
does not act in executive capacity while ordering 
the detention of an accused. While exercising this 
judicial act, it is obligatory on the part of the 
Magistrate to satisfy himself whether the 
materials placed before him justify such a remand 
or, to put it differently, whether there exist 
reasonable grounds to commit the accused to 
custody and extend his remand. The purpose of 
remand as postulated under Section 167 is that 
investigation cannot be completed within 24 
hours. It enables the Magistrate to see that the 
remand is really necessary. This requires the 
investigating agency to send the case diary along 
with the remand report so that the Magistrate can 
appreciate the factual scenario and apply his mind 
whether there is a warrant for police remand or 
justification for judicial remand or there is no 
need for any remand at all. It is obligatory on the 
part of the Magistrate to apply his mind and not 
to pass an order of remand automatically or in a
mechanical manner.”
5. It is argued that the trial court while rejecting 
the release application has not applied its judicial 
mind at all and simply on the oral submission of 
the respondent, release application has been 
rejected. 
6. Learned Sr. Advocate for the applicant 
further submits that prolonged incarceration will 
dilute the stringent condition under Section 45 of 
the PMLA and it has to be relaxed. The delay in 
trial can also be a factor for relaxing the stringent 
conditions, as stipulated, under Section 45 of the 
PMLA. 
7. Now, this Court wants to know if the 
judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the 
case of Manubhai Ratilal Patel (supra) has been 
relied upon by the applicant before the trial court, 
why the trial court has not dealt and considered 
the mandate of Apex Court as reflected from 
paragraph 24 of the judgment as aforesaid while 
passing the order impugned. 8. Assisting counsel of Mr. Arvind Vashishst, 
learned Sr. Advocate, submits that he has placed 
the compilation of judgments before the trial court 
including the judgement of Hon’ble Supreme 
Court in the case of Manubhai Ratilal Patel
(supra) before the trial court but the same has not 
been considered. 
9. Let the same be verified by learned counsel 
for the respondents whether the aforesaid 
judgment was placed reliance before the trial 
court. 
10. List on 09.07.2026.
11. On the next date, Mr. Arvind Vashisht, 
learned Sr. Advocate shall also place before this 
Court compilation of the judgements, which has
been relied upon by them before the trial court.
