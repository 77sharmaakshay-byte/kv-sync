Hon’ble Mr. Justice Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. The instant appeal was heard at length on several occasions. 
Mr. Yogesh Tiwari, learned Standing Counsel for the State, 
though already concluded his argument on 04.8.2025 in reference 
to the issues as framed by the Hon’ble Apex Court and in 
response to this learned senior counsel Mr. D.S. Patni and Mr. 
Piyush Garg for respondents also advanced their arguments on 
05.08.2025 and 16.10.2025.
2. In response to their arguments Mr. Yogesh Tiwari, learned 
Standing Counsel advance his arguments today and submits that 
against the judgment and decree dated 20.10.1984 passed by the 
Trial Court the State preferred an appeal wherein the 
respondent/plaintiff also filed their cross objection and the appeal 
as well as the cross objection were decided by two separate orders 
on the same date i.e. on 16.06.1986. He bring to the notice of this 
court page 180 of the paper book, which is an order passed by the 
learned Civil Judge, Nainital, dated 16.06.1986 passed in Civil 
Appeal No. 113 of 1984, whereby, the cross objection of the 
respondent/plaintiff was rejected. By referring this order Mr. 
Yogesh Tiwari submits that even the respondent/plaintiff take a 2
plea before the First Appellate Court while filing the cross 
objection that the matter relates to the entries made in the revenue 
records and it is settled law that the Revenue Court cannot be 
directed by the Civil Court to correct the entries and not only this 
even First Appellate Court observed that no doubt the order can 
be declared void or illegal but directions to the correct entries 
cannot be given though subsequently holds that the Trial Court 
rightly passed the order for restoring the entries till the decision of 
Board of Revenue. He submits this is infact is the admission of the 
respondent/plaintiff that the Civil Court have no jurisdiction to 
correct the revenue entries.
3. He further submits that the arguments as advanced on the 
previous date by the learned counsel Mr. Piyush Garg that the 
judgment and decree passed by the Trial Court though correct but 
even otherwise the decree can be modified by the Appellate Court
under Order 41 Rule 33 r/w Order 42 Rule 1 of CPC is completely 
misconceived since this is own admission of the 
respondent/plaintiff that the Civil Court have no jurisdiction to 
correct the entries and as such neither the decree can sustain or 
modified.
4. In reference to the argument of the respondents that they 
got occupancy right by virtue of several land reforms Mr. Tiwari 
submits that no benefit in terms of Section 116 of T.P. Act can be 
given to the respondent/plaintiff since the lease already expired 
due to efflux of time in 1954 itself and the land reforms came into 
operation in 1969 in this area and as such the lease already 
expired in 1954 the land in question automatically vest with the 
Government in 1954.
5. Apart from this, he also placed before this court the copy of 
Khatoni of 1371 fasli, wherein, the land in question is recorded as 
“Kesari Hind” and submits that on expiry of the lease the land 3
already vest with the Government. In addition to this, he submits 
that against the order of the Collector the respondent/plaintiff 
also prefer revision under Section 219 of L.R. Act before the 
Commissioner, however, the Commissioner dismiss the revision 
by holding that the lease was granted under the Governments 
Grants Act, therefore the revision is not maintainable.
6. Being aggrieved with the order of the Commissioner they 
further approach to the Board of Revenue and the Board of 
Revenue remand the matter to the Collector with the direction to 
pass a fresh order after giving opportunity, however, against the 
order of Collector a review application was filed before the Board 
of Revenue and the order was reviewed and the order passed by 
the Collector dated 31.12.1981 was confirmed. He submits that the 
order passed on the review application was never been 
challenged by the respondent/plaintiff and it attains finality.
7. In reference to the argument of the learned counsel for the 
respondents that the Collector has no power to expunge the 
entries he submits that such argument as advanced by Mr. Piyush 
Garg is thoroughly misconceived since the Collector has absolute 
power to make necessary changes in the annual register in order
to maintain record of files in terms of Section 33(1) (b) of Land 
Revenue Act and as such the suit filed by the respondent/plaintiff 
is hit by Section 9 of CPC.
8. Put up this matter on 11.12.2025.
