IA No.1/2026 (Bail Application)
With 
CRJA No.157 of 2023
“Ankush Vs. State of Uttarakhand”
Hon’ble Ashish Naithani, J.
Mr. Rajveer Singh, learned counsel through VC along 
with Mr. Ravi Shankar Kandpal, learned legal aid counsel for 
the Appellant.
2. Mr. Vikash Uniyal, learned Brief Holder, for the State of 
Uttarakhand.
3. Appellant was convicted for offence punishable under 
Sections 3(a)/4 of the POCSO Act, and under Sections 363 and 
366 of IPC, vide judgment and order dated 13.10.2023, passed 
by the court of learned Additional Sessions Judge/F.T.S.C., 
Roorkee, District Haridwar in Special Session Trial No.144 of 
2022, “State Vs. Ankush”. Challenging his conviction and 
sentence, Appellant has filed this appeal.
4. The grounds submitted for consideration of the bail by 
the learned counsel representing the Appellant/convict is that the
Appellant is in jail since more than 4 years, and basically there 
are no offences made out against him. He further submits that 
whatever allegations are levelled against the Appellant/convict 
under which he found guilty are not actually well founded. It is 
also admitted fact that the victim and the Appellant/convict were 
in love relationship, and it is false statement that she was 
abducted as the victim herself accompanied the Applicant on her 
own free will. The Appellant is in jail since 22.08.2022, and it is 
requested that the Appellant be enlarged on bail as the victim 
and Appellant got married and since they got married and the 
victim had the consent, the offence cannot be covered as a rape 
or under the POCSO Act. 
5. Learned State Counsel representing the State has opposed the bail application of the Appellant. Firstly, that the 
victim was a minor at the time of the incident and the consent of 
the minor is of no consent. Secondly, though there was a love 
relationship between the victim and the Applicant still he had no 
right to take away the minor without the consent of her legal 
guardian and this itself is an offence which is defined as 
abducted. The medical and the FSL report support the 
prosecution version and clearly indicates that the victim 
underwent a physical exploitation, which clearly comes within 
the bracket of aggravative penetrative sexual assault against a 
minor. Hence, it is requested that the Applicant/convict may not 
be enlarged on bail.
6. After hearing the rival submissions of the learned 
counsel for the parties, this Court finds that grounds for bail are 
not found to be sufficient. Firstly, as per the records, the victim 
is clearly a minor. Secondly, consent of a minor is of no consent 
as per law. Thirdly, as per the statement of the victim, she has 
clearly stated that there was physical relationship made between 
the victim and the Applicant, and the FSL reports confirms the 
presence of semen from her clothing and undergarments that 
were sent for Forensic Science Laboratory and the victim was 
recovered from the rented premises where the 
Applicant/Appellant lived along with the victim. 
7. Without going into the merits, it is also clear that the 
Appellant abducted the victim, who is a minor from the legal
guardianship of her parents, and considering the same, the bail 
application of the Appellant is hereby rejected. 
 (Ashish Naithani, J.)
