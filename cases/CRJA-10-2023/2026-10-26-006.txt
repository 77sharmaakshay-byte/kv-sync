Instant appeal is preferred against the judgment and 
order dated 21.07.2022/22.07.2022, passed in Sessions Trial 
No.44 of 2020, State of Uttarakhand Vs. Sonu, by the court of 
F.T.S.C./Additional Sessions Judge, Roorkee, District Haridwar. By 
it, the appellant has been convicted under Section, 376(a)(b) IPC 
and Sections 3(m)/6 of the Protection of Children from Sexual 
Offences Act, 2012 (“the POCSO Act”) and sentenced accordingly.
The appellant seeks bail during pendency of the appeal.
2. Heard on Bail Application (IA) No.2 of 2024.
3. According to the FIR, on 29.02.2020, at 01:10 p.m., when 
the victim, a young girl, was returning home from school, on the 
way, the appellant dragged her in a vacant house of one Tare. The 
victim raised alarm. Some persons including the mother of the 
victim reached at the spot and the appellant was arrested at the 
spot. The FIR records that the appellant tried to molest the victim. 2
4. Learned counsel for the appellant submits that instant 
case is false; the case is not established; according to the FIR, it 
was Tare’s empty house, whereas as per the statement of the victim 
given during examination, the incident took place in a cowshed; in 
her statement under Section 164 of the Code of Criminal 
Procedure, 1973 (“the Code”) the victim tells that the appellant did 
take her in his own house, whereas in the court she tells that in a 
goat’s room the incident took place, from where she managed to 
escape herself. It is also argued that the FIR records that the 
appellant attempted to touch the private parts of the victim. 
5. It is also argued that at the most it could be a case under 
Section 9, 10 read with 18 of the POCSO Act and the sentence,
which could be imposed on the appellant that he has already 
undergone. 
6. Learned State Counsel submits that the victim is 07 years 
old; she has supported the prosecution case. He fairly concedes 
that the place of incident is different in the story narrated by the 
victim. 
7. It is the stage of bail post conviction. Much of the 
discussion at this stage is to be avoided. To the extent of 
appreciating the controversy the matter may be examined with the 
caveat that any observation made at this stage shall have no 
bearing at any subsequent stage of the case.
8. The place of incident is, in fact, different. According to the 
FIR, the appellant took the victim in Tare’s house whereas during 3
medical examination, the victim tells that the appellant took her in 
a cowshed; in her statement under Section 164 of the Code, the 
victim tells that the accused took her in his house whereas in 
court, she tells that she was taken in goat’s room. The victim has 
further stated in the court that after kissing her the appellant 
opened her button of shirt and lower the pant. Meanwhile she 
raised an alarm and ran away. 
9. Having considered this and other attending factors, we 
are of the view that it is a case in which the execution of sentence 
should be suspended and the appellant be enlarged on bail.
10. The bail application is allowed.
11. The execution of sentence, which is under challenge in 
this appeal shall remain suspended during the pendency of the 
appeal.
12. Let the appellant be released on bail, during pendency of 
the appeal on his executing a personal bond and furnishing two 
reliable sureties, each of the like amount, to the satisfaction of the 
court concerned.
13. List for final hearing in due course.
