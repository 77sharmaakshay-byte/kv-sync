Heard.
On 14.02.2025, this Court has 
requested the Central Pollution Control 
Board (“CPCB”)to visit the area and submit 
a report with regard to the mining activities 
that are being undertaken in the area in 
question particularly as to whether, 
mechanized or automatic machines are 
used or not.
The report has already been 
submitted by the CPCB. 
Today, during the course of hearing 
Mr. Arvind Kumar, Scientist C and Mr. 
Vinay K. Upadhyay, Scientist C, CPCB, 
also joined the proceedings through video 
conferencing.
The petitioner has also filed response 
to the report submitted by CPCB. 
The report of CPCB reveals as if the mining activity is already going on in the 
area in question. There were tyre marks in 
the mining areas. The minerals were found 
deposited in some of the stone crushers 
which were visited by the CPCB team. 
These stone crushers had no CTO.
How it is being done? In fact, on 
14.02.2025, this Court had requested the 
District Magistrate, Haridwar and Senior 
Superintendent of Police, Haridwar to join 
the proceedings through video 
conferencing. They are not present today.
Learned C.S.C. would submit that 
they were to join, but it appears that there 
was some disruption in the video 
conferencing.
Let both of them join the proceedings 
through video conferencing tomorrow at 
2:00 P.M.
During the course of hearing, the
learned counsel for the petitioner submits
that, in fact, the stone crushers were 
closed within the radius of 5 Km from the 
river bed. He would submit that pursuant 
to one such order passed in WPPIL No. 62 
of 2016, 48 stone crushers had already 
been closed, but they were de-sealed 
without any further order of this Court. He 
would also submit that, in fact, National 
Green Tribunal has also passed directions 
for shifting the stone crushers from within 
5 Km limit of river bed. He also refers to 
various orders of CPCB and NMCG. 
Referring a letter dated 16.02.2015 of the 
District Magistrate, Dehradun, he argued 
that, in fact, all the stone crushers within 
5 Km radius of river bed should be closed
so as to prevent illegal mining.
This Court had already passed order 
for banning mining in the area in question. 
But, it appears that orders of this Court 
are not being enforced. This is a serious 
issue.
The Court requested learned counsel 
for the Union of India as to whether the 127 Infantry Battalion (TA) Eco, Garhwal 
Rifles may assist this Court in the matter.
He submits that he will contact the officers 
and will revert tomorrow. 
Let a Senior Officer, who have 
experience in the relevant field, from 127 
Infantry Battalion (TA) Eco, Garhwal Rifles 
may join the proceedings tomorrow at 2:00 
P.M. to assist the Court in the matter. 
The Court posed a question to learned 
C.S.C. as to why the stone crushers may 
not be shifted beyond 5 Km radius of the 
river bed?
In fact, during the course of hearing, 
learned counsel for the Uttarakhand 
Environment Protection and Pollution 
Control Board would submit that in WPMB 
No. 281 of 2025, this Court has already 
passed direction directing the Secretary, 
Mining, Government of Uttarakhand and 
the Uttarakhand Pollution Control Board 
to identify areas in every district, which 
can be earmarked as dedicated zones for 
crushing and dumping of stones/RBM. 
Cannot it be done in District Haridwar by 
locating all 121 stone crushers beyond the 
radius of 5 Kms from the river bed. 
Automatic/electronic monitoring of the 
vehicles and entry of ravannas on the 
portal may also be some of the steps to 
ensure that mining is not being done in the 
area, in which this Court has banned it.
Learned C.S.C. submits that he would 
get instruction and revert back to this 
Court.
