COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
CLCON No. 59 of 2026
Hon'ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Bhupesh Kandpal, learned counsel 
for the petitioner.
2. Mr. P.C. Bisht, learned C.S.C. for the 
respondents.
3. Senior Superintendent of Police, Udham 
Singh Nagar, District Forest Officer, Udham 
Singh Nagar and District Mining Officer, 
Udham Singh Nagar joined the proceeding 
through V.C.
4. Two inspection reports in a pen-drive 
has been placed before this Court and this 
court perused the same and after perusing it 
reveals that regular illegal mining activities are 
going on in the area reference of which has 
been given in the order dated 23.05.2025 
passed in WPPIL No. 56 of 2025. I also perused 
the report of the officers from which it reveals 
that the officials physically inspect the spot, 
however, this Court is of the view that the 
officials are not aware how the inspection 
should be carried out. The Division Bench in 
another order dated 25.08.2025 passed in 
WPPIL No. 56 of 2025 observed as under:-
7. The Government shall also submit a 
report with regard to action to be taken to prevent the 
illegal smuggling of mineral across the border. The 
Government shall also submit an action plan for night￾time surveillance of sensitive areas and the border areas 
where it is alleged that illegal operators carry out 
mining during the night and transport the same before 
day break. In fact, in one case, a District Magistrate in 
one of the border areas had submitted that these illegal 
operators come fully armed with semi-automatic 
weapons, and that they pose a serious threat. If that be the case, we wonder what the police department is up to.
8. The Secretary (Mining) shall personally 
look into this issue and submit an action plan to prevent 
such illegal mining.
9. This Court is of the opinion that it would 
be preferable if the department resorts to aerial 
surveillance by using drones during night-times. The 
department may also consider establishing a control 
room wherein the live images relayed by the drones can 
also be shared on real time basis with the local Police 
Station. The Government may also consider reinforcing 
the strength of the Police Stations and also ensure that 
the policemen in these sensitive areas are equally well 
armed to enforce the law and to defend themselves in the 
course of any engagement.”
5. On perusal of para 9 of the aforesaid 
order, the Division Bench opined that the 
department should prefer to resort aerial 
surveillance by using “Drones” during night￾times and to consider about establishing a 
control room. This order was passed on 
25.08.2025 and almost 11 months have been 
passed but there is no progress and illegal 
mining activities still going on. 
6. The District Mining Officer who joined 
the proceeding through V.C. submits that since 
last two to three years penalty has been 
imposed on the persons who are indulged in 
illegal mining activities. Such an excuse is not 
acceptable. Imposing penalty does not mean 
that illegal mining has been stopped. If the 
department is focusing only on imposing 
penalty that means they are not focusing on 
stopping the illegal mining activities.
7. D.F.O. also joined the proceeding 
through V.C. and submits that he himself 
visited the spot and it reveals that in large scale 
the mining activities are going on.
8. Let Mr. P.C. Bisht, learned Chief 
Standing Counsel may get further instructions 
why department is not using a drone 
surveillance once Division Bench of this Court 
has already observed in the order dated 25.08.2025 passed in WPPIL No. 56 of 2025. 
Learned C.S.C. shall also furnish the details of 
persons against whom penalty was imposed 
on the charge of illegal mining activity and 
how many times penalty were imposed 
because this Court is of the view that if a 
person who indulged in mining activities and 
penalized for more than five times it means he 
acquired his assets movable or immovable 
through proceeds which he achieve through 
illegal mining, therefore, such assets should be 
treated as proceeds of crime.
9. List this case on 12.05.2026.
(Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
