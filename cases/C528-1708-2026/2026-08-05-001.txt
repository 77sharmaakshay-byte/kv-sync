3. After perusing the FIR, what this Court has observed 
that informant of this FIR dated 15.10.2025 bearing FIR No. 
500 of 2025, registered at Police Station – Rudrapur, District –
Udham Singh Nagar, has already filed a Civil Suit No. 203 of 
2025 on 14.07.2025 in the court of learned Civil Judge, 
Rudrapur, District Udham Singh Nagar against four persons 
including present applicants. 
4. It is very strange that after filing of civil suit, the 
informant has lodged the instant FIR No. 500 of 2025 wherein 
he has alleged that he has paid Rs. 3,32,44,000/- to the owner 
of the land i.e. Mr. Anand Prakash Dhawan, Om Prakash 
Dhawan and Rajendra Dhawan and despite receiving the
amount, sale deed has not been executed and even mutation 
has not been carried out. Prima facie, from the FIR, it reveals 
that it is purely a civil dispute and if the informant is having 
any grievance that sale deed is not being executed, he should 
have avail civil remedy by filing a suit for specific 
performance. This is one part. 
5. Another part is duty of the State Police before 
registration of the FIR. The Hon’ble Supreme Court repeatedly 
issuing guidelines for registration of the FIR, and particularly 
in the case of Lalita Kumar Vs. State of U.P, (2014) 2 SCC 1 
wherein the Hon’ble Apex Court issued certain directions as 
reflected from Para No.120 which reads as under:
"120. In view of the aforesaid discussion, we hold: 
120.1. The registration of FIR is mandatory under Section 154 of the 
Code, if the information discloses commission of a cognizable offence 
and no preliminary inquiry is permissible in such a situation. 
120.2. If the information received does not disclose a cognizable 
offence but indicates the necessity for an inquiry, a preliminary 
inquiry may be conducted only to ascertain whether cognizable 
offence is disclosed or not. 
120.3. If the inquiry discloses the commission of a cognizable offence, 
the FIR must be registered. In cases where preliminary inquiry ends in closing the complaint, a copy of the entry of such closure must be 
supplied to the first informant forthwith and not later than one week. 
It must disclose reasons in brief for closing the complaint and not 
proceeding further. 
120.4. The police officer cannot avoid his duty of registering offence if 
cognizable offence is disclosed. Action must be taken against erring 
officers who do not register the FIR if information received by him 
discloses a cognizable offence. 
120.5. The scope of preliminary inquiry is not to verify the veracity 
or otherwise of the information received but only to ascertain whether 
the information reveals any cognizable offence. 
120.6. As to what type and in which cases preliminary inquiry is to 
be conducted will depend on the facts and circumstances of each case. 
The category of cases in which preliminary inquiry may be made are 
as under: 
(a) Matrimonial disputes/family disputes 
(b) Commercial offences 
(c) Medical negligence cases 
(d) Corruption cases 
(e) Cases where there is abnormal delay/laches in initiating criminal 
prosecution, for example, over 3 months' delay in reporting the 
matter without satisfactorily explaining the reasons for delay. 
The aforesaid are only illustrations and not exhaustive of all 
conditions which may warrant preliminary inquiry. 
120.7. While ensuring and protecting the rights of the accused and 
the complainant, a preliminary inquiry should be made time-bound 
and in any case it should not exceed fifteen days generally and in 
exceptional cases, by giving adequate reasons, six weeks' time is 
provided. The fact of such delay and the causes of it must be reflected 
in the General Diary entry. 
120.8. Since the General Diary/Station Diary/Daily Diary is the 
record of all information received in a police station, we direct that all 
information relating to cognizable offences, whether resulting in 
registration of FIR or leading to an inquiry, must be mandatorily 
and meticulously reflected in the said diary and the decision to 
conduct a preliminary inquiry must also be reflected, as mentioned 
above.”
6. Now, the question is despite these guidelines, the 
instant FIR has been lodged. It is also pointed that in this FIR, 
there is no disclosure about the filing of suit by the informant, 
which was filed on 14.07.2025, though the FIR was lodged 
subsequently, on 15.10.2025. 
7. Not only this, in so many cases, what this Court 
observed that in purely civil matters, repeatedly FIRs are 
being registered. This Court in one case stayed the criminal 
proceeding, in which a WILL was executed by father of the 
informant in the year 2003 and on the basis of WILL sale deed 
was also executed and after death of father, informant lodged 
the FIR in the year 2025 alleging that WILL executed by the 
father of informant is forged, and surprisingly, during 
investigation, no ingredient with regard to forged WILL was found despite this charge sheet has been filed under other 
penal provisions. 
8. Now, the question is why the State agencies are 
repeatedly without gone through with the guidelines issued 
by Hon’ble Supreme Court lodging the FIR in civil matters. In 
some cases, when the issue came to the notice of this Court, 
the State informed that there is shortage of staff and most of 
the staff are busy in VIP duty or to maintain law and order 
situation and in addition to this, they have also been assigned 
the task of investigation. 
9. Now, the question if there is shortage of police 
personnel. Whether investigation can be handed over to a 
person, who has no basic knowledge how to investigation is to 
be conducted, since outcome of the investigation is a very 
crucial steps under the criminal jurisprudence since the 
outcome of the investigation is the basic foundation of 
commencement of trial.
10. Let the State Counsel may advise to their officials to 
discuss for establishment of Investigation Branch in the Police 
Department and shortlisted the officials, who are having 
expertise to conduct investigation and also well versed with
the law and those shortlisted candidates should be the task of 
investigation only.
11. Let the Secretary (Home), Director (Prosecution) and 
Director General of Police may file their personal affidavit on 
the issues.
12. Let the personal affidavit be filed within 15 days 
positively.
13. In the meantime, so far as present case is concerned, 
concerned I.O. shall file his affidavit and explain what 
material he has collected in the present case to file charge 
sheet and the SHO concerned shall also file his affidavit 
whether before registration of the FIR he has followed 
guidelines formulated by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the 
case of Lalita Kumari (supra).
14. The matter is being kept part heard and to be listed 
along with C528 No. 1699 of 2026 wherein this Court also 
observed that the dispute is appears to be civil and 
investigation appears to be faulty. 
15. List on 31.08.2026 under the heading of Civil Dispute / 
Faulty Investigation.
