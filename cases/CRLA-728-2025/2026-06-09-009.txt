Instant appeal is preferred against the judgment and 
order dated 22.11.2025, passed in Sessions Trial No.179 of 2022,
State Vs. Manavdeep alias Manav and others, by the court of 
Second Additional Sessions Judge, Kashipur, District Udham Singh 
Nagar. By it, the appellant has been convicted under Section 302 
read with 34 IPC and sentenced accordingly. The appellant
Mandeep alias Manav seeks bail during pendency of the appeal.
2. Heard on bail application.
3. This is the second bail application of the appellant. His 
first bail application has already been rejected on 24.03.2026.
4. Learned counsel for the appellant submits that the 
second bail application has been filed on the ground that the date 
of birth of the appellant as per High School Certificate is 
08.08.2006, which means on the date of incident the i.e. on 2
22.05.2022, he was merely 15 years and 09 months old, a child in 
conflict with law (“the CIL”). The High School Certificate issued on 
31.07.2021 has also been filed as Annexure No.2 in the second bail 
application. 
5. State has filed response to the second bail application and 
in para 6 of the affidavit, the State has admitted the genuineness of 
the High School Certificate of the appellant and it has also been 
verified by the State that as per the school record, the date of birth 
of the appellant is 08.08.2006. 
6. According to the appellant, his date of birth is 
08.08.2006, based on the High School Certificate and the 
genuineness of the High School Certificate has been admitted by 
the State. It makes out the case of bail at this stage.
7. Having considered this and other attending factors, we 
are of the view that it is a case in which the execution of sentence 
should be suspended and the appellant Mandeep alias Manav be 
enlarged on bail.
8. The bail application is allowed.
9. The execution of sentence, which is under challenge in 
this appeal shall remain suspended during the pendency of the 
appeal qua the appellant Mandeep alias Manav.
10. Let the appellant Mandeep alias Manav be released on 
bail, during pendency of the appeal on his executing a personal 3
bond and furnishing two reliable sureties, each of the like amount, 
to the satisfaction of the court concerned.
11. Since post conviction, the appellant Mandeep alias Manav 
has taken a plea that he was a CIL at the time of incident, it would 
be now necessary to determine the date of birth of the appellant. 
12. In the case of Raju Vs. State of Haryana, 2019 SCC 
OnLine SC 258, the issue of juvenility was considered. In that case, 
the Hon’ble Supreme Court observed as follows:-
“16. At this point, it is necessary to briefly discuss the findings of the 
High Court in the impugned judgment [Raju v. State of Haryana, 
2011 SCC OnLine P&H 10820] regarding the age of the accused to 
underscore that it has not conducted the inquiry stipulated as per 
Section 7-A and Rule 12. Before the High Court, the appellant 
submitted a report of the Assistant Commissioner of Police, Bhondsi, 
Gurgaon to the effect that his date of birth was 12-7-1984, thereby 
claiming the benefit of the 2000 Act. This plea was rejected on the 
grounds of failure to raise the plea of juvenility before the trial court; 
non-production of birth certificate in spite of an opportunity being 
granted to do so; absence of the appellant's name in the birth register 
dated 12-7-1984 and for the years 1983-84 and 1984-85; non￾corroboration of the date of birth certificates issued by schools 
attended by the appellant through other documentary evidence; non￾matching of the name on such certificates (Raj Kumar) with the name 
of the appellant as brought on record (Raju); and non-corroboration 
of the address of the appellant through such certificates, which 
simply stated that the date of birth of the student named Raj Kumar 
was 12-7-1984.
17. The High Court evidently did not even frame its discussion in 
terms of whether the evidence brought on record was sufficient to 
conduct an inquiry under the 2000 Act and the 2007 Rules, let alone 
order and conduct such an inquiry. On the contrary, it simply 
recorded that the evidence did not go to show that the appellant was 
a juvenile at the time of the commission of the offence, and proceeded 
to affirm the conviction of the appellant on merits.”4
13. Undoubtedly, a convict may raise the issue of juvenility 
at any stage, therefore, we direct the Registrar (Judicial) of this 
Court to make an inquiry and determine the age of the appellant 
Mandeep alias Manav on the date of commission of offence. He 
shall submit his report before this Court within four weeks. 
14. List after four weeks along with connected matters.
