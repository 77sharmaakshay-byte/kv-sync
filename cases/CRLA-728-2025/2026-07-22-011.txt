The instant appeal has been preferred against 
judgment and order dated 20.11.2025, passed in Sessions Trial 
No.179 of 2022, State Vs. Manavdeep and Others, by the court of 
Second Additional Sessions Judge, Kashipur, District Udham 
Singh Nagar. By it, the appellant has been convicted under 
Sections 302 read with 34 IPC, and sentenced accordingly. 
2. Heard.
3. This appeal has already been admitted.
4. List in due course for final hearing along with the 
connected cases. 
5. Heard on the Bail Application (IA) No.01 of 2025
6. According to the FIR, on 25.05.2022, the appellant 
armed with lathi and danda assaulted the deceased Vishal 
Kamboj, due to which he sustained multiple injuries and died. He 
was taken to hospital. Thereafter, the FIR was lodged. 2
7. Learned Senior Counsel appearing for the 
appellant submits that the FIR is delayed; the incident allegedly 
took place at 01:30 in the afternoon, whereas, the FIR was lodged 
in the midnight at 12:28 hours, and there is no explanation to it.
Learned Senior Counsel has also raised the following arguments 
in his submission:-
i) The appellant is not named in the FIR.
ii) How the name of the appellant surfaced 
during the investigation, it is not clear.
iii) On 26.05.2022, according to the 
Investigating Officer, some recovery was 
made, but the recovery memo does not 
reveal as to at whose instance the recovery 
was made.
iv) The prosecution relies on a CCTV footage 
from an LIC building, but that LIC building 
is not shown in the site plan. 
v) It is a case of no identification.
vi) The CCTV footage was taken into custody 
by the police on 22.07.2022, whereas, the 
appellant was arrested on 26.05.2022 
itself. 
8. Learned State Counsel submits that there have 
been many accused persons in the case, and the bail application 
of the co-convict Arman has already been rejected by this Court 
while considering the evidence of the eye witnesses. It is also 
argued that PW3, Gagandeep, and PW4, Vikram Singh alias Viky, 
both were accompanying the deceased when he was assaulted by 
the appellant and the co-convicts. It is also argued that initially, 
the appellant was not named in the FIR. It was named against 
some persons with other persons also written in the FIR. It is 
argued that subsequently, the CCTV footage was revealed and 
PW7, Sub Inspector Bhagwan Giri Goswami, and PW10, Sub 3
Inspector Vikram Singh Dhami, have categorically stated that 
after examining the CCTV footage, the informant had identified 
the appellant.
9. It is a stage of bail post conviction. Much of the 
discussion is not expected of. Arguments are being appreciated 
with the caveat that any observation made in this order shall have 
no bearing at any subsequent stage of the proceedings. 
10. A father lost his son. FIR was lodged after 12 
hours. What would be its effect? Can it be termed as delayed? It 
would fall for consideration during the hearing of the appeal. 
11. The informant is the father of the deceased. The 
deceased was accompanied by PW3, Gagandeep, and PW4, 
Vikram Singh alias Viky, when the incident took place. Both of 
them have categorically stated that it is the appellant, who had 
assaulted the deceased along with the co-convicts. 
12. Having considered, this Court does not see any 
ground, which may entitle the appellant to bail. Accordingly, the 
bail application deserves to be rejected.
13. The bail application is rejected.
