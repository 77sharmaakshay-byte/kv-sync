3. Appellant is the writ petitioner. Appellant 
sought to quash the order dated 30.10.2012 passed by 
the sixth respondent, who is the Principal of an aided 
Inter College. Further direction was sought to appoint

4. The case of the appellant, in brief, is as 
follows:

Secondly, it is the case of the respondents that the

found as follows: 
“3. After creation of the State of 
Uttarakhand on 09.11.2000, the

have been framed under the 
Uttarakhand School Education Act, 
2006. Under Regulation 91, such

year 2009 such appointment can be

Therefore, there is no provision for

in a grant-in-aid minority institute. 
4. The case of the petitioner further is

7. Learned Senior Counsel appearing for the

Bench of Allahabad High Court in the matter of 
Committee of Management, National Inter College and
others Vs. State of U.P. and others, reported in (2012)

the respondent employee died in the year 1998, yet the 
authorities had granted the benefit of Dying-in￾Harness. 
8. Per contra, learned Brief Holder for the State 
would submit that the relevant provision, which is 
applicable, is the provision contained in Regulation 
103, which proviso was applicable in the State of 
Uttarakhand, as on the date of the death of the mother 
of the appellant and, as per the same, appellant is not 
entitled. 
9. Mr. Anil Kumar Joshi, learned counsel for 
the sixth respondent would reiterate the stand that, 
under Regulation 103, the law which was applicable in 
the State of U.P. on the appointed day, the proviso did 
not extend the benefit of Dying-In-Harness to minority 
aided institutions. He would submit that the 
amendment, which took place in the State of U.P., was 
in the year 2001, by which the proviso to Regulation 
2017:UHC:104486
103 was repealed; but, this will not assist the 
appellant. 
10. It is not in dispute that, under the U.P. 
Education Act, 2006, Regulations have been framed in 
the year 2009. Under Regulation 91 thereof, the Right 
to Compassionate Appointment is given and there is no 
exemption in favour of the minority institutions in
Regulation 91. 
11. As far as judgment of the Allahabad High 
Court is concerned, we cannot agree with it if it is 
understood as laying down that in respect of the death 
which took place in the year 1998, the provision of
Dying-in-Harness, which was not applicable as on the 
date of the death; but, which became applicable 
consequent on the repeal of the proviso in the year
2001 would still be available to an employee. A 
compassionate appointment cannot be claimed as a 
right. Compassionate appointment is made available
either on the basis of the Scheme or a Statute or an 
Order. In the absence of the same, no employee can
claim a right to be appointed by way of compassionate 
appointment. It may be another matter that the 
institution may have made such appointments, as 
found by the learned Single Judge and complained of
by the learned Senior Counsel for the appellant; but, 
again we agree with the learned Single Judge that it 
may not give a legal right to seek a mandamus or have 
the order quashed. 
2017:UHC:104487
12. In short, we may sum up the legal position. 
By way of Regulation inserted in the year 1995, in 
Regulation 103, it provided for appointment by Dying￾in-Harness for employees like the appellant. However, 
it contained the proviso, which rendered it inapplicable 
to minority institutions. The State of Uttarakhand was 
born on 09.11.2000. Therefore, the law contained in 
Regulation 103 became applicable. It was the law, 
which was applicable in the year 2007, when the 
appellant’s mother died. Under the said law, the 
appellant could not possibly claim as a matter of legal 
right the benefit of appointment in Dying-in-Harness in 
the sixth respondent institution being a minority 
institution though receiving aid. The amendment in
the year 2001 in the State of U.P. by which the proviso 
to Regulation 103 was deleted cannot have any effect 
on the legal rights of the parties involved in this case. 
The subsequent enactment of the Regulation under the 
Uttarakhand Education Act in the year 2009 also, 
though it may create a right prospectively, cannot 
advance the case of the appellant as it does not has 
retrospective operation. Therefore, in the absence of 
any legal right given by any law or order or scheme, the 
appellant cannot claim a right to be appointed under 
the Dying-in-Harness Scheme. 
13. Obviously, the letter which was written by 
the Government stating that in the year 2011 the 
Dying-in-Harness was applicable to the minority 
institution also is apparently based on the Regulations 
of 2009. Equally, the fact that the sixth respondent 
2017:UHC:104488
has sent a letter for appointment of the appellant 
under the Dying-in-Harness Scheme may not by itself
give a legal right to the appellant to claim benefit of 
Dying-in-Harness Scheme when the matter is 
considered in the Court. Then, lastly, there is a case of 
Mr. A.K. Joshi, learned counsel appearing for the sixth 
respondent that appellant was considered in the 
interview and he was not found suitable; but, that was 
alongwith all others; but, we need not go into the said 
matter in view of the absence of the legal right. 
14. Accordingly, the Appeal fails and is 
dismissed.
