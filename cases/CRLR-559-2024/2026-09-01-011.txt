3. This criminal revision has been filed 
challenging the order dated 24.07.2024
passed by learned District and Sessions 
Judge in Criminal Appeal No. 67 of 2022, 
which was preferred against the judgment and 
conviction order passed in Criminal Case No. 
140 of 2013 (New No. 1211/2018) under 
Sections 420, 467, 468, 471 of IPC r/w 
Section 120-B and 509 of IPC.
4. Heard.
5. Admit.
6. LCR has already been received.
7. Also heard on Exemption Application (IA 
No. 3 of 2024).
8. Learned counsel for the revisionists 
submits that the order of conviction, which 
was upheld by the Appellate Court cannot be 
sustained inasmuch as the document, which 
was sent to FSL for matching the signature of the Executor of the Will, was admittedly not 
the signature of the Executor and the 
Appellate Court as well as the Trial Court 
relied only on the report of the FSL. It is also 
submitted that the documents, which were 
sent to FSL for matching did not match with 
the original signature. It is further argued that 
in the appellate order, it is mentioned that the 
document, which was sent for FSL, according 
to learned Judge, was receipt of the Sub￾Registrar Office and not the letter requesting 
him to execute the Will in the Ashram. As 
such, the whole basis of conviction is based 
on the wrong documents and thus the case of 
forgery is not made out. Learned counsel for 
the revisionists further submits that earlier 
prosecution case was that the signatures were 
forged by the revisionists and later on the 
prosecution has developed a case story that a 
new line has been added in the body of the 
Will itself. On this basis, it is submitted that a 
new case cannot be developed at the trial and 
appeal stage, without there being any specific 
charge.
9. Per contra, learned State Counsel has 
vehemently opposed the application for 
exemption to surrender on the ground that 
since the conviction is more than three years, 
therefore, the application for exemption to 
surrender could not be entertained. 
Furthermore, it is submitted that the 
revisionists are avoiding the court at the stage 
of trial. Learned State Counsel has further 
relied upon the judgment passed by Hon’ble 
Apex Court in the case of Janardan Ray Vs. 
The State of Bihar and Another, in which, the 
Hon’ble Apex Court has held that reasons are 
to be recorded in writing while granting 
exemption from surrendering.
10. Per contra, learned counsel for the revisionists submits that the Court after 
recording reasons may grant exemption from 
surrendering at the time of filing of revision, 
as has been held by the Division Bench of this 
Court in its judgment dated 26.05.2023 in the 
case of Shubham Singhal Vs. High Court of 
Uttarakhand, Through the Registrar General 
(WPMB No. 84 of 2023).
11. Having heard learned counsel for the 
parties and perusing the records, this Court is 
of the opinion that the document, on the basis 
of which, conviction order has been passed 
itself creates a doubt whether it contains the 
original signature of the testator of the Will or 
not.
12. Considering the submissions, as 
advanced by learned counsel for the 
revisionists under the facts and 
circumstances of the case and also in view of 
the judgment passed by the Division Bench of 
this Court in the case of Shubham Singhal Vs. 
High Court of Uttarakhand, Through the 
Registrar General, the Exemption Application 
is allowed. Revisionist is exempted from 
surrendering before the Trial Court concerned 
in execution of sentence.
13. List this matter on 09.10.2026.
