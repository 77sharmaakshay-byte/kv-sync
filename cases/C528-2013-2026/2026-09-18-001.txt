Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J. 
1. Mr. Altaf Hussain, Mr. Vivek Vaibhav (through 
V.C.) and Ms. Ashwarya Sharma, learned counsel for 
the applicant.
2. Mr. Himanshu Sain and Mrs. Meenkashi 
Sharma, learned A.G.A. for the State.
3. These two petitions are preferred by the 
applicant under section 528 of BNSS, 2023, against the 
order of the Trial Court rejecting the application 
moved under section 451 of Cr.P.C.
4. It is argued that the order passed by the Trial 
Court is patently illegal and against the mandate of 
section 451 of Cr.P.C. It is submitted that in the earlier 
round of litigation the entire Trial Court proceeding 
was challenged but this court dismissed the petitions 
of the accused persons by judgment and order dated 
17.06.2026 and during the pendency of those 
proceedings, this court passed an order by restraining 
the Trial Court from passing any order on pending 
application moved under section 451 of Cr.P.C. and 
being aggrieved with the same the applicant 
approached to the Hon’ble Apex Court by way of Criminal Appeal No. 2525 of 2025 and the Apex Court 
set aside the order of the High Court by holding that 
the order of the High Court restraining the Trial Court 
from passing any order in pending application under 
section 451 of Cr.P.C. is illegal and not tenable in law 
and simultaneously the appeal was disposed of by 
directing that the Trial Court to decide the pending 
application moved under section 451 of Cr.P.C. in 
accordance with law.
5. It is submitted that the three persons namely 
Rakesh Mehra, Madhvi Arora and Anushka Arora 
have been chargesheeted but one of the accused 
persons namely Rakesh Mehra neither join the 
investigation at any point of time, nor appeared before 
the Trial Court and is still absconding. He not assail 
the judgment dated 17.06.2026 before and attains 
finality. It is further argued that so far as the other co￾accused, who are the daughters of Rakesh Mehra are 
concerned they assailed the order dt. 17.06.2026, 
however, their SLP was also dismissed and liberty was 
given to move a discharge application and pursuant to 
such liberty both the discharge application is moved,
which is pending for consideration.
6. It is submitted that Rakesh Mehra one of the 
absconded accused neither approached to the Apex 
Court nor file any discharge application. Even then,
the application moved under section 451 of Cr.P.C. in 
Criminal Case No. 3419 of 2023 ‘State vs. Rakesh 
Mehra’ the Trial Court reject the application on the 
same reasoning, whereby the application moved in 
Criminal Case No. 2435 of 2023 ‘State vs. Madhvi 
Arora and Others’ is rejected.
7. It is argued that so far as Criminal Case No. 3419 
of 2023 is concerned in this case Rakesh Mehra is the 
accused and he has not put appearance before the 
Trial Court, therefore, there was no any occasion for 
the Trial Court to reject the application moved under 
section 451 of Cr.P.C.
8. Apart from this, it is further argued that while
dismissing their earlier petition this court also 
observed that huge amount was siphoned off by 
Rakesh Mehra by crediting the same in the accounts of 
his daughters. Learned counsel for the applicant 
submits that the order is patently illegal and even 
otherwise the Trial Court proceedings are put on hold 
since 2023 only because of the reason that Mr. Rakesh 
Mehra has not turned up to join the Trial Court proceeding. 
9. Learned counsel for the applicant in support of 
her arguments also placed reliance to the judgment of 
Apex Court in the case of Krishnan Narayana vs. 
State of Andhra Pradesh and Others 2026 SCC Online 
SC 1386 and she particularly placed reliance to para￾19, 20 and 22, which are quoted herein as under:
“19. It is also submitted that the alleged resignation dated 
16.06.2023 was fraudulently obtained. The undertaking 
has also been forged. Separate proceedings are pending 
regarding these disputes. The High Court has failed to 
correctly appreciate these facts which has resulted in gross 
miscarriage of justice.
20. Per contra, learned counsel for the respondents 
submits that the appellant has suppressed the fact that the 
vehicles were purchased by utilising the funds of the 
respondent company. 2002 (10) SCC 283.
22. He has also failed to disclose that he had voluntarily 
given an undertaking to the effect that the machines in the 
name of Pure Minerals will remain at the quarry of the 
respondent company till he settles his accounts with the 
company, as the payment for the loan taken from Shriram 
Finance Ltd. on the machines was paid from the account of 
the respondent company.”
10. All these aspects need deliberation and as the 
order impugned is passed after hearing the other side, 
therefore, it is necessary to examine the version of the 
other side.
