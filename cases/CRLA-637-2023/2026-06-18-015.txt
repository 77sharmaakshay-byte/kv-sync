Hon’ble Pankaj Purohit, J. (Oral)
Learned Senior Advocate appearing for the 
appellants would press the Second Bail Application (IA 
No. 6/2024) moved on behalf of appellant/applicant 
no.1, Sadiq @ Chand, and appellant/applicant no.2, Smt. 
Hussnojahan @ Husanara, in CRLA No. 637 of 2023, 
Shri Sadiq & Another vs. State of Uttarakhand.
2. The first bail application of the 
appellants/applicants was rejected by this Court vide 
order dated 26.06.2024.
3. The appellant/applicant no.1-Sadiq @ Chand 
and his wife Husnojahan @ Husanara are facing 
incarceration, on their conviction vide judgment and 
order dated 17.08.2023, passed by learned Additional 
Sessions Judge/FTC (POCSO), Dehradun in Special 
Sessions Trial No.48 of 2019, whereby the appellant￾Sadiq @ Chaand has been convicted under Sections 506, 
120-B IPC and under Section 5(j)(ii)/6 of POCSO Act, 
2012. For the offence under Section 5(j)(ii)/6 of POCSO 2
Act, 2012, he was sentenced with rigorous imprisonment 
of 20 years along with a fine of Rs.25,000/- with default 
stipulation of further three months additional simple 
imprisonment. Appellant-Sadiq has also been sentenced 
to two years rigorous imprisonment with a fine of 
Rs.1,000/- with default stipulation of one month 
additional simple imprisonment under Section 506 of 
IPC; he was further sentenced to 20 years rigorous 
imprisonment and a fine of Rs.1,000/- with default 
stipulation of one month additional simple imprisonment 
under Section 120-B of IPC. All the sentences were 
directed to run concurrently. 
4. Appellant/applicant no.2-Hussnojahan @ 
Husanara has been convicted under Sections 506 and 
120-B IPC and under Section 17 of POCSO Act, 2012. 
She was sentenced to two years rigorous imprisonment 
and a fine of Rs.1,000/-with default stipulation of one 
month additional simple imprisonment under Section 
506 IPC and sentenced to 20 years' rigorous 
imprisonment and a fine of Rs.1,000/-with default 
stipulation of additional simple imprisonment of one 
month under Section 120-B of IPC; she was further 
sentenced to undergo 20 years rigorous imprisonment 
and a fine of Rs.1,000/- with default stipulation of one 
month additional simple imprisonment under Section 17 
of POCSO Act, 2012. All the sentences were directed to 
run concurrently.
5. FIR was registered on 07.01.2019 by the father 
of the victim at Police Station Nehru Colony, District 
Dehradun, alleging that his daughter (victim), aged about 
14 years, was subjected to sexual assault in April 2018 
by appellant/applicant no.1, Sadiq. It is further alleged 
that his wife, appellant/applicant no.2, Hussnojahan, 3
knew about the incident and that both the 
appellants/applicants intimidated the victim not to 
disclose the aforesaid incident to anyone and threatened 
her with dire consequences. In the First Information 
Report, it was also alleged that the victim was seven 
months pregnant.
6. On the basis of the said First Information 
Report, the chik FIR was registered on 08.01.2019. 
Investigation ensued, which resulted in the filing of the 
charge-sheet. The trial proceeded against the 
appellants/applicants, and they have been convicted and 
sentenced as stated above.
7. It is argued by the learned Senior Advocate 
appearing for the appellants/applicants that on 
18.02.2019, the victim delivered a female child. A blood 
sample of the newborn was taken on 26.02.2019, and the 
blood samples of the victim and appellant/applicant no.1 
were also taken, but it was never proved by the forensic 
reports submitted twice, firstly on 03.04.2019 and 
thereafter on 14.11.2019, that appellant/applicant no.1 
was the biological father of the female child so born from 
the alleged sexual assault.
8. According to the learned Senior Advocate, the 
samples were again sent to the Central Forensic Science 
Laboratory, Chandigarh, and even in the report 
submitted by CFSL dated 30.10.2019, the appellant was 
not found to be the biological father of the child so born. 
Thus, according to the learned Senior Advocate, it was 
not proved at all that appellant/applicant no.1 was 
involved in the incident. He further submitted that, in 
such a view of the matter, appellant/applicant no.1 
deserves to be released on bail as the prosecution failed 4
to prove the case against him beyond all reasonable 
doubt.
9. Per contra, the learned State Counsel submits 
that the victim, throughout the trial, proved the case of 
the prosecution, and there is no material contradiction in 
the prosecution case. The medical report also established 
sexual assault upon the victim, who was a minor of 14 
years at the time of the incident. Therefore, merely on the 
ground that the blood samples did not match would not 
come to the rescue of appellant/applicant no.1.
10. Learned State Counsel also submitted that this 
is the second bail application and all the grounds 
advanced by the learned Senior Advocate appearing for 
the appellants/applicants have already been considered 
by this Court while rejecting the first bail application vide 
order dated 26.06.2024.
11. There is no new ground available to 
appellant/applicant no.1, and therefore the second bail 
application deserves to be rejected.
12. So far as the second bail application of 
appellant/applicant no.2, Hussnojahan, is concerned, it 
is argued by the learned Senior Advocate that the 
allegations in the First Information Report as well as in 
the evidence against appellant/applicant no.2 are only of 
threatening. The other allegation is that 
appellant/applicant no.2 made a telephone call to the 
father of the victim asking him to send the victim to her 
residence, as appellant/applicant no.2 was going to 
attend to her daughter-in-law in the hospital where she 
delivered a child. There is no proof that 
appellant/applicant no.2 made a call to the father of the 
victim. He further submitted that when the alleged 5
incident of sexual assault happened, appellant/applicant 
no.2 was not present there; therefore, she cannot be 
convicted under Section 17 of the POCSO Act. He also 
submitted that abetment, as defined under Section 17 of 
the POCSO Act, has not been proved. At the most, at this 
stage, it can be said that appellant/applicant no.2 
threatened the victim not to disclose the incident to 
anyone.
13. Learned Senior Advocate also submits that 
appellant/applicant no.2 has been under incarceration 
for the last two years and ten months. She is a woman 
and, accordingly, may be released on bail during the 
pendency of the appeal by allowing the second bail 
application.
14. Per contra, the learned State Counsel, to some 
extent, accepted that the offence under Section 17 of the 
POCSO Act may not be proved against 
appellant/applicant no.2, Hussnojahan, in view of the 
material available on record. The allegations against 
appellant/applicant no.2 are of threatening the victim 
and calling her to her residence by making a call to the 
victim’s father.
15. Having heard the learned counsel for the 
parties, perusal the record of the trial, and appreciated 
the submissions made by the learned counsel for the 
parties, we are of the view that, so far as 
appellant/applicant no.1, Sadiq @ Chand, is concerned, 
no new ground has been pleaded by the learned counsel 
appearing for the appellants/applicants. During the 
hearing of the first bail application, all the arguments 
were considered by this Court.
16. Since no new ground has been pleaded and 6
argued by the learned Senior Advocate appearing for the 
appellants/applicants in the second bail application, the 
same is rejected so far as it relates to appellant/applicant 
no.1, Sadiq @ Chand.
17. Appreciating the arguments advanced by the 
learned counsel for the parties and upon perusal of the 
record, this Court is of the view that the allegations 
against appellant/applicant no.2 extend only to an 
offence under Section 506 IPC. However, without 
commenting on the merits of the case, 
appellant/applicant no.2, Smt. Hussnojahan @ 
Husanara, deserves to be released on bail.
18. Accordingly, the second bail application is 
allowed so far as it relates to appellant/applicant no.2, 
Smt. Hussnojahan @ Husanara. Let appellant/applicant 
no.2, Smt. Hussnojahan @ Husanara, be released on bail 
upon executing one personal bond and furnishing two 
sureties of the like amount to the satisfaction of the court 
concerned.
