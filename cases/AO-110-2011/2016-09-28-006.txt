Hon’ble Servesh Kumar Gupta, J.
 Both appeals as have been arisen out of same 
verdict, hence are being taken up together and 
adjudicated by this common judgment. 
 The accident, losing of the life by Mr. Gyan 
Prakash Joshi aged about 32 years (son of the 
applicant) and insurance cover have not been denied. 
The only dispute raised is on the quantum of 
compensation awarded by the Tribunal. 
 Learned counsel for the Insurance Company 
has stressed that since the deceased/youth Mr. Gyan 
Prakash Joshi was a bachelor, hence the findings of the

Tribunal deducting 1/3rd for his personal expenses is 
erroneous. As per the norms laid down in several 
judgments, it would have been half. 
 I do agree with the argument raised. If the 
annual income of the deceased, which has been 
evaluated to the tune of Rs.75,600/-, if made half, then 
it comes to Rs.37,800/-. So, this amount is found as 
the annual dependency in this case. 
 Now, the question of multiplier has also been 
agitated. Learned counsel for the Insurance Company 
has relied upon the precedents of the Constitutional 
Bench of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of New India 
Assurance Co. Ltd. Vs. Shanti Pathak (SMT.) and others,
reported in (2007) 10 SCC 1, wherein it has been 
categorically held that where the mother and father of 
the deceased/youth were running in the mid 60s, then 
multiplier of ‘5’ would be applied, rather than the 
multiplier of ‘17’ based on the age of the 
deceased/youth. 
 In yet another judgment of the Hon’ble Apex 
Court in the case of Shakti Devi vs. New India 
Insurance Company Ltd. and another, reported in 
(2010) 14 SCC 575, the same dictum was reiterated. 
 On the other hand, learned counsel for Smt. 
Saraswati Joshi (appellant) has relied upon the 
judgment of Amrit Bhanu Shali and others vs. National 
Insurance Company Ltd. and others, reported in (2012) 
11 SCC 738, wherein the Division Bench of Hon’ble 
Apex Court has opined that the multiplier should be

determined on the basis of the age of the deceased and 
not on the basis of the dependants. Since, all these 
precedents are contradictory, but at the same time, the 
Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Munna Lal Jain and 
Another vs. Vipin Kumar Sharma and others, reported in 
(2015) 6 SCC 347, wherein it was held as under:- 
 “In the absence of any statutory and a 
straitjacket formula, there are bound to be grey areas 
despite several attempts made by Supreme Court to lay 
down the guidelines. Compensation would basically 
depend on the evidence available in a case and the 
formulas shown by the courts are only guidelines for 
the computation of the compensation. That precisely is 
the reason the courts lodge a caveat stating 
“ordinarily”, “normally”, “exceptional circumstances”, 
etc., while suggesting the formulae.” 
 So, having considered all these 
precedents/laws laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court 
and feeling that Smt. Saraswati Joshi (appellant), the 
sole surviving dependant even at the time of accident, 
was around 69 years of age and she would be 
approaching 76-77 years at present, therefore, I feel 
that the multiplier of ‘8’ will be appropriate in the 
present controversy. Applying this multiplier on 
Rs.37,800/-, the amount comes to Rs.302,400/-. 
 Rest of the amount granted by the Tribunal 
on any other score is not disturbed by this Court.
