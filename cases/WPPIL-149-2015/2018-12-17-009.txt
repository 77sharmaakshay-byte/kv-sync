Ramesh Ranganathan, C.J. (Oral) 
 The report of the Lok Ayukt dated 04.07.2012 makes 
disconcerting reading. The Lok Ayukt has pointed out that as against 
an extent of 415 meter recorded in the “M” Book, only 379 meter 
length of pipeline was found to have been laid on physical 
verification; there was a variation of 36 meter length of pipeline; in 
another area, while the length of the pipeline is reflected in the “M” 
Book as 935 meters, on physical verification, only 835 meter length of 
pipeline was found on the ground; and, thereby, there was a short fall 
of 82 meter length of pipeline. The Lok Ayukt had identified certain 
officers who were responsible, and had recommended that an FIR be 
lodged against them, and the matter be investigated by police officials. 
2. Curiously, a high powered committee was constituted, 
(consisting of one Executive Engineer and two Assistant Engineers 
from the very same Department whose officers are alleged to have 
misappropriated funds), which absolved all these officers, and opined 
that these pipelines were damaged because of land-slide. The report 
does not, however, disclose as to when the land-slide took place, as 
also the basis for their conclusion that the damage to the pipelines was 
on account of a land-slide. The place, where land-slide had allegedly 
occurred, is also not reflected in the said report. 2
3. Prima facie, it does appear that the Committee, of 
officers of the very same Department, was constituted only to cover 
up the entire issue and to absolve those, who were guilty of 
misappropriation of funds earmarked for utilization in the Schemes to 
provide irrigation and drinking water needs of the subject villagers, of 
all blame. While the report of the Lok Ayukt and the Committee’s 
report are both of the year 2012, and more than 6 years have since 
elapsed, no action appears to have been taken to repair the damaged 
pipelines even till date, and the villagers are, for no fault of their, 
being deprived of much needed water both for drinking and irrigation 
purposes. 
4. While Sri S.S. Chauhan, learned Deputy Advocate 
General, on instructions, would submit that, in certain areas, the open 
canals were damaged by the villagers themselves to ensure that water 
flows into their lands, what is not stated is the efforts, if any, made by 
the Department to identify the culprits who had damaged the pipeline 
/ canal, and why no action was taken against them. 
5. If the pipeline had been damaged because of a land-slide, 
then the villagers concerned cannot be said to have damaged the 
pipeline for its flow into their fields. If, on the other hand, the 
pipeline was damaged by the villagers, the question of it being 
damaged by land-slide would not arise. Further, even if the pipeline 
had been damaged because of land-slide, there would be remnants of 
the pipeline traceable in the area. It is also not clear whether the area, 
where these pipelines were found missing, are areas which suffered 
from a land-slide or not. These issues can be resolved, and the 
persons responsible for such lapses identified, upon a counter affidavit 
being filed by the Chief Engineer who, we are told, heads the 
Department of Irrigation. 
6. The Chief Engineer will submit a detailed counter￾affidavit explaining the basis on which the three member Committee 
had arrived at the conclusion that the officers, named in the report of 3
the Lok Ayukt were above blame; and the time frame within which 
damaged pipelines will be repaired to ensure that water (both for 
irrigation and drinking purposes) is made available to the villagers. 
6. Post on 2nd January, 2019. 
7. Let a certified copy of this order be issued, by 
19.12.2018, on payment of the prescribed charges.
