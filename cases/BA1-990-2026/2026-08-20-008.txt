The instant C528 petition is preferred by the present 
applicant Dhananjay Giri, S/o Sri Jatashankar Giri against the 
order of remand dated 22.05.2026 and 29.05.2026 passed by the 
learned Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate, Haldwani whereby 
the remand of applicant (in custody) is accepted for the offence 
punishable under Sections 111(4), 111(2)(b) of BNS, 2023 and 
Section 420 of IPC.
2. Brief facts of the case are that Case Crime No.94 of 2025 was 
registered on the basis of first information report dated 24.07.2025 
lodged at P.S. Kathgodam, District Nainital implicating the 
present applicant and unknown officials of Punjab National Bank, 
Branch at Railway Bazar, Haldwani for the offence punishable 
under Section 420 of IPC. During investigation the notice under 
Section 35(3) of BNSS 2023 was given to the applicant since the 
offence punishable under Section 420 of IPC is punishable for less 
than seven years in terms of decision of Hon’ble Supreme Court in 
the case of Arnesh Kumar vs. State of Bihar 2014(8) SCC 273. 
Since there was an apprehension of arrest to the applicant, 
he filed an Anticipatory Bail Application No.120 of 2026 before the 
First Additional District and Session Judge, Haldwani District 
Nainital but the anticipatory bail application was rejected on 
11.05.2026 by observing that on perusal of the case diary and the 
fact that already a notice under Section 35(3) of BNSS, 2023 is 
already given, which itself reveals that there is no need to arrest 
the applicant and as such the apprehension of arrest is baseless. In 
1para 10, the learned First Additional Session Judge further 
observed that the Investigating Officer followed the guidelines of 
Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Arnesh Kumar (supra). 
3. What has happened on 20.05.2026, Section 111 of BNS 2023 
was added and on the next date i.e. on 21.05.2026 the applicant 
was arrested and on 22.05.2026 an application was moved before 
the Trial Court for taking the applicant on remand under Section 
111(4), 111(2)(b) of BNS, 2023 which was accepted and then the 
applicant moved bail application which was rejected then he 
approached to the Additional District Judge then on 30.05.2026 
another application was moved for seeking remand under Section 
420 of IPC and after acceptance the application for remand the bail 
application was rejected and now the applicant is also praying for 
bail.
4. It is argued by Mr. Mukul Talwar, learned Senior Counsel 
that the I.O. committed illegality while taking the applicant in 
custody in reference to Section 111 of BNS, 2023 since Section 111 
came into force after the enactment of BNS 2023 from 01.07.2024, 
therefore, for attracting Section 111 of BNS, 2023 there should be 
an act on or after 01.07.2024. It is submitted that there is no such 
incident on or after 01.07.2024 for attracting Section 111 of BNS, 
2023, therefore, taking custody of the applicant under Section 111 
of BNS 2023 and for taking further remand under Section 111 of 
BNS 2023 as well as for Section 420 of IPC though Section 420 of 
IPC was already in the FIR when it was registered is contrary to 
the mandate of Act No.45 of 2023, whereby BNS, 2023 was enacted 
w.e.f. 01.07.2024. 
5. On the previous date, learned counsel for the applicant 
placed reliance to a judgment of Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of 
State of Gujrat vs. Sandip Omprakash Gupta 2024(14) SCC 401 as 
well as in the case of State of Maharashtra vs. Shivaji 2015 (14) 
SCC 272 and after considering both the judgments this Court 
passed an order on 05.06.2026, the relevant extract of which are 
being extracted hereinbelow:-
“3. In this petition the applicant ‘Dhananjay Giri’ challenging 
the remand order dated 22.05.2026 and 29.05.2026 passed by the 
Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate, arising out of Case Crime 
No. 94 of 2025. By order of remand dated 22.05.2026 the applicant 
2was taken on remand for the offences punishable under sections 
111(4), 111(2)(B) of BNS, 2023.
In reference to this order of of remand dated 22.05.2026 it is 
argued by the learned counsel for the applicant the remand for 
section 111(4) and section 111(2)(b) of BNS, 2023 is bad in law 
since in the order of remand there is no any reference of any such 
incident alleged to be committed after promulgation of BNS, 2023, 
which is one of the ingredient for constituting an offence, which is 
termed as “continuing unlawful activity” as defined under 
explanation (iii) of section 111 of BNS, 2023. In reference to this 
he placed reliance to a judgment of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the 
case of State of Gujarat vs. Sandip Omprakash Gupta 2024(14) 
SCC 401, wherein, the earlier judgment of Hon’ble Apex Court in 
the case of State of Maharashtra vs. Shivaji 2015 (14) SCC 272 
was discussed in para-30 onwards and then held that it is only if an 
organized crime is committed by the accused after promulgation of 
the MCOCA, then he may on the basis of the previous charge￾sheet the cognizance can be taken for the offence under section 3 
of the MCOCA
By referring this judgment, it is argued that for constituting 
the offence punishable under section 111(4) it is essential to the 
prosecution to take a remand of accused if any offence is 
committed after promulgation of BNS, 2023, which came into 
force w.e.f. 01.07.2024. At page 46 he has given the chart of the 
criminal antecedent of the present applicant and all those cases are 
prior to the promulgation of BNS, 2023. The criminal antecedent 
may be relevant for taking the applicant on remand under sections 
111(4) and 111(2) (b) of BNS, 2023, if any offence is committed 
by the applicant after promulgation of BNS, 2023 i.e. after 
01.07.2024.
4. In addition to this, he submits that for constituting the offence 
punishable under section 111(4) the second requirement is that 
there should be an “organized crime syndicate”, which also is 
defined under section 111(1)(i) of BNS, 2023, which defines 
“organized crime syndicate”, means, a group of two or more 
persons who acting either singly or jointly as a syndicate or gang, 
indulged in any continuing unlawful activity. He submits that this 
aspect is also not been reflected from the order of remand since 
there is no details of those persons, who are associated in group 
with the present applicant for constituting an “organized crime 
syndicate” to continue unlawful activity.”

8. As pointed out by Mr. Mukul Talwal, learned Senior counsel 
all the nine cases as shown against the applicant pertains to the 
incidents which are much prior to the date of enactment of BNS, 
2023. Now the question is if all cases pertains to the incident prior to 
01.07.2024 then whether the Investigation Officer was right for 
4seeking remand of the applicant for Section 111 of BNS, 2023. At this 
juncture, Section 111 of BNS, 2023 is being reproduced hereunder:-
“111. (1) Any continuing unlawful activity including kidnapping, 
robbery, vehicle theft, extortion, land grabbing, contract killing, 
economic offence, cyber-crimes, trafficking of persons, drugs, weapons 
or illicit goods or services, human trafficking for prostitution or ransom, 
by any person or a group of persons acting in concert, singly or jointly, 
either as a member of an organised crime syndicate or on behalf of such 
syndicate, by use of violence, threat of violence, intimidation, coercion, 
or by any other unlawful means to obtain direct or indirect material 
benefit including a financial benefit, shall constitute organised crime.
Explanation.—For the purposes of this sub-section,—
(i) “organised crime syndicate” means a group of two or more 
persons who, acting either singly or jointly, as a syndicate or gang 
indulge in any continuing unlawful activity; 
(ii) “continuing unlawful activity” means an activity prohibited by 
law which is a cognizable offence punishable with imprisonment 
of three years or more, undertaken by any person, either singly or 
jointly, as a member of an organised crime syndicate or on behalf 
of such syndicate in respect of which more than one charge-sheets 
have been filed before a competent Court within the preceding 
period of ten years and that Court has taken cognizance of such 
offence, and includes economic offence; 
(iii) “economic offence” includes criminal breach of trust, forgery, 
counterfeiting of currency-notes, bank-notes and Government 
stamps, hawala transaction, mass-marketing fraud or running any 
scheme to defraud several persons or doing any act in any manner 
with a view to defraud any bank or financial institution or any 
other institution or organisation for obtaining monetary benefits 
in any form. 
(2) Whoever commits organised crime shall,-
(a) if such offence has resulted in the death of any person, be 
punished with death or imprisonment for life, and shall also be 
liable to fine which shall not be less than ten lakh rupees; 
(b) in any other case, be punished with imprisonment for a term 
which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to 
imprisonment for life, and shall also be liable to fine which shall 
not be less than five lakh rupees. 
(3) Whoever abets, attempts, conspires or knowingly facilitates the 
commission of an organised crime, or otherwise engages in any act 
preparatory to an organised crime, shall be punished with imprisonment 
for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend 
to imprisonment for life, and shall also be liable to fine which shall not be 
less than five lakh rupees. 
(4) Any person who is a member of an organised crime syndicate shall be 
punished with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than five 
years but which may extend to imprisonment for life, and shall also be 
liable to fine which shall not be less than five lakh rupees. 
5(5) Whoever, intentionally, harbours or conceals any person who has 
committed the offence of an organised crime shall be punished with 
imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than three years but 
which may extend to imprisonment for life, and shall also be liable to 
fine which shall not be less than five lakh rupees: 
Provided that this sub-section shall not apply to any case in which the 
harbour or concealment is by the spouse of the offender. 
(6) Whoever possesses any property derived or obtained from the 
commission of an organised crime or proceeds of any organised crime or 
which has been acquired through the organised crime, shall be 
punishable with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than 
three years but which may extend to imprisonment for life and shall also 
be liable to fine which shall not be less than two lakh rupees. 
(7) If any person on behalf of a member of an organised crime syndicate 
is, or at any time has been in possession of movable or immovable 
property which he cannot satisfactorily account for, shall be punishable 
with imprisonment for a term which shall not be less than three years but 
which may extend to imprisonment for ten years and shall also be liable 
to fine which shall not be less than one lakh rupees.”
On plain reading of Section 111 of BNS, 2023 it is very clear 
that there should be a continuing activity to attract Section 111 of 
BNS, 2023 but there is no such material to take the applicant on 
remand for Section 111 of BNS, 2023.
