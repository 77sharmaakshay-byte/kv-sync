COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
ABA No. 1194 of 2025
Hon'ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Vikas Bahuguna, learned counsel 
for the applicants.
2. Mr. Tumul Nainwal, learned A.G.A. 
with Ms. Rangoli Purohit, learned Brief 
Holder for the State.
3. Mr. Ramji Srivastava, learned counsel 
for the complainant.
4. Present applicants Sunil Kumar and
Anita, who is the wife of the applicant 
no.1, are praying for anticipatory bail in 
reference to FIR dated 23.11.2021, bearing 
FIR No. 0076 of 2021, registered at Police 
Station Purola District Uttarkashi, lodged 
by one Ravindra Kumar Kheda, implicating 
both the applicants, for the offences 
punishable under sections 420, 467, 468, 
471 read with Section 120-B of IPC.
5. It is contended by the learned counsel 
for the applicants that the allegations as
alleged in the FIR relates to a registered 
sale deed dated 13.09.2007, whereby the 
applicants purchased a piece of land from 
one Digamber Singh, and the allegation is 
that a false declaration has been shown by 
producing a forged certificate that the 
applicants belongs to the family of 
“Farmer”. He submits that both the 
applicants are the bonafide purchaser and 
by paying full consideration the sale deed 
was executed in favour of both of them by 
one Digamber Singh on 13.09.2007 and 
now at a belated stage of more than 13 
years, the FIR has been lodged by the respondent/ complainant with ulterior
motive and purposes. Apart from this, he
submits that, not only this, two suits were 
filed by both the applicants and one suit 
was decreed and other one was dismissed 
against which the appeal is pending for 
adjudication. 
6. He further submits that the 
allegations as alleged prima facie appears 
to be civil in nature. He further submits 
that earlier the applicants challenged the 
First Information Report in WPCRL No. 
2302 of 2022 wherein interim protection 
was granted by the Coordinate Bench on 
19.12.2022, however, since now on 
completion of the investigation the charge 
sheet has been filed in which the 
cognizance has been taken therefore, there 
is an apprehension that the applicants may 
be taken into custody. He further submits 
that the anticipatory bail application after 
filing of the charge sheet is also 
maintainable in view of the judgment
rendered by the full Bench of this Court 
dated 24.08.2023.
7. He further submits that both the
applicants are innocent and have been 
falsely implicated by the complainant by 
lodging First Information Report that too 
after 13 years of the sale deed, therefore, 
taking into consideration that now the 
charge sheet has been filed and still there 
is an apprehension of arrest, the protection 
may be granted to the applicants. Learned 
counsel for the applicants place before this 
Court the recent judgment of the Apex 
Court in the case of Mohammed Rasal.C 
and another vs. State of Kerala and 
another 2025 Livelaw (SC) 884, wherein it 
was held that the concurrent jurisdiction is 
conferred upon the Sessions Court and the 
High Court to entertain a prayer for pre-arrest bail under Section 482 of the BNSS 
(formerly, Section 438 CrPC), the 
hierarchy of Courts demands that no 
person seeking such remedy should be 
encouraged or allowed to directly approach 
the High Court for exercising jurisdiction 
under Section 482 of the BNSS (formerly, 
Section 438 CrPC) by passing the 
jurisdiction of the concerned Sessions 
Court.
8. He apprise to this Court, that now the 
aforesaid judgment has been referred to 
the Larger Bench and the matter is still 
pending for consideration, however, he 
further submits that in this judgment also, 
the Hon’ble Apex Court also observed that 
in an extra ordinary circumstances, the 
High Court, for reasons to be recorded may 
entertain an application for pre-arrest bail 
directly. He submits that this is a case in 
which the sale deed was registered in the 
year 2007 and the FIR has been lodged at 
a very belated stage after 13 years in the 
year 2021 that too by a stranger and 
taking into consideration that the 
applicants are the bonafide purchaser, 
purchased a piece of land from one 
Digamber Singh by registered sale deed 
dated 13.09.2007 and their names were 
also recorded in the revenue record, and,
furthermore, earlier the interim protection 
was granted to the both the applicants and 
since now the charge sheet has been filed 
and still the applicants may be granted the 
pre-arrest bail. 
9. On the other side, Mr. Tumul Nainwal, 
learned A.G.A. for the State, submits that 
after thorough investigation the charge 
sheet has been filed, in which the trial 
court already took cognizance and the
applicants has a remedy to challenge the 
entire proceedings if he is so advised by filing a petition under Section 528 of 
Cr.P.C. However, he prays for and granted 
four weeks’ time to file counter affidavit.
10. Mr. Ramji Srivastava, learned counsel 
for the complainant also pray for and
granted time to file counter affidavit. Two 
weeks thereafter is granted to the 
applicant to file rejoinder affidavit.
11. Having heard the arguments of 
learned counsel for the parties, for an 
interim measure, without expressing any 
opinion on the merits of the case, the 
applicants shall be released on interim 
anticipatory bail subject to furnishing of 
personal bond with two reliable sureties, 
each of the like amount, to the satisfaction 
of the Court Concerned.
12. List this case in the week 
commencing 05.01.2026.
(Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
