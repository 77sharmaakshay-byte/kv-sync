The First Bail Application (No.4098 of 2019) of 
the appellant Bablu was rejected on 29.06.2020 on 
merit. 
2. The First Bail Application (No.4601 of 2020) of 
the appellant Naresh was dismissed on 26.10.2021 as 
not pressed.
3. The Second Bail Application (No.4826 of 
2024), filed in Criminal Appeal No.690 of 2019, will be 
treated as a leading case.
4. The appellants have been convicted under 
Section 302 read with Section 34 of the Indian Penal 
Code, 1860 and they have been sentenced to undergo 
life imprisonment vide judgment dated 05.11.2019/
07.11.2019, passed by learned District and Sessions 
Judge, Haridwar.
5. According to the prosecution case, a case 
under Section 307 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 
against the appellants was pending. Dhir Singh 
(deceased) was a witness of the prosecution in the said 
case. On 09.10.2008 at around 07:00 p.m., Mainpal, the 3
informant of the First Information Report, was at his 
house with his brother Dhir Singh and Raj Kumar. The 
appellants Bablu, Naresh with the appellant Neetu came 
there. Appellant Neetu opened fire from a country made 
pistol on Dhir Singh & Naresh and Bablu assaulted Dhir 
Singh with sticks and Palkati (a sharp edged weapon) 
because Dhir Singh did not compromise with them in the 
said case i.e. Section 307 of the Indian Penal Code, 
1860. Dhir Singh sustained serious injuries in this 
incident. He was taken to the hospital where he died on 
11.10.2008 during his treatment.
6. Mr. Akshay Pradhan, learned counsel 
appearing for the appellant Bablu submitted that the 
appellant has been falsely implicated in the present 
matter. The alleged eye witnesses are brothers of the 
deceased. They are interested witnesses. Consequently, 
their statements cannot be accepted. The post-mortem 
report does not support the case of the prosecution. 
7. Mrs. Pushpa Joshi, learned Senior Advocate for 
the appellant-Naresh contended that the evidence of 
Dhir Singh had been completed in the case of Section 
307 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860 before this incident. 
Therefore, the prosecution failed to prove the motive 
attributed to the appellant for commission of the crime. 
The appellant, aged about 64 years, is languishing in jail 4
since 13.04.2018. Therefore, this fact can be a factor in 
considering bail. In support of her submission, she has 
relied upon a judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in 
“Akhtari BI (SMT) Vs. State of M.P.”, (2001) 4 SCC 
355, where the Hon’ble Supreme Court observed:
“We feel that if an appeal is not disposed of 
within the aforesaid period of 5 years, for 
no fault of the convicts, such convicts may 
be released on bail on such conditions as 
may be deemed fit and proper by the court. 
In computing the period of 5 years, the 
delay for any period, which is requisite in 
preparation of the record and the delay 
attributable to the convict or his counsel 
can be deducted. There may be cases 
where even after the lapse of 5 years the 
convicts may, under the special 
circumstances of the case, be held not 
entitled to bail pending the disposal of the 
appeals filed by them.”
8. Mr. K.S. Bora, learned Deputy Advocate 
General, submitted that murder was committed with an 
intention that the deceased was a witness to the earlier 
attempt to murder case. The prosecution has examined 
sixteen witnesses and all the witnesses including the 
witness Mainpal (PW1) and Rajkumar (PW2), eye 
witnesses, have supported the case of the prosecution. 
He further submitted that 16 pellets were recovered 
from the dead body of the deceased. 5
9. Mr. Arvind Vashisth, learned Senior Advocate, 
contended that the prosecution has established its case 
beyond all reasonable doubt and hence, the appellants 
are not entitled for release on bail. 
10. The appellant Bablu had opportunity to raise 
all his contentions on the previous occasion and he had 
actually raised the said contentions. Therefore, it is not 
open to the appellant Bablu to make successive bail 
applications even on the grounds already rejected by 
this Court. 
11. It is well established law that proof of motive 
is not sine qua non in a case of murder. 
12. While long pendency of an appeal can be a 
factor in considering bail, the murder of a witness is a 
serious matter that can justify denial of bail. 
13. In “Chandrakeshwar Prasad alias Chandu 
Babu vs. State of Bihar and Another”, (2016) 9 SCC 
443, the Hon’ble Supreme Court set aside the bail 
granted to the respondent-accused who was charged 
with the offence of facilitating murder of a witness in a 
case in which he was being tried. 
14. At this stage, a detailed appreciation of 
evidence shall affect the merits of the appeal. If a strong 
prima facie ground is disclosed for substantial doubt 6
about the conviction, it may be ground to grant the bail. 
Such a ground does not appear in the present case. 
Therefore, the bail applications are liable to be rejected 
and hence, the bail applications are accordingly rejected.
15. A copy of this order be placed on the record of 
the Criminal Appeal No.164 of 2020.
