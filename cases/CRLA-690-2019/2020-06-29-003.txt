This application is filed seeking for suspension of 
sentence and for bail, so far as appellant/ accused nos. 1 and 2 
are concerned. 
2. The trial court by its order dated 07.11.2019 has 
convicted both the accused for the offences punishable under 
sections 302 read with section 34, 201 IPC and under section 
3(2)(5) of Scheduled Caste/Scheduled Tribes Act with fine of Rs. 
50,000/- each and sentenced them to undergo life 
imprisonment.
3. The plea of the appellants’ counsel is that the 
recoveries do not match the injuries caused on the deceased. 2
The alleged eye-witnesses are brothers of the deceased, and,
therefore, are interested witnesses. Consequently, their 
statements cannot be accepted. Even though 16 pellets were 
recovered from the body of the deceased, the same are not 
relatable to the recoveries made by the prosecution. 
4. Shri Arvind Vashistha, the learned senior advocate, 
appearing for the complainant’s counsel submits that the 
prosecution has established his case beyond all reasonable 
doubt and hence, the appellants are not entitled for release on 
bail. 
5. Learned assistant government advocate submits that 
in the face of the evidence of the eyewitnesses the prosecution 
has established his case. The recovery of the 16 pellets, even if,
is to be accepted, would not render the prosecution case to be 
doubtful. 
6. On hearing learned counsels, we are of the view that 
the application for bail is required to be rejected. 
7. There are two eyewitnesses to the incident. They have 
both narrated the manner in which the incident has occurred. 
Even though, they were cross-examined by the accused, nothing 
worthwhile has been elicited in the cross-examination. One of 
the reasons for conviction is the statements of the eyewitnesses. 
8. However, the contention of recovery of 16 pellets, 
which are not relatable to the weapon used, is a matter to be 
considered at the stage of final hearing. On the face of the 
statements of the eyewitnesses, the rest of the prosecution case 
cannot be discarded. Even otherwise, these contentions require 
to be considered at the stage of final hearing. For the purposes 
of considering the application for bail, we are of the view that 
there is substantial prima facie case against the appellants that3
does not warrant their release on bail. Consequently, 
application no. 4098 of 2019 is dismissed. 
9. Post for hearing in the usual course.
