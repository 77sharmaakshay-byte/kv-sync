The challenge is made to the conviction and sentence of 
the appellant Harjinder Singh Sandhu recorded in Special Sessions 
Trial No. 436 of 2019, State v. Harjinder Singh Sandhu, by the court 
of Special Sessions Judge, Rudrapur, Udham Singh Nagar, on 
06.03.2024. By it, the appellant has been convicted under Section
354 IPC and Section 9/10 of the Protection of Children from Sexual 
Offences Act, 2012 (“the POCSO Act”) and sentenced as hereunder:-
“(i) Under Section 354 IPC – rigorous 
imprisonment for a period of three years 
and a fine of Rs. 10,000/-. In default of 
payment of fine, to undergo additional 
simple imprisonment for a period of three 
months. 
(ii) Under Section 9/10 of the POCSO Act –
rigorous imprisonment for a period of five 
years and a fine of Rs. 10,000/-. In default 
of payment of fine, to undergo additional 2
simple imprisonment for a period of three 
months. 
2. The appeal is already admitted. 
3. Lower court record has already been received. Let a 
paper book be prepared and given to learned counsel for the parties 
as per rules.
4. List for final hearing on 21.07.2025.
5. Heard on bail application. 
6. According to the prosecution case, the victim, a young 
girl of 15 years had joined boxing training in a stadium where the 
appellant was a Coach. On 17.07.2019, according to the 
prosecution, the appellant sexually assaulted the victim thrice. Last 
time, when the victim was sexually assaulted by the appellant, she 
slept him and ran away. The matter was reported to the incharge of 
the stadium long after the incident. The trial proceeded and the 
appellant has been convicted and sentenced as stated hereinbefore. 
7. Learned counsel for the appellant would submit that the 
appellant himself is a victim of conspiracy; the FIR is quite late; the 
presence of DW1 Neeraj Pujari has been concealed, who was present 
at the place of incident and who has not supported the prosecution 
case; after the alleged incident, the victim came out of the hall as 
told by her, but the site plan does not show that the incident took 
place anywhere outside the hall. It is also argued that if the victim 
had reported the matter to PW 7 Rajwant Kaur, she also does not 
reveal the incident and when the victim was examined by the doctor, 3
she revealed only one incident and not three incidents, which 
allegedly took place on the date of incident. 
8. Learned State Counsel would submit that the inquiry 
was conducted in the matter; the victim has supported the 
prosecution case during inquiry and during trial; other witnesses 
have also supported the prosecution case.
9. Learned counsel for the appellant has also argued on 
one aspect that in the inquiry, the victim and her family members 
had compromised the matter saying that if the appellant is 
transferred, they would not like to proceed with the matter. 
10. The appellant is a convict now. He does not have benefit 
of the presumption of innocence. The victim in her statement given 
during trial has stated about three instances, which took place on 
17.07.2019 when she was sexually assaulted. The first incident, 
according to her, took place when she was stretching. The second 
incidence took place, according to her, when she was doing 
strengthening exercise and thereafter when she was sexually 
assaulted and she moved out from there and was sitting, when 
again the appellant further sexually assaulted her. The site plan 
shows three separate places where the incident allegedly took place.
11. It is true that the FIR is not prompt. It is also true that 
during the inquiry by the Department, the victim and her family 
members stated that they wanted to compromise the matter 
provided the appellant is transferred, but it does not dilute the 
prosecution case. The victim has supported the prosecution case. 
The other witnesses have also supported the prosecution case.4
12. Having considered, this Court is of the view that it is not 
a case in which the execution of sentence should be suspended and 
the appellant be enlarged on bail. Accordingly, the bail application 
deserves to be rejected.
13. The bail application is rejected.
