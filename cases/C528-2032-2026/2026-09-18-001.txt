Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J. 
1. Mr. Harshpal Sekhon, learned counsel for 
the applicant.
2. Mr. Sandeep Sharma and Mrs. Rangoli 
Purohit, learned A.G.A. for the State.
3. By the instant petition the applicant is 
challenging the order dated 07.07.2026, whereby, 
he is directed to deposit 20 percent of the 
statutory amount of compensation i.e. Rs. 
3,98,000/- within a period of 60 days. 
4. The present applicant was convicted by the 
Trial Court under section 138 of N.I. Act against 
which he has preferred an appeal and the 
conviction and sentence was stayed subject to this 
condition that the applicant shall deposit 20 
percent of the statutory amount of compensation 
within 60 days.
5. It is argued by the learned counsel for the 
applicant that the applicant moved an application 
before the Appellate Court for suspension of 
conviction and sentence by giving reference that 
he is facing acute financial hardship and is not in 
a position to deposit the statutory amount, 
however, the Appellate Court without waiving off 
the condition for depositing 20 percent of the 
amount in terms of section 148 of N.I. Act directed 
the applicant to deposit 20 percent of the amount 
by suspending the order of conviction and 
sentence. He submits that the order impugned is a 
non speaking order since the issue of financial 
status of the applicant is nowhere dealt with. 
6. After perusing the order, it reveals that the UKHC010159072026
2026:UHC:8354
concerned Appellate Court have not dealt with 
the issue of financial status of the applicant 
though the conditions as stipulated under section 
148 of N.I. Act is directory and not mandatory.
7. In such view of the matter, the instant 
petition is disposed of finally by setting aside the 
impugned order and the matter is remanded to 
the Appellate Court with a liberty to the applicant 
to move a fresh application for waiving off the 
condition of deposit of 20 percent of the amount 
in terms of section 148 within a period of 15 days 
and if such an application is moved the Appellate 
Court is directed to decide the same by a reasoned 
and speaking order as per the mandate decision of 
Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Jamboo 
Bhandari vs. M.P. State Industrial Development 
Corporation Limited and Others 2023 Live Law 
SC 776.
