21. At the stage of consideration of bail, this Court is not required to 
conduct a meticulous examination of evidence or record findings 
touching upon the merits of the rival claims. Nevertheless, the Court is 
required to examine whether, on the basis of the material presently 
available, a prima facie case exists, and whether the liberty of the 
Applicants can be balanced against the nature and gravity of the 
accusations, the possibility of misuse of liberty, and the larger interest 
of justice. 
22. The FIR in the present case alleges that the son of the informant, 
namely Tushar Lohni, was kidnapped by the present Applicants, taken 
to Faridabad, subjected to assault and intimidation, and threatened over 
mobile phone. The allegations levelled in the FIR are undoubtedly 
serious in nature. However, at the same time, the material brought on 
record by the Applicants reveals that the dispute between the parties is 
not one arising out of a sudden or isolated occurrence, but rather 
emerges from prior acquaintance, financial transactions, and continuing 
discord between the parties. 
23. The record placed before this Court further reflects that prior to 
registration of the present FIR at Mukhani, Applicant Alok Ranjan 
Tiwari had already instituted FIR No. 0060 of 2025 at Police Station 
Bhupani, Faridabad, against Tushar Lohni and others, alleging cheating, 
criminal breach of trust, criminal intimidation, and conspiracy. The said 
FIR appears to contain allegations regarding monetary transactions, 
alleged inducement to transfer substantial sums of money, misuse of 
financial instruments, and other acts attributed to Tushar Lohni and 
persons associated with him. The existence of the aforesaid FIR, which 
admittedly predates the present proceedings, cannot be ignored

28. At this stage, it would also be apposite to observe that deprivation 
of personal liberty before conclusion of trial cannot be permitted to 
assume the character of punitive detention. The Hon’ble Supreme Court 
in Sanjay Chandra v. CBI, (2012) 1 SCC 40, has observed that the 
object of bail is to secure the appearance of the accused during trial and 
that detention before conviction carries substantial punitive content. 
29. Having regard to the nature of accusations, the background 
circumstances emerging from the record, the existence of cross 
litigation between the parties, the nature of evidence presently 
appearing against the Applicants, the period of incarceration undergone, 
and without expressing any opinion on the merits of the case, this Court 
is of the opinion that the Applicants have made out a fit case for grant 
of bail. 
ORDER
30. The bail applications are allowed. 
31. Let the Applicants, namely Alok Ranjan Tiwari and Kapil Tiwari, 
be released on bail in connection with FIR No. 0113 of 2025, registered

under Sections 137(2), 140(2), and 61(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya 
Sanhita, 2023, upon each of them furnishing a personal bond and two 
reliable sureties, each of the like amount, to the satisfaction of the court 
concerned.
