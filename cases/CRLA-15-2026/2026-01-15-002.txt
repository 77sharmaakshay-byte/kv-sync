The present application has been filed by the Appellant seeking 
exemption from surrender pursuant to the judgment and order dated 
22.12.2025 passed by the learned Additional Sessions Judge (Third), 
Rudrapur, District Udham Singh Nagar, in Sessions Trial No. 164 of 
2018, whereby the Appellant has been convicted and sentenced.
2. Learned senior counsel appearing for the Appellant submits that 
the Appellant has preferred the present criminal appeal challenging the 
conviction, that arguable issues arise for consideration, and that the 
Trial Court had granted time to surrender. It is contended that 
insistence on surrender at this stage would cause irreparable prejudice 
to the Appellant.
3. Learned counsel for the State opposes the application, 
contending that once a person stands convicted, the normal rule is 
custody, and exemption from surrender is not a matter of course. It is 
submitted that the statutory framework does not contemplate such an 
exemption, and that the Appellant must submit to the consequence of 
conviction unless the sentence is suspended in accordance with law.
4. It is further submitted that, since the Appellant has not appeared 
after conviction and has been recorded as absconding, notices have

5. The Court has heard learned counsel for the parties and perused 
the record.
6. The power of the appellate court to suspend the execution of the 
sentence and to release a convicted person on bail during the 
pendency of an appeal is a statutory power traceable to Section 430 of 
the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.
7. Significantly, neither the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 
2023, nor the earlier Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, contains any 
provision that contemplates “exemption from surrender” after 
conviction. The statutory scheme is clear; once a conviction is 
recorded and a sentence is imposed, the sentence becomes operative 
immediately, unless its operation is stayed or suspended by the 
competent court in exercise of statutory power.
8. The law draws a clear distinction between the pre-conviction 
and post-conviction stages. Prior to conviction, the presumption of 
innocence operates and liberty is the rule. Upon conviction, however, 
custody becomes the norm, and liberty can be claimed only through 
the statutorily recognised mechanism of suspension of sentence and 
consequential release on bail. Grant of exemption from surrender, in 
the absence of suspension of sentence, would effectively neutralise the 
conviction and disturb the statutory balance between trial and 
appellate stages.
9. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Daulat Singh v. State of Madhya 
Pradesh (order dated 30.07.2024)Special Leave Petition (Criminal) 
Diary No(s). 20900/2024, has categorically observed that the Code 
does not contemplate an application seeking exemption from 
surrender pending appeal or revision and has cautioned against 
expanding inherent powers to permit such relief, particularly where 
findings of guilt have already been recorded. The Supreme Court has

10. In the present case, the Appellant stands convicted after a full￾fledged trial. At this stage, granting exemption from surrender would 
amount to bypassing the statutory mechanism provided for suspension 
of sentence and would neutralise the effect of the conviction without 
judicial examination under the appropriate provision.
11. The record reflects that the Appellant was on bail during the 
course of the trial. Upon conviction and sentencing on 22.12.2025, the 
learned Trial Court cancelled the personal and surety bonds and 
granted time to surrender.
12. It is borne out from the record that subsequent to conviction, the 
learned Trial Court was constrained to issue a Non-Bailable Warrant 
on account of the Appellant’s non-appearance. The police report 
indicated that the Appellant was not found at the address furnished to 
the Court, leading to further coercive steps.
13. The plea of illness was raised before the Trial Court not by the 
Appellant himself, but through his surety on the basis of medical 
documents. The learned Trial Court, while taking such documents on 
record, did not suspend the sentence or dispense with custody and 
proceeded to secure the Appellant’s presence.
14. Apart from the above, this Court finds that the present 
application also suffers from a fundamental procedural defect. Rule 
3(4) of Chapter XVIII of Part III of the Rules of 1952 as applicable to 
Uttarakhand High Court mandates that in a case where a sentence of 
imprisonment has been awarded, the petition of appeal or the 
application for revision shall contain a certificate signed by the 
Advocate stating that the accused was not on bail or, if he was on bail, 
that he has surrendered. The relevant Rule reads as follows:

15. The use of the word “shall” leaves no manner of doubt that the 
requirement is mandatory in nature.In the present case, the record 
reveals that the Appellant has not surrendered before the Trial Court, 
nor has the certificate as mandated under the Rules been annexed with 
the present proceeding.
16. In the absence of an order suspending the sentence, the 
Appellant is legally obliged to surrender and undergo the sentence 
imposed. Granting an exemption from surrender in such 
circumstances would be impermissible.
17. It is also material to note that the learned Trial Court, after 
considering the conduct of the Appellant and the material placed on 
record, fixed 17.01.2026 as the date for securing his presence, and 
issued consequential directions to the Senior Superintendent of Police 
for execution of the Non-Bailable Warrant, as well as to the concerned 
hospital to verify the medical claim.
18. In cases involving serious offences and severe sentences like 
that of the present matter, post-conviction custody is the rule, and any 
deviation therefrom must be strictly in accordance with statutory 
provisions. Granting exemption from surrender in such cases, without 
suspension of sentence, would undermine the discipline of criminal 
adjudication and erode public confidence in the administration of 
justice.
