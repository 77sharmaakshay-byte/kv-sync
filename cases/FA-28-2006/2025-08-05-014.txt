1. Today, in respect of the issues framed by Hon’ble Supreme 
Court, Mr. D.S. Patni, learned Sr. Advocate assisted by Mr. 
Piyush Garg, learned counsel for the respondents have placed 
reliance on one of the judgments rendered by Hon’ble Supreme 
Court in the case of State of U.P. Vs. Zahoor Ahmad and another 
1973 (2) SCC 547 particularly by referring paragraphs 13 to 17,
which are being extracted herein as under:
“13. The lease in the present case was for the purpose of 
erecting a temporary rice mill and for no other purpose. The 
mere fact that the State is the lessor will not by itself make it a 
Government grant within the meaning of the Government 
Grants Act. There is no evidence in the present case in the 
character of the land or in the making of the lease or in the 
content of the lease to support the plea on behalf of the State 
that it was a grant within the meaning of the Government 
Grants Act.
14. The High Court in the present case relied on a Bench 
decision of that Court. That is the case of Lala Kishun 
Chand v. Sheo Dutta [ILR 1958 All 879] . The land in that 2
case belonged to the Government and was nazul. The 
management thereof vested in the notified area of the Bindi 
Board. The land was taken on lease by the defendant for a 
period of 1½ years in the first instance and thereafter for 4½ 
years. After the expiry of the lease the defendant was permitted 
by the Board to continue in occupation as tenant and the rent 
used to be realised from him. In the meantime, the plaintiff 
obtained a lease in regard to the land from the Commissioner. 
The plaintiff could not get possession. The plaintiff filed a suit 
in the city civil court. The learned Single Judge of the High 
Court affirmed the decision of the courts below that the 
defendant was a trespasser and the defendant's right as a 
lessee came to an end at the expiry of the lease in 1909. The 
High Court on appeal held that the lessee remained in 
possession after the termination of the lease and therefore there 
was holding over within the meaning of Section 116 of the 
Transfer of Property Act. The contention which was advanced 
that Section 2 of the Government Grants Act rendered the 
provisions of the Transfer of Property Act inapplicable was not 
accepted. The High Court correctly held that when the court is 
called upon to construe an instrument granting land by the 
Government it shall construe irrespective of the provisions of 
the Transfer of Property Act. The provisions of Section 116 of 
the Transfer of Property Act were correctly held by the High 
Court to be operative in that case.
15. In the present case the High Court correctly found on the 
facts that the respondent after the determination of the lease 
held over. Even if the Government Grants Act applied Section 
116 of the Transfer of Property Act was not rendered 
inapplicable. The effect of Section 2 of the Government Grants 
Act is that in the construction of an instrument governed by 3
the Government Grants Act the court shall construe such 
grant irrespective of the provisions of the Transfer of Property 
Act. It does not mean that all the provisions of the Transfer of 
Property Act are inapplicable. To illustrate, in the case of a 
grant under the Government Grants Act, Section 14 of the 
Transfer of Property Act will not apply because Section 14 
which provides what is known as the rule against perpetuity 
will not apply by reason of the provisions in the Government 
Grants Act. The grant shall be construed to take effect as if the 
Transfer of Property Act does not apply.
16. Section 3 of the Government Grants Act declares the 
unfettered discretion of the Government to impose such 
conditions and limitations as it thinks fit, no matter what the 
general law of the land be. The meaning of Sections 2 and 3 of 
the Government Grants Act is that the scope of that Act is not 
limited to affecting the provisions of the Transfer of Property 
Act only. The Government has unfettered discretion to impose 
any conditions, limitations, or restrictions in its grants, and 
the rights, privileges and obligations of the grantee would be 
regulated according to the terms of the grant, notwithstanding 
any provisions of any statutory or common law.
17. For these reasons the decree of the High Court that the 
respondent held over within the meaning of Section 116 of the 
Transfer of Property Act is upheld. The appeal is dismissed 
with costs.”
2. In addition to this, they also placed reliance on one of the 
Notification dated 30.06.1969, which was issued in exercise of
power conferred by Clause (b) of sub-Section (1) of Section (2) of 
the U.P. Z.A & L.R. Act (U.P. Act No. 01 of 1951) which was given 4
effect to from 01.07.1969 applicable to all estates or parts thereof 
owned by the State Government, situated in villages within the 
Tehsils / Peshkaris of Haldwani, Kaladungi, Ramnagar, Kiccha, 
Bazpur, Sitarganj or Khatima in the Nainital District and also 
situated in the Kham Villages of Garhwal Bhabhar Government 
Estate in the Garhwal District in which no person has right as an 
intermediary, as defined in Clause 12 of Section (3) of the said 
Act. By referring this notification, Mr. Patni, submits that Sections
130 and 131 were substituted by this notification whereby 
bhumidhari rights were given to the respondents, since they were 
having occupancy tenancy over the land in question. He further 
submits that aforesaid notification dated 30.06.1969 was issued by 
invoking powers conferred by Section 2 (1) (b) of the U.P.Z.A. L.R 
Act, which covers the land owned by Central Government and 
State Government. 
3. In addition to this, they by giving reference of Section 16 of 
the U.P.Z A. & L.R. Act learned counsel argued that by virtue of 
old entries in revenue record, respondents get occupancy right 
over the land in question, and as such, whosoever in 1356 Fasli 
year, was in possession, shall be deemed to be hereditary tenant 
and therefore, by virtue of Notification dated 30.06.1969, 
respondents became Bhumidhars. 
4. Mr. D.S. Patni, learned Sr. Advocate further submits that 
original lease deed of 1924 is a special lease and in this lease deed,
there is reference that same was under Government Grants Act. 
He further submits that since the original lease deed of 1924 is 
special lease and it gives right to transfer lease hold rights and 
there is recital in the sale deed of 1947 that the property can be 
transferred with prior approval / sanction of the Deputy 
Commissioner and there is acknowledgment to this fact that 5
Deputy Commissioner in 1948 directed for entry of names of 
predecessor of the respondents in the revenue record, therefore, 
presumption will go in favour of the respondents in terms of 
Section 90 and 114 of the Indian Evidence Act and the permission 
of the Deputy Commissioner is also reflected in the order of the 
Commissioner, Kumaon whereby names of the predecessors of 
the respondents were recorded in the revenue record. In addition 
to this, Mr. D.S. Patni, learned Sr. Advocate submits that even by 
virtue of Notification dated 30.06.1969 were right accrued in 
favour of the respondents. He further submits that in fact, U.P. 
Tenancy Act, 1939 is not applicable in view of first schedule 
which pertains to the area to which the Act will not apply in the 
first instance. In addition to this, he submits that sale deed of 1947 
was never challenged by the State Government. 
5. Mr. Piyush Garg, learned counsel for the respondents 
concluded their arguments and submits that by virtue of 
Notification dated 30.06.1969, since respondents became 
Bhumidhar, therefore, answers of all the issues framed by 
Hon’ble Supreme Court goes in favour of the respondents.
6. Mr. Yogesh Tiwari and Mr. Pradeep Hairiya, learned State 
Counsel submits that Notification dated 30.06.1969 is not at all 
applicable, since original lease deed of 1924 expired by efflux of 
time in the year 1954 and as such, the land in question, 
automatically, vest with the Government. They further submits 
that as per Clause (h) of the 1924 Lease Deed, no right accrued in 
favour of the respondents, which clearly stipulates that if lessee
dies heirless before the expiry of the lease deed, as also on the 
expiry of the lease, the land hereby demised and all the buildings 
standing on the land shall revert to the lessor without 6
compensation, therefore, by virtue of condition (h) no right were 
accrued in favour of the respondents. 
7. Arguments heard. Judgment reserved.
