COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
SA No. 66 OF 1987
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Yogesh Tiwari, learned Standing Counsel for 
the appellant.
2. Mr. D.S. Patni, learned Sr. Advocate assisted by 
Mr. Piyush Garg, learned counsel for the respondent 
no. 2/1 and 2/2.
3. Mr. Lalit Sharma, learned counsel for the 
respondent no. 2/3. 
4. In further of arguments advanced by Mr. Yogesh 
Tiwari on the previous date, Mr. Tiwari submits that 
no legal and enforceable rights are accrued in favour of 
the plaintiffs to claim Bhumidhari right over the land 
in question which according to them derive from 
Manohar Lal. He further submits that on the date, 
when suit was filed, there was no cause of action, since 
before institution of suit, eviction proceedings were 
already initiated by filing eviction suit, which they had 
admitted in paragraph 13 of the plaint. He further 
submits that so far as revenue entries are concerned, 
revenue entries were expunged by the Collector which 
was already challenged before the Revenue Court, 
therefore, on the date when suit was filed, there was no 
cause of action and the only the course of action
available to the plaintiffs was to pursue pending 
proceedings before the Revenue Court as well as to 
pursue pending eviction proceedings before the 
Prescribed Authority. Finally, Mr. Tiwari concluding 
his arguments by submitting that the suit filed by 
plaintiff was not maintainable and instituted without 
cause and further order passed by the trial court was 
without jurisdiction and as such, the instant appeal is 
liable to be allowed. 
5. Mr. D.S. Patni, learned Sr. Advocate for the 
respondents submits that the Collector has no power to 
expunge the revenue entries by an administrative 
order. Furthermore, the defendant appellant initiated 
earlier eviction proceedings, however, by virtue of the 
1972 Act, the eviction proceedings was transferred to 
Prescribed Authority under the P.P. Act and in that
proceedings, the appellant / defendant admitted that 
plaintiffs are Bhumidhar. He further submits that there 
was another mutation proceeding, which was initiated by the plaintiffs, which was allowed by order dated 
07.08.1980 against which revision was filed, which was 
dismissed and in this proceeding also the appellant/
defendant admitted that plaintiffs are bhumidhars
which they derived from one Shri Manohar Lal. He 
further submits that there was another proceeding 
under the Land Acquisition Act initiated pursuant to 
the Notification dated 16.09.1982 and in that
proceedings the plaintiffs were shown to be 
bhumidhars of the land in question. Subsequently, the 
land, in question, which was under acquisition 
proceedings was released and brought out of the 
purview of acquisition proceedings by the order of Sub 
Divisional Magistrate dated 02.02.1979. He further 
submits that in three proceedings, the appellants 
admitted that plaintiffs are bhumidhars, therefore, 
there was occasion for the Collector to expunge the 
revenue entries by an administrative order, and as 
such the order of Collector was completely an illegal
order. He further submits that since the administrative 
order of the Collector was per se illegal and void ab 
initio, therefore, there was no occasion for the plaintiffs 
to challenge the same and that is reason, in relief (b) in 
the suit, the plaintiffs prayed for restoration of the 
revenue entries and in respect of relief (a), Mr. Patni, 
submits that there was an apprehension of eviction by 
force that too by the Collector, therefore, plaintiff 
prayed for perpetual injunction to restrain Collector 
from initiating eviction proceeding. He also pointed 
out that it is very surprising that initial eviction 
proceedings was initiated by Collector under his own 
signature but surprisingly by an administrative order 
the Collector expunged the revenue entries and this is 
nothing but a deliberate attempt on the part of the 
Collector to evict the plaintiffs from the land in 
question and such an attempt should be treated as an 
attempt to grab the land in question in which the 
plaintiff were having bhumidhari rights which the 
appellant themselves admits in the earlier proceeding. 
6. List tomorrow for further arguments.
