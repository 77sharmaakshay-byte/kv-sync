4. The present writ petition under Article 
226 of the Constitution of India has been 
filed seeking issuance of a writ of mandamus 
restraining the respondents from carrying 
out any construction of the Udalgaon Link 
Motor Road over the petitioner’s land 
situated at Village Udalgaon, District 
Bageshwar, without acquiring the same in 
accordance with law and without payment of 
due compensation. It is further prayed that a 
writ of mandamus be issued directing the 
respondents to conduct a fresh survey of the 
said motor road after obtaining the consent 
of the villagers of Village Udalgaon, inasmuch 
as, according to the petitioner, no consent or 
No Objection Certificate (NOC) has been 
given by her till date.
5. Heard on the Stay Vacation Application 
(I.A. No. 5 of 2026).
6. Learned counsel for the petitioner 
would submit that construction work of the 
Udalgaon Link Motor Road has already 
commenced and road cutting is being 
undertaken without the consent of the 
petitioner and without acquisition of her land 
or payment of compensation. It is contended 
that in these circumstances, the Coordinate 
Bench of this Court, vide order dated 
14.01.2026, while issuing notice to 
respondent no.5, restrained the respondents 
from making any construction over the land 
situated in Village Udalgaon, District 
Bageshwar.7. Per contra, learned counsel appearing 
for respondent no.5 would submit that after 
completion of all requisite legal formalities, a 
work order was issued by the Public Works 
Department on 22.11.2025 and construction 
commenced immediately thereafter. It is 
submitted that approximately 50% of the 
work has already been completed. It is 
further stated that in the year 2019, a field 
survey was conducted and road alignment 
was finalized, and during a general meeting 
with the local residents, no objection was 
raised by any villager. It is also asserted that 
the petitioner had given a No Objection 
Certificate for the construction of the said 
road.
8. In response, learned counsel for the 
petitioner vehemently denies the assertion 
that the petitioner ever gave any NOC. It is 
further contended that, as per information 
obtained under the Right to Information Act, 
the requisite forest clearance for construction 
of the road has not yet been granted.
9. To this, learned counsel for respondent 
no.5 would submit that the real brother of 
the petitioner had issued a No Objection 
Certificate on 15.06.2019. It is further 
submitted that the said brother had also 
approached this Court by filing WPMS No. 
3062 of 2025 on the same issue, which was 
dismissed by a Coordinate Bench vide 
judgment and order dated 04.11.2025. In the 
said judgment, it was observed that the 
alignment of a motor road falls within the 
exclusive domain of technical experts, 
particularly in hilly areas where challenging 
topography and unique geographical 
conditions necessitate multiple surveys 
before finalization. The Court further 
observed that roads constitute the lifeline of 
society, especially in hilly regions where, in 
the absence of proper road connectivity, 
patients often have to be carried on foot over 
long distances to access medical treatment.
10. Learned counsel for respondent no.5 
would further submit that the present writ petition has been filed with deliberate 
suppression and misrepresentation of 
material facts, with an intent to mislead the 
Court and obtain an interim order so as to 
obstruct a public infrastructure project 
meant for the benefit of the rural community. 
It is pointed out that even in the writ 
petition, the petitioner had requested that 
the road be constructed as per the old 
survey, and in response thereto, the District 
Magistrate, Bageshwar, categorically stated 
that construction is being carried out strictly 
in accordance with the old survey. The 
District Magistrate also clarified that a 
proposal has been prepared to obtain 
consent and approval from the Forest 
Department for any forest land falling within 
the road alignment. It is contended that the 
said reply does not state that the entire road 
is passing solely through forest land, and 
therefore, the petitioner’s assertion to that 
effect is misleading. Hence, it is submitted 
that the interim order deserves to be vacated. 
It is further contended that the plea 
regarding absence of NOC is misconceived, 
as there has been no partition of land 
between the petitioner and her brother.
11. In view of the aforesaid facts and 
circumstances, the interim order dated
14.01.2026 is liable to be modified to the 
extent that respondent no.5 is permitted to 
continue construction of the Udalgaon Link 
Motor Road strictly in accordance with the 
sanctioned plan. It is clarified that if any 
land belonging to the petitioner is utilized for 
the purpose of construction of the road, the 
petitioner shall be at liberty to seek 
appropriate compensation or damages, in 
accordance with law, from the competent 
authority.
12. Accordingly, the Stay Vacation 
Application stands disposed of.
