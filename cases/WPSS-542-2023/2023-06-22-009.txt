Hon’ble Ravindra Maithani, J. (Oral)
These petitions have been listed on mentioning. 
The date had already been fixed in these matters on 
22.05.2023.2
2. Learned counsel for the petitioners would 
submit that, in fact, the circumstances have necessitated 
the petitioners to move an interim relief application 
seeking stay of further proceedings with regard to an 
examination that is being conducted by the respondent 
no.5. Uttarakhand Subordinate Service Selection 
Commission (“the Commission”) pursuant to advertisement 
dated 10.04.2023.
3. Heard on Interim Relief Application No.2 of 
2023.
4. The case of the petitioners, in nutshell, has been 
recorded by this Court in its order dated 09.05.2023 as 
follows:-
“It is the case of the petitioners that the 
respondent no.5 conducted an examination for 
recruitment to the post of Foresters through one 
recruiting agency the National Stock Exchange for 
Information and Technology (“for short, the NSEIT”). 
Initially, when some persons came to know that the 
NSEIT is blacklisted in many States, they filed a Writ 
Petition (S/S) No. 1165 of 2021 before this Court for 
change of recruitment agency. But, on behalf of 
respondent nos. 4 and 5, it was assured to the Court 
that respondent no.4 the NSEIT is not negligent 
anywhere and the examination would be conducted as 
per Rules and Regulations. It is argued that the entire 
examination process, which was conducted in eighteen 
phases, has been scraped by the respondent no.5 and 3
they have advertised another schedule for the 
conducting examination, which is to begin from 
11.06.2023, this is Annexure 7 to the writ petition, 
which is questioned in this writ petition.
Learned counsel for the petitioners would submit 
that the wrong doers, if any, may be identified by 
respondent no.5. The whole examination process which 
took place in eighteen phases, it need not be scraped in 
toto. It is submitted that the examination was on online 
mode. The tracking would be quite convenient to track 
the wrong doers.”
5. The matter was heard on 09.06.2023. During 
the course of arguments, it was revealed that, in fact, the 
examination was cancelled, merely on the basis of the 
pattern of answering the questions by some of the 
candidates. It compelled this Court to discuss the issue as 
to whether the same answering pattern with regard to all 
620 candidates, who appeared in the examination may be 
analysed.
6. The answer given by the respondent no.5, the 
Commission as well as respondent no.4 the NSEIT was in 
affirmative. What happened on 09.06.2023 is recorded in 
its order as follows:-
“It is argued that total 620 candidates were called 
for document verification. The respondent no.5, the 
Commission could have examined the logs of each 
candidate to ascertain as to whether it was a case of 
mass copying or not. 
During the course of argument, when chargesheet 
of the instant case has been perused, it is revealed that 4
the Investigating Officer (“IO”) has isolated some 
candidates based on their attempts to the question 
papers. For example, the IO has concluded that one of 
the candidates did attempt 98 questions in 7 minutes 
and 48 seconds. One of the candidates has attempted 96 
questions in 19 minutes and 10 seconds. Based on it, 
the IO found their activities doubtful. In fact, there were 
6 candidates, who have been distinctly identified by the 
IO. 
Learned counsel for the petitioners would submit 
that similarly, the respondent no.5, the Commission, 
with the help of the respondent no.4, the NSEIT, could 
segregate the candidates by reading their logs, which is 
available with the respondent no.5, the Commission. 
When the Court posed this question to learned 
counsel for the respondent no.5, the Commission, and 
the respondent no.4, the NSEIT, learned counsel 
appearing for the respondent no.5, the Commission 
would submit that as yet, the Commission has not 
studied the entire log, but it may be studied with the 
help of the expert from the NSEIT or from the police 
department, if required. The Court requested learned 
counsel appearing for the respondent no.4, The NSEIT 
as to whether any technical person from NSEIT could 
assist the Court in the hearing. 
After a while, Mr. Sudhir Savant, General Manager 
Business from the NSEIT joined the proceedings through 
video conferencing. On a query of the Court, he would 
submit that the logs of all the 620 candidates may be 
examined to study the pattern of the questions they 
attempted; as to when the questions were attempted. 
The Court, in fact, further posed a query whether can 
there be a graphical presentation of such attempts as 
well, to study the pattern as to how the questions were 
attempted? When were they attempted? Were they 
quickly done in a slot or were they spread over the time 
allotted for the examination? 
He would submit that it could also be done very 
conveniently because the entire log is with the 5
respondent no.5, the Commission or with the 
respondent no.4, the NSEIT as well. 
It is a case in which after final declaration on one 
exam conducted by the respondent no.5, the 
Commission, the entire process has been cancelled by 
the respondent no.5, the Commission, in order to 
maintain purity in the public examination. The intention 
cannot be doubted by anyone. But the question that 
falls for consideration is that if the candidates, who did 
adopt unfair means in the examination, could be 
segregated conveniently, without any difficulty, should 
the recourse for cancellation of mass copying be 
adopted? 
Therefore, this Court has undertaken this 
exercise, as stated hereinabove.
In view of what is stated, hereinabove, this Court 
requests the respondent no.5, the Commission, to study 
the logs of each of the 620 candidates to examine the 
pattern of their attempts to the questions; the duration 
when they were attempted; The time when they were 
attempted? Like it was studied by the IO, as concluded 
in the chargesheet. The respondent no.5 may take the 
assistance of the respondent no.4, the NSEIT, or their
experts and if required, the IO or Senior Police Officers 
may be contacted for that purpose.
The Court requests the Director General of Police, 
Uttarakhand, to extend all assistance to the respondent 
no.5, the Commission, in this endeavour, if so required. 
This exercise, the Court expects, should be completed 
within 15 days from today. 
After studying the logs of each of the 620 
candidates, the respondent no. 5, the Commission, shall 
constitute a Committee, inviting experts, maybe from the 
respondent no.4, the NSEIT, also, or as the respondent 
no.5, the Commission deems fit and form an opinion on 
the following:-
(i) Whether the candidates, who used unfair 
means in the examination, could be 
segregated? If so, who are they. And if such 6
segregation can be done, what is basis for 
it?
(ii) Whether the segregation of the candidates, 
who shall used unfair means, is not 
possible? If so, why? Give its reasons. 
Such report shall be filed before the Court on or 
before 30.06.2023. 
List this matter on 05.07.2023, just after fresh 
cases.”
7. Learned counsel appearing for the petitioners 
would submit that instead of proceeding as per direction of 
the Court dated 09.06.2023, the respondent no.4, the 
Commission expedited the process of recruitment, which 
they have initiated now. They did not constitute any 
Committee. They did not inform the respondent no.4 the 
NSEIT. They conducted the examination on 11.06.2023 
and declared the result on 16.06.2023 and now they are 
proceeding further with the physical examination test on 
27.06.2023.
8. It is submitted that if the respondent no.5, the 
Commission is not restrained to proceed further pursuant 
to the advertisement dated 10.04.2023, which is Annexure 
11 to the writ petition, it may defeat the very purpose of 
filing the instant writ petition. The speed with which the 
respondent no.5, the Commission is moving, it might 
happen that before the next date fixed, they may declare 
the result.7
9. Learned counsel for the respondent no.5, the 
Commission would submit that pursuant to the order 
dated 09.06.2023, a committee has already been 
constituted. The respondent no.5, the Commission has no 
intention to defeat the very purpose of the petition. They 
are in the mode of compliance of order dated 09.06.2023 of 
this Court. It is submitted that the further proceedings 
with regard to the recruitment pursuant to the
advertisement dated 10.04.2023 in no manner, would 
cause harm to the petitioners because the result may be 
subject to the final outcome of the writ petition.
10. Learned counsel for the respondent no.5, the 
Commission would submit that pursuant to the 
advertisement dated 10.04.2023, the recruitment process 
for three posts is underway, including the Forester, which 
is involved in the instant petition.
11. At it, learned counsel for the petitioners would 
submit that they seek restrained order qua recruitment to 
the post of Forester.
12. Learned counsel for the respondent no.4 the 
NSEIT would submit that they did not receive any 8
communication from the respondent no.5, the Commission 
in view of the order dated 09.06.2023 of this Court. 
Learned counsel, who joined through video conferencing 
for the respondent no.4 the NSEIT would submit that, in 
fact, yesterday the respondent no.4 the NSEIT had written 
to the respondent no.5 the Commission with regard to the 
subject and it was sent through e-mail.
13. One examination of the Foresters has been 
cancelled on the ground that unfair means were used in it. 
In the instant petitions challenge is made to that 
cancellation. On 09.06.2023, this Court had made an 
endeavour to identify as to whether these candidates, who 
used unfair means can be segregated? The respondent 
no.5, the Commission was required to look into it with the 
assistance of the respondent no.4 the NSEIT. The 
respondent no.4 the NSEIT had agreed on that date to 
render all kind of assistance in the matter. But, the 
respondent no.5 did not move even till today. Statement is 
not given even today that the respondent no.4 the NSEIT 
has been informed to assist the respondent no.5, the 
Commission.
14. On the other hand, it is explicit that after 
conducting the examination on 11.06.2023, the result has 9
been declared on 16.06.2023 and now on 27.06.2013 
further steps are to be taken by the respondent no.5, the 
Commission in the matter. These facts taken cumulatively 
compels this Court to pass an interim order. As an interim 
measure, till the next date of listing, the respondent no.5, 
the Commission shall not proceed further for recruitment 
on the post of Forester, pursuant to the advertisement
dated 10.04.2023.
15. List on the date fixed.
16. Interim relief application stands disposed of 
accordingly.
