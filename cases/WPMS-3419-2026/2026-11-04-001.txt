2. By means of the present petition, the petitioners 
have sought the following reliefs:-
a) Issue a writ, order or direction in the nature of 
certiorari quashing the judgment dated 
17.08.2023 (contained as Annexure No.5 to the 
writ petition page no.55 to 80) passed by 
Settlement Officer Consolidation, in Appeal 
No.72/2022-2023, Vinod Kumar and others Vs. 
Arvind Kumar and others and further the 
judgment dated 24.09.2026 (contained as 
Annexure No.7 to the writ petition page no.89-
103) passed by Deputy Director, Consolidation/ 
Collector, Haridwar, passed in Revision 
No.122/2023-24, U/s 48(1) of Consolidation of 
Holding Act, 1953.
3. The brief facts, as narrated by the learned counsel 
for the petitioners, are that the matter emanates from 
proceedings under Section 9-A(2) of the U.P. 
Consolidation of Holdings Act, 1953.4. The learned senior counsel for the petitioners has 
invited the attention of the Court to the judgment dated 
9th September 1999 passed by the Consolidation Officer, 
Roorkee (Purvi), whereby, by virtue of the said order, a 
division of Khata No. 160 was made amongst the co￾tenure-holders of the said khata.
5. The learned senior counsel would further submit 
that thereafter, the petitioners purchased land in respect 
of another Khata No. 324, Khasra No. 1555, and would 
submit that the khata number was different and the land 
was altogether distinct. He would further submit that, 
under Rule 109-A of the Uttar Pradesh Consolidation of 
Holdings Rules, 1954, an incorrect entry was made in 
Khatauni No. 324. The matter was agitated before the 
Consolidation Officer regarding the said incorrect entry, 
and the Consolidation Officer, Roorkee, District 
Haridwar, vide judgment and order dated 17.05.2019, 
allowed the objections of the petitioners and cancelled 
the wrong amaldaramad/execution under Rule 109-A in 
respect of Khata No. 324 (Gata No. 1555).
6. Aggrieved by the said judgment and order dated 
17.05.2019, the affected party preferred an appeal before 
the Settlement Officer Consolidation, and the Settlement 
Officer Consolidation, vide judgment and order dated 
17.08.2023, allowed the appeal and set aside the 
judgment and order dated 17.05.2019. Aggrieved by the 
appellate order dated 17th August, 2023 passed by the 
Settlement Officer Consolidation, Roorkee, the 
petitioners herein preferred a revision, which was 
dismissed along with other revisions vide judgment and order dated 24th September 2026.
7. The learned senior counsel appearing on behalf of 
the petitioners would submit that the appellate court as 
well as the revisional court has erred in relying upon 
Section 52 of the U.P. Consolidation of Holdings Act,
1953. He would submit that, the rectification of the 
amaldaramad/execution had been carried out in the 
Khatauni in respect of Khata No. 324, Gata No. 1555, 
since there was no bar of Section 52 of the U.P. 
Consolidation of Holdings Act, 1953. He would further 
submit that, as the provisions of Rule 109-A would 
reflect, the same is relatable to Section 52(2) of the U.P. 
Consolidation of Holdings Act, 1953, and, therefore, the 
appellate court and the revisional court have erred in 
allowing the appeal and dismissing the revision, 
respectively.
8. He would further submit that the petitioners are 
bona fide purchasers for value and that, from the entry in 
Khatauni No. 324 relatable to Gata No. 1555, it is 
evident that the property was purchased by the 
petitioners from the tenure-holder/vendor, namely, Jai 
Kishore Singhal, who was duly recorded in Khata No. 
324, and that thereafter, due mutation was carried out in 
the said khata. He would further submit that, by virtue of 
the orders passed by the appellate court and the 
revisional court, the rights of the petitioners would be 
prejudiced, inasmuch as there is an imminent threat of 
dispossession. He would thus submit that he is ready to 
take steps to serve the respondents expeditiously, in 
addition to the normal mode of service.9. In view of the facts and circumstances of the case, 
since the petitioners appears to be bona fide purchasers 
for value and, pursuant to the sale deed executed in their 
favour, their names were also mutated in the revenue 
records, i.e., Khata No. 324 relatable to Gata No. 1555, 
the matter requires deliberation.
10. Admit.
11. Issue notice to the respondents. Steps shall be 
taken within 48 hours. In addition to the normal mode of 
service, the petitioners may also serve the respondents 
through dasti.
Interim Relief Application (IA No. 1 of 2026)
12. Since the petitioners are seeking an interim order 
to protect their possession, it is, therefore, provided, as 
an interim measure, that the respondents shall maintain 
status quo with regard to the possession of the property 
in question till the next date of listing.
13. List on 01.12.2026.
