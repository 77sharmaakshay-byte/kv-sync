COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
WPMS No. 1570 of 2024
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Jitendra Chaudhary and Dr. 
Kartikey Hari Gupta, learned counsel for 
the petitioners.
2. Mr. Arvind Vashistha, learned Senior 
Advocate assisted by Mr. Hemant Singh, 
learned counsel for the respondents.
3. A suit for partition was filed bearing 
O.S. No. 123 of 1980 under Section 176 of 
UPZA&LR Act in which a preliminary decree 
was passed on 23.08.1980 and final 
decree was passed on 16.10.1980.
4. For recalling both the preliminary and 
final decree, a restoration application was 
filed by one Succha Singh and his minor 
sons Hari Singh and Ranjit Singh on 
14.05.1982, however, the said restoration 
application was rejected on the ground 
that Succha Singh and his minor sons were 
not recorded tenure holder over the land in 
question and during the pendency of the 
suit the property was sold by Baljeet Singh 
to Succha Singh. It has further been 
observed that if they had any grievance 
they can move the application before 
deciding the suit and after final 
adjudication restoration application has 
been moved which is time barred. The 
order rejecting the restoration application 
dated 11.07.1983 is placed on record as 
Annexure-5 and the relevant extract is at 
page 71 of the paper book.
5. The order dated 11.07.1983, whereby 
restoration application was rejected was 
challenged in an appeal bearing Appeal No. 
141 of 1983 in the Court of Commissioner. 
On instructions, it is submitted that on 
28.09.1983, the Commissioner closed the proceedings of the appeal by observing 
that the suit stands abated. Thereafter two 
revisions have been preferred by Succha 
Singh, i.e. Revision No. 113 of 1983 and 
Revision No. 113-A of 1983 in the Court of 
Commissioner Meerut Division, Meerut. So 
far as Revision No. 113-A of 1983 is 
concerned, the same was preferred against 
the preliminary decree dated 23.08.1980 
and so far as Revision No. 113 of 1983 is 
concerned, it was preferred against the 
final decree dated 16.10.1980. Copies of 
memo of both the revisions were supplied 
by Mr. Arvind Vashistha, learned Senior 
Advocate for the respondents.
6. This Court perused the same and 
what this Court found that this fact has not 
been disclosed by Succha Singh in both the 
revisions, i.e., Revision No. 133A of 1983 
and Revision No. 133 of 1983 that their 
restoration application was already 
dismissed on merits on 11.07.1983.
7. Mr. Jitendra Chaudhary, learned 
counsel for the petitioners submits that 
both the revisions were admittedly time 
barred and the same was filed without any
delay condonation application. 
8. It is submitted that in both the 
revisions without condoning the delay, the 
orders have been passed by the Court of 
the Commissioner by observing that since 
the village has come under consolidation 
operation the revision along with the suit 
shall stand abated. Both the revisions 
decided on 28.09.1983.
9. Mr. Arvind Vashistha, learned Senior 
Advocate for the respondents in order to 
justify both the orders passed in both the 
revisions gives reference of Section 5(2)(a) 
of the U.P. Consolidation of Holdings Act, 
1953 which reads as under:
“5. Effect of [notification under Section 4)(2)]
1. …………….
2. Upon the said publication of the notification 
under Sub-section (2) of Section 4, the following further consequences shall ensue in 
the area to which the notification relates, 
namely-
(a) every proceedings for the correction of 
records and every suit and proceedings in 
respect of declaration of right or interest in 
any land lying in the area, or for declaration 
or adjudication of any other right in regard to 
which proceedings can or ought to be taken 
under this Act, pending before any court or 
authority whether of the first instance or of 
appeal, reference or revision, shall, on an 
order being passed in that behalf by the court 
or authority before whom such suit or 
proceeding is pending, stand abated:
Provided that no such order shall be passed 
without giving to the parties notice by post or 
any other manner and after giving them an 
opportunity of being heard:
Provided further that on the issue of a 
notification under sub-section (1) of Section 6 
in respect of the said area or part thereof, 
every such order in relation to the land lying 
in such area or part as the case may be, shall 
stand vacated.
(b)……………………..
10. Learned Senior Advocate submits that 
by virtue of Section 5(2)(a) of the U.P. 
Consolidation of Holdings Act, 1953, the 
Revisional Court has rightly passed the 
order in the revisions that the suit shall 
stand abated. In response to this, Mr. 
Jitendra Chaudhary, learned counsel for 
the petitioners submits that said provision 
will apply only in respect of the pending 
suit and on the date when the order was 
passed in both the revisions, the suit was 
already decided much prior to that, 
therefore, by virtue of this section the 
Revisional Court was not correct in 
observing that the suit stands abated 
because on the date when the Revisional 
Court passed the order, the suit was not 
pending.
11. Now the question which still remains 
on which both the counsel will address that 
whether the Revisional Court was right in 
observing that the suit stands abated 
particularly in revision of those persons who were not party to the suit and their 
restoration application was dismissed 
much prior to it.
12. List this case on 21.06.2024.
