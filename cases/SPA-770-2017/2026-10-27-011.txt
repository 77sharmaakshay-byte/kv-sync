Review Application (MCC No.12440 of 2025) 
5. Heard on review application.
6. Learned State Counsel submits that this appeal was 
wrongly connected with writ petition filed by Narendra 
Singh & another [Writ Petition (S/B) No.616 of 2018] and 
was decided by a common judgment dated 22.02.2024, 
although, the issue involved in this appeal was entirely 
different from the issues raised in Writ Petition (S/B) 
No.616 of 2018.
7. It is contended that in Writ Petition (S/B) No.616 of 
2018, validity of Rules framed by State of Uttarakhand for 
regularization of ad hoc, contract or part-time employees 
was challenged on the ground that as per law declared by 
Hon’ble Supreme Court in State of Karnataka & others vs. Uma Devi & others, reported in 2006 (4) SCC 1, no 
one can be regularized in service before completing 
minimum period of ten years, while the Regularization 
Rules, 2013 provide for regularization of those who have 
completed five years of service. In Writ Petition (S/B) 
No.616 of 2018, this Court held as follows:-
“6. In the present case, there was an interim order of stay dated 
04.12.2018, that no orders of regularization will be passed by the 
State Government, under the 2013 Rules. Hence, since 
04.12.2018 no regularization orders have been passed. All those 
persons, who have been regularized as per the 2013 Rules before 
04.12.2018, have worked for more than 13-14 years now. With 
respect to other candidates, who have to be regularized now, they 
can be regularized, as per the 2013 Rules, by taking into 
consideration 10 years’ service, instead of 05 years, as of now.
7. Accordingly, the 2013 Rules are being modified that, after 
04.12.2018, 10 years’ period will be taken into consideration for 
regularization.”
8. Learned State Counsel submits that State has filed 
this special appeal challenging judgment rendered by 
learned Single Judge in different writ petitions, whereby 
State Authorities were directed to regularize writ petitioners 
in those cases under Regularization Rules, 2013. He 
submits that since this Court, in the judgment dated 
22.02.2024, rendered in Writ Petition (S/B) No.616 of 
2018, held that period of five years prescribed in 
Regularization Rules, 2013 shall be read as ten years, 
therefore, the issue raised in the present special appeal 
could not have been decided together with Writ Petition 
(S/B) No.616 of 2018.
9. We find substance in the contention raised by learned 
State Counsel- review applicant. Since in the judgment 
impugned in this intra-court appeal, there is a direction 
issued to State Authorities to regularize writ petitioners by 
applying Regularization Rules, 2013, while in Writ Petition 
(S/B) No.2013, this Court had held that five years’ period 
prescribed in Regularization Rules, 2013 to be read as ten 
years, therefore, this appeal was wrongly decided with Writ 
Petition (S/B) No.616 of 2018.
10. We, accordingly, review the judgment dated 
22.02.2024 qua Special Appeal No.770 of 2017 and restore this special appeal to its original number.
11. List on 17.11.2026 for hearing on merits.
12. Review application (MCC No.12440 of 2025) stands 
disposed of.
