Arguments heard but not concluded. 
On behalf of the petitioners, it is 
argued that it is not a case of mass 
copying. 
Learned counsel for the petitioners 
would submit that, in fact, this was online 
exam with all the safeguards maintained 
by the Commission with the recruiting 
agency, which is National Stock Exchange 
Information Technology (“the NSEIT”), the 
respondent no.4. 
It is argued that total 620 candidates were called for document verification. The 
respondent no.5, the Commission could 
have examined the logs of each candidate 
to ascertain as to whether it was a case of 
mass copying or not. 
During the course of argument, 
when chargesheet of the instant case has 
been perused, it is revealed that the 
Investigating Officer (“IO”) has isolated 
some candidates based on their attempts 
to the question papers. For example, the 
IO has concluded that one of the 
candidates did attempt 98 questions in 7 
minutes and 48 seconds. One of the 
candidates has attempted 96 questions in 
19 minutes and 10 seconds. Based on it, 
the IO found their activities doubtful. In 
fact, there were 6 candidates, who have 
been distinctly identified by the IO. 
Learned counsel for the petitioners 
would submit that similarly, the 
respondent no.5, the Commission, with 
the help of the respondent no.4, the 
NSEIT, could segregate the candidates by 
reading their logs, which is available with 
the respondent no.5, the Commission. 
When the Court posed this question 
to learned counsel for the respondent 
no.5, the Commission, and the 
respondent no.4, the NSEIT, learned 
counsel appearing for the respondent 
no.5, the Commission would submit that 
as yet, the Commission has not studied 
the entire log, but it may be studied with 
the help of the expert from the NSEIT or 
from the police department, if required. The Court requested learned counsel 
appearing for the respondent no.4, The 
NSEIT as to whether any technical person 
from NSEIT could assist the Court in the 
hearing. 
After a while, Mr. Sudhir Savant, 
General Manager Business from the 
NSEIT joined the proceedings through 
video conferencing. On a query of the 
Court, he would submit that the logs of all 
the 620 candidates may be examined to 
study the pattern of the questions they 
attempted; as to when the questions were 
attempted. The Court, in fact, further 
posed a query whether can there be a 
graphical presentation of such attempts 
as well, to study the pattern as to how the 
questions were attempted? When were 
they attempted? Were they quickly done 
in a slot or were they spread over the time 
allotted for the examination? 
He would submit that it could also
be done very conveniently because the 
entire log is with the respondent no.5, the 
Commission or with the respondent no.4, 
the NSEIT as well. 
It is a case in which after final 
declaration on one exam conducted by the 
respondent no.5, the Commission, the 
entire process has been cancelled by the 
respondent no.5, the Commission, in 
order to maintain purity in the public 
examination. The intention cannot be 
doubted by anyone. But the question that 
falls for consideration is that if the 
candidates, who did adopt unfair means in the examination, could be segregated 
conveniently, without any difficulty, 
should the recourse for cancellation of 
mass copying be adopted?
Therefore, this Court has 
undertaken this exercise, as stated 
hereinabove. 
In view of what is stated, 
hereinabove, this Court requests the 
respondent no.5, the Commission, to 
study the logs of each of the 620 
candidates to examine the pattern of their 
attempts to the questions; the duration 
when they were attempted; The time when 
they were attempted? Like it was studied 
by the IO, as concluded in the 
chargesheet. The respondent no.5 may 
take the assistance of the respondent 
no.4, the NSEIT, or their experts and if 
required, the IO or Senior Police Officers 
may be contacted for that purpose. 
The Court requests the Director 
General of Police, Uttarakhand, to extend 
all assistance to the respondent no.5, the 
Commission, in this endeavour, if so 
required. This exercise, the Court expects, 
should be completed within 15 days from 
today. 
After studying the logs of each of the 
620 candidates, the respondent no. 5, the 
Commission, shall constitute a 
Committee, inviting experts, maybe from 
the respondent no.4, the NSEIT, also, or 
as the respondent no.5, the Commission 
deems fit and form an opinion on the following:-
(i) Whether the candidates, who 
used unfair means in the 
examination, could be 
segregated? If so, who are they. 
And if such segregation can be 
done, what is basis for it?
(ii) Whether the segregation of the 
candidates, who shall used 
unfair means, is not possible? If 
so, why? Give its reasons. 
Such report shall be filed before the 
Court on or before 30.06.2023. 
List this matter on 05.07.2023, just 
after fresh cases.
