 Mr. Vikas Bahuguna, Advocate present for the

4. The petitioner who is a builder has been given a 
land by the State Government which is being managed by 
State Infrastructure and Industrial Development Corporation 
of Uttarakhand Limited (in short “SIDCUL”). The petitioner 
had committed an apparent default in the year 2016 by not 
paying the installments which at the relevant time was Rupees 
Fourteen Crores. Subsequently, the petitioner has filed the 
present writ petition before this Court stating that although 
the amount has been paid but since he has paid the part 
amount, he should not pay the recovery charges. 
5. The case of the petitioner before this Court was that 
he was not liable to pay the recovery charges to the State 
Government. Petitioner has also cited certain pronouncements 
in the case of Sukhvinder Singh Vs. State of Uttarakhand 
and others passed in WPMS No. 788 of 2012 and other 
connected matters, decided on 28.07.2014 as well as the 
judgment of Allahabad High Court in the case of Mahrajwa 2
and others Vs. State of U.P. & others, reported in 2013 
(119) RD 717, in order to buttress his argument. 
6. This Court had granted an indulgence to the 
petitioner by passing the following order on 01.05.2017:- 
“Mr. Vikas Bahuguna, Advocate, present for the 
petitioner. 
Mr. P.C. Bisht, Standing Counsel, present for the 
State/respondent Nos. 1 to 3. 
Mr. Rakesh Thapliyal, Advocate, present for the 
respondent Nos. 4 and 5. 
Three weeks’ time is granted to the respondents to 
file counter affidavit. 
List this matter on 01.06.2017 in the daily cause 
list. 
In case, the petitioner deposits the entire amount, 
including the recovery charges, it is made clear that 
this aspect whether the recovery charges are liable 
to be paid or not by the petitioner will the examined 
at the time of final determination of the writ 
petition.” 
7. Thereafter, the petitioner was also directed by this 
Court vide order dated 05.06.2017 to deposit an amount of 
Rupees One Crore within a period of two weeks i.e. on or 
before 19.06.2017 as the petitioner was default in payment. 
Even that time the only question before this Court was 
whether the petitioner was liable to pay the recovery charges 
or not. 
8. All the same, this petition has come up before this 
Court on a time extension application, wherein the petitioner 
seeks three weeks’ time to deposit the said amount, which has 
not been deposited by him. No plausible reasons have been 
assigned by the petitioner as to why the order dated 
05.06.2017 has not been complied with. Only legal 
submissions are being made by the petitioner that he is not 3
liable to pay the recovery charges. All such submissions will be 
heard at an appropriate stage. This is a matter which is still 
sub judice and the Court will hear all the factual as well as 
legal submission of the petitioner, subject to the fact that he 
complies with the order dated 05.06.2017, which was for 
depositing of Rupees One Crore. Since this has not been done, 
no further indulgence can by granted by this Court. 
9. Time extension application stands dismissed. 
10. List this case for hearing in due course. 
 (Sudhanshu Dhulia, J.)
