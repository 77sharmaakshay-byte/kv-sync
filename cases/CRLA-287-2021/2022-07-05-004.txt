These two applications have been filed by 
the appellants / applicants for suspension of 
sentence and grant of bail. The appellants have 
convicted under Sections 201, 302/120B of the 
Indian Penal Code, 1860 and sentenced them to 
undergo imprisonment for life and to pay fine of 
Rs. 25,000/- each and in default, to further 
undergo simple imprisonment of four months 
under Section 302 IPC; to undergo rigorous 
imprisonment for five years and to pay fine of 
Rs. 10,000 each and in default, to further undergo 
simple imprisonment of three months under 
Section 201 IPC.
Admittedly, the case of the prosecution is 
based on circumstantial evidence. It is apparent 
from the record that there is a last seen evidence 
against appellant – Snehlata Chauhan and she 
had made an extra judicial confession to PW5 
Smt. Rajkumari Chauhan, who is her sister inlaw (Jethani) and then there is recovery of purse 
of the deceased on the statement of appellant –
Amit Parley. Death of the deceased was 
determined to be homicidal in nature. Moreover, 
it is apparent from record that both the 
appellants were not on bail during trial.
Keeping in view the aforesaid 
circumstances, we are of the opinion that the 
arguments advanced by the respective learned 
Senior Counsel for the appellants are, in fact, 
matter to be considered regarding re￾appreciation of the evidence, at the final stage of
hearing of the appeals. At this stage, we are of 
the prima facie view that the judgment passed by 
the learned IV Additional Sessions Judge, 
Dehradun is correct and there is no need to 
suspend the sentence and grant bail to the 
appellants. 
In that view of the matter, we are inclined 
to dismiss the applications. Accordingly, both 
the applications are dismissed being devoid of 
merits. 
List these matters on 06.09.2022 for final 
disposal. 
In the meantime, learned counsel for the 
appellants shall obtain paper book from the 
Registry.
