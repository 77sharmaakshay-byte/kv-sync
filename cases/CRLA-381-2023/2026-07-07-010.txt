Hon'ble Ravindra Maithani, J. (Oral)
Hon'ble Alok Mahra, J.
The instant appeal is preferred against the 
judgment and order dated 16.03.2023 passed by learned 
Second Additional Sessions Judge, Rudrapur, District Udham 
Singh Nagar in S.T. No. 87 of 2018 ‘State Vs. Harjeet Singh @ 
Kala and Others’, whereby the appellant has been convicted 
and sentenced for the offence punishable under Sections 147, 
148, 307/149, 302/149 I.P.C.
2. The appellant seeks bail during pendency of the 
appeal.
3. Heard learned counsel for the parties on the bail 
application and perused the record.
4. According to the prosecution case, the father of 
PW1, Angrej Singh, was allegedly murdered pursuant to a UKHC010098262023
2
criminal conspiracy on 17.07.2015, in which the co-convicts 
were arraigned as accused. It is alleged that, owing to the said 
incident, the co-convicts had been continuously pressurizing 
PW1 Angrej Singh and his family members to compromise the 
murder case. On account of this previous enmity, it is further 
alleged that on 07.01.2018 at about 1:15 p.m., the appellants, 
along with the co-convicts, unlawfully assembled and 
attacked PW1 Angrej Singh, Daljeet Singh, Ravinder Singh, 
Kuldeep Singh, Harbans Singh, and others with sharp-edged 
weapons, lathis, dandas, and a country-made pistol. As a 
result of the alleged assault, two persons succumbed to their 
injuries and died at the spot.
5. Learned counsel for the appellant/applicant would 
submit that in the murder case relating to the father of PW1 
Angrej Singh, the present appellant was neither named as an 
accused nor is he related to the deceased or any member of 
the deceased's family. It is further submitted that the 
appellant had no prior enmity, connection, or concern 
whatsoever with PW1 Angrej Singh or his family members. 
Learned counsel would further contend that, according to the 
prosecution case, the appellant was allegedly carrying a 
danda during the incident; however, no injury attributable to 
a danda was found either on the deceased or on any of the 
injured persons. 
6. It is also argued that PW2 Kuldeep Singh has not 
stated anything regarding the presence of the appellant at the 
place of occurrence, and in this regard, reliance has been UKHC010098262023
3
placed upon paragraph 57 of the testimony of PW2 Kuldeep 
Singh. It is further contended that, on the date of the alleged 
incident, a Nagar Kirtan was being held in the locality, and 
the appellant, being a resident of another village, had come 
only to participate in the said religious procession. It is 
argued that the appellant has been falsely implicated in the 
present case merely due to his presence in the area, and that 
he had neither any motive nor any reason to assault or attack 
either the deceased or the injured persons.
7. Learned State Counsel would submit that the 
appellant has been convicted with the aid of Section 149 IPC. 
He would further submit that PW1, Angrej Singh, has 
specifically referred to the CCTV footage and, in his deposition 
before the Trial Court, identified the appellant as one of the 
assailants who was seen attacking with a danda. Learned 
State Counsel has also drawn the attention of the Court to the 
relevant portion of the testimony of PW1, Angrej Singh. It is 
further contended that PW2, Kuldeep Singh, and PW6, 
Rajvinder Singh, are both injured eye-witnesses, who have 
consistently attributed a specific role to the present appellant 
in the commission of the offence. Learned State Counsel 
submits that the appellant has failed to make out a case for 
grant of bail.
8. Learned Senior Counsel appearing on behalf of the 
victim would submit that the present case pertains to a case 
of double murder; that, the co-convicts were already facing 
prosecution for the murder of the father of PW1 Angrej Singh UKHC010098262023
4
and, after being released on bail in the said case, they, in 
furtherance of their common object, committed the present 
offence with the help of the appellant/applicant. It is further 
submitted that the presence and participation of the appellant 
are clearly discernible from the CCTV footage. Besides, the 
injured eyewitnesses, namely PW1 Angrej Singh and PW6 
Rajvinder Singh, have specifically attributed a role to the 
appellant in the occurrence. Learned Senior Counsel would 
argue that the mere fact that no specific injury is shown to 
have been caused by the appellant with the danda is of no 
consequence, as he was an active member of the unlawful 
assembly that attacked the deceased as well as the injured 
persons. His conviction with the aid of Section 149 of the 
Indian Penal Code is, therefore, legally sustainable. It is, thus, 
submitted that, considering the gravity of the offence and the 
evidence available on record, the appellant/applicant does not 
deserve the concession of bail.
9. It is argued that it makes no difference whether 
any injury was sustained due to hitting of the appellant or 
not, since he was a member of the unlawful assembly which 
attacked the deceased and the injured persons. He has, 
therefore, been rightly convicted with the aid of Section 149 
IPC. Hence, it is contended that the present case is not a fit 
case for grant of bail.
10. Replying the said argument, learned counsel for 
the applicant would submit that, in fact, according to the 
prosecution itself, the applicant had masked his face at the UKHC010098262023
5
time of the incident. In such situation, it is argued that it was 
not possible for any of the witnesses to identify or recognize 
the applicant.
11. In reply, learned State Counsel would submit that 
PW1 Angrej Singh has categorically deposed that although the 
appellant had covered his face with a mask at the time of the 
incident, the mask slipped while he was assaulting the 
victims, enabling the eyewitnesses to clearly recognize and 
identify him. It is further submitted that the testimony of 
PW1, coupled with the other prosecution evidence, sufficiently 
establishes the identity and participation of the appellant in 
the occurrence. 
12. It is a stage of bail post conviction. Much of the 
discussion is not expected of. Arguments are being 
appreciated with the caveat that any observation made in this 
order shall have no bearing at any subsequent stage of the 
proceedings. 
13. As stated, this is an application for bail post￾conviction. The applicant does not have the privilege of the 
presumption of innocence, having already been convicted by 
the trial Court. It is the prosecution case that the co-convicts 
were accused in the murder of the father of PW1 Angrej Singh 
in the year 2015 and were pressurizing PW1 Angrej Singh to 
compromise the said case. When the compromise did not 
materialize, the applicant, along with the co-convicts, 
attacked PW1 Angrej Singh and others, resulting in the death 
of two persons and injuries to several others. The applicant UKHC010098262023
6
has been assigned the specific role of carrying a danda and 
participating in the assault. The prosecution witnesses have 
also stated that the applicant is visible in the CCTV footage.
14. Having considered the submissions advanced and 
the material available on record, this Court does not find any 
ground to enlarge the applicant on bail during the pendency 
of the appeal. Accordingly, the application for suspension of 
sentence and grant of bail (IA No. 2 of 2023) is rejected.
15. List the criminal appeal for final hearing in due 
course.
