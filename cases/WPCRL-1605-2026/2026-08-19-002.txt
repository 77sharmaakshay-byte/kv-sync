2. It has come on record that Dinesh Chandra 
expired on 24.12.2024, whereas the alleged gift 
deed was executed on 20.12.2025, i.e. nearly one 
year after his death. Despite this, the said gift 
deed purports to have been executed by Dinesh 
Chandra himself. It is, therefore, prima facie 
evident that the gift deed could not have been 
executed by Dinesh Chandra. It is further 
surprising that the petitioner, in whose favour the 
alleged gift deed was executed, has alone been 
arrayed as an accused, whereas neither the 
attesting witnesses to the gift deed nor any other 
person associated with its execution has, so far, 
been made an accused.
3. On the previous occasion, learned State 
Counsel was directed to obtain specific 
instructions as to why the F.I.R. has been 
registered against the petitioner alone and as to 
what steps have been taken during the course of 
investigation against the other persons who were 
connected with or witnessed the execution of the 
alleged gift deed. Today, on the basis of written 
instructions, learned State Counsel submits that 
the investigation is still in progress and that, surprisingly, till date, the statement of no person 
has been recorded by the Investigating Officer.
4. Having perused the record, this Court finds 
that the facts emerging on record raise serious 
questions regarding the authenticity and manner 
of execution of the alleged gift deed. Since Dinesh 
Chandra had already expired on 24.12.2024, the 
execution of the gift deed dated 20.12.2025, 
purportedly by him, prima facie indicates that 
some other person may have impersonated 
Dinesh Chandra and executed the said 
document. The role of the persons who were 
present at and attested the execution of the 
registered gift deed, particularly the two witnesses 
who purportedly identified the executant as 
Dinesh Chandra, also requires proper 
investigation. It is further significant that the 
alleged gift deed is a registered document and 
was executed before the Sub-Registrar, 
Dehradun. The process of registration ordinarily 
involves taking photographs of the executant at 
the time of registration. The Investigating Officer 
ought to have collected the relevant registration 
record, including the photograph(s) taken at the 
office of the Sub-Registrar, and compared the 
same with the photograph of Dinesh Chandra 
available on the registered gift deed and/or with 
other authenticated photographs or identity 
documents of the deceased. The failure to 
undertake such basic and material investigative 
steps, coupled with the fact that no statement 
has yet been recorded, prima facie reflects a 
serious lapse in the investigation.
5. Learned State Counsel is directed to file 
counter affidavit, within a period of two weeks.
