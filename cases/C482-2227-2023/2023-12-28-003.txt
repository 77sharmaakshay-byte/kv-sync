COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
Compounding Appl. No. 1 of 2023
In
C482 No. 2227 of 2023
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Lalit Sharma, learned counsel 
through V.C. and Mr. Bharat Chaudhary, 
learned counsel for the applicant.
2. Mr. Saurabh Pandey, learned Brief 
Holder for the State.
3. Mr. Naveen Singh Bisht, learned 
counsel for the complainant.
4. The present application, preferred 
under Section 482 Cr.P.C., is moved along 
with the compounding application which is 
supported with the affidavits of the 
applicant and the complainant-respondent 
no. 2.
5. The brief facts of the case are that 
FIR was lodged by respondent no. 2 who 
was having power of attorney of one Sm.
Prabha Rani in which after investigation
final report has been filed on 03.06.2011,
wherein it is mentioned that Smt. Prabha 
Rani is not the owner of the property in 
question. After filing of the final report, 
protest petition was filed by respondent 
no. 2 which was allowed and the matter 
was sent back for further investigation and 
after further investigation charge sheet 
was filed against the applicant for the 
offence punishable under Sections 420, 
467, 468, 471 and 120B IPC wherein 
cognizance was taken. 
6. Now C482 application has been 
moved along with the compounding application with this contention that only 
due to some misunderstanding the FIR 
was lodged by respondent no. 2 and now 
both the parties have settled their dispute 
amicably, and, since both the applicant 
and respondent no. 2 have settled their 
dispute, therefore, continuation of the 
proceeding is nothing but a would be a 
futile exercise.
7. Parties are present in the Court today 
and they are identified by their respective 
counsel. Xerox copies of the Aadhar card 
signed by the parties themselves and 
attested by their respective learned 
counsel are filed and kept on record. The 
applicant, particularly respondent no. 2 on 
interaction submits that they have
resolved their dispute amicably and 
respondent no. 2 does not want to pursue 
this proceeding.
8. Learned counsel for the applicant 
submits that in view of the law laid down 
in the case of Nikhil Merchant v. CBI 
and another, (2008) 9 SCC 650 as well 
as in the case of Gian Singh v. State of 
Punjab and another, (2013) 1 SCC 
(Cri) 160, the offence may be 
compounded.
9. Mr. Saurabh Pandey, learned Brief 
Holder for the State submits the offences 
punishable under sections 467, 468, 471 
and 120B IPC are non compoundable but 
he submits that since the dispute relates 
to some property and now the parties 
have settled their dispute, therefore, 
continuation of this proceeding appears to 
be a futile exercise in view of their 
settlement. He further submits that in 
view of the law laid down in Gian 
Singh(supra) the offences may be 
compounded.
10. After hearing the arguments of the learned counsel for the parties and learned 
Brief Holder for the State, this Court is of 
the view that the compounding application 
deserves to be allowed.
11. The compounding application is 
allowed. The proceedings of Criminal Case 
No. 3512 of 2022, State vs. Praveen 
Chhetri & others, in respect of offences 
punishable under Sections 420, 467, 468, 
471 and 120B IPC, pending in the court of 
Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate, 1st, 
Dehradun are hereby quashed in respect 
of the applicant.
12. The C482 application is disposed of 
finally.
13. Pending application(s), if any, also 
stands disposed of.
(Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
