Having heard the learned counsel 
for the petitioners, it emerges that the 
respondent nos.5 to 8 (private 
respondents) have been entrusted the 
charge to work as Assistant Regional 
Inspectors (Technical) in their 
respective offices. They do not posses 
any qualification to work as such. They 
have been entrusted to work as such 
Inspector in addition to their original 
work, as has been shown in column 
no.3 of the annexure no.1, which is the 
order passed by the Transport 
Commissioner on 17th January, 2014. 
It has been argued that as per the 
judgment of the Division Bench of this 
Court rendered in Special Appeal No.53 
of 2006, the working of such private 
respondents, as Inspectors afore-stated, 
is against the Uttar Pradesh Transport 
(Subordinate) Technical Service Rules, 
1980 and hence, the State Government 
was directed to ask the Public Service 
Commission for making direct 
recruitment on the posts of Such 
Inspectors. Accordingly, the Inspectors 
were recruited by the Commission and 
they were given postings. 
As emerging out from the facts, it 
appears that certain offices were still 
vacant and do not have any person to 
perform the duties of such Inspectors. 
So the State Government, by way of this 
impugned order, has asked these private respondents to work as such 
Inspectors. 
This writ petition has been filed seeking 
to quash such order of the Government 
dated 17th January, 2014.
In the facts, as have been 
highlighted above, the Court thinks it fit 
to admit the petition. Notices have 
already been received by the State 
counsel on behalf of respondent nos.1 to 
3 and by Shri B.D. Pande, Advocate on 
behalf of respondent no.4.
Issue notices to the respondent 
nos.5 to 8. 
List up this matter after four 
weeks. In the meantime, counter 
affidavit may be filed. 
Urgency application is, 
accordingly, disposed of.

In paragraph no.15 of the petition, 
the petitioner has quoted a precedent of 
Hon’ble the Apex Court for seeking 
favorable order. I think that this is 
against the Rule 28 of the Rules, which 
is equally applicable to all the 
pleadings. Besides, there is a 
fundamental principle of the pleadings 
that plead facts and not the law. The 
writ petition as well as stay application 
stands dismissed. 
Registry is directed to return the 
petition with liberty to the petitioner to 
take it back after removing the defects. 
Urgency application stands 
disposed of.

Having heard the learned counsel of 
the petitioner as well as State counsel, it 
appears that some First Information 
Report was lodged against one Shri 
Hridesh Mehra (contractor), who was 
awarded some construction work by the 
Forest Department. During investigation, 
the name of the petitioner Mr. Anand Ram 
emerged out indicating his implications in 
the matter to some extent. To make it 
clear, the Investigating Officer issued 
notice under Section 41 (1) of Code of 
Criminal Procedure, as amended w.e.f. 
01.11.2010, asking Mr. Anand Ram to 
appear before him for recording his 
statement. This notice was issued on 
29.09.2013, asking him to appear before 
the Investigating Officer on 01.10.2013 at 
11:00 a.m. in Police Station Ranikhet, 
District Almora, but since then he is 
defying his appearance. This notice was 
sent to him four months back. The Court 
cannot encourage such defiance on the 
part of Mr. Anand Ram. The petition is 
devoid of any merit and is hereby 
dismissed. However, it is made clear that 
Mr. Anand Ram shall put his appearance 
before the Investigating Officer forthwith, 
but if his arrest in the matter is needed by 
the Investigating Officer, then the same 
will be effected only after procuring the 
permission from his superiors and not 
otherwise.
Stay application stands rejected.
Urgency application is, accordingly, 
disposed of.
