Hon’ble Sharad Kumar Sharma, J.
This is a bunch of 21 writ petitions, which engages consideration of 
common question of facts and law but before this Court deals with the rival 6
contentions which has been raised by the counsel for the parties, I am of the 
view, that in each of the cases a precise factual backdrop is necessarily required 
to be considered in order to meet out the arguments which had been extended by 
the learned Addl. C.S.C. with regards to the interpretation assigned by him to 
the observations and the findings which has been recorded by the Hon’ble Apex 
Court in para 53 of the judgment of Uma Devi’s case which he intends to attract,
that it would create an embargo for the purposes of consideration of claim of the 
petitioners for grant of regular appointment, as against the post held by them 
respectively, from their initial appointment on daily wage basis on which they 
have worked for over more than two to three decades.
2. The factual details of each of the writ petitions are as under:-
3. In Writ Petition (S/S) of 910 of 2021 the petitioner was appointed 
with the respondents as a daily wager in Forest Department of Munsiyari Range, 
Forest Division, Pithoragarh way back in 1986. At the time when he was in 
service with respondents in the capacity of a Chaukidar, he was paid with the 
minimum of salary which was settled to be a consolidated amount of 
Rs.15,750/- till he attained the age of superannuation on 31.07.2019. The 
petitioner had contended, that for the purposes of grant of a regular status to him 
he had approached the writ courts earlier too by filing various writ petitions,
where the orders were passed to the department to consider the claim for grant 
of minimum of pay scale to the daily wager employees, who were working with 
the Forest Department, but in the absence of considering their claim for 
regularization, the petitioner contends, that he was constrained to file the present 
writ petition seeking his regularization on the ground, that as a consequence of 
the judgment rendered by the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of State of Uttar 
Pradesh vs. Putti Lal as reported in (2002) (2) UPLBC 1595, as decided by 
the Hon’ble Apex Court on 21.02.2002, in the concluding part of the said 
judgment, the principles laid down for the purposes of grant of regular status, to 
a daily wager employee working in the forest department, the same has been 
equally made applicable to those daily wagers working in the Forest 
Department, who after the division of the State were working within the 
territorial jurisdiction of State of Uttarakhand. 7
4. Exercising its powers under the principles laid down by the said 
judgment of Putti Lal, the State has framed the Rules called as “Uttaranchal 
Forest Department Regularization (on Group ‘D’ Post) of Daily Wages 
Appointment Rules 2003 and particularly the stipulations which has been 
provided therein requiring to be necessarily considered for the purposes of grant 
of regular status to the daily wager employees are the parameters, which has 
been provided under Rule 4 of the said Rules, which is extracted hereunder:-
“4. (1) Any Person Who:-
(a) was directly appointed on daily wages basis on a Group “D” 
post in the Forest Department under Government 
service before June 29, 1991 and is continuing services as 
such on the date of commencement of these rules; and 
(b) possessed requisite qualification prescribed for regular 
appointment for that post at the time of such appointment on 
daily wage basis under the relevant service shall be 
considered for regular appointment in permanent or 
temporary vacancy, as may be available in the Forest 
Department in Group “D” post, on the date of 
commencement of these rules on the basis of his record and 
suitability before any regular appointment is made in such 
vacancy in accordance with the relevant service rules or 
orders.
(2) In making regular appointments under these rules, 
reservations for the candidate belonging to the Scheduled 
Caste, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes of citizens 
and other categories shall be made in accordance with the 
Uttar Pradesh Public Services (Reservation for Scheduled 
Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes) Act 
(Uttaranchal Adaptation and Modification order 2001, and 
the Uttar Pradesh Public Services (Reservation for physical 
Handicapped, Dependents of freedom fighters and Ex￾Serviceman) Act, 1993 amended from time to time and the 
orders of the Government in force at the time of 
regularization under these rules and 
Note:- In this connection the provisions of reservation shall be 
fixed vide Uttaranchal Govt. Karmik Department Dehradun’s 
letter No.1144/Karmik-2-2001/53(1)/2001, dated 18.07.2001 
and 1415/Karmik-2/2001, dated 30.08.2001 for the state of 
Uttaranchal.”
5. In fact, if Sub Clause (a) of Sub Rule (1) of Rule 4 of the Rules of 
2003, is taken into consideration, it has rather stipulated that all those employees 
who were appointed on daily wage basis as Group ‘D’, employee with the Forest 8
Department and they were in service before the cut off provided therein i.e. 
29.06.1991 and are continuing in the services on the date of commencement of 
this Rule, that is 19.02.2003, they would be considered for regularization of their 
services, based on the stipulations provided under Clause (b) of the Sub Rule (1) 
of Rule 4 of the Rules of 2003 as extracted above.
6. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.2294 of 2018, the petitioner herein has 
come up with the case before this Court, that he was initially engaged as daily 
wager in the Forest Department in October 1984 and was appointed at Saini 
Lisa Depot and after having worked satisfactorily with the respondents, he had 
attained the age of superannuation on 31.10.2017. The petitioner has contended 
in his writ petition that when his claim for regularization was not being 
considered despite of having been appointed with the respondents as a daily 
wager ever since 1984, he had earlier filed a writ petition before the Allahabad 
High Court being Writ Petition (S/S) No.15687 of 1988, alongwith other 
similarly situated employees and the High Court of Judicature at Allahabad had 
allowed the writ petition by the judgment of 25.02.1993 alongwith yet another 
Writ Petition (S/S) No.450535 of 1993, as decided on 26.10.1993 whereby 
both the writ petitions were allowed, with an observation, that the daily wagers
who were working in the Forest Department were entitled to be paid with the 
minimum of pay scale as admissible to that of a regularly appointed employee 
working in the Department and simultaneously they were also held to be entitled 
for the payment of Dearness Allowance etc. 
7. These two judgments of the Coordinate Bench of Allahabad High 
Court were put to challenge in Special Leave Petition, before the Hon’ble Apex 
Court and the same was dismissed by the judgment of 01.10.1994 and thereby 
affirming the entitlement of the daily wager thus appointed, who were 375 daily 
wager in number, to be entitled for the minimum of pay scale and to be 
considered for regularization, after formulation of the scheme for regularization 
of these employees. 
8. The petitioner has contended that under an identical set of 
circumstances there was yet another writ petition which was preferred before 
this Court being Writ Petition (S/S) No.843 of 2003, which was disposed of by 9
the Coordinate Bench of this Court on 24.07.2008, thereby granting the retiral 
benefits and the employees who had rendered their services for more than 10 
years, in the Department were determined to be granted regular status, and the 
said judgment of the Coordinate Bench dated 24.07.2008 on being challenged in 
a Special Appeal No.225 of 2008, has been affirmed with the dismissal of the 
Special Appeal by the judgment of 27.04.2020. 
9. In the third set of litigation which also constitutes as to be the part 
of the pleadings of the present writ petition i.e. Writ Petition (S/S) No.1293 of 
2003, was finally decided by this Court on 17.10.2011 and as a consequence 
thereto, the petitioners of those writ petitions, who were directed to be paid the 
minimum of wages and the Dearness Allowances as it was made payable on the 
same. But still despite of having remitted the minimum of pay scale, the 
petitioner was not regularized into the services till he attained his age of 
superannuation on 31.10.2017. Consequently, he had preferred the present writ 
petition praying for issuance of a writ of mandamus to the authorities to pay the 
Dearness Allowance and other additional retiral benefits, which he would have 
been otherwise entitled, had his services been regularized under the 
Regularization Rules, as applicable or under the scheme framed as a 
consequence of the judgment of the Hon’ble Apex Court rendered on 
01.10.1994.
10. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.866 of 2020, the factual backdrop is that 
the petitioner has come up with the case that he was appointed with the 
respondents as daily wager in 1976 and later on his services were placed in the 
work charge establishment in September 1980 and thereafter his services were 
even regularized on 10.07.2003. But, despite of having rendered the services 
after regularization till he attained the age of superannuation on 31.01.2016, the 
respondents had not paid the retiral benefits to the petitioner though he contends 
that his claim would be falling for consideration under the principles laid down 
by the Hon’ble Apex Court in Prem Singh’s case as decided by the Hon’ble 
Apex Court on 01.09.2019.
11. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.867 of 2020, the petitioner had 
contended and has placed on record almost an identical plea to the effect that he 10
after having been appointed as a daily wager as Forest Guard on Seasonal basis 
in the year 1986, the petitioner was allocated to discharge his duties in Fatehpur 
Range and while working in the said capacity, he had attained the age of 
superannuation in November 2018 and has retired as a Forest Guard. He 
contends, that since he has rendered the services for a period more than 10 years 
his claim for regularization deserves to be considered even as per the principles 
which has been laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court in Uma Devi’s case; to be 
read with the judgment of State of Uttar Pradesh and others vs. Prem Singh. 
12. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.869 of 2020 factually the case of the 
petitioner is that looking to the work exigency, the petitioner was appointed as 
daily rated employee way back in the year 1977, he was later on placed in a 
work charge establishment in September 1980 and thereafter his services were 
even regularized on 10.07.2003, and since having worked for over more than 22 
years of continuous service till the date of his regularization of his services, yet 
even after having attained the age of superannuation on 31.05.2016, the 
petitioner was denied to be paid with the retiral benefits on the ground, that the 
requisite period of service required to be considered for the payment of retiral 
benefits that cannot be determined because his date of regularization happens to 
be of 10.07.2003, he would not be entitled for the retiral benefits. Hence he has 
filed this writ petition praying for a writ of mandamus to the respondents to 
release the pension, arrears of gratuity, leave encashment and other retiral 
admissible dues to the petitioner after taking into consideration the services 
discharged by him after he being placed in the work charge establishment in the 
September 1980. 
13. The petitioner of Writ Petition (S/S) No.1093 of 2020 Bimla Devi, 
the widow of her Late husband Gopal Singh, who was appointed with the 
respondents as ‘Shramik’ in the year 1986. He met with the sad demise on 
01.11.2019 and it is contended by the petitioner that the Late husband of the 
petitioner Mr. Gopal Singh, had preferred a writ petition being Writ Petition 
(S/S) No.675 of 2015 and as a consequence of the judgment rendered in Writ 
Petition on 23.03.2017, in compliance of thereto his services were regularized in 
Almora, Forest Division till he met with the sad demise on 01.11.2019. Hence, 
the widow of the late employee contends, that since his services were 11
regularized as a consequence of the judgment rendered by this Court on 
23.07.2017, she would be entitled for the payment of the family pension, as it 
would have been otherwise be payable having considered the 33 years of 
continuous service, rendered by her late husband from the date of initial 
appointment made in the year 1986 and hence, she has prayed for that the 
respondents may be directed to sanction the family pension and the arrears as a 
consequence thereto and also has claimed for consideration of her claim for 
compassionate appointment under the Dying-in-Harness Rules of 1974. 
14. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.1098 of 2020 the petitioner was 
appointed with the respondents as back as on 01.09.1978 as a ‘Shramik’ and he 
had contended that in terms of the Government Order, which was then issued by 
the State Government i.e. dated 01.07.1989, the employees who have 
continuously worked in the Department for a period of 10 years and above, they 
were supposed to be considered for the grant of regular status and since the 
petitioner contended that till the time of issuance of Government, he has already 
completed 20 years of service, he was entitled to be granted the benefit of said 
Government Order, apart from it that he would be falling within the zone of 
consideration for regularization as per the cut off date provided under the Rules 
of 2003, which has been framed by the State of Uttarakhand in the light of the 
judgment of Putti Lal’s case; but the respondents still despite of the aforesaid 
fact, they have not considered the claim of regularization and ultimately the 
petitioner attained the age of superannuation in November 2017.
15. The petitioner to the present writ petition has also come up with 
the case that under a similar set of circumstances, there had been a writ petition 
being Writ Petition (S/S) No.843 of 2003, which was preferred by some of the 
employees who have rendered the services for a period of more than 10 years 
and they have claimed for grant of retiral benefits, which was disposed of by the 
Coordinate Bench of this Court vide its judgment dated 24.07.2008. The said 
judgment on a challenge being given in Special Appeal No.225 of 2008 was 
dismissed by the judgment of 27.04.2010 by the Division Bench of this Court 
but still despite of the aforesaid judgments rendered in his favour coupled with 
the fact that the principles laid down in the judgment of Prem Singh’s case 
reported in (2019) 10 Supreme Court Cases 516 was applicable. The petitioner 12
was not extended with the retiral benefits though despite of the fact, that he had 
attained the age of superannuation from the respondents department in 
November 2017.
16. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.1138 of 2020 the petitioner had come up 
with the case, that the petitioner was appointed with the respondents as a daily 
wager employee ‘Shramik’ in the year July 1979 and after having being 
appointed in the said capacity, since his claim for regularization was not being 
considered, the petitioner alongwith other 144 similarly placed employees had 
preferred the Writ Petition (S/S) No.15627 of 1988, the writ petition as allowed 
by the Allahabad High Court vide its judgment dated 25.02.1993 and similarly 
as per the directions issued therein it was directed the State Government would 
consider the regularization of the petitioner by framing a scheme within a period 
of six months from the date of the judgment. Against the said judgment, the SLP 
preferred before the Hon’ble Apex Court, was also dismissed on 01.10.1994 and 
hence the petitioner contends that he would be entitled to be granted and given 
with the same treatment as that it had been meeted out to the other employees of 
the Writ Petition (S/S) No.15627 of 1988, by the respondents by the payment 
of minimum of salary and the Dearness Allowance and other service benefits 
after treating his services as to be regularly appointed with the respondents 
department. 
17. In this case the petitioner has further elaborated his contention in 
support of his relief claimed in the writ petition, that though he had attained the 
age of superannuation on 31.07.2018, but he submitted that an issue had been 
regularly creeping up before the Court and in one of the writ petition, being 
Writ Petition (S/S) No.687 of 2015. The claim for regularization of the 
petitioner in the department was considered by the Coordinate Bench of the 
Court and the writ petition was finally disposed of directing the respondents to 
regularize the services of the petitioner and the Special Appeal preferred, as a 
consequence of the judgment dated 23.03.2017, had been dismissed on 
25.06.2018. As against the judgment of the Division Bench, the State preferred a 
SLP before the Hon’ble Apex Court. The same was also dismissed on 
06.09.2018; but still the petitioner’s claim was not considered till he attained the 
age of superannuation on 31.07.2018 The petitioner has submitted in this writ 13
petition, that if the wider principles laid down in the matter of Madan Mohan 
Chaudhary vs. State of Uttarakhand is taken into consideration, the principles 
which has been laid down by the Constitution Bench of the Hon’ble Apex Court,
as decided on 06.11.2011, it has been held that an identically placed employees 
in a Government Department, cannot be discriminated in the matters of grant of 
continuity of services and for the grant of consequential service benefits, may be 
their status was that of a temporary employee having been engaged with the 
respondent-department and after having been placed in the work charge 
establishment, they would too be entitled to be granted with the regular status 
and the consequential service benefits including retiral benefits in the light of the 
principles, which has been subsequently laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court 
in Civil Appeal No.6798 of 2019 in Prem Singh’s case.
18. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.1146 of 2020 the petitioner has come up 
with the case in the writ petition, that the petitioner was inducted initially in the 
department of the respondents as a daily rated employee in 1966. He had 
worked for continuous 40 years, in the department till his services were 
regularized on 28.01.2004. Even after having being regularized into the services 
as Group ‘D’ employee he was still not extended the retiral benefits and the 
pensionary benefits, which would have been payable to him after including,
therein the period of service which was rendered by him with the respondents,
but the same was not remitted even after his attainment of age of superannuation 
on 31.07.2008. The petitioner submitted that when despite of the consistent 
claim raised by him for the payment of gratuity amount and the other retiral 
benefits the same was not paid, the petitioner had initiated the proceedings under 
the Payment of Gratuity Act by way of Case No.PGA 20 of 2018, Shri Khyali 
Ram Joshi vs. Divisional Forest Officer Civil Soil Conservation Forest 
Division, Ranikhet and the Controlling Authority, thus constituted under the 
Payment of Wages Act had allowed the application and directed the respondent 
no.3 to release the gratuity in favour of the petitioner and as a consequence 
thereto of the effect of the judgment of 30.02.2019, the controlling authority 
under the Payment of Gratuity Act, issued a recovery certificate against the 
Forest Department and ultimately as a consequence of issuance of recovery 
certificate they have deposited a sum of Rs.3,1,980/- on 27.06.2019, which was 
ultimately remitted to the petitioner. Petitioner submits that apart from the 14
gratuity amount, which was paid in pursuance to the judgment of 27.06.2019,
when the other retiral benefits for example pension and arrears payable on it was 
not remitted to him, he has raised his grievances before the Divisional Forest 
Officer, Ranikhet on 24.06.2020 but it was of no avail, hence the present writ 
petition. 
19. In Writ Petition (S/S) 1363 of 2020, the petitioner’s case is that he 
was appointed with the respondents on 01.01.1987 as a daily rated employee and 
was posted at Range Officer, Office Baijnath and he too contended that he was 
one of the petitioner’s of Writ Petition (S/S) No.15627 of 1988, preferred 
before the Allahabad High Court which was ultimately allowed with direction to 
the respondents to pay minimum of pay scale and to frame a scheme for 
regularization of such similarly placed daily wagers, and the said judgment of 
the Allahabad High Court, as rendered on 25.02.1993 and 26.10.1993 in the two 
respective writ petitions, which were decided by the Allahabad High Court had 
been affirmed by the Hon’ble Apex Court with the dismissal of the SLP of the 
State, on 01.10.1994. The petitioner has contended, that similarly there had 
been other matters too, which were decided even by this Court, but still despite 
of having rendered continuous 33 years of service with the respondents, despite 
of the fact that he was holding the requisite qualification for the post on which 
he has worked, in exclusion to the consideration of his claim for the grant of 
retiral benefits entitling him to be regularized under the Rules of 2003 when he 
was not extended the benefit, hence he has preferred the present writ petition for 
issuance of a writ of mandamus for payment of retiral benefits and other dues 
after determining the same from the date of his retirement i.e. 31.05.2020.
20. In Writ Petition (S/S) No.1364 of 2020 the petitioner had come up 
with the case that she was engaged as daily wager with the respondents and she 
too was posted in Baijnath Range, as back as on 01.09.1982 and she too had 
similarly pursued the matter as that in the case of Dhyan Singh, in Writ Petition 
(S/S) No.1363 of 2020 and being anguished due to the non remittance of her 
retiral benefits, as it has been claimed by her in the light of the judgment 
rendered in Putti Lal’s case; as well as in the Prem Singh’s case; she has 
additionally submitted, that there was yet another phase of recent litigation, 
which was decided by the Coordinate Bench of this Court in Prakashi Lal’s 15
matter and the said judgment of Coordinate Bench, was also put to challenge in 
SLP No.571 of 2019 State of Uttarakhand and others vs. Prakashi Lal, the 
same was dismissed by the Hon’ble Apex Court, on the basis of the judgment of 
Hon’ble Supreme Court of Prem Singh’s case and hence she has contended that 
after having superannuated on 31.12.2015; her entire tenure of service right 
from 01.09.1980, till date of attainment of her superannuation is required to be 
considered for the purpose of grant of regular status and the retiral benefits, as 
would have been otherwise admissible to be payable to her since having being 
appointed prior to cut off provided under the Rules of 2003 as framed in 
pursuance to the directions issued by the Hon’ble Apex Court.
21. The petitioner of Writ Petition (S/S) No.1366 of 2020 had

31. The above bunch of writ petitions after having heard the learned
counsel for the parties, I am of the view that looking to the judicial precedents as

particularly the principles as enunciated in the Habib Khan’s judgment rendered

above. As a consequence of the recommendations made by the judgment of

Rules called as the Uttarakhand Forest Department Regularization, Rules and as 
already extracted above, Rule 4 contemplates a cut off period, which has to be 
reckoned and taken into consideration i.e. 29.06.1991, for the purposes of 
granting a regular status to the employees, if they were in service on the date of

adopted by this Court for the purposes of considering the case of the petitioner, 
factually in all the cases, firstly all the petitioners have been appointed prior to 
29.07.1991. Hence, in view of the basic intention of Sub Clause (A) of Sub Rule 
(1) of Rule 4, if they were satisfying the said conditions provided under Rule 4 
of Regularization, they ought to have been taken into consideration by the 
respondents by giving them a regular status and the consequential benefits if 
they had satisfied the conditions of Clause A of Sub Rule (1) of Rule 4.
32. But however, the counsel for the respondents i.e. Addl. C.S.C. has 
attempted to draw a distinction by referring to Sub Clause (b) of Sub Rule (1) of 
Rule 4, contending therewith that Sub Clause (a) of Rule 4, cannot be isolatedly

1. That the candidate who was expected to be considered for regularization

33. At this stage itself, this Court feels it to be apt to answer the first 
objection taken by the learned counsel for the respondents with regards to as to 
what impact would it carry for the purposes of considering the regularization of 
those employees, which stipulates a fulfilment of a condition of holding a 
requisite qualification. As already observed above in the respective cases, the 
petitioner as on the respective date of the appointment invariably in all the cases 22
are shown to have a Class VIIIth pass certificate with them. Thus, the 
qualification aspect has to be considered from the prospective, that it would be 
the qualification required under rules on the date of inductment into the services 
34. The learned counsel for the respondents in order to substantiate his 
initial objection of qualification by attracting Sub Clause (b) has not been able to 
substantiate his arguments, that under Rules of 2003, what was the qualification 
prescribed, which an employee appointed as a daily wager was required to be 
having on the date of his regularization. Hence, in the absence of there being an 
elaboration of his arguments pertaining to the qualification aspect, I am of the 
view that at the time when the petitioners were respectively introduced into the 
services by the competent authority even in the capacity of a daily wager, a 
presumption would be drawn, that the competent authority, who would be 
appointing them as a daily wager, at the relevant point of time must have 
considered this aspect as to what would have been the actual and requisite 
qualification required and it goes without saying, that their inductment even in 
the status of a daily wager and their continuance in service for decades together,
it must have had been done by the respondents, after having considered the 
aspect of their requisite qualification. 
35. There is another logic for the said reasoning, that the procurement 
of the requisite qualification for being appointed as a daily wager, no exception 
has been carved out under any statute or legislature at the induction level, which 
was prevalent at that relevant point of time and that is why the provisions for the 
purposes of regularization was directed to be framed under a scheme as per the 
directions issued by the Hon’ble Apex Court in Putti Lal’s judgment, as a 
consequence thereto, the Rules of 2003 had been framed. What is relevant to be 
considered here in the context of qualification particularly if Clause (b) is 
considered it uses the language “possess requisite qualification prescribed”
requisite qualification prescribed and under the normal terminology and as per 
the definition of the prescription provided under General Clauses Act, it would 
be read as a qualification, which is prescribed under the Rules. 
36. But unfortunately, the Rules of 2003 are silent with regards to the 
aspect, as to what would be the requisite qualification for an employee to be 23
considered for being appointed or regularized under the Rules of 2003. Hence,
particularly in the absence of there being a specific case made out by the 
respondents in relation to each and every individual petitioners, that he was not 
having the requisite qualification for appointment, the contention of the Addl. 
C.S.C. cannot be taken into consideration in the absence of there being specific 
pleading to the said effect in the counter affidavit, filed by them in some of the 
writ petitions. 
37. There is another reason, even in the worst situation, if it is 
presumed, that as against the respective date of appointment, which in some of 
the cases even relegates back to the year of 1966 even, and in most of the cases 
where the petitioners have already rendered more than three decades of service,
at this stage when they have attained their respective age of superannuation, the 
qualification would be treated as the qualification provided at the time of initial 
appointment, it would be presumed, that with the gradual process of service 
rendered by them in the department and which was admittedly derived by the 
respondents as class IV employee, the presumption goes, that they had requisite 
qualification by way of an experience too in order to enable them, to be 
continued with the services and to be granted a regular status under Rules of 
2003. 
38. There could be another aspect also and that has also to be 
considered, it is that if the nature of work in which the petitioners were 
respectively engaged for example, that of Labourer, Gate Keepers, Shramik 
Forest Guard etc., it is not entailing a dispensation of a skilled services, where 
this qualification which has been prescribed much later, has to be stringently 
construed for the purposes of considering the claim of regularization, which has 
been consistently postulated by the judgments rendered by the Division Bench 
of this Court directing to consider for regularization and after having considered 
their respective tenure of service rendered by them with the respondents. Hence, 
in this context the contention of the learned counsel for the respondents, so far it 
relates to the requirement of possession of the requisite qualification is not 
acceptable by this Court. 24
39. One more reason, which this Court feels it to be relevant, to 
consider is that our country is functioning in a democratic set of society where 
the welfare of citizens covered and as defined under Article 5 of the Constitution 
of India, the welfare concept plays a predominant role, as per the preamble of 
the Constitution. Welfare Scheme of the State or the welfare intention of 
constitution cannot be deceived or over ruled by the respondents by their overt 
act of deriving the services of the respective petitioners for a considerable long 
period and then taking a stand that the petitioners did not had the required 
qualification. Even if, it is presumed or accepted even that they were not 
qualified at the relevant time, when they were inducted into the services as a 
daily wager, it was and rather more incumbent on the respondents, that they 
should have first scrutinized the credentials of the respective petitioners, when 
they were inducted into the services and having not done, so now they cannot 
take the advantage of their own wrong, by way of a defence of qualification due 
to their own inaction of not having diligently discharge their responsibility,
while inducting the petitioners as daily wager in the department. 
40. The petitioners, if at all they were disqualified to be inducted as 
daily wager Class IV employee, on account of lack of qualification, the 
respondents were supposed to have taken an appropriate action, within an 
appropriate time of dispensing their services and having not done so and 
permitting them to continue to serve with the respondents persistently for more 
than three decades in most of the cases, now they cannot be permitted to take 
liberty and benefit of their own inaction, to deny the benefit to the petitioners for 
the grant of retiral benefits and regular status and that too by taking an 
advantage of their own follies, as this stand of the respondents would amount to 
be an unfair labour practice, on their part which at least a public agency, which 
has been statutorily created under an Act and the law as applicable in the State 
cannot be permitted to take that liberty to take the benefit of being in a dominant 
position, as against the poor masses, who had spent their life serving them, at 
their wishes and directions. 
41. The second argument, which has been raised by the counsel for the
respondents in opposition to the contention of the writ petitioners is that the 
petitioners would not be falling within the zone of consideration for 25
regularization, in the light of the embargo laid down in para 53 of the judgment 
of the Constitutional Bench of the Hon’ble Apex Court as reported in (2006) 4

Devi & others particularly the reference has been made to para 53 of the said 
judgment, which is extracted hereunder:-
“53. One aspect needs to be clarified. There may be cases where 
irregular appointments (not illegal appointments) as explained in 
S.V. Narayanappa (supra), R.N. Nanjundappa (supra), and B.N. 
Nagarajan (supra), and referred to in paragraph 15 above, of duly 
qualified persons in duly sanctioned vacant posts might have been 
made and the employees have continued to work for ten years or 
more but without the intervention of orders of courts or of 
tribunals. The question of regularization of the services of such 
employees may have to be considered on merits in the light of 
the principles settled by this Court in the cases above referred 
to and in the light of this judgment. In that context, the Union of 
India, the State Governments and their instrumentalities should take 
steps to regularize as a one time measure, the services of such 
irregularly appointed, who have worked for ten years or more in 
duly sanctioned posts but not under cover of orders of courts or of 
tribunals and should further ensure that regular recruitments are 
undertaken to fill those vacant sanctioned posts that require to be 
filled up, in cases where temporary employees or daily wagers are 
being now employed. The process must be set in motion within six
months from this date. We also clarify that regularization, if any

42. After having elaborately considered the argument extended by the 
learned Addl. C.S.C., in fact, para 53 could be read only in the context of the 
qualification of a person, which is required to be possessed by him to be 
employed at the induction level into the services and it would also be extended 
to be read, for the purposes that the regularization, in relation to those 
employees, who have completed 10 years of service, could still be considered 
provided there is a vacant post. If para 53 is read in its totality, it is not creating 
an absolute bar or an obstacle, that a regularization cannot be considered of 
those daily wager employees, who had been admittedly appointed by the 
respondents even on an irregular basis. It is a case of the respondents in the 
counter affidavit that the appointment of the petitioners had been irregular 
appointment. In that eventuality, to provide a regularity, even if it is presumed,26
that it is an irregular appointment, I am of the view, that in view of one of the 
aspects of qualification which has been dealt with above, the aspect pertaining to 
the sanctioned post which was also one of factors provided under para 53 of the 
judgment, extracted above, will not be attracted under the peculiar 
circumstances in the instant case for the reason being that the very continuance 
of services of the petitioners by the respondents, may be in the capacity of a 
daily wager for such a long tenure of service of three decades, it goes without 
saying that the work was perennial in nature and there was a work exigency 
available and existing with the respondents and hence, the presumption would 
be that they were working against the sanctioned posed, because in some of the 
cases as it has already been observed, that the respondents on their own volition 
had regularized the services of some of the daily wagers and yet have deprived 
the extension of service benefits even after having regularized their services. 
The very fact, that their services were regularized, it goes without saying, that 
the regularization must have been done by the respondents as against the 
sanctioned cadre strength and not otherwise. 
43. In that view of the matter and for the reasons, which has already 
been dealt with above, coupled with the principles, which has been laid down by 
the various judgment, I am of the view that all the writ petitions deserve to be 
allowed and the widows of the late employees, would still be entitled to be 
extended with the benefits of regularization and the consequential benefits i.e. 
the retiral benefits to them considering, the continuity of the services of their late 
husbands. These parameters could further be brought within the frame work as it 
has been provided under the various judgments rendered by the Hon’ble Apex 
Court and particularly some of them which were emanating from this Court 
itself, for example in a Division Bench judgment rendered by this Court as 
reported in [2011 (87) ALR 645], Madan Mohan Chaudhary vs. State of 
Uttaranchal and Others, the Division Bench of this Court in the matters of 
Madan Mohan Chaudhary vs. State of Uttaranchal and Others though it 
related to the Irrigation Department, had widely laid down the principles, under 
which circumstances the grant of regular status to a daily wager employee, has 
to be taken into consideration for the purposes of extension of benefit of 
regularization and the consequential monetary benefits accruing to them. If the 27
said judgment is considered and particularly the observations, which has been 
made in para 5 of the judgment, which has been extracted hereunder:-
“5) Before further discussion we think it just and proper to quote 
here Article 358(a) of the Civil Service Regulations (for short 
CSR), which reads as under: 
“358. (a) Except for Compensation gratuity, an officer’s service 
does not in the case of Superior and Inferior services qualify till he 
has completed twenty years of service.” 
Article 361 of CSR provides as under: 
“361. The service of an officer does not qualify for pension unless 
it conforms to the following three conditions:-
First – The service must be under Government. 
Second – The employment must be substantive and permanent. 
Third – The service must be paid by Government 
[These three conditions are fully explained in the following 
Section.]” The condition indicated in Article 361 of the CSR has 
been explained in the following Articles:
Articles 362 to 367 elucidates the condition No. 1 of Article 361 of 
the CSR. Similarly, Article 368 to 384 of the CSR elucidates the 
second condition of Articles 361 and Articles 385 to 394 relates to 
condition No. 3 of Article 361 of the CSR. 
Next relevant provision is contained in Article 368 of CSR. The 
same reads as under:-
“368. Service does not qualify unless the officer holds a substantive 
office on a permanent establishment.” 
Now we come to other relevant Articles of CSR. Article 370 of 
CSR reads as under: 
“370. Continuous temporary or officiating service under the 
Government of Uttar Pradesh 9 followed without interruption by 
confirmation in the same or any other post shall qualify except –
i) periods of temporary or officiating service in a non￾pensionable establishment, 
ii) periods of service in a work-charged establishment, and 
iii) periods of service in a post paid from contingencies.”
As such, Clause (ii) of Article 370 of CSR clearly provides that no 
period of service rendered in workcharge establishment is to be 
counted towards service to qualify the period of service for the 
purpose of pensionary benefits. 
A perusal of Article 361 read with Article 368 and 370 of the CSR 
clearly indicates that the service does not qualify unless the officer 
holds a substantive office on a permanent establishment and that 
the period of service in a workcharged establishment will not 
qualify service for the purpose of pension. The underlying reason is 
that a workcharged employee is not holding a substantive post on a 
permanent establishment. 
Another relevant Article of CSR is Article 465. This Article reads 
as under: 28
“465(1). A retiring pension is granted to a government servant who 
is permitted to retire after completing qualifying service for twenty￾five years or on attaining the age of fifty years. 
(2) A retiring pension is also granted to a government servant who 
is required by Government to retire after completing twenty-five 
years or more of qualifying service.”
Article 465 applies to those who are seeking voluntary retirement 
or compulsory retirement, and similar provision is contained in 
Fundamental Rule 56.”
44. The same has been yet again considered as to how the term 
“sustentative and permanent appointment”, has to be construed under the given 
set of circumstances in the light of the provisions provided under the Civil 
Services Regularization. In fact the wider principles though it was in relation to 
the Civil Services Regularization, but the principles which has been laid down 
as it has been provided in para 8, 9 and 10 of the said judgment would be 
applicable in the present writ petitions too, which is extracted hereunder:-
“8) The genesis of receiving a pension is indicated in Article 361 
of the CSR. One such condition is that the employment must be 
substantive and permanent which is reiterated in Article 368 of the 
CSR. Article 370(ii) excludes periods of service spent in a 
workcharged establishment for the purpose of calculating the 
qualifying service. 
The Government Order dated 01.07.1989 talks about temporary 
employees in a Government Service retiring without being made 
permanent, and are therefore not getting pensionary benefits in 
view of 12 Article 368 of the CSR, which requires an employee to 
hold a permanent post. Para 2 of the aforesaid G.O. indicates that 
such Government employee, namely, temporary employees, who 
have worked for a minimum period of 10 years in a regular service, 
would be given pensionary benefits in the same manner as given to 
a permanent employee. A temporary employee, even though 
temporary is working on a substantive post, though not permanent. 
In this light, the Government thought fit to include temporary 
employees for the purpose of receiving pensionary benefits. A 
workcharged employee is not working on a substantive post and is 
specifically excluded under clause (ii) of Article 370 of the CSR. 
Consequently, the period rendered in a workcharged establishment 
cannot be included for claiming pension. Sub Rule (8) of Rule 3 of 
the U.P. Retirement Benefit Rules, 1961, supports this view. Said 
sub Rule defines qualifying service with the note that if a person 
serves in a pensionable job, then in work-charge establishment, and 
again there after in regular service, such interruption would not be 
disqualification. Similar provision is contained in Article 422 of the 
CSR. 29
9) On behalf of the writ petitioner / respondent reference is 
made to Rule 2 of Temporary 13 Government Servant (Termination 
of Service) Rules 1975, which defines ‘temporary service’. 
According to said Rule 2, ‘temporary service’ means officiating or 
substantive service on a temporary post or officiating service on a 
permanent post under the Uttar Pradesh Government. These Rules 
of 1975, are not applicable to the work-charged employees. Clause 
(d) of Rule 4 of Temporary Government Servant (Termination of 
Service) Rules 1975, provides that these rules are not applicable to 
the employees serving in a work-charge establishment. In our 
opinion, service rendered in work-charge establishment, before 
regularization is not a temporary service for the purposes of regular 
service. It is relevant to mention here that without there being a 
post, a person cannot hold it either as a temporary employee or 
permanent employee. In Para 4 of State of Himachal Pradesh Vs. 
Suresh Kumer (1996) 8 Supreme Court Cases 562, it is observed by 
the Apex court that work-charge employees perform the duties of 
transitory and urgent nature so long as the work exists (in a 
particular project). In our opinion, only because a work-charge 
employee was engaged in one after another projects does not make 
his services regular without there being a permanent post. 
10) In State of Mysore Vs. S. V. Narayanappa, A.I.R. 1967 S.C. 
1071 : (1967) 1 SCR 128, the Apex 14 court has held that the word 
‘Regular’ or ‘Regularization’ does not mean ‘permanence’. Even 
after regularization, confirmation may be needed in the service. 
This view was again reiterated by the Apex court in B.N. Nagrajan 
Vs. State of Karnataka, A.I.R. 1979 S.C. 1676 : (1979) 4 S.C.C. 
507. That being so, in our opinion, word ‘NIYAMIT’ (regular) used 
in the Government Order dated 01.07.1989 only refers to the 
temporary employees in regular service, yet to be confirmed.”

6. The pari materia provision contained in Rule 3.17(ii) of the
