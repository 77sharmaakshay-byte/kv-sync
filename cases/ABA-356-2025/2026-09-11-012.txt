Hon'ble Alok Mahra, J.
Mr. Sandeep Tiwari and Mr. Yogesh 
Kumar Sharma, learned counsel for the 
applicants.
2. Mr. Pramod Tiwari, learned A.G.A. for 
the State.
3. Mr. Nikhil Singhal, learned counsel 
for complainant/applicant.
4. By means of the present application, 
the applicants seek anticipatory bail in 
connection with F.I.R./Case Crime No. 0155 
of 2025, dated 27.03.2025, registered at 
Police Station SIDCUL, District Haridwar, for 
the offences punishable under Sections 420, 
467 and 506 of I.P.C.
5. Learned counsel for the complainant 
would submit that a Coordinate Bench of 
this Court, vide order dated 16.04.2025, had 
granted anticipatory bail to the applicants. 
He would further submit that, in the said 
order, the Coordinate Bench had taken note 
of the statement made by the applicants in paragraph 35 of the anticipatory bail 
application, wherein they had undertaken to 
pay the entire amount of ₹2.05 crores, along 
with a further amount of ₹20 lakhs received 
in cash from the complainant, within a 
period of 15 days. It is submitted that, 
relying upon the affidavit and undertaking 
furnished by the applicants, the Coordinate 
Bench had granted interim protection to 
them, however, the applicants failed to 
honour the said undertaking.
6. Learned counsel for the complainant 
would further submit that the applicants 
thereafter filed a compliance affidavit on 
16.08.2025, wherein, in paragraph 5, they 
furnished a chart indicating the proposed 
schedule for repayment of the entire 
outstanding amount in six instalments; that,
as per the said schedule, the entire amount 
was to be repaid by 31.01.2026, however, 
despite the expiry of the said period, the 
applicants have failed to comply with the 
undertaking furnished before the Coordinate 
Bench as well as the undertaking contained 
in the compliance affidavit.
7. It is submitted that an amount of ₹1.40 
crores is still outstanding against the 
applicants, therefore, the applicants are not 
entitled to the discretionary relief of 
anticipatory bail, having failed to comply 
with the orders of this Court and the 
undertakings furnished by them. Learned 
counsel further submits that, on account of 
such non-compliance, the complainant has 
moved an application seeking cancellation of 
the anticipatory bail granted to the 
applicants.
8. Per contra, learned counsel for the applicants would submit that, owing to 
financial hardship, the unfortunate demise 
of the son of applicant nos. 1 and 2, and 
other unforeseen circumstances, the 
applicants could not arrange the requisite 
funds or dispose of their property for making 
payment to the complainant. He, therefore, 
seeks a further period of six months to clear 
the entire outstanding amount. He would 
further submit that applicant no. 2 is 
present before the Court and is ready to 
hand over a cheque of ₹15 lakhs to the 
complainant today itself, while the 
remaining outstanding amount shall be paid 
within a period of six months.
9. Learned counsel for the complainant 
would submit that the complainant is 
agreeable to accept the cheque of ₹15 lakhs 
today. He would further submit that a 
further amount of ₹25 lakhs may be paid 
within 15 days from today and, thereafter, 
five months' time may be granted to the 
applicants for payment of the remaining 
outstanding amount.
10. Learned counsel for the applicants does 
not dispute the aforesaid proposal put forth 
on behalf of the complainant. He has 
handed over a cheque of ₹15 lakhs to 
learned counsel for the complainant in 
Court today.
11. Heard learned counsel for the parties 
and perused the material available on 
record.
12. It is not disputed that the applicants 
have, on two occasions, failed to honour the 
undertakings furnished before this Court 
and have not complied with the orders 
passed by the Coordinate Bench and such conduct would disentitle the applicants from 
seeking further indulgence of this Court. 
However, considering the circumstances 
placed before the Court, particularly the 
unfortunate demise of the son of applicant 
nos. 1 and 2 and the consequent difficulty 
stated to have been faced by them in 
arranging the requisite funds through 
disposal of their property, and purely on 
humanitarian grounds, this Court considers 
it appropriate to grant one final opportunity 
to the applicants to clear the outstanding 
dues and honour the undertaking furnished 
before this Court.
13. Accordingly, as a last opportunity, the 
applicants shall pay a further amount of ₹25 
lakhs to the complainant on or before
12.10.2026. The applicants shall thereafter 
pay the remaining outstanding amount of ₹1 
crore in installments, in such a manner that 
the entire outstanding amount is cleared
within a period of six months from today. 
The applicants shall strictly adhere to the 
aforesaid schedule and shall ensure timely 
payment of each installment.
14. List this case on 14.10.2026 for placing 
on record the compliance of the aforesaid 
order.
15. It is made clear that the indulgence 
granted to the applicants by this Court is 
being extended as a last opportunity. 
(Alok Mahra, J.)
