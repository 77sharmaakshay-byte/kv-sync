4. Heard on Bail Application (IA No.1/2025).
5. Learned counsel for the appellant submits that the 
prosecution witnesses, primarily the relatives of the 
deceased, are interested witnesses, and their testimonies 
suffer from material contradictions and improvements. No 
independent witness has been examined to corroborate the 
allegations of dowry harassment, casting serious doubt on 
the prosecution’s version. He further submits that the 
appellant was on bail during the trial and never misused 
the liberty granted to him.
6. Per contra, learned State Counsel opposed the bail 
application on the ground that the deceased was subjected 2
to cruelty and died under unnatural circumstances within 
seven years of marriage. Furthermore, both PW1 and PW2, 
in their statements, confirmed that the deceased was 
harassed by the accused for dowry demands and that the 
accused beat the deceased on 10.08.2021 and 11.08.2021, 
just before her death.
7. Upon hearing the learned counsel for the appellant as 
well as the learned State Counsel and perusal of the 
material on record, this Court is of the considered view that 
the present case does not warrant the grant of bail at this 
stage. The allegations against the appellant are grave and 
serious in nature. The deceased is stated to have died under 
unnatural circumstances within seven years of marriage, 
attracting the presumption under Section 113-B of the 
Indian Evidence Act. The statements of prosecution 
witnesses PW1 and PW2, though related to the deceased, 
are consistent and specific with regard to the harassment 
faced by the deceased at the hands of the accused in 
connection with dowry demands. Accordingly, the bail 
application is dismissed at this stage.
8. List in the month of March, 2026 for final hearing.
