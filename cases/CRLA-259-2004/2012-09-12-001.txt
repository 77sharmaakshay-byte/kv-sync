Barin Ghosh, C.J. (Oral)
 A First Information Report was lodged by Tara Chand, who, 
in course of trial, deposed as PW1. This First Information Report was 
lodged on 9th December, 1992 at about 10:05 AM. In the Report, it 
was stated that there is a water outlet from the house of PW1, which 
was being blocked by Kali Ram on the previous morning, when PW1 
requested Kali Ram to see that sufficient space is left so that the water 
outlet is not altogether closed. On this, there was a quarrel between 
PW1 and Kali Ram and, on the next date at around 8:00 AM, ten 
people, armed with deadly weapons, came to the house of PW1, when 
PW1, his brother Virendra Singh, victim, and the son of PW1 Khem 
Singh, who deposed as PW3, were sitting. Those ten people and, in 
particular, appellant Ashok held out to PW1, PW3 and Virendra Singh 
that today they will be done away with. It was reported in the First 
Information Report that hearing the same, Virendra Singh, PW1 and 
PW3 tried to disperse from the place, but Ashok fired from his gun 
causing injury to Virendra Singh, which injury ultimately caused 
death of Virendra Singh. This First Information Report was 
investigated and, in course thereof, a licensed double barrel gun was 
recovered from the custody of the father of Ashok, Kali Ram. The 
same was sent for obtaining opinion of the Ballistic Expert along with 
the empty shells recovered from the place of incident. The dead body 
of Virendra Singh was also sent for post-mortem and the Post Mortem 
Report suggested that Virendra Singh received eight injuries, all 
caused by pellets, and 3rd, 5th and 6th injuries were blackened. The 
Investigating Officer, in course of investigation, also prepared a site 
plan of the place of occurrence, as also, of the genus of the 
occurrence. The Investigating Officer, in course of investigation, 
found that while Virendra died of ante-mortem injuries sustained by

him through pellets; PW1, Shukh Pal, who deposed as PW2, as well 
as PW3 along with one Shyam Singh, Mithlesh Singh, wife of Khem 
Singh, Khushi Ram, Suman, Raghubir, Vikram and many others also 
received injuries. After investigation was over, a charge sheet was 
filed and, on the basis thereof, charges were framed against 9 people, 
as in the meantime one of those ten people had died. After conclusion 
of the trial, six of those nine have been acquitted and remaining three, 
namely, Ashok, Anil and Pramod have been convicted. Ashok was 
charged for offences punishable under Section 302, Section 307 read 
with Section 34, and Section 452 of the Indian Penal Code. He has 
been convicted for offences punishable under the said sections. Anil 
and Pramod were charged for offences punishable under Section 302 
read with Section 34, Section 307 read with Section 34 and Section 
452 of IPC. They have also been convicted for offences punishable 
under the said sections. Appellants Ashok, Pramod and Anil have 
been appropriately sentenced. 
2. Being aggrieved by the conviction and sentence, Ashok, 
Pramod and Anil have preferred an independent appeal each. In view 
of acquittal of six accused people, the prosecution has also preferred 
an appeal. Having regard to the fact that the matters spring from the 
same incident, the appeals are considered and decided together. 
3. There is hardly any scope of interference with the 
Government Appeal, inasmuch as, in course of evidence tendered by 
the prosecution, no specific role was ascribed to any of those six 
accused people, who have been acquitted. We, accordingly, dismiss 
the Government Appeal. 
4. According to the appellants, the conviction is based only on 
the oral evidence given by PW1, PW2 and PW3. It is the contention 
of the appellants that in the background of the facts and circumstances 
of the case, the evidence, so tendered by PW1, PW2 and PW3, should 
not be believed. Learned Government Advocate submitted that the 
incident complained of occurred in the broad day light in the presence 
2012:UHC:48074
of eye witnesses and there is no just reason not to believe the eye 
witness accounts given by the eye witnesses in course of tendering 
evidence. 
5. It has been accepted by the prosecution through the 
witnesses, who deposed on behalf of prosecution, namely, PW1, PW2 
and PW3 that there were several pre-existing disputes inter se PW1 
and his family on the one hand and appellants Ashok, Pramod and 
Anil and their family on the other. In a situation of that nature, it is a 
requirement for the Court to scrutinize the evidence of alleged eye 
witnesses more cautiously and carefully. According to PW1, Kali 
Ram was constructing a wall in the morning of 8th December, 1992. 
According to him, the construction was such that the same would 
cause hindrance to the outflow of water from the out-let of the house 
of PW1. According to PW1, he asked Kali Ram to see that the 
construction do not cause complete blockage of outflow of water. 
According to PW1, when he had said so to Kali Ram, that ensued 
quarrel between him and Kali Ram. How and when the said quarrel 
came to an end and what was the impact of that quarrel has not been 
said by PW1. According to PW3, the wall was being raised with the 
assistance of masons. PW1 did not say presence of masons. The site 
plan prepared by the Investigating Officer and tendered in evidence by 
him suggests that a part of the land was dug up and then filled up. 
The site plan does not suggest construction of a wall by masons. 
According to the First Information Report, construction of the wall 
was being made. According to the evidence of PW1 and PW3, there 
was no resistance to the construction. Kali Ram, therefore, succeeded 
in his mission of constructing the wall. Whether the wall left passage 
of outlet of water or it completely blocked the same, was not sorted 
out in the morning of 8th December, 1992. In the circumstances, Kali 
Ram and his family would come on the next day at the house of PW1 
armed with deadly weapons for the purpose of causing death and 
injuries to the members of the family of PW1 somehow requires a 
pinch of salt for digestion. According to the First Information Report, 
victim Virendra Singh, his brother Tara Chand (PW1) and the son of 
2012:UHC:48075
Tara Chand, Khem Singh (PW3) were together, when the raiding 
party came yelling that they will be done away with and hearing that 
all the three tried to disperse and, at that time, Ashok fired, which 
injured Virendra Singh. In course of tendering evidence, PW1 stated 
that he and PW3 made the first motive of dispersing after Virendra 
Singh sustained gun shot injury. It is true that PW2 Sukh Pal, a 
neighbour of PW1, deposed in support of the prosecution. It is also 
true that PW2 sustained simple injury caused by firearm. Sukh Pal 
did not say, who caused that injury to him. At the same time, the 
prosecution held out that, while Ashok caused injury to Virendra, Anil 
caused injury to Mithilesh and Pramod caused injury to PW3. PW2 
was a neighbour of PW1. According to his evidence, he came to the 
spot after, at least, the first gunshot was fired. 10 people, who 
allegedly came to the house of PW1, came there to cause harm to 
PW1 and his family members; one of them allegedly fired, hearing the 
sound of the fire, PW2 came. He, thereafter, received injury. PW2, in 
course of his evidence, did not hold out, why he was targeted. 
According to PW1, Ashok was about 8-10 yards away from Virendra 
Singh at the time when Virendra Singh was shot at. The prosecution 
has not tried to establish before the court below that, if pellets are 
fired from a distance of 24 feet, the same can cause blackening by the 
side of the pellet injury. Except the licensed double barrel gun of the 
father of Ashok, no other firearm was seized. Anil said to have fired 
from a country made pistol and Pramod said to have fired from a gun, 
but none of them were seized. The seized double barrel gun was sent 
for obtaining opinion of the Ballistic Expert. The Ballistic Expert 
opined that, it is not possible to say, whether the double barrel gun 
sent to him was used in firing the cartridges also sent to him. Those 
cartridges were said to have been collected from the place of incident. 
6. PW1 Tara Chand was the brother of the victim. PW3 
Khem Singh was the nephew of the victim. PW1 and PW3 alongwith 
PW2 Sukh Pal sustained injuries at the time when victim was injured. 
Sukh Pal was the neighbour, whereas Tara Chand and Khem Singh 
2012:UHC:48076
were relatives of Virendra Singh, victim. There is no presumption 
that an injured person always speaks the truth. At the same time, the 
evidence of related people is required to be scrutinized closely. There 
is no dispute that there were pre-existing disputes between the parties. 
According to the prosecution story, the incident is not a fall out of 
previous enmity between the parties. According to the prosecution, 
the same was fall out of an incident, which had taken place on the 
previous morning. As aforesaid, the incident in question, which had 
taken place on the morning of 9th December, 1992, is in relation to 
construction of a wall. It is not the case of the prosecution that the 
wall was demolished. The Investigating Officer in his site plan could 
not locate any wall. It is, therefore, doubtful, whether in relation to 
construction of any wall, there was any altercation between PW1 and 
Kali Ram. Even if such altercation took place, how the same came to 
an end on 8th December, 1992 has not been attempted to be 
established. If Kali Ram succeeded in constructing the wall, why Kali 
Ram will be so enraged that he will organize, in addition to him, 9 
people to raid PW1 and his brother, has also not been attempted to be 
explained. The accused people were taken into custody. They were 
interrogated threadbare. Apart from the licensed double barrel gun, 
no other gun or other weapon was recovered. 
7. There cannot be any dispute that, if evidence of 
eyewitness is acceptable, on the strength thereof alone, conviction can 
be upheld, but it is obligatory on the part of the Court to scrutinize all 
and every evidence placed before it, including the evidence of the 
eyewitness, being totally unbiased. Because a person claims that he is 
witness to an incident, the Court has to believe the same, is not the 
rule of law. It is obligatory on the part of the Court to satisfy itself 
that yes, the person, who claims to be an eyewitness is, in fact, an 
eyewitness. In the instant case, if the Court attempted to discharge its 
said obligation, the conclusion would be that neither PW1, nor PW2, 
nor PW3 is an eyewitness to the incident accounted for by them 
before the Court. The motive or the raison d’etre for the incident has 
not been proved. When the eyewitnesses accounted for the motive / 
2012:UHC:48077
raison d’etre and failed to establish the same, the Court is duty bound 
to become suspicious about the alleged eyewitnesses. The reason for 
the incident, as above, is too weak to sustain the story, as has been 
made out by the prosecution, for the reasons already indicated above. 
According to the First Information Report, when PW1, PW3 and the 
victim started dispersing, Ashok fired. In course of evidence, PW1 
stated that he and PW3 started dispersing after victim was fired. 
Virendra Singh sustained firearm injuries, 3 of them were blackened. 
Ashok fired upon Virendra Singh from 24 feet away. Nothing was 
brought on record that a pellet wound, caused from 24 feet away, 
could create blackening by the side of the wound. The one and the 
only weapon produced, in course of trial, turned out to be a licensed 
gun and there is no certainty that the said gun was used for firing the 
cartridges, shells whereof were found from the place of incident. In 
the circumstances, while the court below has given benefit of doubt to 
the other 6 accused people and, accordingly, acquitted them, it was 
obligatory on the part of the court below, also, to give benefit of doubt 
to each of the appellants and to exonerate them. 
8. We, accordingly, allow the appeals, except Government 
Appeal, which stands dismissed; set aside the judgment under appeals 
and acquit the appellants of the charges framed against them. 
Appellants are on bail. They need not surrender. Their bail bonds are 
cancelled and sureties are discharged. 
9. Let a copy of this judgment be sent to the court below along 
with Lower Court Records.
