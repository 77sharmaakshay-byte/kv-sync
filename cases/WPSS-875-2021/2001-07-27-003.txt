 ORDER 
Hon’ble Sharad Kumar Sharma, J.
(Via Video Conferencing)
Before answering the arguments, which had been 
extended by the learned counsel for the parties to the Writ Petition, 
the brief facts are required to be considered, as are narrated in the 
present Writ Petitions, it is to the effect that the petitioners herein
have given a challenge to the impugned advertisement of 30th June, 
2021, as it had been issued by respondent No. 5, for the purposes of 
undertaking the process of selection on the post of Monitoring 
Assistant and Laboratory Assistant, which admittedly falls to be the 
posts, which are group-C post, as per the Rules called as “U.P. 
Pollution Control Board Service Rules, 1995”, hereinafter to be 
referred as the ‘Rules of 1995’. 2
2. The admitted case of the petitioners, as pleaded in the 
Writ Petitions are that with the sanctioning of the project by the 
Central Pollution Control Board, six air pollution monitoring stations 
were to be installed at different places in the State of Uttarakhand, as 
it had been sanctioned by the Government Order of 25th January, 
2008, and accordingly, for the purposes of operating and managing 
the affairs of those air quality monitoring stations, thus installed, the 
Uttarakhand Environment Prevention and Pollution Control Board; 
had organised a walk in interview and for undertaking the process of 
the same, the posts were advertised on 20th May, 2012, to be filled in 
on contractual basis. 
3. The petitioners contend that they responded to the said 
advertisement of 20th May, 2012, and they were ultimately granted an 
appointment on a contractual basis though initially for a period of one 
year, but later on, their services continued in the said status in terms,
of their terms of the appointment, which was settled between them as 
per the agreement executed between the Pollution Control Board and 
the petitioners, as provided in annexure-9 to the Writ Petition. 
4. At this stage, this Court is refraining itself to deal with 
the terms and conditions of the contract of appointment of the 
petitioners, because it may not be relevant to consider them at this 
stage. 
5. The petitioners have contended that their appointment 
which was made way back in 2012, as a consequence of the 
culmination of the process of selection on contractual basis; by 
issuance of a select list on 1st June, 2012, they have contended that the 
subsequent advertisement, which was made and impugned in the 
present Writ Petition, i.e. 13th June, 2021, proposing to make 
recruitment for 8 posts of Monitoring Assistant and 7 posts of 
Laboratory Assistant, the process, which has now been adopted by 
respondent No. 5, by introducing the written examination also along 3
with the interview, it has been argued that this is a process contrary to 
the provisions and procedure provided under the Rules of 1995. 
6. At this stage, this Court feels it to be necessary to make a 
reference to Rules 15 and 16 of the Rules, which contemplates two 
different processes of recruitment, i.e. by way of interview and by 
way of written examination, i.e. in both the circumstances by direct 
recruitment. 
7. The learned Senior Counsel for the petitioners, Mr. 
Rajendra Dobhal, had argued that the process of written examination, 
which is contemplated under Rule 16, if it is read in correlation with 
the implications of Rules 15, which also relates to the recruitments to 
be made on Group-C post, the implications of written examination 
contemplated under Rule 16, would only apply and would be attracted 
to those Group-C posts, where the written examination is 
contemplated under the Appendix, which has been made as part of the 
Rules of 1995. 
8. He submits that in accordance with the post, with which, 
we are concerned, which has been referred in Entry 89 to the 
appendix, i.e. the Monitoring Assistant, in its column 4, it has 
provided process of selection, to be by way of an interview and as 
such, the said process of selection would be governed by Rule 15 and 
not by Rule 16. 
9. In furtherance of his argument to substantiate that there is 
a distinction in the applicability of Rule 15 and 16, the learned Senior 
Advocate, had made reference to the Entry 26 of the appendix, which 
relates to yet again a Group-C post, where there are two sources of 
recruitment i.e. by way of direct recruitment, as well as by way of a 
promotion, wherein, the written examination is one of the processes 
contemplated to be resorted to for appointment by holding a written 
examination. In fact, the learned Senior Counsel, Mr. Rajendra 4
Dobhal, wants to submit that Rule 16, will only come into play in 
those circumstances where the written examination itself, is provided 
to fill in those Group –C post, where the written examination itself is 
included in the process of selection provided in the appendix. 
Otherwise, Rule 16 will not be applicable to be applied on those posts 
which are otherwise required to be filled in under Rule 15 by holding 
the process of selection, as he has argued in co-relation to his plea 
raised in the light of the Entry 89 of the appendix to the Rules of 
1995. 
10. On the other hand, the learned counsel for the respondent 
No. 5, had made reference to the Rules called as Uttarakhand (Outside 
the Purview of Uttarakhand Public Service Commission) Direct 
Recruitment of Group-C Post Rules, 2008. 
11. The Counsel for respondent No.5 has submitted that 
these Rules, since they have been framed under the proviso to Article 
309 of the Constitution of India, in view of Rule 2, it would be having 
an overriding effect to the Rules 1995. This argument of the learned 
counsel for respondent No.5, Mr. N.S. Pundir, is not acceptable by 
this Court, for the reason being that the Rules under Article 309 of the 
Constitution of India, governing the processes of recruitment for the 
posts which are lying outside the purview of the Public Service 
Commission, belongs to Group-C post, since being a general rule, it 
will not have an overriding effect particularly when the processes of 
recruitment in the department of the respondents is regulated by the 
specific set of Rules, framed under Sections 12 and 13 of the Pollution 
Control Act, and where the posts of Monitoring Assistant as per,
Entry 89, is to be filled in by walk in interview to be read with Rule 
15. 
12. Hence, this Court is tentatively of the view that the 
general rules will not prevail over the special rule of selection,5
particularly when the Rules itself provides the process and would have 
precedence over the general rules. 
13. When this matter was taken up on the previous occasion, 
the learned Counsel for the Pollution Control Board, Mr. Aditya 
Pratap Singh, was directed to complete the instructions, and as per the 
instructions, which he has received from the respondents, he has been 
instructed to submit before the Court, that in fact the post of 
Monitoring Assistant, is a post, which is required to be filled in, in 
accordance with the process as provided in the appendix, i.e. by way 
of resorting to the procedure of walk in interview. 
14. In these peculiar eventualities, the learned counsel for the 
petitioners, who has preferred these Writ Petitions for number of 
reliefs, which are extracted hereunder, has confined his relief so far it 
relates to giving challenge to the process of advertisement dated 30th
June, 2021 and for the time being, it is stated by learned Senior 
Advocate, Mr. Rajendra Dobhal, that he is not pressing, his relief 
No. ‘B’. 
“A. Issue a writ order or direction in the nature of 
certiorari quashing the advertisement dated 30.06.2021 
(Annexure No.1). 
B. Issue a writ order or direction in the nature of 
mandamus directing the respondents no.2 to consider the 
Petitioners against the said vacancies and to grant them Regular 
Appointment and the regular pay-scale admissible to the post 
with the Uttarakhand Environment and Pollution Control Board 
against the said Sanctioned Posts. 
C. Pass any other order that this Hon’ble Court may 
deem fit in the interest of Justice.”
15. Hence, relief ‘B’ to the Writ Petition, would be deemed 
to be not pressed by the petitioners. 6
16. While scrutinizing his propriety to the argument extended 
in relation to relief 1, and for the reason already given above, and 
particularly in view of the instructions, which has been parted by the 
counsel for the Pollution Control Board, that the post of Monitoring 
Assistant and Laboratory Assistant, they are to be governed by the 
procedure of selection which is provided under Appendix-2 to Rule of 
1995, i.e. by way of walk in interview, it is hereby, by way of an 
interim measure directed that the respondents No. 5, may proceed 
with the process of selection under the strength of the said 
advertisement of 30th June, 2021, but exclusively by way of resorting 
to the process provided under the Appendix to the Rule of 1995. 
However, the decision of the selection process may still be carried out 
and completed, but that would abide by the final decision of the Writ 
Petition. 
17. The respondents may file their counter affidavit within a 
period of three weeks from today. 
18. List these Writ Petitions as soon as the counter affidavit 
is filed by the respondents.
