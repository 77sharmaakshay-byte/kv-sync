Hon’ble Alok Mahra, J.
Mr. D.C.S. Rawat along with Mr.

learned counsel for the applicant.
2. Mr. Deepak Bisht, learned Deputy 
A.G. for the State.
3. Mr. Prince Chauhan, learned counsel 
for respondent no.2.
4. Present application has been filed 
seeking quashing of the impugned order 
dated 08.04.2025 passed by the learned 
District and Sessions Judge in Special 
Sessions Trial No. 51 of 2024, arising out 
of F.I.R./Case Crime No. 0204 of 2024, 
registered at Police Station Kotwali 
Kotdwar, District Pauri Garhwal, along 
with the Look Out Circular and 
consequential proceedings issued against 
the applicant.
5. Learned counsel for the applicant 
would submit that the applicant is 
aggrieved by the order dated 08.04.2025, 
whereby the applicant has been declared 
absconding and a standing arrest warrant 
has been issued against him. It is further 
submitted that the applicant has already 
filed WPCRL No. 1119 of 2024 challenging 
the aforesaid F.I.R./Case Crime. The 
Coordinate Bench of this Court, vide order 
dated 16.10.2024, after considering the 
facts and circumstances of the case, was 
pleased to grant interim protection to the 
applicant with a direction that during the 
pendency of the writ petition no coercive 
measures shall be taken against the 
applicant pursuant to the aforesaid 
F.I.R./Case Crime. 
6. Learned counsel would further 
submit that the order dated 16.10.2024 
was duly apprised to the concerned Investigating Officer. However, the 
Investigating Officer failed to place the said 
order before the concerned trial court. 
Consequently, despite the protection 
granted to the applicant being operative till 
25.03.2025, the learned trial court 
proceeded to initiate proceedings under 
Section 84 of the B.N.S.S. and Section 299 
Cr.P.C., and thereafter the charge-sheet 
has also been submitted against the 
applicant and also passed the impugned 
order dated 08.04.2025, hence, the 
impugned order is unsustainable in the 
eyes of law. 
7. Per contra, learned counsel for 
respondent no. 2 would vehemently 
oppose the submissions advanced by 
learned counsel for the applicant. 
However, he seeks two weeks’ time to file 
objections in the matter. 
8. In the interest of justice, two weeks’ 
time is granted to respondent no. 2 to file 
objections. 
9. Meanwhile, the effect and operation 
of the impugned order dated 08.04.2025, 
passed in Special Sessions Trial No. 51 of 
2024 arising out of F.I.R./Case Crime No. 
0204 of 2024, shall remain stayed till the 
next date of listing. 
10. List this case on 22.04.2026 along 
with connected case.
