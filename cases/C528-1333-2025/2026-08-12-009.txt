Hon'ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. D.C.S. Rawat, learned counsel with Mr. 
Mr. Aditya Kumar and Hemant Mishra, learned 
counsel for the applicant.
2. Mr. Tumul Nainwal, learned A.G.A. for the 
State.
3. Mr. Harsh Vardhan Dhanik learned 
counsel with Mr. Prince Chauhan, learned Legal 
Aid counsel for the respondent/complainant.
4. The officer concerned who submitted the
preliminary inquiry report on 26.06.2022 joined 
the proceeding through V.C. in compliance of the 
previous order. The I.O. concerned who 
conducted the investigation also joined the 
proceeding through V.C.
5. In this case what this Court has observed 
that except the testimony of the victim and the 
informant there is no link evidence for 
involvement of the present applicant in 
commission of this crime. At the time of the 
incident the victim was 7 years old and the 
informant (mother of the victim) lodged the FIR
by alleging that there is “penetrative sexual 
assault” on the victim and on conclusion of the 
investigation charge sheet has been filed based on the testimony of the victim , informant 
(mother of the victim) and one friend of the 
informant and except this neither there is any 
scientific evidence nor any corroborative 
evidence. Not only this, the informant has some 
matrimonial dispute with her husband who is 
biological/natural father of the victim who was 
also examined and denied the allegation 
straightaway by saying that all the allegations, as 
alleged by the informant, are false.
6. After gone through with the records what 
this Court has observed that the investigation 
appears to be faulty since based on only the 
testimony of the victim and her mother charge 
sheet has been filed in absence of any scientific 
evidence.
7. Why such faulty investigation is being 
conducted this is a debatable question. Now, in 
some cases this Court take serious note that in 
large number of cases faulty investigations are 
being conducted in a very haste manner.
8. Mr. Tumul Nainwal, learned A.G.A. 
submits that in this particular case the applicant 
never cooperate with the investigation and he 
was absconding and the proceeding under
Section 82 and 83 Cr.P.C. was also initiated but 
the fact remains why the applicant was 
absconding whether because of the FIR or for
other reasons. The Hon’ble Apex Court in the 
landmark decision in the case of Joginder Kumar 
vs. State of U.P., (1994) 4 SCC 260 observed that 
the arrest should generally be avoided except in 
heinous crime but this court in many cases
observed that instead of going with fair 
investigation Investigating Officers are focusing 
on arrest.
9. Now the question is whether arrest is 
necessary for fair investigation. There may be the 
reason that applicant is absconding since there is 
an apprehension of arrest under the garb of FIR 
lodged by the informant who is real sister of the 
applicant. This issue has now to be dealt with 
because in most of the cases the I.O. come up 
with the stand that the accused persons against whom FIR is lodged are not cooperating with the 
investigation.
10. Mr. Harsh Vardhan Dhanik, learned 
counsel who is replaced by another counsel was 
asked to assist the Court since on the previous 
date he was fully prepared in the matter and a 
specific question was asked from him whether 
testimony of the victim and the informant are 
sufficient to convict the applicant, and he fairly 
submits that only on the basis of the testimony of 
the victim and the informant the accused cannot 
be convicted, if it is so then why only on the 
testimonies of the victim and informant charge 
sheet has been filed.
12. List this matter on 31.08.2026.
13. On that day learned counsel for the parties 
including learned A.G.A. may address on the 
issue of faulty investigation as well as whether 
based on the testimonies of victim and mother 
applicant can be convicted in absence of any 
scientific and corroboration evidence.
