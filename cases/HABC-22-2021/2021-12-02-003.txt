Hon’ble Ravindra Maithani, J. (Oral)
In the instant habeas corpus petition,
directions have been sought to produce the corpus of Mr. 
Vikas Singh Yadav, who was confined in the Vikalp Nasha 
Mukti Evam Punarwas Kendra, Near State Bank, Delhi 
Road, Pakbara, District Moradabad, Uttar Pradesh
(Respondent no.6, hereinafter referred to as, “the 
Centre”). 
2. This Court directed that the corpus of Mr. 
Vikas Singh Yadav be produced before the Court. On 
30.11.2021, Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav was produced before 
the Court. The Court questioned him and his reply has 
been noted in the first order, passed by this Court on 
30.11.2021, which is as hereunder:-
“3. The Court questioned Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav, he 
would submit as hereunder:-2
(i) I was practicing Advocate in the High Court of 
Uttarakhand, Nainital. My in-laws and my family 
members lodged a missing report at Police Station 
Majhola, District Moradabad. On 03.09.2021, my in￾laws took me from Bhimtal and to Moradabad. On the 
same night, my in-laws pressurized me that I should 
stay in Moradabad. They snatched my mobile phone, 
my ID Cards, my ID of Nainital High Court, they also 
took `4,500/- from me. 
(ii) On the next day, they handed me over to Mr. Parvez. I 
was handed over at Reliance Petrol Pump, Delhi Road, 
Moradabad. Thereafter, Mr. Mohd. Parvez along with the 
two persons, took me in their Nasha Mukti Centre. On 
the same day, they asked me to sign on a file. For the 
last three months, I am confined in that Centre. I do not

(iii) In their Centre, there is a Sanjivini Booti, if anyone has 
any problem with any person, either with regard to

Massih was unmarried. 
(iv) The Centre keeps people there for a longer time. In fact 
there are persons, who have been detained in the 
Centre for more than 13 months now. I can even name

3. When Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav was produced, 
his wife also appeared before the Court and claimed that 
Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav is unwell. The Centre was also 
represented through its Director Mohd. Parvez. It was told 
on behalf of the Centre by Mohd. Parvez that Mr. Vikas 
Singh Yadav is a Smack addict and he has been handed 
over to Mohd. Parvez by his family members. The Court, 
thereafter, in para 14 of its first order observed as 
hereunder:-4
“14. The Court is only concerned now with the 
safety of Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav. He may not be 
detained. At present he shall not be sent to the 
respondent no.6 Centre. But, his health condition 
(especially mental health) needs to be examined before 
he is freed. In case doctors advise that Mr. Vikas Singh 
Yadav needs special care, the matter may then be 
considered accordingly. Therefore, before proceeding 
further in the matter this Court shall now separately 
direct examination of Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav by 
doctors. Once report is received, the matter shall be

4. Accordingly, on the same day, directions were 
issued for medical examination of Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav. 
A report was received and the Chief Medical 
Superintendent, B.D. Pandey District Hospital, Mallital, 
Nainital recommended that Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav should 
undergo thorough examination through panel of doctors 
in Medical College Haldwani, District Nainital. It was so 
directed. 
5. Today report is received from Medical College 
Haldwani, District Nainital. According to it, there is no 
psychiatric disorder to Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav. 
6. Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav is free to move,
wherever he wants. 5
7. The habeas corpus petition is allowed 
accordingly. 
8. The question is, whether the Court should stop 
here? Or should the Court examine issues, which have 
been raised in the matter? 
9. Today, learned counsel for the petitioner would 
submit that in the affairs of the Centre a detailed inquiry 
should be ordered by the State of Uttar Pradesh, as per 
the provisions of the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 (for 
short, “the Act”), which provides punishments also under 
Chapter XV of the Act.
10. Learned counsel representing the State of Uttar 
Pradesh (the respondent no.3, SSP Moradabad) would 
submit that it is a very serious matter; the Centre, it 
appears, has violated the condition of the registration. 
11. Learned counsel for the State of Uttar Pradesh 
would submit that he would write to the concerned 
authorities, so that the matter may be looked into. 
12. Learned counsel appearing for the State of 
Uttarakhand would submit that it is a serious matter, in 
which, FIR must be lodged. He also submits that, in fact, 6
there have been instances of irregularities or atrocities 
allegedly done in the Mental Healthcare Institutions in the 
State of Uttarakhand. He would refer to some incidents of 
Dehradun, where according to him, two girls ran away 
from a rehabilitation Centre and when they were 
apprehended, they refused to be admitted in the 
rehabilitation Centre on the ground that they were 
sexually assaulted in the rehabilitation centre. 
13. During the course of hearing, Mrs. Premlata 
Yadav, who introduced herself as the wife of Mr. Vikas 
Singh Yadav also intervened. 
14. Today, Mr. Vikas Bahuguna, learned counsel 
representing Mrs. Pramlata Yadav would submit that Mr. 
Vikas Singh Yadav had signed the form for his admission 
in the Centre.
15. At it, Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav has responded 
that he was forced to sign inside the Centre; he had not 
willingly signed it. 
16. Generally this Court should refrain from 
making any observation with regard to the conduct of any 
institution, individual or the system. But, this Court 
would be failing in its duty if this Court does not observe7
as to what happened during the hearing of this case. On 
30.11.2021, when corpus of Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav was 
produced before the Court, the Court wanted to see the 
documents maintained by Mohd. Parvez, the respondent 
no.6 with regard to Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav. There was a 
discharge form in it, which was though signed, but not 
filled up. Today, when this Court again asked Mohd. 
Parvez, to produce that document. Mohd. Pravez 
produced the same form, but now it is filled up. 
17. The Court asked Mohd. Parvez, as to when was 
it filled up? He would reply that it was filled up yesterday. 
It speaks in volume. This Court leaves it at it. 
18. In fact, on 30.11.2021, Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav

He has named them and that was recorded in the order

20. The laudable object for enacting the Act need 
no reiteration. Mental illness is not something strange, it 
is like any other illness. The Act has intended to give 
dignity to the persons suffering from mental illness; the 
Act has further respected the dignity and privacy of a 
person suffering from mental illness; the Act has further 
regulated the admission of the persons in Mental 
Healthcare Establishment. There are specific provisions 
which deals with it. It may be a hazardous situation, if a 
person sound in mind without any mental illness, is 
confined in any institution or home or where persons with 
mental illness are confined. 
21. This Court in the case of Dr. Vijay Verma vs. 
Union of India and others, (2018) SCC Online 
Uttarakhand 519, has considered the implementation 
aspect of the Act in the State of Uttarakhand and in para 
52 of its judgment, number of directions have been given. 
What happened to those directions? 
22. Though the issue, at present, does not belong 
to the State of Uttarakhand, but reference has been made 9
to the incidents in District Dehradun and it is submitted 
on behalf of the learned counsel for the State of 
Uttarakhand itself that this Court should examine the 
implementation aspect of the Act in the State of 
Uttarakhand. This Court considers to examine that 
aspect, as well.
23. The corpus Mr. Vikas Singh Yadav was 
detained by the Centre in contravention of the provisions 
of the Act. He had to remain for almost three months in 
the Centre. This act of the respondent no.6 may not be 
termed as a mistake. It is a question of liberty of a person 
snatched without in accordance with law. Mr. Vikas 
Singh Yadav has narrated the story of inhuman treatment 
and living conditions in the Centre. This Court does not 
know how far is that true. It may definitely be a matter of 
inquiry.
