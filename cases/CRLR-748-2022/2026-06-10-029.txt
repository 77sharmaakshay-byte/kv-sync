COURT’S OR JUDGE’S ORDERS
CRLR No. 748 of 2022
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. B.S. Adhikari, learned Amicus Curiae 
for the revisionist.
2. Mr. J.S. Virk, learned Deputy Advocate 
General with Mr. Tumul Nainwal, learned 
A.G.A for the State.
3. Mrs. Manisha Bhandari, learned counsel 
for respondent no.2.
4. Mr. Vikram Singh Dhapola, learned 
counsel for respondent no.4.
5. Mr. Rajat Mittal, learned counsel for 
respondent no. 5 and 6.
6. Mr. B.S. Adhikari, was appointed as an 
Amicus Curiae in order to assist this Court. He
placed before this Court the short synopses 
along with the two decisions of Apex Court the 
copy of which in advance has also been served 
to the counsel for the other side. Mr. Adhikari 
argued that primarily in the instant revision the 
locus of the revisionist has to be examined, since 
the appeal which he has preferred before the 
Session Court was rejected on the ground of 
locus. On a locus he seriously attacked on the 
conduct of the prosecution the manner in which 
the prosecution make an attempt to frustrate the 
trial court proceedings. The first submission 
which he has advanced is with regard to an 
application moved by the APO under Section 
321 of Cr.P.C. for withdrawal of the proceeding 
which was rejected by the trial court on 
17.09.2018.
He submits that though the application 
was rejected on 17.09.2018 but the fact remains iswhy the prosecution moved such an application 
under Section 321 of Cr.P.C. particularly when 
after thorough investigation and after collecting 
the material evidence, the charge sheet was filed, 
therefore, there was no question to move any 
such application under Section 321 of Cr.P.C. for 
withdrawal of the proceeding.
The another point which he has argued is 
that out of 28 prosecution witnesses only 18 
witnesses were came in witness box though all 
the witnesses were the public servant and all of 
them were under the control of the State 
Government.
The third submission is with regard to 
production of 65 B certificate which has not been 
produced in support of the electronic evidences
collected during investigation. He submits that 
why the concerned I.O. had not produced 65 B 
certificate when he collect electronic evidences 
and it’s a deliberate laps on the part of the 
prosecution.
7. The another point which he has raised is 
with regard to the non-production of original
Notification dated 05.03.2016 which pertains to 
imposition of Section 144 of Cr.P.C in the area 
where the incident took place. He submits that 
this document was with the police department 
and non production of this document is also 
deliberate attempt on the part of the prosecution. 
The next submission which he has advanced is 
with regard to the electronic evidence produced 
by the accused. He submits that trial court 
committed manifest illegality while taking 
cognizance on electronic evidences produced by 
the witnesses in absence of 65 B certificate. He 
submits that in one side the trial court have not 
taken into consideration the electronic evidences 
produced by the prosecution in absence of 65 B 
certificate and on the other side the electronic 
evidences produced by the accused were taken 
into consideration in absence of 65 B certificate. 
He submits that non production of 65 B 
certificate in reference to electronic evidences produced by the prosecution is not the 
negligence on the part of the I.O. rather it is a 
blunder mistake on his part.
8. The another submission which he has 
advanced is that according to the prosecution,
PW1, PW2, PW3, PW4, PW5, PW7, PW10 and 
PW12 are the star witnesses and they support 
the case of the prosecution but the Trial Court 
acquit the accused persons only on the basis that 
there are minor contradictions on their
statement. Lastly, he submits that in this 
particular case since beginning the conduct of 
the prosecution was not fair after 
commencement of the trial, and now after 
acquittal by the trial court, based on the opinion 
of three members committee the prosecution 
have decided not to file the appeal against the 
acquittal. He submits that the revisionist herein 
being a public spirited person and animal lover 
in such a compelling circumstances filed an 
appeal against the order of acquittal but the 
prosecution take a plea of locus of the revisionist 
which itself reveals that any how the prosecution 
want to close the proceeding and now the 
similar plea of locus they are taking in the 
instant revision.
He submits that opinion of committee has
no binding precedent. He submits that this case 
is exemplar one wherein the complainant was 
the victim, lodged an FIR naming those who 
were involved in the incident despite this, the 
order of acquittal is not challenged by the 
prosecution.
9. Mr. B.S. Adhikari in support of the locus 
placed reliance to the judgment of Apex Court in 
the case of PSR Sadhanantham Vs. 
Arunachalam and Another (1980) 3SCC 141
which is a constitutional bench judgment which 
was further followed in the case of Amanullah 
Vs. State of Bihar (2016) 6SCC 699.
In response to the submission of Mr. 
Adhikari, Mr. Virk, learned Addl. Advocate 
general submits that non production of 65Bcertificate is certainly lacuna on the part of the 
I.O. In reference to application moved under 
Section 321 Cr.P.C., he submits that he has no 
clear instructions in this regard and submits that 
he will address on this issue on the next date. 
On number of witnesses who have been 
examined by the trial court he submits that
quality of witnesses has to be seen by the Trial
Court and not the number of witnesses,
therefore, the submission to this extent is not so 
fatal in view of section 134 of the Evidence Act. 
In reference to the non production of 
notification dated 05.03.2016, he submits that he 
has no instruction why the same was not 
produced and even otherwise its the duty of the 
trial court to summon that document if it has 
some relevance and for this prosecution cannot 
be blamed. He submits that in absence of 65B 
certificate the trial court should not take into 
consideration the electronic evidence produced 
by the accused and finding to this effect is not 
acceptable. 
10. On the request of Mr. Virk, list this case on 
02.07.2026.
