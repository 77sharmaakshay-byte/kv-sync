4. The instant petition has been preferred by the 
applicant Sanjay Sharma S/o Ramesh Chandra Sharma 
challenging the order dated 09.06.2026 passed by 1st
Additional Sessions judge, Roorkee, District Haridwar 
in Miscellaneous Criminal Case No. 44 of 2026 
‘Maateshri Enterprises vs. State of Uttarakhand and 
Another’, whereby, the application for suspension of 
conviction and sentence as well as bail application was 
rejected on the ground that the same is not 
maintainable since the appeal preferred against the 
conviction has not been admitted so far.
5. The brief facts of the case are that the present 
applicant was convicted by the learned Judicial 
Magistrate II, Roorkee, District Haridwar in Complaint 
Case No. 797 of 2025 a proceeding arising out of 
Section 138 of N.I. Act with the sentence of two months 
simple imprisonment with the fine of Rs. 24,000/-
pursuant to the judgment dated 31.01.2026.
6. Being aggrieved with the judgment and order 
dated 31.01.2026 an appeal was preferred by the 
applicant in the court of 1st Additional District and 
Session Judge, Roorkee, on 24.03.2026, which was time 
barred, wherein, the notices were issued. Along with this appeal an applicant was moved for suspension of 
conviction and sentence along with the bail 
application, however, the same has been rejected by 
the order impugned by observing that since the appeal 
has yet not been admitted so far, therefore, the 
application moved for suspension of conviction and 
sentence as well as for bail are not maintainable.
7. Learned counsel for the applicant submits that 
infact the entire amount of fine as imposed by the Trial 
Court while convicting the appellant under section 138 
of N.I. Act has been paid to the 
complainant/respondent. The complainant/ 
respondent is being represented by Mr. Sachin Kumar 
Sharma, learned counsel who appears through V.C. 
and make a statement that she has received the entire 
amount from the present applicant and an application 
was moved before the Appellate Court on 12.06.202 
that now the parties have settled their dispute. He 
submits that in terms of section 147 of N.I. Act every 
offence punishable under the N.I. Act is 
compoundable. He submits that this fact was brought 
to the knowledge of the Appellate Court that now the 
parties have settled their dispute but the matter was 
postponed. He submits that since now the parties have 
settled their disputes and the applicant was taken into 
custody on 07.06.2026, therefore, the offence may be 
compounded by set asiding the order of conviction 
and the applicant be released on bail.
8. Though learned counsel for the applicant 
praying for compounding of the offences but there is 
no any such application in the petition but taking into 
consideration that the entire amount has been paid 
which the respondent/complainant admit, at this 
juncture the liberty is given to the parties to move an 
appropriate application in terms of section 147 of N.I. 
Act.
9. Put up this matter on 13.07.2026.
10. In the meantime, it is directed that the applicant 
herein be released forthwith subject to furnishing a 
personal bond and two reliable sureties each of the like 
amount to the satisfaction of the court concerned.
