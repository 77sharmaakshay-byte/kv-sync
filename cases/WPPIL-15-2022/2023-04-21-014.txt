The Court made the following: 
ORDER: (per Hon’ble The Chief Justice Sri Vipin Sanghi) 
 The affidavit of the Central Government has 
been filed, and perused.  2
2. The said affidavit states that the 46th Expert 
Appraisal Committee (EAC) meeting was held during 15th￾17th February, 2022, wherein officials from National 
Mission for Clear Ganga (NMCG), State Government, 
Collector Haridwar, and Uttarakhand Forest Development 
Corporation also participated. In the said meeting, it was 
recommended that no mining activity will be undertaken 
in the area for which EC has been granted, i.e., between 
Raiwala to Boghpur, until the EAC reviews the final report 
submitted by IIT Kanpur, and also the report / 
observation of special committee to be constituted, as 
suggested. The affidavit further states, that on 
20.04.2022, a notification has been issued, stating that 
ECs of all minor mineral, including sand mining, shall be 
dealt with by the State Level Committee, irrespective of 
the mining lease area. From the same, it appears that 
the ball is now in the court of the State Environmental 
Impact Assessment Authority (SEIAA), which would now 
have to consider the matter further in continuation of the 
recommendation of the EAC made as a result of the 
meeting held between 15th–17th February, 2022. The 
SEIAA should, therefore, proceed to act in furtherance of 
the said recommendation, which we have taken note of 
here-in-above, and without any further delay, decide :  3
whether, or not, the activity of mining sand and other 
minor minerals should be permitted in the aforesaid 
stretch of river Ganga. 
3. Let the said decision be taken before the next 
date, and be placed before this Court. It goes without 
saying that the decision should be based upon all relevant 
considerations, which should be reflected in the decision 
which is taken. Needless to say, that the State 
Environmental Impact Assessment Authority should take 
its decision based only on merits, without being influenced 
by the orders passed by us. The said decision be placed 
before us on the next date. 
4. In our order dated 15.12.2022, in para 16, we 
had observed that it was absolutely essential for the State 
Government to set up a completely independent 
Complaint Redressal System, to look into the complaints 
of illegal mining, screening or crushing of river bed 
materials. The Redressal mechanism should remain 
independent of, and unconnected with the State’s 
administration, if it has to work effectively and 
meaningfully. This is because, often, complaints are 
received that the local administration is either inactive, 
unconcerned, or is mixed up with the mining licensees. 
Such complaints, therefore, cannot be left for examination  4
and disposal by the very same authorities, who are 
involved in the matter of enforcement of the conditions of 
the mining license. We had, therefore, directed the State 
to evolve a completely independent Complaint Redressal 
Mechanism, by inter alia, drawing reputed and 
independent persons, who are retired members of the 
Judiciary, Bureaucracy, reputed environmental experts, 
and activists. We had required the State to place the 
independent Mechanism before this Court. However, the 
said mechanism has not been brought on record.
