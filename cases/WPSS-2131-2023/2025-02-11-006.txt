COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
WPSS No.2131 of 2023
Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned 
Senior Counsel, assisted by Mr. 
Chandra Shekhar, learned counsel for 
the petitioner.
2. Mr. Pradeep Hairiya, learned 
Standing Counsel for the State.
3. Ms. Devika Tiwari, learned counsel 
for respondent No.1.
4. Mr. Vinay Kumar, learned counsel 
for respondent No.4.
5. There are four candidates, namely, 
Aman Kumar, Ankit Kumar, the present 
petitioner and the respondent No.4-
Yogesh Kumar. Aman Kumar and Ankit 
Kumar both obtained higher marks in 
comparison to the petitioner-Mahesh 
Kumar and respondent No.4-Yogesh 
Kumar and both of them had given the 
first preference of posting/appointment 
in Tourism Development Board, 
however, the Tourism Development 
Board withdrew their requisition, 
therefore, no selection were made in the 
said department.
6. These two candidates, namely,
Aman Kumar and Ankit Kumar had 
given the second preference to Doctor 
R.S. Tolia Academy. So far as the 
petitioner and respondent No.4 are 
concerned, both of them had also given 
first preference of appointment in Doctor 
R.S. Tolia Academy.
7. Mr. C.D. Bahuguna, learned 
Senior counsel submits that since the petitioner has obtained higher marks in 
comparison to respondent No.4 and is 
also higher in the merit, therefore, he 
should be given appointment to the 
Department for which he has given the 
first preference i.e. Doctor R.S. Tolia 
Academy, but ignoring his merit and 
marks, the offer of appointment has 
been given to the respondent No.4.
8. Ms. Devika Tiwari, learned counsel 
for respondent No.1 submits that since 
the Tourism Development Board 
withdrew their requisition, therefore,
Aman Kumar and Ankit Kumar both of 
them have a preferential right to get 
appointment to Dr. R.S. Tolia Academy 
in comparison to the petitioner and 
respondent No.4, since both of them 
were higher in merit in comparison to 
these two persons.
9. It has been informed that both of 
these persons, namely, Aman Kumar 
and Ankit Kumar, have been given 
appointment to the different department 
instead of this academy and the 
appointment to this academy has been
offered to the respondent No.4.
10. Admittedly, there is some 
procedural mistake on the part of the 
respondent No.1, which they admit 
under the RTI information supplied to 
the petitioner, which is enclosed in this 
writ petition and if there is a mistake 
then it has to be rectified.
11. Mr. Vinay Kumar, learned counsel 
for respondent No.4 objects to this and 
submits that the petitioner and the 
respondent No.4 both were offered 
appointment pursuant to the said selection in the year 2023 and now, at
such a belated stage, there is no any 
justification for seeking rectification of 
such mistake. Furthermore, he submits 
that the appointment of respondent 
No.4 has not been challenged and only 
this much relief has been sought that 
the allotment of the posting of the
candidate on the post of 
Stenographer/Personal Assistant be 
made strictly, as per the preference,
which should be based on their merit.
12. Apart from this, Mr. Vinay Kumar 
submits that if at such a belated stage 
when the appointment were offered in 
the year 2023 any interference is called 
for, then it will affect the future 
prospects of the respondent No.4.
13. Since there is apparently a mistake 
on the part of respondent No.1 in 
allotment of posting, therefore, such a 
mistake should be rectified.
14. Let the entire exercise be 
completed by the respondent No.1 for 
rectification of the mistakes, which they 
have committed in allotment of the 
Department and place the rectification 
of mistake before this Court in a sealed
envelope. The entire exercise be
completed within in two weeks from 
today.
15. Put up this matter on 03.03.2025 
at 2:00 PM.
