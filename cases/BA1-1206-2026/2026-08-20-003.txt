4. The instant bail application is moved by the 
present applicant Sanjay Jain, S/o Late Gurvant 
Rai Jain for seeking regular bail in reference to 
FIR dated 24.05.2026, bearing FIR No. 0190 of 
2026 lodged by one Vinod Prakash Agrawal, S/o 
Late J.P. Agrawal implicating as many as six 
persons including present applicant his wife Ritu 
Jain, Ayush Jain (S/o present applicant) and his 
wife Priya Jain, Naveen Garg from whom 
applicant took a loan and last one Nishant Garg
(S/o Naveen Garg) for the offences punishable 
under Sections 318(4), 336(3), 338, 340(2), 61(2) of 
BNS 2023.
5. Mr. Sandeep Tandon, learned counsel for 
the applicant argued that lodging of this FIR by 
the complainant Vinod Prakash Agrawal is 
apparently a glaring example of malicious 
prosecution which is evident from the fact that 
the applicant’s wife Smt. Ritu Jain informant 
himself entered with a registered agreement to 
sell on 14.10.2024 wherein in terms of Clause 7 it 
is agreed that the flat will be sold out free from all encumbrances which reads as under:-

6. He submits that there is no dispute that the 
aforesaid agreement dated 14.10.2024 is 
registered one signed by Smt. Ritu Jain, w/o the 
applicant and the informant. Mr. Tandon pointed 
out that not only this subsequently sale deed was 
executed in favour of the respondent on 
24.04.2025 which is annexed as annuxure-2. He 
submits that after execution of sale deed after 
almost one year informant lodged the FIR on 
24.05.2026 bearing FIR No. 1090 of 2026 P.S. 
Kotwali Dehradun wherein not only applicant 
but his wife, son and daughter-in-law have also 
been implicated including the person from whom 
the applicant’s wife took loan is also implicated 
along with his son.
7. Mr. Tandon argued that lodging of FIR by 
the informant appears to be an abuse of process 
of law on two accounts firstly though in the FIR 
he has disclosed about the agreement to sell 
dated 14.10.2024 but he has not mentioned in 
words about Clause 7 of the agreement; secondly 
sale deed was also executed on 24.04.2025 and 
the informant was fully aware about the clause 7 
of the agreement. He further submits that the 
applicant’s wife, his son and daughter-in-law 
have already been granted interim bail by the 
Coordinate Bench in anticipatory bail 
application. He submits that though investigation 
is still going but the investigating officer in a 
hurried manner arrest the present applicant 
without any cogent material and without 
examining that informant was fully aware about 
Clause 7 of agreement dated 14.10.2024. He 
further submits that the applicant is languishing 
in jail since 29.05.2026 and has no criminal 
antecedent and is seeking bail. He further 
submits that not only this after execution of sale deed wherein peaceful possession is given to the 
informant which the counsel for the informant 
has admitted wherein the informant is 
constructing office over there.
8. On the other side Mr. Sahil Mullick learned 
counsel who appears for the informant submits 
that it is a case of cheating which is evident from 
the fact that in the sale deed dated 24.04.2025, 
there is no reference that flat is free from all 
encumbrance and the applicant and his wife 
never apprise whether flat is free from 
encumbrance.
9. On this Mr. Tandon submits that 
respondent/informant never give any such 
notice before lodging FIR and the person from 
whom applicant’s wife took loan fraudulently 
executed a sale deed for which civil suit is filed
for cancellation of sale deed and even otherwise 
the FIR does not discloses any cognizable offence 
since the informant was aware about Clause 7 of 
the agreement which he has deliberately not 
disclosed in the FIR and, as such, the FIR has 
been lodged with mala fide intent.
10. Mr. B.C. Joshi, learned A.G.A. for the State 
submits that the investigation is still going on. 
11. List this case on 10.09.2026.
12. On that day Mr. Joshi, learned A.G.A. shall 
apprise on the following issues:-
(i) Whether before registration of the FIR 
guidelines as formulated by the Hon’ble Apex 
Court in the case of Lalita Kumari vs. State of U.P. 
(2014) 2 SCC 1 was followed?
(ii) Whether before arresting the present 
applicant the I.O make an attempt to verify the 
genuineness of the allegations?
(iii) Whether the investigation was conducted 
to find out whether there is any mala fide intent 
or not in registration of the FIR?
13. In the meantime, taking into consideration 
that the dispute is prima facie arising out of an
agreement to sell dated 14.10.2024 and 
subsequent there to on 24.04.2025 a sale deed was 
executed in favour of informant and he also take 
physical possession, this Court is of the view that at this juncture applicant deserves for interim 
bail. 
14. The applicant is admitted on interim bail 
for a period of one month to be reckoned from 
the date of his released.
15. Let the applicant “Sanjay Jain” be released 
on interim bail for a period of one month to be 
reckoned from the date of release on his 
executing a personal bond and furnishing two 
reliable sureties of the like amount to the 
satisfaction of the court concerned subject to the 
following conditions:-
(i) The applicant shall surrender his passport, 
if any, to the I.O. after being released on bail and 
shall fully cooperate with the investigation.
(ii) Neither the applicant nor the informant 
make any attempt to contact with each other by 
any mode including electronic media
16. In the meantime they may exchange their 
objection.
17. Put up this matter on 17.09.2026.
