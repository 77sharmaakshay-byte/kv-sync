The respondent No. 1 is the daughter￾in-law of the revisionist Nos. 1 and 2. She is 
said to have initiated the proceedings under 
Section 12 of the Protection of Women from 
Domestic Violence Act, 2005, wherein, she 
has by virtue of the conjoint reading of 
Sections 12, 18, 19 and 20 of the Act and 
being daughter-in-law had sought a direction 
from the Court by way of a permission to 
reside in a house which is existing and 
constructed by the revisionists on khasra No. 
2351, having an area of 3034 sq. feet (282 sq. 
meter) situated at Mauza Enfield Grant, 
Pargana Pachwadoon, District Dehradun. 
The Additional Chief Judicial 
Magistrate by the judgment dated 13th
November, 2017, impugned in the revision, 
had issued direction to the revisionists not to 
commit any domestic offence against the 
respondent No. 1 and had also permitted the 
respondent No. 1 to reside over the housed 
property of revisionist. The learned 
Magistrate had also directed to return the 
stridhan of the respondent No. 1. 
Aggrieved against the said order, an 
appeal was preferred by the present revisionist 
and the same has been dismissed by the 
learned Additional Sessions Judge, Vikas 
Nagar, Dehradun by the impugned judgment 
dated 12th September, 2018 and, 
consequently, the present revision. 
The argument as extended by the 
learned counsel for the revisionists is that the 
property as described hereinabove, over which, right of residence has been sought by 
the respondent by invoking Section 12 could 
not have been granted in her favour in view of 
the embargo as contained Section 17 of the 
Act, which contemplates that right to reside 
would accrue in “share holding” house only. 
Section 17 of the Act reads as under : 
“17. Right to reside in a shared 
household.—(1) Notwithstanding anything 
contained in any other law for the time being 
in force, every woman in a domestic 
relationship shall have the right to reside in 
the shared household, whether or not she has 
any right, title or beneficial interest in the 
same. 
(2) The aggrieved person shall not be 
evicted or excluded from the shared 
household or any part of it by the respondent 
save in accordance with the procedure 
established by law.” 
Share holding has been defined under 
Sub-Section (s) of Section 2 of the Act. 
Section 2 (s) reads as under : 
“(s) “shared household” means a 
household where the person aggrieved lives 
or at any stage has lived in a domestic 
relationship either singly or along with the 
respondent and includes such a household 
whether owned or tenanted either jointly by 
the aggrieved person and the respondent, or 
owned or tenanted by either of them in respect 
of which either the aggrieved person or the 
respondent or both jointly or singly have any 
right, title, interest or equity and includes 
such a household which may belong to the 
joint family of which the respondent is a 
member, irrespective of whether the 
respondent or the aggrieved person has any 
right, title or interest in the shared 
household;” The property, in question, where the 
respondent No. 1 has tried to seek right to 
reside is an exclusively purchased property of 
the revisionists and would not constitute to be 
a share holding property as defined under 
Sub-section (s) of Section 2 of the Act of 
2005. 
Considering the aforesaid arguments, 
the revision would stand admitted.
