26.09.2024 CRLA No. 493 of 2024
Hon’ble Vivek Bharti Sharma, J.
Mr. Piyush Sammal, counsel for the 
appellant.
2. Mr. Pratiroop Pandey, A.G.A. for the State.
3. Present criminal appeal is preferred against 
the impugned judgment and order dated 27.08.2024 
& 28.08.2024 passed by learned Additional Sessions 
Judge, Khatima in Session Trial No. 27 of 2021, 
whereby the appellant/convict has been convicted 
under Section 304B, 498A I.P.C. and Section 4 of 
Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.
4. Heard on bail application (IA 1 of 2024)
5. Counsel for the appellant/convict would 
submit that the appellant is the husband of the 
deceased; that, there is no evidence against the 
appellant/convict for allegation in respect of demand 
of dowry from the deceased; that, there is no 
external injury on the body of the deceased, which 
prove cruelty on the part of the appellant/convict; 
that, on the date of the death of the deceased, the 
appellant/convict being an Army Officer i.e. 
Hawaldar was posted at Kupwada in Jammu & 
Kashmir and for about four months prior to the said 
incident, the appellant/convict never came to his 
parental home; that, when he came to know about 
the death of the deceased he reached his parental 
home on 13.05.2019.
He would further submit that in the cross 
examination of PW1/mother of the deceased she 
stated that infact the deceased was not happy with 
the marriage as she did not want to marry with the 
appellant/convict but under family pressure, she 
married with the present appellant/convict.
6. Counsel for the appellant/convict would 
further submit that the appellant/convict was on bail 
during trial and he never misused the liberty of bail; 
that, the decision of the appeal will take 
considerable time, therefore, the appellant/convict is 
entitled for bail.7. State counsel vehemently opposed the bail 
application on the ground that serious allegations 
have been leveled against the appellant/convict, 
however, he would admit that the appellant/convict 
was on bail during trial and he never misused the 
bail granted to him.
8. Having considered the submissions of learned 
counsel for the parties and without expressing any 
opinion on the final merits of the case, the 
appellant/applicant is admitted to bail. 
9. Let the appellant/applicant be released on bail, 
during the pendency of present criminal revision, on 
furnishing bail bond with two sureties in the amount
of ₹40,000/- and personal bond of the like amount to 
the satisfaction of the learned court concerned.
10. Bail application stands disposed of 
accordingly.
11. List this case on 12.03.2025 for final hearing.
(Vivek Bharti Sharma, J.)
