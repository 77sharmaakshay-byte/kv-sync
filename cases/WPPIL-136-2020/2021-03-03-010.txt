Mr. Kishore Kumar, learned

Mr. K.N. Joshi, learned Deputy

State/respondent nos. 1 and 2. 
The petitioners have raised a 
limited issue before this Court,
namely that the State has failed to 
enact any policy or rules with 
regard to premature release of
convicting prisoners, after they 
have completed 14 years of 
sentence.
However, the conditions of the 
jails in the State is a cause of
concern as the entire jail 
population, whether under trial or 
convicted prisoners, are under judicial custody. Therefore, this 
Court would like to know not only 
about the physical condition of the 
jails throughout the State, but also 
about the reformative techniques 
being used to reform the prisoners.
Furthermore, this Court would also 
like to know the sanctioned 
strength of the jail department, the 
working strength of the department 
and the vacancies that continued to 
exist, if any, in the jail department. 
This Court would also like to know 
the budgetary allocation for the jail 
department. Repeatedly the Hon’ble 
Supreme Court has prescribed 
guidelines and has made 
recommendations for the 
improvement of the prison system 
in India. Unfortunately, the prisons 
in India continued to be rather medieval in their functioning. It is 
high time that they are transported
from the Middle Ages to the Modern 
Age. Moreover, it is imperative that 
the under-trials are weaned away
from the criminal way of life, and 
the convicted prisoners are 
reformed to the extent that they 
can be brought back to the society
as contributory members of the 
society. Thus, the entire set up of 
the prison department needs to be 
reformed and resurrected.
Hence, this Court directs the 
Director General of Prison, to 
submit a complete report, on the 
points mentioned hereinabove. The 
said report shall be submitted on or 
before 23.03.2021.List this case on 24.03.2021.
