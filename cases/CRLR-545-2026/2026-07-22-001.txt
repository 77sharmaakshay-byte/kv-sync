Hon'ble Alok Mahra, J.
Mr. Sachin Panwar, learned counsel 
for the revisionist.
2. Ms. Pushpa Bhatt, learned Addl. 
Advocate General along with Mr. S.C. 
Dumka, learned A.G.A. and Mr. Nikhil 
Bisht, learned Brief Holder for the State.
3. Present criminal revision has been 
preferred against the judgment and order 
dated 10.07.2026 passed by the learned 
Sessions Judge, Uttarkashi in Criminal 
Appeal No. 24 of 2025, whereby the appeal 
preferred by the revisionist against the 
judgment and order dated 03.04.2025 
passed by the learned Chief Judicial 
Magistrate, Uttarkashi in Criminal Case 
No. 44 of 2025 has been dismissed and the 
conviction and sentence recorded by the 
trial court have been affirmed. By the 
judgment dated 03.04.2025, the learned 
Chief Judicial Magistrate convicted the 
revisionist for the offence punishable 
under Section 138 of the Negotiable 
Instruments Act, 1881 and sentenced him 
to undergo simple imprisonment for a period of six months and to pay a fine of 
₹5,20,000/-. In default of payment of fine, 
the revisionist has been directed to 
undergo further simple imprisonment for a 
period of one month.
4. Admit the revision.
5. Issue notice to the respondent, 
returnable at an early date.
6. Let the Lower Court Record be 
summoned.
7. Heard on the bail application (IA No. 2 
of 2026).
8. Learned counsel for the revisionist 
would submit that both the courts below 
have failed to properly appreciate the oral 
and documentary evidence available on 
record and have recorded the conviction in 
disregard of the material brought on record 
by the defence; that, the cheque in 
question was never issued by the 
revisionist to the complainant towards any 
legally enforceable debt or liability; that, 
the cheque was drawn on a joint account 
maintained by a partnership firm, which 
could be operated only with the signatures 
of both the authorized partners.
9. He would further submit that the 
alleged cheque, presented by the 
complainant contained only one signature 
and did not bear the signature of the other 
authorized signatory, rendering it invalid 
for presentation; that, in such 
circumstances the cheque ought not to 
have been processed for payment and, 
therefore, the endorsement of the bank 
returning the cheque with the remark 
"Funds Insufficient" is contrary to the 
mandate governing the operation of the 
account; that, that this specific defence 
was consistently raised by the revisionist before the trial court as well as the 
appellate court, but neither court recorded 
any finding thereon nor examined the 
effect of the operating instructions 
governing the joint account.
10. Learned counsel would further submit 
that the revisionist had already submitted 
an application before the concerned bank 
on 30.03.2021 seeking closure of the said 
account, whereas the cheque in question 
was presented for encashment only on 
29.11.2023; that, this material 
circumstance, according to the revisionist, 
has also not been considered by the courts 
below while recording the conviction; that, 
the defence evidence adduced by the 
revisionist has not been considered in its 
proper perspective and the impugned 
judgments suffer from non-appreciation of 
material evidence, giving rise to substantial 
questions requiring consideration in the 
present revision; that, the revisionist 
remained on bail during the trial as well as 
during the pendency of the criminal appeal 
and has never misused the liberty so 
granted or violated any condition of bail.
11. Per contra, learned State Counsel 
opposed the bail application. However, he 
does not dispute the fact that the 
revisionist remained on bail during the 
trial as well as during the pendency of the 
criminal appeal and that there is no 
allegation of misuse of the liberty granted 
to him.
12. Having considered the rival 
submissions advanced by learned counsel 
for the parties, the nature of the 
controversy involved, and without 
expressing any opinion on the merits of the 
revision, this Court is of the view that the 
revisionist has made out a fit case for grant of bail during the pendency of the present 
criminal revision.
13. Accordingly, the bail application (IA 
No. 2 of 2026) is allowed.
14. Let the revisionist be released on bail 
on his furnishing a personal bond along 
with two reliable sureties of the like 
amount each to the satisfaction of the 
court concerned, subject to such other 
conditions as the said court may deem fit 
and proper to impose.
15. The application seeking stay of 
realization of fine is also allowed. 
Accordingly, the realization of the fine 
imposed upon the revisionist by the courts 
below shall remain stayed during the 
pendency of the present criminal revision.
16. List the revision for hearing in due 
course.
(Alok Mahra, J.)
