Hon’ble Rakesh Thapliyal, J.
1. Mr. Vinay Kumar, learned counsel for the 
petitioner, Mr. S.N. Babulkar, learned Advocate 
General, assisted by Mr. C.S. Rawat, learned Chief 
Standing Counsel and Mr. Rajeev Singh Bisht, learned 
Additional Chief Standing Counsel and Mr. Pradeep 
Hairiya, learned Standing Counsel for the State and 
Mr. B.D. Pande, learned counsel for UKSSSC.
2. In all these writ petitions, on the 
previous date i.e. on 13.12.2024, this Court framed 
four questions, which are being reproduced 
herein:-
“13. After gone through the provisions of 
Uttarakhand Act No.20 of 2014, i.e. the 
Uttarakhand Subordinate Service Selection 
Commission Act, 2014, as well as the entire Rules 
framed by the Commission by invoking power, as 
conferred by Section 23, and further the 
procedure, as adopted by the respondent
Commission, primarily, this Court is of the view 
that the Commission failed to maintain the 
secrecy of selection, which itself is reflected from 
Annexure-10, the press note dated 29.01.2024, 
wherein there is a reference that the news 
relating to selection, in question, got viral in social 
media. Therefore, this Court is taking very serious 
view in the matter, for which, this Court wants 
the assistance of learned Advocate General. 
Therefore, this Court is framing certain questions, 
so that the learned Advocate General may 
address on these issues.
“i.Whether the Uttarakhand Subordinate Service 
Selection Commission was right in publishing 
provisional merit list in its website for the purpose of inviting objections and representations from 
the candidates, as it is reflected from the 
advertisement dated 25.10.2023 (Annexure-6), 
advertisement dated 10.11.2023 (Annexure-7), 
and advertisement dated 08.01.2024 (Annexure￾8). 
ii. Whether Section 18 of the Uttarakhand Act 
No.20 of 2014 empowers the Commission to 
publish provisional merit lists in its website, to 
forward the one select list to the Appointing 
Authority. 
iii. Whether the Commission was right to 
entertain the complaints received from different 
sources (as reflected from press note dated 
29.01.2024) in mid of the selection process, 
particularly, when the select list was not 
forwarded to the Appointing Authority as per 
Section 18 of the Act.
iv. Whether the Commission was right in 
constituting a Committee for verifying the 
location of firm to ascertain the genuineness of 
certificates of the candidates, and based on the 
said report, selection of petitioners put on hold.”
3. In pre-lunch session, Mr. Pradeep 
Hairiya, learned Standing Counsel, give the answer of 
all the questions as above in ‘No’. On that date, the 
Commission was also directed to place before this 
Court the details of the members of the Expert 
Committee as well as their report in the sealed 
envelope, but, despite this, they have not complied 
with the order and have not produced the report in 
the sealed envelope.
4. After gone through the provisions of the 
Uttarakhand Act No. 20 of 2024 and the Regulations 
framed by the Commission by invoking the powers 
conferred by Section 23 of the Act, the Court was of 
the view that the Commission has no power to 
publish the provisional merit list in it’s website since by virtue of Section 18 of the Act, the Commission 
have to send the select list to the Government, but, 
surprisingly in these cases, what has happened,
thrice the provisional merit list was published in the 
website for inviting objections from the candidate 
and during mid of selection process, some news got 
viral in the social media and the Recruiting Body took 
cognizance on that and thereafter Recruiting Body 
received some more complaints and then constitute 
a Committee.
5. Today, certain written instructions have 
been placed before this Court by Mr. B.D. Pandey,
learned counsel for the Commission and one 
document is the office memorandum dated 
21.11.2024, which pertains to the Constitution of the 
Committee of five persons for verifying the 
certificates of some candidates.
6. This written instruction also contains a 
report and from the report, it reveals that after spot￾inspection, the Committee submitted it’s report.
7. It is very strange that in mid of selection 
process, the Recruiting Body gave the task to a 
Committee to verify certificate after visiting the site 
to examine whether that establishment from where 
the certificates have been issued are located in place 
or not.
8. After gone through the written 
instruction and observation, as recorded in my previous order, it appears that the Recruiting Body 
could not maintain the secrecy of the selection, 
which is evident from the fact that thrice the 
provisional list was published in the website inviting 
the objections from candidates. To maintain the 
secrecy of the selection is the prime duty of the 
Recruiting Body, which they have failed to do so and 
this is the very serious issue because since last so 
many years, various selections were carried out by 
the Recruiting Bodies, but, there is no transparency 
and in mid of the selection, so many litigants 
approach to this Court questioning the manner in
which the Recruiting Body proceed with the selection 
process. This is really very shocking, which certainly 
requires deep scrutiny.
9. Put up this matter on 03.01.2025, on top 
of Board, keeping the matter as part heard.
10. By that time, the Commission shall 
produce the records relating to the selection process 
including the decision for taking cognizance on the 
news, which got viral in social media including the 
details of the complaints received and the report of 
the Expert Committee including details of each 
member of the Committee constituted.
(Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
