C528/1161/2026
Hon’ble Alok Mahra, J.
Mr. Pankaj Singh Chauhan, learned 
counsel for the applicants.
2. Mr. V.S. Pal, learned A.G.A. along 
with Mr. Nikhil Bisht, learned Brief Holder 
for the State.
3. Present C-528 application has been 
filed to quash the impugned order dated 
22.04.2026 passed in Misc. Complaint No. 
8746 of 2024, whereby learned court 
summoned the applicant for the alleged 
offences punishable under Sections 316(2) 
B.N.S. as well as the entire proceedings of 
the aforesaid case pending in the court of 
learned 5th Additional Chief Judicial 
Magistrate, Haridwar.
4. Learned counsel for the applicants 
would submit that the present dispute 
arises out of matrimonial discord between 
the applicants and respondent no.2. 
Applicant no.1 is the husband of 
respondent no.2, while applicant nos.2 and 
3 are her father-in-law and mother-in-law, 
respectively; that, the marriage between 
applicant no.1 and respondent no.2 was 
solemnized on 03.06.2015; that, due to 
matrimonial differences, the parties have 
been living separately for a considerable 
period. It is submitted that respondent no.2 
had earlier lodged an F.I.R. dated 
18.11.2019 against applicant nos.1 and 2 
under Sections 498-A, 323 and 504 I.P.C. 
and Sections 3/4 of the Dowry Prohibition 
Act; that, the applicants challenged the said proceedings before this Court by filing a 
petition under Section 482 Cr.P.C., which 
came to be dismissed by a Coordinate 
Bench, however, the criminal proceedings 
were subsequently quashed by the Hon’ble 
Supreme Court vide judgment and order 
dated 02.08.2024.
5. Learned counsel further submits that, 
during the pendency of the aforesaid 
disputes, applicant no.1 had filed a petition 
under Section 9 of the Hindu Marriage Act 
before the competent Court at Bengaluru 
seeking restitution of conjugal rights as the 
applicant no.1 is living at Bengaluru and 
respondent no.2 is living at Muzzaffarnagar; 
that, the said petition was allowed and 
decreed in favour of applicant no.1 by the 
learned Principal Senior Civil Judge, 
Bengaluru vide judgment and decree dated 
17.08.2019; that, despite the said decree, 
respondent no.2 neither resumed 
cohabitation with applicant no.1 nor 
challenged the decree before any higher 
forum, and therefore the same has attained 
finality; that, respondent no.2 had also 
initiated proceedings under the Protection of 
Women from Domestic Violence Act against 
the present applicants, regarding non￾return of her stridhan allegedly retained by 
applicant no.1 and his family members,
which were dismissed by the learned 5th 
Chief Judicial Magistrate, Dehradun vide 
judgment and order dated 28.03.2023.
6. Learned counsel further submits that 
respondent no.2 had also instituted 
proceedings under Section 13 of the Hindu 
Marriage Act on substantially similar 
allegations; that, the said proceedings were 
also dismissed vide judgment and order 
dated 22.07.2024. It is further submitted that applicant no.1 had lodged an F.I.R. 
under Section 420 I.P.C. at Police Station 
Whitefield, Bengaluru against respondent 
no.2 alleging that she had taken away 
jewellery and other valuable articles 
belonging to applicant no.1 and his family 
members while leaving the matrimonial 
home without their knowledge or consent; 
that, in the said case, respondent no.2 was 
granted anticipatory bail by the competent 
Court. Learned counsel contends that, after 
having failed in the aforesaid proceedings 
before different forums, respondent no.2 
has now filed the present complaint under 
Section 316(2) B.N.S. only with the 
intention of harassing the applicants and 
exerting pressure upon them on the basis of 
false and unfounded allegations.
7. Learned counsel for the applicants 
further submits that detailed objections 
dated 15.01.2025 were filed before the 
Court below, specifically pointing out that 
the complaint was not maintainable in law 
and constituted a misuse of the judicial 
process. It was also stated that respondent 
no.2 had been repeatedly instituting false 
and vexatious proceedings against the 
applicants after failing to secure relief in 
earlier litigations. Learned counsel submits 
that a bare perusal of the impugned 
summoning order dated 22.04.2026 would 
show that the learned Magistrate has 
merely reproduced the allegations contained 
in the complaint, the statements recorded 
under Sections 223 and 225 BNSS, and the 
contents of the legal notice allegedly issued 
by respondent no.2. 
8. He would further submit that the 
learned Magistrate has failed to record any 
independent judicial satisfaction or reasons demonstrating how the essential ingredients 
of the offence punishable under Section 
316(2) BNS are made out against the 
applicants. Therefore, the impugned 
summoning order and the consequential 
criminal proceedings are liable to be set 
aside as being unsustainable in law.
9. This Court finds some substance in 
the submission made by learned counsel for 
the applicant, which definitely requires 
deliberation.
10. Issue notice to respondent no.2, 
returnable at an early date.
11. Steps to be taken within one week.
12. List the matter after receipt of the 
service report. 
13. Considering the submissions 
advanced by learned counsel for the 
applicant, it is directed that, till the next 
date of listing, further proceedings of Misc. 
Complaint No. 8746 of 2024, pending in the 
court of learned 5th Additional Chief 
Judicial Magistrate, Haridwar shall remain 
stayed.
9. Stay application (I.A. No.1 of 2026) 
stands disposed of.
