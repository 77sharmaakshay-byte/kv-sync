COURT’S OR JUDGES’S ORDERS
AO No.99 of 2025
Hon’ble G. Narendar, C.J.
Hon’ble Alok Mahra, J.
Heard Mr. Sanpreet Singh Ajmani and Ms. Amitoz 
Kaur, learned counsel for the appellant. 
2. Learned counsel for the appellant would submit that a 
decree came to be passed by Commercial Court, Delhi and,
subsequently, the execution came to be filed under Order 21 of 
CPC before the Commercial Court, Haldwani and, by order 
dated 20.12.2024, the appellant was directed to submit the 
details of his property for satisfaction of the decree in terms of 
Order 21 Rule 41(3) CPC and hearing was adjourned to 
07.02.2025. 
3. It is the case of the appellant that he had engaged the 
services of the counsel, namely, Mr. D.K. Singh, but the 
counsel failed to appear before the Court and the Court, taking 
note of the absence of any representation, proceeded to invoke 
the provisions of Sub-Rule 3 of Rule 41 of Order 21 of CPC 
and directed issuance of warrant of arrest for detention of the 
appellant in civil prison for a period of six months. It further 
proceeded to issue warrant, process of attachment of the 
property of the judgment debtor and directed that the matter be 
listed on 24.03.2025. It is the case of the appellant that,
immediately upon coming to know about the order passed, the 
appellant made an application for recall of the order dated 
07.02.2025. Matter came up for consideration on 24.03.2025 
and, on 24.03.2025, the Court proceeded to pass the following 
order. 
“Application for permission to take steps for issuance of 
warrant & process of attachment. Heard. Allowed in 
view of order dt. 07/2/25. 
Application by JD seeking recall of order dt. 07/2/25.
Objections if any be given on next date. Put up on 
22/4/25 for hearing. Order dt. 07/2/25 be complied”
4. On a reading of the order, it is apparent that the order is 
by a prejudiced mind. Firstly, the provision of Sub-Rule 3 provides for detention for a period of only three months,
whereas the Court has erroneously ordered detention for six 
months; that apart, Court has failed to see that the party has 
acted with alacrity by filing an application for re-call. The 
Court while ordering filing of objections on the same, at the 
same time, directed re-issuance of the warrant after the party 
has appeared before the Court. If the petitioner is arrested it 
would certainly be a fait accompli, as once the warrant is
executed, nothing would remain to be considered with the 
application for recalling the earlier order. 
5. Learned counsel for the appellant submits that if 
opportunity had been given to the appellant, the appellant
would have complied with the command of the order dated 
24.12.2024; that, the coercive orders passed by this Court have 
now caused piquant situation whereby the appellant is virtually 
prevented from approaching the same court to comply with the 
direction. 
6. In that view and in view of the submissions that the
appellant is willing to file his objections and also list of 
properties as directed by the trial Court, the execution of the 
order dated 24.03.2025 is stayed, subject to the condition that 
the appellant shall submit the list of properties to the Executing 
Court on the next date of hearing i.e. 22.04.2025. In the event, 
appellant fails to submit the list of properties, held by him, or 
means to satisfy the debt, the protection granted today shall 
stand revoked automatically. 
7. In the event, such a list is submitted or there is 
compliance with the order dated 24.03.2025, the protection 
granted under instant order shall continue till further orders. 
8. Issue notice to the respondent.
9. List this case on 20.05.2025.
(Alok Mahra, J.) (G. Narendar, C.J.)
