1. These are applications filed on behalf of the Appellants seeking 
suspension of sentence and grant of bail during pendency of Criminal 
Appeal Nos.418 of 2025 and 419 of 2025, arising out of judgment 
dated 05.06.2025 and sentencing order dated 10.06.2025 passed by the 
learned Sessions Judge, Champawat in Sessions Trial No. 04 of 2021. 
2. Since both appeals arise out of the same judgment and the 
applications for suspension of sentence involve common questions, 
they are being heard together and are being decided by this common 
order. The impugned judgment has resulted in conviction of the 
Appellants under Sections 304 Part I, 324 and 352 IPC, with the 
principal sentence being 10 years’ simple imprisonment.

4. An FIR came to be registered on the same day, initially invoking

applications seek release on bail during pendency of the appeals, and

relief.

questions of fact and law, particularly with regard to the correctness of 
conviction under Section 304 Part I IPC and the applicability of Section 
34 IPC. 
8. Learned Counsel would submit that the learned trial court itself 
did not find the case fit for conviction under Section 302 IPC and 
altered the conviction to Section 304 Part I IPC, which indicates that

Ashish Naithani J.23. At the outset, it is to be noted that while considering such 
applications, this Court is not required to undertake a detailed re￾appreciation of evidence. However, a limited examination is necessary 
to ascertain whether the appeals raise arguable issues and whether 
continued incarceration is justified in the facts of the case. 
24. The learned trial court has not found the case fit for conviction 
under Section 302 IPC and has instead convicted the Appellants under 
Section 304 Part I IPC. This, prima facie, indicates that the question of 
nature of intention and applicability of the appropriate penal provision 
remains an arguable issue in appeal. 
25. From a prima facie reading of the material on record, the case 
proceeds on allegations of a joint assault. At the same time, the issue of 
individual role attribution and the extent of shared intention under 
Section 34 IPC is a matter which would require detailed consideration 
at the stage of final hearing. 
26. The case of the State rests, inter alia, on the testimony of an 
injured witness and supporting material including recovery and 
scientific evidence. Without expressing any final opinion, this Court 
finds that the scope, consistency and evidentiary value of such material 
are issues which are open to examination in appeal. 
27. It is also not in dispute that the Appellants were on bail during the 
course of trial and there is no allegation that they misused the liberty 
granted to them. 
28. The Appellants have already undergone a substantial period of 
incarceration and, having regard to the pendency of criminal appeals, it

29. At the same time, the offence in question has resulted in the loss 
of life, and the seriousness of the offence cannot be ignored while 
considering the prayer for bail. 
30. Thus, this Court is required to balance the nature of accusation, 
the manner in which conviction has been recorded, the arguable issues 
raised in appeal, and the period of incarceration already undergone. 
31. Having considered the aforesaid factors, this Court is of the view 
that the case for grant of bail deserves to be considered on the basis of 
the role attributed to each of the Appellants. 
32. Without expressing any final opinion on the merits, this Court 
finds that the case of Appellant Rakesh Chaudhary stands on a 
relatively different footing at this stage, insofar as the question of 
individual role and extent of liability is concerned, which constitutes an 
arguable issue in appeal. 
33. However, insofar as Appellant Ajay Deopa is concerned, 
considering the nature of allegations, the conviction recorded, and the 
overall circumstances, this Court is not inclined to grant bail at this 
stage.

Chaudhary in Criminal Appeal No. 418 of 2025 is allowed.
