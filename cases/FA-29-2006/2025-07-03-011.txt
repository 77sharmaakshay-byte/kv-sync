1. Matter is remitted by the Hon’ble Apex Court by the 
judgment and order dated 18.05.2023 for fresh 
adjudication. Yesterday, the instant second appeal was 
admitted on the issues framed by the Hon’ble Apex Court 
in para 34 of the judgment.
2. Mr. Yogesh Tiwari, learned Standing Counsel argued 
that instant second appeal is arising out of the original suit 
filed by the plaintiffs/respondents Birendra Kumar and 
Ravi Kumar in O.S. No. 174 of 1984 which was instituted 
for seeking perpetual/mandatory injunction. 
3. The basis of the institution of suit was that the 
plaintiffs were recorded in the revenue record as 
Bhumidhar and they inherited the right from his father 
who became Sirdar/Bhumidhar as per the U.P. Tenancy 
Act. The father of the plaintiffs got right from one Manohar 
Lal who was recorded as Asami and rights of plaintiffs 2
were accrued from 1948 entries of the revenue records.
On 31.12.1981, the District Magistrate expunged all the 
entries which was in respect of the land in which the 
plaintiffs claiming the right which, in fact, was initially 
leased out to one John Vaughan in 1924 which was 
expired in the year of 1954. 
In the plaint, it was contended that the plaintiffs got 
information of the order expunging their entries on 
20.01.1982, as contended in para 2 of the plaint. It was
also contended that the order, expunging the entries was 
also challenged before the Revenue Court. In para 15 of 
the plaint it was also contended that the land in question 
is not under the Government Grants Act and in para 16 it 
is contended that the plaintiffs got information from third 
party that the Government is initiating eviction proceeding 
and this information was received on 20.08.1983 which, in 
fact, was the cause of action of the institution of the suit. 
4. In response to the plaint, written statement was also 
filed. In para 17 question of jurisdiction was raised 
particularly by giving reference to relief 2(b) wherein a 
relief was sought to restore the plaintiff’s name in 
Khatauni and other revenue records which was struck off 
by the District Magistrate, Nainital by order dated 
19.12.1981. It was specifically stated in the written 
statement that the relief, as sought, can only be 
adjudicated by the Revenue Court as the same pertains to 
the entries in the revenue records. It was further 
contended in the written statement that the land in 
question, as leased to Mr. John Vaughan was granted 
under the Government Grants Act.
5. Mr. Tiwari, learned Standing Counsel further submits 
that the land in question, by virtue of lease, was given to3
Mr. John Vaughan for specific purposes and uses, subject 
to this condition that it can be transferred with the
approval of the District Commissioner. 
6. In the suit as many as in total 9 issues were framed 
and issue no. 1 and 2 goes to the root of the matter which 
was answered in favour of the plaintiffs by observing that 
the plaintiffs are Bhumidhar.
7. Mr. Tiwari, learned Standing Counsel submits that 
issue no. 4 as framed by the trial court was whether the 
order of the Collector, Nainital dated 31.12.1981 is illegal, 
void or ineffecting, as alleged in para 10 and 11 of the 
plaint. By referring this issue, Mr. Tiwari submits that issue 
no. 4 was surprisingly answered in favour of the plaintiffs, 
though, for deciding issue no. 4, the trial court have no 
jurisdiction and it was completely prerogative of the 
Revenue Court for which the plaintiff already approached
the Revenue Court which they admit in the plaint. The 
observation and finding of trial court in respect of Issue 
no. 4 which was answered by the Trial Court in favour of 
the plaintiffs was already quoted in the previous order 
dated 26.06.2025.
Mr. Tiwari further submits that the trial court while 
decreeing the suit of the plaintiffs have not properly dealt 
with issue no. 4 as well as issue no. 1 and 2 and argued 
that no such issue was framed with regard to the point of 
jurisdiction and without framing the issue with regard to 
the jurisdiction the relief, as sought, in the plaint was 
decreed by the Trial Court. He submit that decreeing suit 
in respect of relief no. (b) is completely without 
jurisdiction.4
8. Against judgment decreeing the suit, first appeal was 
preferred by the State and the same was also dismissed 
by confirming the order of the trial court and being 
aggrieved, the instant second appeal has been preferred. 
It is submitted by Mr. Tiwari, learned Standing Counsel 
that in addition to the issues as framed by the Hon’ble 
Apex Court in para 34 of the judgments there are other 
substantial questions of law which were already proposed 
at the time of filing of the second appeal. Mr. Tiwari by 
proposing substantial questions of law submits that instant 
second appeal has to be admitted on issue no. 1 and 4, as 
proposed at the time of filing of the appeal.
9. Yesterday, Mr. Piyush Garg submits that the instant
second appeal has to be decided on the issues, as framed 
by the Hon’ble Apex Court in para 34 of its judgment, and, 
as such, no new fresh question of law can be framed. On 
such submission this Court admitted the second appeal
only on the issues framed by the Hon’ble Apex Court.
10. Whether any fresh substantial question of law can be 
framed or not, it is settled principle of law that even at the 
stage of final hearing the same can be framed. In 
response to this Mr. Piyush Garg, advanced his arguments 
by supporting the judgment passed by the trial court and 
first appellate court. 
11. Mr. D.S. Patni, learned Senior Advocate submits that 
the Collector by an executive order cannot expunge the 
revenue entries and this issue goes to the root of the 
matter since suit preferred by plaintiffs-respondents is 
based on this issue. Let the State may answer to this. 
12. On the joint request, put up this matter on 
09.07.2025 immediately after fresh. It is made clear that 5
since the matter is old and remanded by the Hon’ble 
Supreme Court with the direction to decide afresh within 
time bond manner therefore the matter will be heard on 
day to day basis.
