3. Heard learned counsel for the parties on 
bail application. 
4. This is an appeal filed under Section 
374(2) Cr.P.C. against conviction recorded by 
learned I Additional Sessions Judge, Dehradun, 
vide judgment and order dated 22.11.2012, in 
S.T. No. 173 of 2007. By the said judgment, 
appellant has been convicted for offences 
punishable under Section 302/34, 364/34 and 
201/34 IPC. He was sentenced to imprisonment 
for life along with fine of Rs.1000/- under 
Section 302/34 IPC; 10 years rigorous 
imprisonment along with fine of Rs.500/- under 
Section 364/34 IPC, and rigorous imprisonment 
for two years along with fine of Rs.500/- under 
Section 201/34 IPC. All the sentences were 
directed to run concurrently. 
5. Challenging his conviction, the appellant has filed this appeal, and has sought bail during 
the pendency of present appeal. 
6. It is contended that appellant has already 
suffered incarceration for more than 16 years 
without remission, and 19 years incarceration 
with remission. It is further contended that it is 
a case of circumstantial evidence, and 
appellant’s conviction is based on last seen 
evidence. However, PW2 and PW6 both stated 
that appellant was last seen in the company of 
the deceased at 12:30 P.M. on 09.08.2005. 
There is evidence on record that the deceased 
had telephonically contacted PW8 at 09:15 P.M., 
in the same night, which belies the story of last 
seen evidence. It is further stated that except 
the testimony of PW2 and PW6, there is no other 
evidence to nail the appellant in the case. It is 
further contended that PW2 is servant of the 
family of the deceased, while PW6 is the 
daughter of the informant. Therefore, they 
cannot be termed as independent witnesses. 
7. Learned counsel for the appellant further 
submits that there is delay of two days in 
lodging the FIR; the name of the accused, as 
mentioned in the FIR, is Dinesh, whereas the 
name of the appellant is Rajneesh, which is 
corroborated from the school certificates, which 
aspect has been dealt with by the trial court in 
paragraph 30 of its judgment. 8. Per contra, learned State counsel opposed 
the bail of appellant, and submits that the 
appellant has a criminal history, and, therefore, 
he does not deserve any leniency, and if 
enlarged on bail, he may again commit similar 
offence. 
9. Having regard to the period of 
incarceration undergone by the appellant, we 
are inclined to release the appellant on bail. 
10. Accordingly, the bail application is allowed. 
11. Let the appellant / applicant-Dinesh @ 
Rajneesh be released on bail, during the 
pendency of present criminal appeal, on his 
executing a personal bond and furnishing two 
reliable sureties, each of like amount, to the 
satisfaction of the Court concerned. The 
appellant’s release on bail shall also be subject 
to the condition that he shall report before the 
Police State, Bhawanpur, District Meerut, once in 
each fortnight, and shall not leave the District 
Meerut without prior permission of the 
concerned Magistrate. 
12. List the appeal for hearing on 28.12.2023.
