Upon hearing the learned Counsel, the Court made the following Order: 
(per Sri Rakesh Thapliyal, J.)
1. Shri T.A. Khan, Sr. Advocate, for the petitioners, will 
supply a copy of the writ petition to Ms. Anjali Bhargava, counsel 
for respondent no. 3 – Gram Sabha. 
2. Since respondents no. 5 – 8 are pro forma respondents, 
therefore, at this stage, there is no need to issue notice to them.
3. Petitioners – plaintiffs filed a suit no. 22/107 of 1985 – 86 
under Section 229-B of the U.P.Z.A. & L.R. wherein on 26.06.1986
temporary injunction was granted and petitioners were also 
given exemption under Section 80 (2) of the CPC. This order 
dated 26.06.1986 was made absolute by the order dated 
05.11.1986 with a direction to the plaintiffs – petitioners to make 
Forest Department, defendant. Against the order dated 
05.11.1986, Z.A. Appeal No. 35 / 106 of 1986-87 was preferred 
before the Additional Commissioner (Judicial) and the Additional 2
Commissioner (Judicial) allowed the said appeal and set aside the 
order dated 05.11.1986 vide the order dated 10.12.1990. Against 
this order, plaintiffs – petitioners preferred Second Appeal No. 93 
of 1990-1991 before the Board of Revenue, which was dismissed 
on 11.07.1994. Thereafter, defendant no. 1 moved an application 
under Order 7 Rule 11 CPC, which was dismissed by the 
Assistant Collector vide order dated 19.01.2006, against which 
Z.A. Appeal No. 42 / 2005-06 was filed by defendant no. 1, which 
was also dismissed on 11.07.2007. Against this order dated 
11.07.2007, defendant no. 1 preferred a Revision No. 03 of 2007 
before the Additional Chief Revenue Commissioner, which was 
also dismissed on 25.07.2009. Thereafter, the defendant no. 1 
moved an application for adjournment on the ground that the 
plaintiffs filed a review application before the Board of Revenue 
which is pending, however, that application was rejected on 
15.04.2010 and by the same order the Assistant Collector closed 
the opportunity of defendant no. 1 to file written statement. 
Against this order dated 15.04.2010, defendant no. 1 filed Z.A. 
Appeal No. 31 / 2009-10 before the Commissioner, which was 
also dismissed on 14.02.2013. Then, Second Appeal No. 136 of 
2014-15 was preferred by defendant no. 1 before the Board of 
Revenue and the Board of Revenue vide order dated 26.05.2015 
directed to file written statement by 09.06.2015 and pursuant to 
the said order, written statement was filed by defendant no. 1. 
Against the order dated 26.05.2015, plaintiffs filed writ petition 
before this High Court being WPMS No. 1287 of 2015. The writ 
petition was allowed by the Coordinate Bench of this Court vide 
order dated 03.12.2021 and order of the Board of Revenue dated 
26.05.2021 was quashed, however, liberty was granted to 
defendant no. 1 to move an application for conversion of second 
appeal to revision and if such application is filed, the same shall 3
be considered as per law. Thereafter, application was moved and 
the second appeal was converted to revision no. 136/2014-2015. 
The Board of Revenue by order dated 08.05.2023 allowed the 
revision and set aside the order dated 14.02.2023 as well as order 
dated 15.04.2010 passed by the Assistant Collector, First Class. By 
this order the Board of Revenue granted an opportunity to file 
written statement by the next date and the trial court was 
directed to expedite the suit within a period of three months. 
4. Now being aggrieved by the order dated 08.05.2023 passed 
by the Board of Revenue, present writ petition has been preferred 
by the petitioner. 
5. Mr. Piyush Garg, counsel for the petitioner, submits that 
since by order dated 10.12.1990 passed by the Additional 
Commissioner, temporary injunction order dated 15.11.1986 was 
set aside whereby order granting temporary injunction dated 
26.08.1986 was made absolute and there was no exemption under 
Section 80 (2) CPC because once this order dated 15.11.1986 was 
set aside then whole order dated 26.08.1986 will go whereby 
exemption from 80 (2) as well as temporary injunction was 
granted. 
6. Mr. Piyush Garg, counsel for the petitioner, submits that 
after the order dated 10.12.1990, since the order granting 
exemption under Section 80 (2) CPC came to an end and, 
therefore, suit was not maintainable and that was the reason 
defendant moved an application under Order 7 Rule 11 CPC.
7. This submission is strongly opposed by Mr. T.A. Khan, Sr. 
Advocate, for the petitioner. 
8. For deciding the matter, version of the respondent no. 1 is 
required and only for that purpose, ten days’ time is granted to 4
the respondent no. 1 to file counter affidavit and thereafter, one 
week’s time is granted to the petitioner to file rejoinder affidavit, 
thereafter. 
9. List this case on 18.08.2023. 
10. Meanwhile further proceeding of case no. 22/107 of 1985-86 
pending in the Court of Assistant Collector, First Class, 
Kaladungi, is stayed till 18.08.2023.
