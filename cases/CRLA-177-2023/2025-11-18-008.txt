Hon’ble G. Narendar, C.J.
Hon’ble Subhash Upadhyay, J.
Mr. Rajendra Dobhal, learned Senior 
Counsel assisted by Mr. Shubhang Dobhal, 
learned counsel for the appellant/applicant.
2. Mr. J. S. Virk, learned Deputy Advocate 
General for the State of Uttarakhand. 
3. The appellant is seeking short term bail 
for a period of 180 days in ST No.87 of 2018 
wherein the appellant/applicant came to be 
convicted for the offences punishable, among 
other offences, under Sections 302 and 307 
IPC, registered at P.S. Sitarganj, District 
Udham Singh Nagar.
4. This Court has been consistently 
disposing of such applications by the following 
order:-
“Learned Deputy Advocate General would 
take this Court through Rule 504 of the 
Uttarakhand Jail Manual (Rules), 2023, which 
deals with ‘Parole & Furlough’. Rule 504(ii) deals 
with objectives of releasing a prisoner on parole 
and furlough, and the same are detailed in 
clauses (a) to (h). Rule 504(ii)(c) enables the 
authorities to release the prisoner on parole or 
furlough to maintain and develop his self￾confidence. 
Learned Deputy Advocate General would 
further take this Court through Rule 512 of the 
Uttarakhand Jail Manual Rules, 2023 to contend 
that such applications are to be placed before the 
competent Authority, i.e. I.G. (Prisons), and the 
competent Authority is required to consider the 
release of the convict in terms of the provisions 
of Rule 512 of the Uttarakhand Jail Manual Rules, 
2023.
In that view, the Short Term Bail 
Application is disposed of by granting liberty to 
the applicant / appellant to submit an Application to the competent Authority / I.G. (Prisons), and 
if such an Application for release on parole is 
preferred by the applicant / convict, the same 
shall be considered and disposed of by the 
competent Authority within one week.
In the event such application is rejected, it 
is open to the applicant/ appellant to seek 
remedy in accordance with law. 
Registry to forward the copy of this order 
to the competent authority by Email and with a 
copy to the learned Deputy Advocate General. 
In view of the short window of time 
available, we request the learned Deputy 
Advocate General to communicate the orders.”
5. Learned counsel for the appellant would 
place reliance on an order passed by a Co￾ordinate Bench, of which one of us was a 
member, in Criminal Appeal No.210 of 2019,
wherein the Co-ordinate Bench took note of 
the age and adverted to the medical condition 
of appellant in paragraph no.8 and has 
concluded in paragraph no.9 as under:
“8. In fact, during the course of hearing, 
letter addressed to Jail Superintendant by the 
Senior Resident, Cardiology Department, AIIMS 
Rishikesh, has also been placed for the perusal of 
the Court. Let it be taken on record. It records as 
follows:-
“This is regarding Patient Dalbeer 
Singh, 75/M, UHID: 20250044893, on 
11.04.2024, admitted in cardiology unit 2, 
on 11.04.2024, under Dr Barun Kumar, 
prisoner at CENTRAL JAIL, SITARGANJ, 
UTTARAKHAND, with a diagnosis of 
CAD/CCS III/RECURRENT SYNCOPE (2 
Degree AV BLOCK)/NSR/LVEF 60%. The 
patient needs LBB pacemaker (DUAL 
CHAMBER PERMANENT 3 PACEMAKER- MRI 
COMPATIBLE). Its estimated cost is 
approximately 2.5 Lakh Rupees. I 
sincerely request you to look into the 
matter and arrange for finances of the 
same.”
9. The health condition, which is 
deteriorating of the appellant-Dalveer Singh, is a 
circumstance, which commands this Court to 
release the appellant-Dalveer Singh on interim 
bail for a period of 6 months.”6. Based on the said finding bail application 
of the 80 years old was considered to enable 
him to have treatment in a hospital of his 
choice and in the company of his family 
memebers.
7. In the instant case, learned Deputy 
Advocate General would submit that the 
appellant has been admitted to AIIMS, 
Rishikesh and he is being treated both at 
AIIMS Rishikesh and Sushila Tiwari Hospital, 
Haldwani for a coronary condition. 
8. In that view, we are of the opinion that 
considering the age of the appellant i.e. 65 
years old, we deem it appropriate to direct the 
Jail Authority concerned to permit any one 
family member to remain with the appellant in 
the hospital and also permit the appellant to 
have home cooked food and other articles of 
comfort may also be provided with the 
permission of the Jail Authority.
9. IA No.6 of 2025 stands ordered 
accordingly. Notwithstanding the disposal, we 
grant liberty to the appellant to make 
appropriate application under Rule 504 of the 
Uttarakhand Jail Manual (Rules), 2023, as 
noted supra, and seek for parole.
10. If such an application is made, the same 
shall be considered and disposed of by the 
competent authority within two weeks.
11. List this case in due course.
