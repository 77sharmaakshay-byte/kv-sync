grant of bail during the pendency of Criminal Jail Appeal filed by the 
Appellant Sandeep, who stands convicted under Sections 363, 366A,

2. Heard learned counsel for the parties and perused the records.
3. Learned counsel for the Appellant submitted that the State has failed to 
prove its case beyond a reasonable doubt, and the judgment of conviction 
suffers from serious factual and legal infirmities. 
4. It is contended that the FIR was lodged after an unexplained delay of five 
days, even though the younger brother of the victim was allegedly an 
eyewitness to the occurrence. Yet, his statement was neither recorded by 
the Investigating Officer nor was he produced during trial, which suggests 
suppression of vital evidence.
5. It is contended that the failure to bring forth any impartial witness 
significantly erodes the credibility of the State’s version. Specifically, 
reference is made to her deposition regarding the use of a handkerchief to 
cause unconsciousness, without any corresponding medical or forensic 
investigation.
6. The learned counsel also raised doubts about how the site plan was 
prepared, arguing that the Investigating Officer (PW7) had no independent 
knowledge of the location and acted solely on the version of the victim, 
who admitted during cross-examination that she had not revisited the place 
of confinement, thereby raising questions about the authenticity of the 
mapping.
7. The age of the victim was also challenged, citing that the victim claimed 
to be studying in Class 9th at the time of the incident, whereas PW5 (the 
victim’s former school teacher) deposed that she had passed Class 7th and 
left school in April 2021, months before the alleged incident in September 
2021. No school admission record or certificate from the new school has 
been placed on record to verify her actual age.

10. Learned State Counsel, on the other hand, opposed the bail application 
and submitted that the judgment of conviction is based on a thorough 
appreciation of oral and documentary evidence. No case for grant of bail is 
made out. 
11. It was argued that the offence committed by the Appellant is of a grave 
and heinous nature, involving the repeated sexual exploitation of a minor 
girl. The victim's evidence is consistent and credible, and the trial court 
has provided valid reasons for relying on her testimony.
12. It was further submitted that the medical evidence, coupled with the 
circumstances surrounding the recovery of the victim from Bhopal and the 
arrest of the accused from Haridwar, aligns with the State’s version. The 
omissions or irregularities in investigation, even if assumed, cannot 
undermine the entire case when the victim’s testimony substantiates the 
core allegations.
13. It was also pointed out that the trial court has addressed the issue of 
delay in FIR, and found that the delay was understandable given the 
sensitive nature of the case and the minor status of the victim.
14. At the outset, it must be noted that the Appellant has been convicted for 
offences which involve not only the abduction of a minor girl but also her 
repeated sexual exploitation over an extended period. The victim’s 
statement (PW1) clearly narrates the sequence of events, her forced

15. The delay in filing FIR has been explained in the evidence and 
judgment of the trial court, and this Court is not inclined to reappreciate 
the entire evidence at the bail stage. Moreover, the non-examination of the 
brother or independent witnesses, although noteworthy, does not vitiate 
the trial when the victim's testimony is found to be consistent and credible. 
16. The victim’s statement is corroborated by other circumstances, such as 
her recovery from Bhopal, away from her residence, and her identification 
of the accused. The alleged contradiction about the site map and the place 
of confinement may raise issues relevant to the appeal but do not appear to 
destroy the State’s case for the purpose of suspension of sentence.
17. The issue relating to the age of the victim has been addressed by the 
trial court. The discrepancy in the class she was studying in, and absence 
of documents from her current school, are matters for final adjudication 
and cannot be a conclusive basis to grant bail at this stage, particularly 
when the victim is a minor based on available documents and oral 
testimony.
18. The Appellant has relied heavily on the fact that he has served more 
than four years in custody and has completed one-third of the sentence. 
While the period of incarceration is relevant, it must be weighed against 
the gravity of the offence and the nature of the conviction. 
19. In cases involving sexual offences against children, the courts must 
exercise great caution before granting bail post-conviction, as such crimes 
deeply affect the victim’s dignity, physical and emotional well-being, and 
public confidence in the justice system.
20. This Court finds no compelling reason to take a view different from 
that of the learned trial court at this stage. The judgment of conviction

ORDER
In view of the above discussion and considering the serious 
nature of the offence, the age of the victim, the strength of prosecution 
evidence as assessed by the trial court, and the absence of any 
exceptional circumstances, this Court does not find it to be a fit case for 
grant of bail.
Accordingly, the bail application is rejected.
