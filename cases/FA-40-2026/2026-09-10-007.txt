 Heard learned counsel for the parties and 
perused the record.
 This is an appeal against the judgment 
and decree dated 24.06.2025, passed in 
Original Suit No.5 of 2024, Sanjay Kumar 
Vs. Vijay Kumar and others, by the court of 
Civil Judge (Sr. Div.), Bageshwar, by which, 
the appellant has been restrained from 
interfering into the construction that is being 
raised by the respondent in the property-in￾dispute. 
 Today, learned counsel for the 
respondent submits that the construction 
has been completed; some minor works is 
required to be done, which includes opening 
of ceiling shuttering, internal plaster and 
placing a shutter, but he would submit that 
due to status quo order with regard to nature 
of construction over the property-in-question 
passed by this Court on 24.07.2026, this 
could not be done by the respondent. He 
seeks permission of the Court to vary the status quo order so as to permit the 
respondent to open the ceiling shuttering, to 
complete the internal plaster and placing a 
shutter on the construction. 
 Learned counsel for the appellants 
submits that the respondent is not fair with 
the Court. In objection to the delay 
condonation application, in para 4, the 
respondent has stated that the impugned 
decree has already been satisfied and 
execution has been completed. Reference has 
also been made to the order dated 
16.06.2026, passed in Civil Execution No.7 
of 2025, Sanjay Kumar Vs. Vijay Kumar and 
others, by the court of Civil Judge (Sr. Div.), 
Bageshwar (“the execution case”), which is 
part of the objection that is filed by the 
respondent on the delay condonation 
application. This order dated 16.06.2026 of 
the execution case records that respondent 
has completed the construction. The 
construction of lintel and wall was done in
the presence of the court’s Ameen and police 
force and nothing remains to be done in the 
execution. 
 It is argued that, if it is so, why should 
now the respondent be permitted to carry 
out further construction? Now, if any change 
is permitted, it would have an effect of 
changing the status quo order, which can 
only be done by an application that may be 
filed in view of Order 39 Rule 4 of the Code of 
Civil Procedure, 1908.
 Learned counsel for the appellant would 
also submit that in the urgency application, 
the respondent has only stated that he needs
to do the internal plaster and remove the 
ceiling shuttering, but now, during the 
course of arguments one more task, which is 
placing of a shutter on the construction has 
also been added.  A few facts are admitted. The suit has 
been decreed against the appellants. The 
appeal has been filed with delay condonation 
application. Execution case was also filed. A 
copy of order dated 16.06.2026 of the 
execution case is on record, which records
that on that date, the respondent gave a 
statement that with the help of police and 
court’s Ameen the lintel and wall 
construction has been done and now, 
nothing is to be done at present, whatever is 
to be done that shall be done in future. In 
full satisfaction it was recorded that the 
decree has been executed. This is what the 
respondent has stated in his objection to the 
delay condonation application in para 4. 
 Undoubtedly, on mere asking, an 
injunction order may not be vacated. After all 
injunction order is granted after hearing the 
parties based on the material that is 
available on record. 
 Para 13 and 14 of this Court’s order 
dated 24.07.2026 are as below:- “13. At this stage, leraned counsel for the appellants 
would submit that now a decree stands against the 
defendants/appellants and if the effect and operation 
of the same shall not be stayed during the pendency 
of the suit then the rights of the 
defendant/appellants shall be prejudiced. He would 
be further submit that the construction is already 
over by the plaintiff/respondent over the property in 
question, hence status quo should be maintained on 
the property in question so as to protect the rights of 
the parties.
14. In such an eventuality, since the appeal is 
admitted and the matter is to be adjudicated on its 
own merit finally, it is provided as an interim 
measure, till the next date of listing the parties shall 
maintain status quo with regard to the nature of the 
construction over the property in question.”
 A bare perusal of the above makes it 
abundantly clear that the Court was made to 
understand that the decree has been 
executed and the construction is already 
over. Accordingly, the Court directed the parties to maintain the status quo with 
regard to nature of the construction over the 
property-in-question.
 The order dated 16.06.2026 of the 
execution case records that decree has been 
satisfied. It also records that some work is to 
be done in future. Largely, it is stated that 
lintel was laid and wall had been raised.
 In view of the foregoing discussion, this 
Court is of the view that the status quo order 
may be modified and the respondent may be 
permitted to complete the work because as 
stated status quo order was granted under 
the premise that the construction is over. 
 Therefore, this Court permits the 
respondent to do the internal plaster, open 
the ceiling shuttering and lay a shutter. This 
Court makes it clear that the respondent 
shall not make any alteration except what is 
permitted today and he shall file an affidavit 
to this effect before this Court along with 
photographs before the next date fixed. The 
status quo order dated 24.07.2026 is 
modified accordingly. 
 List for final hearing in due course.
