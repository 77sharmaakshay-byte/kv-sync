 The appellant has been convicted by the trial 
court for the offences punishable under Sections 
302/34, 307/34, 504/34 and 506/34 of IPC. Co￾accused Vishal has been exonerated of the charges 
leveled against him. It is the contention of learned 
counsel for the appellant that the conviction order, 
which was passed with the aid of Section 34 of IPC is 
illegal and unsustainable in the eyes of Law, in as 
much as, the co-accused Vishal has already been 
acquitted by the trial court and a person cannot be 
convicted taking the aid of Section 34 IPC. It is also 
submitted that two other co-accused are facing trial 
before the Juvenile Justice Board. 
 The sum and substance of learned counsel for 
the appellant is that Section 34 of IPC cannot be 
attracted in case of single accused. It is also the 
contention of learned counsel for the appellant that 
PW1, PW 2 and PW 3 have not corroborated the 
prosecution story. Medical evidence is also not in 
support of the same. The appellant is in jail since 
06.09.2014. The incident took place when the 
deceased along with the appellant & other co-accused 
were playing cricket. It was in the heat of sudden and 
grave provocation, in which Ranjeet (not the 
appellant) inflicted a blow of bat on the head of the 
victim. 
 Considering the facts of the case, this Court is 
of the opinion that bail should be granted to the 
appellant. 
 Accordingly, let the appellant Ankit @ Baba be 
released on bail during the pendency of this appeal on 
his executing a personal bond and furnishing two 
sureties each in the like amount to the satisfaction of 
C.J.M. concerned. 
 It is made clear that there is no stay against the 
fine which has been imposed on the appellant.
