Hon'ble Pankaj Purohit, J.
The petitioner has filed the writ petition with the 
following reliefs.
(i) “A suitable writ order or direction in the nature of 
mandamus commanding the respondent No. 1, 2 and 
3 to transfer the investigation of FIR No.29 of 2024 
under Section 306 of IPC, Police Station Nehru 
Colony District Dehradun (Annexure no.3 to the writ 
petition) to any independent investigation agency 
particularly C.B.C.I.D or C.B.I. from respondent no.4 
and direct the respondent no.5 or 6 to investigate 
the matter in accordance with law.”
2. Brief facts of the case are that the eldest son

of the petitioner namely Mukul Bhatt aged about 24 
years left for Delhi on 25.11.2023 at around 7:50 p.m. 
in a Taxi for railway station Dehradun. On the next 
morning i.e. 26.11.2023 when the petitioner called his 
son to enquire whether he has safely reached Delhi the 
call was picked by police of police station Nehru Colony 
who informed the petitioner that his son has died after 
he met with an accident by train. The petitioner 
thereafter rushed to Nehru Colony Police Station where 
he was informed that his son has been taken to 
Coronation Hospital, Dehradun. He was also informed 
that his son got cut under train near Hanuman Temple 
Mohkampur. As the petitioner believed that his son 
could not commit suicide he asked the Nehru Colony 
Police Station to register an FIR. After a preliminary 
enquiry he was informed by the police that he called 
one person named Piyush before his death and talked 
to him for about 20 minutes. He was also informed that 
he made more than 25 calls to Ashruti but the police 
did not register an FIR thereafter he moved an 
application to the S.S.P. which was also not paid any 
heed. Thereafter, he filed an application to the Judicial 
Magistrate under Section 156 (3) who in turn ordered 
to register an FIR. The petitioner suspects that the local 
police is conducting the investigation in a hazy manner,
therefore, he is before this Court to transfer the 
investigation to C.B.I./C.B.C.I.D.

5. Learned Counsel for the C.B.I. relying upon 
the counter affidavit states that it is a settled principle 
of law that the High Court should be slow in 
transferring each and every investigation to C.B.I and 
such orders should not be passed as a matter of 
routine or merely because a party has leveled some 
allegations against the local police. 
6. Learned counsel for the petitioner relying on 
the basis of rejoinder affidavit stated that the 
investigation in the present case has not been 
conducted properly and has been done in hasty 
manner which is clear from the fact that the police 
officials fail to register the FIR and also fail to provide
the progress report despite repeated request made to 
them. He further states that the police officials are 
themselves admitting that the entire data could not be 
recovered from the mobile phones of the deceased 
therefore, conclusions could not be drawn that the 
accused persons are not involved in abetting the 
suicide of the deceased.

7. He further relied in the case of Minor 
Irrigation & Rural Engineering Services U.P. and 
others Vs. Sahngoo Ram Arya and another 
(2002(5) SCC 521 in which the Hon’ble Apex Court 
observed that the High Court has power to order CBI 
enquiry if after considering the material on record it 
comes to a conclusion that such material discloses 
prima facie case calling for investigation by the CBI or 
any other similar agency. 
8. Having heard the submissions made by 
learned counsel for the parties and after perusal of the 
material available on record and taking into 
consideration that the alleged incident is of the year 
2023 and after going through the counter affidavit of 
respondent nos. 1 to 4 this Court is of the considered 
opinion that the police has conducted the investigation 
in a proper and lawful manner with due diligence. The 
police has conducted a thorough investigation in 
collecting the evidence viz: recovering the CDR, 
recovering the chats, duly taking the statements of 
Zomato delivery boy, GTM guards, GRP personnels and 
collecting the Google location of the deceased. The 
investigation conducted did not implicate any person in 
abetting the suicide of the deceased. Therefore, it 
cannot be said that any greater good will happen if the 
investigation is transferred to CBI/C.B.C.I.D. moreover 
Hon’ble Apex Court in multiple judgments has opined 
that the investigation should not be transferred to CBI 
in a routine manner and should only be transferred in 
exceptional situations where it become necessary to 
5UKHC010112012025
2026:UHC:10631
provide credibility to the investigation. Recently, in 
Royden Harold Buthello & Anr. Vs. State of Chattisgarh 
& Ors., SLP (Crl.) No. 2454 of 2022, decided on 
28.02.2023, the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India has 
declined to refer an investigation to CBI and observed 
that “That extraordinary power must be exercised 
sparingly, cautiously and in exceptional situations 
where it becomes necessary to provide credibility and 
instill confidence in investigations or where the incident 
may have national and international ramifications or 
where such an order may be necessary for doing 
complete justice and enforcing the fundamental rights. 
Otherwise, CBI would be flooded with a large number 
of cases and with limited resources, may find it difficult 
to properly investigate even serious cases and in the 
process lost it credibility and purpose with 
unsatisfactory investigations.” 
9. Accordingly, the writ petition is dismissed.
