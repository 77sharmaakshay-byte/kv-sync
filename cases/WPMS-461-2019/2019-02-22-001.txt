WPMS No. 461 of 2019 
Hon’ble Lok Pal Singh, J.
Mr. G.K. Verma, Advocate for the petitioner. 
Ms. Monika Pant, Standing Counsel for the 
respondents. 
Heard. 
Present writ petition has been filed seeking the 
following reliefs:- 
(i) Issue a writ order or direction in the nature of 
certiorari quashing the impugned order dated 
05.01.2019 passed by the respondent no. 3 (i.e. 
Annexure non. 3 to this writ petition). 
(ii) Issue a writ order or direction in the nature of 
mandamus directing stay the operation and effect of 
the impugned order dated 31.01.2019 passed by the 
respondent no. 3 (i.e. annexure no. 3 to this writ 
petition). 
In pursuance to the tender issued by the 
respondents, bid of the petitioner was accepted; after 
completion of the contract work, completion 
certificate has been issued in the favour of the 
petitioner and after verification of the bills, produced 
by the petitioner, payment was made to the 
petitioner. 
It is the contention of the learned counsel for 
the petitioner that after period of four years of the 
final payment to the petitioner, recovery of Rs. 
3,70,652.75/- has been issued against petitioner by 
respondent no. 3 vide order dated 31.01.2019 and 
petitioner is directed to deposit the same amount by 
25.02.2019 otherwise same shall be adjusted from 
the account of the petitioner’s firm. It is further 
contended that after completion of contract and final 
payment, contract does not subsist and the recovery 
notice dated 31.01.2019 has been passed without 
authority of law. 
Mrs. Monika Pant, Senior Standing Counsel 
for the respondents would submit that since there is 
an arbitration agreement in regard to the contract 
awarded to the petitioner, in such circumstances, the 
writ petition is not maintainable and if petitioner is 
aggrieved, he can invoke provision contained in 
Section 9 of the Arbitration Act for interim 
protection. In support of her submission, learned Senior Standing Counsel has placed reliance on the 
judgment of Division Bench of this Court rendered 
in SPA No. 945 of 2018 M/S Bharat Construction 
vs. State of Uttarakhand. 
The facts of the judgment M/s Bharat 
Construction (supra) are that the appellant/petitioner 
filed writ petition before the learned Single Judge 
seeking writ of mandamus directing the respondents 
to release payment to the petitioner in respect of the 
works done by the petitioner. The Division Bench 
dismissed the special appeal saying that there is an 
arbitration clause in the contract agreement, 
therefore petitioner may avail remedy available to 
him and affirm the order of the learned Single Judge. 
The facts of the present case are entirely 
different. In the present case, contract had been 
awarded to the petitioner, who completed the work 
awarded to him and after satisfying with the 
completion of work, final payment has been made to 
the petitioner. Since, after completion of work 
department has made final payment to the petitioner, 
the contract agreement automatically terminated, 
therefore, in view of this Court contract is not 
subsists on the date of passing of the impugned 
order. The petitioner cannot be relegated to avail 
remedy under section 9 of the Arbitration Act. The 
alternative remedy is discretion of the Court. 
 Having heard learned counsel for the parties, 
and also considering the facts and circumstances of 
the case, as an interim measure, it is directed that till 
the next date of listing, effect and operation of 
impugned order dated 31.01.2019 shall remain 
stayed. 
The submission of the learned Standing 
Counsel that writ petitioner is not maintainable is 
overruled. Learned counsel for the respondents to 
file counter affidavit within four weeks. 
List this case after four weeks. 
Let defect(s) be cured in the meanwhile. 
 (Lok Pal Singh, J.) 
Parul 22.02.2019
