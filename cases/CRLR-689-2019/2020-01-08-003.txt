List this Criminal Revision for final 
determination in the week commencing 17th
February, 2020, under the head of hearing. 
The revisionist is a convict for 
commission of offence under Sections 120-B, 
420, 467 and 468 IPC. The learned Judicial 
Magistrate vide its judgment dated 30th
November, 2013, had convicted the revisionist 
for the commission of offence under Section
120-B IPC and sentenced him to undergo a 
simple imprisonment of six months and a fine 
of Rs.500/- was imposed against him; for the 
offence under Section 420 IPC, the revisionist 
was sentenced to undergo simple 
imprisonment of three years and a fine of 
Rs.1,000/- was imposed upon him; for the 
offence under Section 467 IPC, the revisionist 
was sentenced to undergo simple 
imprisonment of three years and a fine of 
Rs.1,000/- was imposed upon him; and for the 
offence under Section 468 IPC, the revisionist 
was sentenced to undergo simple 
imprisonment of three years and a fine of 
Rs.1,000/- was imposed upon him. 
The said judgment of conviction 
rendered by the Trial Court on a challenge 
given in Criminal Appeal No. 40 of 2013, 
Ganesh Singh Tolia Vs. State of Uttarakhand 
was modified and the revisionist was directed 
to undergo the modified sentence of three months for the offence under Section 120B 
IPC; one year and six months for the offence 
under Section 420 IPC; one year and six 
months for the offence under Section 467 IPC; 
and one year and six months for the offence 
under Section 468 IPC. 
It has been observed by the learned 
counsel for the revisionist that the revisionist, 
who has been convicted for the offence by the 
Trial Court, on a challenge being given in the 
Appeal, the sentence later on stood modified 
by the Appellant Court. 
It has come on record that the 
revisionist has been shown to be on bail 
during the Trial as well as during the 
pendency of the Criminal Appeal. There is 
nothing on record as such to point that the 
revisionist has got any criminal antecedents or 
he has ever misused the bail granted to him by 
the Courts below.
In such an eventuality, the revisionist is 
directed to be released on bail subject to the 
condition of furnishing of his personal bond 
and two sureties, each of the like amount, to 
the satisfaction of the Magistrate concerned. 
However, it is made clear that the release of 
the revisionist would be subject to the 
depositing of the penalty as imposed by the 
Courts below, which will be a condition 
precedent prior to his release.
The bail application stands disposed of 
accordingly.
