2. Learned counsel for the applicant 
submits that the applicant never issued 
any cheque in favour of the complainant, 
and she is therefore not the “drawer” of 
the cheque as defined under Section 138 
of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 
1881. It is further submitted that, in the 
facts of the present case, the alleged 
cheques have been signed solely by the 
husband of the applicant, as evidenced 
by the copies of the cheques annexed to 
the application. Although, the account 
from which the cheques were drawn is a 
joint account, the signatures appearing 
thereon are only those of the husband 
and not of the applicant.
3. Learned counsel further submits 
that it is settled legal position that, 
under Section 138 of the N.I. Act, only 
the person who actually signs and issues 
the cheque i.e. the drawer, can be 
prosecuted and that a joint-account 
holder cannot be held liable unless the 
cheque has been signed by each and 
every account-holder and the applicant 
cannot be regarded as the drawer of the 
cheques in question. Consequently, no 
proceedings under Section 138 of the 
N.I. Act can legally lie against her, and 
the present complaint against her is 
liable to be quashed as being devoid of sanction of law and an abuse of the 
process of criminal courts.
4. Issue notice to the respondent, 
returnable at an early date.
5. List the matter after receipt of the 
service report.
6. Having considered the submissions 
advanced by learned counsel for the 
applicant, it is hereby directed that, till 
the next date of listing, further 
proceedings of Complaint Case No.171 of 
2025, pending before the Court of the 
learned Judicial Magistrate-IInd, 
Haridwar, shall remain stayed, qua the 
applicant.
7. Stay application (I.A. No.1 of 2026) 
stands disposed of.
