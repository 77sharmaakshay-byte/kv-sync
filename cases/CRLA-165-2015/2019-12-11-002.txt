Hon’ble Ravindra Maithani J. (Oral)
 Appellants Vimal Bairagi and Nishi Bairagi have been 
convicted and sentenced in Sessions Trial No. 81 of 2014, State vs. 
Vimal Bairagi and others, under Sections 498A and 302 IPC read 
with Section 34 IPC.
2. Both the appellants have filed applications for bail in the 
appeal. 
3. Heard learned counsel for the parties.
4. Learned counsel appearing for the applicant Vimal 
Bairagi would argue that the basis of conviction is dying declaration 
alone, which is not reliable for the following reasons:
(i) The deceased was 86% burnt and on the next day to the
date of the incident, her statement was recorded. On that 2
date, she was not in a position to give the dying 
declaration. 
(ii) The doctor, who certified about the condition of the 
deceased, is not on the top or end of the dying 
declaration. It is on the margin. It appears that the 
certificate was obtained after thought. 
(iii) All the fingers of the deceased were burnt, as has been 
stated by the Doctor, who conducted the postmortem 
and in such circumstances, how they may have affixed 
her thumb impression on the dying declaration? It also 
creates doubts about the dying declaration. 
It is argued that it is a fit case for bail. 
5. Learned counsel for the appellant Vimal Bairagi 
relied upon the principles of law as laid down in the case of 
Surinder Kumar vs. State of Haryana 2011 (10) SCC. In the case 
of Surinder Kumar (supra), the Hon’ble Supreme Court 
discussed the principles, which govern the acceptability of 
dying declaration and in para 11 of the judgment, reiterated 
the principles already laid down on the subject by the Court. 
6. On behalf of the appellant Nishi Bairagi, learned 
counsel for the appellant would argue that the appellant lived 
separately from the family of the deceased; there was a dispute 
of land; it is the appellant Nishi Bairagi, who took the deceased 
to the hospital and spent money on her treatment. Learned 
counsel would also argue that the dying declaration was 
recorded almost a month prior to the death of the deceased; 
there is no other witness of it; the incident happened after 13 
years of the marriage and there is no pairokar of the appellant. 
Hence, it is a fit case for bail.3
7. It is true that the learned court below relied upon 
the dying declaration of the victim, which was recorded on 
15.01.2014 by PW7 Mohan Singh Bisht, the Tehsildar. The 
question is as to whether the case for bail has been made out.
At this stage, much scrutiny of the evidence is also not desired.
8. A dying declaration, which does not contain 
certificate of the doctor, cannot be rejected on that sole ground 
so long as the person recording the dying declaration was 
aware of the fact as of the condition of the declarent to make 
such dying declaration. (See Laxman vs. State of Maharashtra, 
(2002) 6 SCC 710) Percentage of burn has less to do with the 
acceptability of dying declaration. In the case of Bhagwan vs. 
State of Maharashtra, (2019) 8 SCC 95, the Hon’ble Supreme 
Court, inter alia, held that the mere fact that the patient 
suffered 92% burn injuries would not stand in the way of 
patient giving a dying declaration which otherwise inspires 
the confidence of the Court and is free from tutoring, and can 
be found reliable. 
9. PW 6 Dr. Mohd. Junaid has categorically stated 
that he certified the condition of the deceased before the dying 
declaration is recorded and he also certified that while giving 
her statement, the deceased was fully conscious. PW 7 Mohan 
Sigh Bisht recorded the dying declaration. He also verified it. 
10. In so far as the thumb impression is concerned, in 
the postmortem report it is not recorded that all the fingers of 
the deceased were so burnt that the thumb impression could 
not have been obtained. 
11. Having considered all the circumstances, this 
Court is of the view that the appellants are not entitled to be 4
enlarged on bail. Hence the bails applications deserve to be 
rejected. 
12. The Bail applications are rejected.
