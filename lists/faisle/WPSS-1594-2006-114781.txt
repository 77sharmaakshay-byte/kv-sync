WPSS/1594/2006 of KULDEEP SINGH Vs STATE and ORS
WPSS/1594/2006 · 2013-05-08 · DISMISSED

HIGH COURT OF UTTARAKHAND AT NAINITAL
Writ Petition No.1594 of 2006 (S/S)
Kuldeep Singh
S/o late Shri Makar Singh ... Petitioner
Vs.
State of Uttarakhand & others ... Respondents
Hon'ble Sudhanshu Dhulia, J.
1. Heard Mr. Rajesh Sharma, Advocate for the petitioner and
Mr. Lalit Miglani, Brief Holder for the State.
2. The father of the petitioner was a Constable in Uttarakhand
Police, who died in harness on 30.10.2001 leaving behind his
wife, two sons and a daughter. At the time of his death, the
petitioner (i.e. one of the sons of the deceased) was 15 years of
age and the wife of the deceased (i.e. petitioner's mother) was
already employed in a government department. After attaining
the age of majority, the petitioner moved an application before
the authority concerned for compassionate appointment under
the provisions of U.P. Recruitment of Dependents of Government
Servants Dying-in-Harness Rules, 1974 (hereinafter referred to as the
Rules).
3. The State Government has vehemently objected the claim
of the petitioner that Rule 5 of the Rules specifically provides that
in case the spouse of the deceased Government servant is not
already employed under the Central Government or a State
Government, the dependants of the deceased Government
servant may be given a suitable employment on compassionate
ground. Rule 5 of the Rules is quoted hereunder:-
"5. Recruitment of a member of the family of the
deceased. - (1) In case a Government servant dies in
harness after the commencement of these rules and
the spouse of the deceased Government servant is not
already employed under the Central Government or a
State Government or a Corporation owned or
controlled by the Central Government or a State
Government, one member of his family who is not
already employed under the Central Government or a

-2-
State Government or a Corporation owned or
controlled by the Central Government or a State
Government shall, on making an application for the
purposes, be given a suitable employment in
Government service on a post except the post which
is within the purview of the Uttar Pradesh Public
Service Commission, in relaxation of the normal
recruitment rules if such person -
(i) fulfils the educational qualifications prescribed for
the post,
(ii) is otherwise qualified for Government service, and
(iii) makes the application for employment within
five years from the date of the death of the
Government servant:
................................................"
4. In other words, as per the aforesaid provision the
compassionate appointment can only be made to a dependant of
a deceased who dies in harness provided the spouse of the
deceased is not in the employment of Central Government or
State Government at the relevant time. Admittedly, the spouse of
the deceased (i.e. mother of the petitioner) was already employed
in the Education Department of Uttarakhand. Therefore, this
Court is of the opinion that the petitioner was not eligible for
appointment on compassionate ground.
5. There was another hurdle with the petitioner, viz., his
father died in the year 2001 and the petitioner moved an
application for compassionate ground in the year 2005 after he
attained the age of majority i.e. after 4 years. The appointment
on compassionate grounds is made only in order to ease the
burden and remove the immediate hardship of the family which
has fallen upon the family due to the death of its only bread-winner. The whole idea is that the bereaved family may be able to
tide over the financial crisis fallen upon it. In the instant case,
the petitioner's father died in 2001 and, at a belated stage, i.e.
after 3 years, the petitioner filed an application for appointment
on compassionate ground.

-3-
6. In any case, the petitioner was not even eligible for
appointment, his claim is not liable to be considered in view of
the provisions of Rule 5 of the Rules as stated above. Moreover,
the petitioner relied upon a recommendation made by the
Inspector General of Police (Personnel) by which it was stated
that some relaxation can be given in Rule 5 of the Rules. This
Court is of the opinion that the said relaxation cannot be done by
the Government by an administrative order as the Rule 5 is
presently governed by Article 309 of the Constitution of India.
Moreover, the relaxation of such rules will have detrimental
effect in the services and will open a pandora's box, therefore, no
relevance whatsoever is to be given to the recommendation made
in favour of the petitioner.
7. In the light of aforesaid, the Court does not find any merit
in the writ petition. The writ petition fails and is hereby
dismissed accordingly. No order as to costs.
 (Sudhanshu Dhulia, J.)
Dated 08th May, 2013 Rawat
